## Why Marketing Agencies Need a CRM

A CRM for marketing agencies is the system that keeps your new business pipeline healthy while your team delivers for existing clients.

Marketing agencies operate under a unique tension: the people who win new business are often the same people who deliver the work. When delivery gets intense, prospecting stops. When a big client churns, the pipeline is empty. An agency CRM makes relationship management systematic rather than dependent on individual memory.

Here are the pain points a marketing agency CRM addresses:

- **New business pipeline chaos**: Leads arrive from referrals, inbound inquiries, RFPs, networking events, and LinkedIn outreach. Without a structured [pipeline](/features/pipeline), follow-ups are inconsistent and opportunities slip through the cracks.
- **Client relationship gaps**: Account managers know their clients deeply, but that knowledge lives in their heads and inboxes. When someone goes on leave or leaves the agency, continuity suffers. [Contact management](/features/contact-management) with shared records and activity history helps.
- **Retainer blindness**: Which contracts end this quarter? Without visibility, renewal conversations happen too late.
- **Scattered communications**: Client emails in personal inboxes, briefs in chat tools, meeting notes in documents. An agency CRM with a [unified inbox](/features/email-integration) brings client conversations next to the client record.
- **No upsell visibility**: A client on a social media retainer could benefit from paid media management, but nobody surfaces the opportunity because the data is not connected.
- **Handoff failures**: A deal is won, but the transition from sales to delivery loses context: scope, expectations, key contacts, pricing discussions.

## What Is a CRM for Marketing Agencies?

A CRM for marketing agencies (also called an agency CRM or digital agency CRM) is software that helps marketing, advertising, and creative agencies manage client relationships, track new business opportunities through a sales pipeline, keep client conversations in one place, and maintain visibility across the agency lifecycle, from lead to pitch to retainer to renewal.

A marketing agency CRM handles the dual-track nature of agency work: winning new clients and servicing existing ones. It connects business development with account management, so nothing falls between the cracks when a prospect becomes a client.

A modern CRM for agencies also works with AI to draft pitch emails, summarize client call notes, and suggest next actions, which takes administrative work off account managers and new business teams.

## Key Features of a CRM for Marketing Agencies

Choosing the right CRM for your marketing agency means evaluating features against how agencies actually work. Here is what Customermates offers, and where its limits are.

### Visual Pipeline Management

Track every opportunity from initial inquiry to signed contract on a drag-and-drop [kanban board](/features/pipeline). See which pitches need follow-up, which proposals are pending, and which deals are ready to close. Customermates has one configurable deal stage column, with a probability per stage for weighted pipeline totals. To keep new business, upsells, and partnerships apart, add a single-select field such as "Deal type" and save a view or group the board by it.

### Client and Contact Management

Every client organization gets a record with key contacts (marketing directors, CMOs, procurement leads), activity history, and linked deals. Store client data with typed custom fields in your [contact management](/features/contact-management) system: retainer value, contract dates, services delivered, campaign types, and satisfaction score. When a new team member takes over an account, the context is there.

### Retainer and Contract Tracking

Use custom fields to track contract start and end dates, monthly retainer values, hours allocated, scope of services, and renewal status. A saved view sorted by contract end date shows which contracts expire this quarter. Customermates does not send reminders, and it does not track hours used; that data stays in your time tracking tool unless you sync it into a custom field through the REST API.

### Campaigns and Client Reporting

Record campaigns as deals on the client organization, or as services on those deals, with custom fields for campaign type and status. [Dashboards](/features/reporting) show pipeline and revenue by account, service line, or owner for internal reviews. Campaign performance data stays in your ad and analytics platforms; Customermates does not import it natively.

### Following Up on New Business

Speed-to-response matters when competing for new agency business. Customermates has no built-in workflow builder, email sequences, or reminders; follow-up runs through interfaces you control, described under [workflow automation](/features/workflow-automation):

- **Routines on Customermates Cloud:** the Mate assistant runs a saved prompt on a schedule or on a CRM event, for example listing proposals that have not moved for three days and drafting a follow-up for each for you to review. Starter includes [[commercial.entitlement.starter.includedRoutinesPerUser]] routine per user and Pro includes [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedRoutinesPerUser]].
- **Webhooks and REST:** send new or changed deals to your own systems, or create deals from your website form tool.
- **n8n, operated separately:** connect through the Customermates community node, REST, or webhooks to build your own flows, such as posting new RFP inquiries to your team chat.

### Unified Inbox

On Pro and above, the [unified inbox](/features/email-integration) brings email (Gmail, Outlook, IMAP), LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Instagram, and Telegram into Customermates, where you link each conversation to the right contact. When a CMO emails about expanding scope or a prospect replies to your LinkedIn outreach, the team sees the conversation next to the contact's history. SMS and phone calls are not channels, and the inbox is not available self-hosted.

### AI Agents That Operate Your Agency CRM

External AI clients such as Claude or ChatGPT connect through [MCP](/docs/mcp) with [[derived.mcp.tools.total]] tools, so an AI agent can read and write your CRM directly and do the data entry account managers avoid: moving deals through stages, updating retainer fields, and logging meeting notes. You choose and pay for the AI client. Ask an agent to draft pitch emails, summarize client meeting notes, outline a proposal from the deal record, or review won and lost deals. On Customermates Cloud, the built-in Mate assistant works inside the app, drafts replies in the unified inbox on Pro, and asks for your confirmation before it sends a message or deletes anything.

### Project Handoff from Sales to Delivery

When a deal closes, the transition from business development to account management needs to be smooth. With scope discussions, pricing, key contacts, and expectations stored on the deal and client record, your delivery team starts with the full picture rather than a forwarded email chain.

### API and Integrations

Connect Customermates with your project management tools (Asana, Monday, ClickUp), time tracking, invoicing systems, and marketing platforms through the REST API and webhooks. These connections are customer-built; n8n, which you run separately, is one way to build them without writing an application yourself.

## How to Choose the Right CRM for Your Marketing Agency

Here is what to evaluate when choosing an agency CRM:

1. **Dual-track workflow support**: Can you manage new business development and existing client accounts in the same system without them interfering?
2. **Pipeline flexibility**: Can you customize stages to match your actual sales process, and separate new business, upsells, and partnerships?
3. **Retainer and contract management**: Can you track contract dates, retainer values, and renewal status with custom fields?
4. **Automation**: Which follow-ups, reminders, and notifications are built in, and which do you have to build with external tools? Are automations included or sold as add-ons?
5. **Integration capabilities**: Does it connect to your project management, time tracking, invoicing, and marketing tools, natively or through an API?
6. **Data control**: Where is your data stored, which subprocessors are involved, is a DPA available, and can you self-host if your clients require it?
7. **Pricing transparency**: Are there per-client fees, tiered feature locks, or hidden costs that scale with your client base?
8. **Setup effort**: How much configuration and data migration does it take before your team can work in it?
