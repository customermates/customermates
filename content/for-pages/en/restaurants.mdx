## Where CRM fits in a restaurant

A point-of-sale system handles orders and payments. Reservation and hospitality platforms handle tables, bookings, visit history, guest preferences, and loyalty. A CRM is most useful for relationship-led revenue that behaves like a sales process: catering, private events, corporate dining, venue partnerships, and supplier relationships.

Customermates covers that sales side. It is not a guest CRM.

| Restaurant need | Customermates | Restaurant operations system |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Corporate account context | Organizations, contacts, relations, notes, and activity | Billing, order, and account execution |
| Catering or event opportunity | Deals, custom stages, filters, and Kanban | Menu, quote document, deposit, booking, and event plan |
| Next commercial action | Task linked to a CRM record | Booking deadline, staffing, production, and delivery schedule |
| Individual conversation | Unified inbox from Pro | Reservation and official event communication |
| Integrations | REST, webhooks, MCP, and a separately run n8n instance | POS, reservations, loyalty, payment, inventory, and events |

## How a catering or event enquiry runs

A corporate catering enquiry is a genuine CRM opportunity: a contact represents the buyer or planner, an organization represents the company, and a deal represents the commercial conversation. The event itself remains in booking and operational tools.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add the corporate customer">
    Add the business, venue partner, or organization and connect the buyer,
    assistant, event planner, or other stakeholders.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the deal">
    Add a deal for the catering request, private event, corporate dining
    relationship, or partnership under discussion.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Configure commercial stages">
    Use stages such as "New enquiry", "Qualified", "Requirements confirmed",
    "Proposal sent", "Customer deciding", "Won", or "Closed".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep only the event basics">
    Store the event month, customer type, source, or external booking identifier
    rather than copying the operational event file.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Record the next action and hand off">
    Attach a task for the follow-up. Menu, dietary details, quote document,
    deposit, booking, staffing, production, and service remain in their
    specialist systems.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Corporate accounts, partners, and suppliers

Organizations and contacts preserve the relationship between the restaurant and a corporate customer, hotel, venue, event agency, concierge, or local partner. A source field can show how the opportunity arrived. Filtered views let you review open deals by stage, source, or customer.

Supplier relationships can also use organization and contact records for business context and the next conversation. Purchase orders, delivery schedules, price lists, food-safety records, inventory, and invoices remain in procurement, inventory, or accounting software.

Customermates does not calculate catering revenue, guest lifetime value, average spend, loyalty performance, supplier score, or event profitability. Financial and operational figures keep coming from the system that calculates them.

## Guest data and privacy boundaries

Do not use general CRM fields to create a parallel record of restaurant visits, favorite tables, meal history, dietary restrictions, or allergies. Those values can be sensitive, become stale quickly, and require operational controls Customermates does not provide.

For an event opportunity, keep only the business relationship context needed by the sales team. Detailed attendee, menu, dietary, allergy, seating, and service information belongs in the approved booking or event system. Permissions apply by resource and action, not by field; you can limit read access to the records a user is assigned to. Customermates holds no certification.

## Messaging, campaigns, and AI

The [unified inbox](/features/unified-inbox) is available on Customermates Cloud from Pro upward for email, LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Instagram, and Telegram. It brings individual conversations into the CRM and links them to contacts. It does not capture reservations, create guest profiles, send bulk marketing, or run birthday, loyalty, and re-engagement campaigns. Messages attach to the contact, not to an event deal. Self-hosted deployments do not include the inbox.

An external AI client you connect through [MCP](/docs/mcp) can read and edit corporate contacts and deals within your permissions. Guest preferences, visit analysis, allergy data, menus, proposals, and automated marketing are out of scope.

Tasks have no due dates or recurrence. To create tasks by date or send approved messages automatically, run your own scheduler or a separately operated [n8n workflow](/features/workflow-automation).

## Connections to restaurant systems

If a POS, reservation, event, or accounting system exposes a suitable interface, you can build a connection that exchanges only the basics (customer, deal, external booking ID) through the [REST API and webhooks](/features/api), MCP, or a separately run n8n instance.

You own provider contracts, consent, data minimization, mapping, validation, retries, and duplicate handling. There is no native connection to OpenTable, Resy, SevenRooms, Toast, or other POS, reservation, loyalty, inventory, ordering, or payment systems. To move existing corporate accounts and contacts in, Customermates imports Excel workbooks (XLSX), one record type at a time; there is no CSV import or scheduled sync.
