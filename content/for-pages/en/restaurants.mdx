## Where CRM fits in a restaurant

A point-of-sale system handles orders and payments. Reservation and hospitality platforms handle tables, bookings, visit history, guest preferences, and loyalty. A CRM is most useful for relationship-led revenue that behaves like a sales process: catering, private events, corporate dining, venue partnerships, and supplier relationships.

Customermates provides that business-development layer. Calling it a guest CRM would imply capabilities it does not have.

| Restaurant need | Customermates | Restaurant operations system |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Corporate account context | Organizations, contacts, relations, notes, and activity | Billing, order, and account execution |
| Catering or event opportunity | Deals, custom stages, filters, and kanban | Menu, quote document, deposit, booking, and event plan |
| Next commercial action | Task linked to a CRM record | Booking deadline, staffing, production, and delivery schedule |
| Individual conversation | Managed unified inbox from Pro | Reservation and official event communication |
| Integration surface | REST, webhooks, MCP, and separate n8n option | POS, reservations, loyalty, payment, inventory, and events |

## A supported catering and event workflow

A corporate catering enquiry is a genuine CRM opportunity: a person represents the buyer or planner, an organization represents the company, and a deal represents the commercial conversation. The event itself remains in booking and operational tools.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the corporate account">
    Add the business, venue partner, or organization and connect the buyer,
    assistant, event planner, or other stakeholders.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the opportunity">
    Add a deal for the catering request, private event, corporate dining
    relationship, or partnership under discussion.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Configure commercial stages">
    Use stages such as new enquiry, qualified, requirements confirmed, proposal
    prepared externally, customer deciding, won, or closed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep a bounded event reference">
    Store the event month, account type, source, or external booking identifier
    rather than copying the operational event file.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Record the next action and hand off">
    Attach a task for manual follow-up. Menu, dietary details, quote document,
    deposit, booking, staffing, production, and service remain in their
    specialist systems.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Corporate accounts, partners, and suppliers

Organizations and contacts preserve the relationship between the restaurant and a corporate customer, hotel, venue, event agency, concierge, or local partner. A source field can show how the opportunity arrived. Filtered views can help a user review open deals by stage, source, or account.

Supplier relationships can also use organization and contact records for business context and the next conversation. Purchase orders, delivery schedules, price lists, food-safety records, inventory, and invoices remain in procurement, inventory, or accounting software.

Customermates does not calculate catering revenue, guest lifetime value, average spend, loyalty performance, supplier score, or event profitability. Any financial or operational measure remains authoritative in the system that calculates it.

## Guest data and privacy boundaries

Do not use general CRM fields to create a parallel record of restaurant visits, favorite tables, meal history, dietary restrictions, or allergies. Those values can be sensitive, become stale quickly, and require operational controls Customermates does not provide.

For an event opportunity, keep only the business relationship context needed by the sales team. Detailed attendee, menu, dietary, allergy, seating, and service information belongs in the approved booking or event system. Customermates permissions operate by resource and action, not by field or individual record, and no hospitality or GDPR compliance certification is claimed.

## Messaging, campaigns, and AI

The managed [unified inbox](/features/unified-inbox) is available from Pro upward for supported email and messaging channels. It brings individual conversations into the CRM and allows participants to be associated with contacts. It does not capture reservations, create guest profiles, send bulk marketing, or run birthday, loyalty, and re-engagement campaigns. A message is not automatically attached to an event deal. Self-hosted deployments do not include the inbox.

An authorized external AI client can use supported [MCP](/docs/mcp) actions on CRM records. It can operate supported corporate-contact and opportunity data within user permissions. It does not infer guest preferences, analyze visits, write allergy data, create a verified menu or proposal, or run autonomous marketing.

Tasks have no native due dates or recurrence. Date-driven task creation or an approved external message requires a customer-operated scheduler or separate [n8n workflow](/features/workflow-automation). That workflow and its providers remain customer-operated.

## Connections to restaurant systems

If a POS, reservation, event, or accounting system exposes a suitable interface, a customer-built connection can exchange a minimal account, opportunity, or external booking reference through the [REST API and webhooks](/features/api), MCP, or the separate n8n integration.

The customer owns provider contracts, consent, data minimization, mapping, validation, retries, and duplicate handling. No native OpenTable, Resy, SevenRooms, Toast, POS, reservation, loyalty, inventory, ordering, or payment synchronization is claimed. Customermates also has no CRM record importer.
