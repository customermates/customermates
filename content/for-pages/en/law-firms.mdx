A prospective client writes while you are in court, and by the time you reply they may have called the next firm. Customermates, an open-source CRM made in Germany, keeps every inquiry and client conversation on the record with a dated next step. On Customermates Cloud from Pro, Mate or your own AI agent (Claude, ChatGPT or any client that speaks MCP) drafts the reply when you ask or on a routine you set up; you review, edit and send. It is a relationship CRM, not practice management: docketing, documents, time, billing and trust accounting stay in a tool such as MyCase or, in Germany, Actaport or RA-MICRO, compared in the table below. If you want one system for everything, practice management with a lead module fits better.

## CRM or practice management: which one do you need?

Many "legal CRM" lists mix two kinds of product. Practice management runs the matter after engagement. A CRM runs the relationship before, around and after it. Customermates publishes this page; vendor details were checked on 8 October 2026 on each vendor's own website.

| If you need | Look at | What the vendor's site lists |
|---|---|---|
| Cases, documents, calendar and billing in one product | [MyCase](https://www.mycase.com/) | Case management, billing and payments with time tracking, a client portal, web-based client intake forms and document generation |
| Legal intake with marketing automation and e-signature | [Lawmatics](https://www.lawmatics.com/) | Client intake, legal CRM, marketing automation, e-signature for engagement agreements, time and billing; integrates with Clio, MyCase and Filevine |
| German practice management with files, deadlines and beA | [Actaport](https://www.actaport.de/) | Cloud practice management for files across the mandate, deadlines, documents, RVG billing workflows and one mailbox for email, beA and a client portal |
| German practice management including accounting | [RA-MICRO](https://www.ra-micro.de/) | Law office software covering addresses, fees, financial accounting and enforcement, with e-file and beA |
| A general sales CRM with legal intake templates | [Pipedrive](https://www.pipedrive.com/de/industries/crm-for-law-firms) | Auto-filled documents, client intake templates and trackable e-signatures, plus third-party apps |
| A relationship CRM next to your practice management, with a unified inbox and AI drafts | Customermates | Our own product, described on the rest of this page |

A workable split is two systems: practice management for the matter, Customermates for intake, referrals and the long relationship with each client. Store the matter number from your practice management system on the deal, so you can find the same matter in either system. Nothing syncs on its own; a link between the two needs an automation of your own (see below).

## What a law firm needs from a CRM

- **Speed on new inquiries.** A prospective client who waits a day may call the next firm. You need a list of inquiries nobody has answered, oldest first.
- **A conflict search before engagement.** Former clients, adverse parties and their companies must be findable by name, in one system rather than in partners' address books.
- **Referral sources you can count.** If other lawyers, accountants and former clients send you work, you need to know who sent what, and whom you have not thanked.
- **One client, many matters.** A company you advised on a lease returns for an employment dispute three years later. The history should be on one record.
- **Confidential correspondence.** Not every colleague should read every client thread, and the CRM must respect that.

## A law firm setup in Customermates

A new workspace starts without lists: you create the lists you need (contacts, organizations, deals or your own) in **Data model**, or let AI setup build them in the cloud. Here is one way to map five lists for a firm; every field name is an example.

| Record type | Used for | Example custom fields |
|---|---|---|
| Contacts | Clients, adverse parties, opposing counsel, referrers, experts | Relationship (single choice: Client, Former client, Adverse party, Opposing counsel, Referral source, Expert), Last thanked (date) |
| Organizations | Client companies, adverse companies, referring firms | Relationship (same options), Related entities (text) |
| Deals | Each inquiry, and the matter once engaged | Stage, Practice area (single choice), Source (single choice: Referral lawyer, Referral accountant, Former client, Website, Event), Referred by (text), Conflict search done on (date), Matter number (text), Fee model (single choice) |
| Services | Your fee items, each with a price | Initial consultation, Flat fee contract review, Block of hours |
| Tasks | Next steps, assigned to a person | A "Due on" date field you add, because tasks have no built-in due date |

In **Data model** you can rename any list under **General**, for example Contacts to Clients and Deals to Matters, or keep Deals and read it as "inquiry or matter". Link every party to the deal: the client, the adverse party and the referrer each become a linked contact or organization. Deals have assigned users, so the responsible lawyer and the associate can both be on the matter.

### Intake stages

The deal stage is a single choice field you define, and each option can carry a probability for the weighted pipeline. A starting point:

1. New inquiry (10%)
2. Consultation scheduled (20%)
3. Conflict search done (30%)
4. Engagement letter sent (60%)
5. Engaged (100%)
6. Declined or not engaged (0%)

Weighted values are your fee items times your own probabilities, not a forecast. On the [pipeline board](/features/pipeline) each inquiry is a card you drag to the next stage. After "Engaged", the matter work moves to practice management, and the deal stays as the record of where the client came from.

### How the conflict search works, and where it stops

Global search (Cmd or Ctrl+K) matches record titles and searchable field values in the lists you may read, finds contacts by any email address or phone number in their contact details and looks into the text of attached files. So a conflict search in Customermates is only as good as this habit: every adverse party and every related company becomes its own contact or organization, linked to the matter, with Relationship set. Do not rely on a name that appears only in free text such as a note. Search returns only what your role may read, and with Assigned read access you find only the records assigned to you, so run it with a role that reads all clients. On the list pages you can also filter a text field such as Related entities with "contains".

A link between a deal and a contact does not say in which role the contact took part. If a person was an adverse party in one matter and a client in another, write that in the contact's notes and check the linked deals. Customermates is a search, not a conflict-check system: it does not flag conflicts, and the assessment stays with the lawyer and your firm's procedure.

### Five saved views

1. **Unanswered inquiries:** deals in New inquiry, sorted by created date, oldest first.
2. **Engagement letters out:** deals in Engagement letter sent, sorted by last update.
3. **Referral sources to thank:** contacts with Relationship Referral source, sorted by Last thanked.
4. **Where work comes from:** deals grouped by Source, with a dashboard widget that counts engaged deals per Source.
5. **Quiet former clients:** organizations with Relationship Former client, sorted by last update, oldest first.

Share **Quiet former clients** with the whole firm or with selected roles, so every partner works from the same list. More on lists and filters is in the [records guide](/docs/app-records).

### Client conversations in one inbox (Pro and above)

In Customermates Cloud on Pro and higher, the [unified inbox](/features/unified-inbox) brings email from Gmail, Outlook or IMAP together with LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Instagram and Telegram, and you reply on the original channel. Link a participant to a contact once, and their conversations appear on that contact's timeline; linking a conversation to a deal is a separate step, by you or by Mate. SMS and phone calls are not channels.

Two details matter for a firm. Conversations from a lawyer's own accounts are private: colleagues, admins included, see a thread only after the owner shares it. And Pro includes [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedAccountsPerUser]] connected account per user, so a shared intake mailbox that the whole team works from needs Business, which includes [[commercial.entitlement.business.includedAccountsPerUser]] accounts per user and can share a whole account.

### Intake follow-up with Mate (Customermates Cloud)

Mate is the assistant in Customermates Cloud and uses hosted AI credits. Ask it in the chat, for example: "Create a deal for the inquiry from this email, link the sender as a contact and set the stage to New inquiry." For the daily check, set up a [routine](/docs/app-routines) on a schedule, for example every day at 8:00, with instructions like these (a weekdays-only schedule can be set by asking Mate, not in the dialog):

```text
Find deals in the stages New inquiry and Consultation scheduled that have
not been updated for 2 days. For each one, read the latest inbox
conversation with its linked contacts. If the client wrote last and has
no answer, save a short, polite reply as a draft in that conversation
that offers two consultation slots. Do not send anything. End with a
list: deal, stage, days since the last update, draft saved yes or no.
```

## What Customermates does not do for law firms

- No deadline calendar, docketing or statute-of-limitations tracking. A date field on a deal is information, not a deadline control, and tasks have no due dates or reminders.
- No document management. Keep the matter file in your document system and its link on the deal; single documents can go into a File field, and files in messages stay in the inbox thread.
- No time tracking, billing, invoices or trust accounting. A webhook fires when a deal changes, so an n8n workflow you run separately can pass an engaged client to your billing tool if that tool has an API.
- No web intake forms, no e-signature and no client portal. A form tool can create deals through the REST API.
- No beA connection, no SMS or phone integration, no calendar booking and no mobile app.
- No certification such as ISO 27001 or SOC 2.

## Client data and professional secrecy

- In Customermates Cloud, the primary production database is configured in the AWS Frankfurt region. That is a product setting, not a contractual data-residency guarantee. Hosting and the other subprocessors, US providers among them, are on the [subprocessors page](/subprocessors), and a [data processing agreement](/dpa) is available.
- Your confidentiality rules decide whether a cloud CRM may hold client information. In Germany, [§ 43e BRAO](https://dejure.org/gesetze/BRAO/43e.html) requires a contract in text form that binds the provider to confidentiality, and client consent where the service serves a single mandate. The Customermates DPA has a general confidentiality clause; whether it meets those requirements is for your firm to check before client data goes in.
- A custom role with Assigned read access limits an associate or a contract lawyer to the records assigned to them. The audit log records who changed which record, on every plan.
- To decide yourself where client data lives, [self-host](/features/self-hosted) the open-source core. A self-hosted installation has no inbox, no Mate and no routines; MCP, REST and webhooks remain.

## Frequently asked questions

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="What is a CRM for law firms?">
Software for the client relationship around the matter: inquiries and intake stages, referral sources, former clients and the people and companies on the other side. Practice management covers the matter itself: files, deadlines, time and billing. Many firms use both.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What are the top CRM tools for lawyers?">
It depends on which job you mean. For practice management with intake built in, English-language options include MyCase, and German firms at products such as Actaport or RA-MICRO. For intake with marketing automation, Lawmatics is legal-specific. For a relationship CRM with a unified inbox next to your practice management, Customermates fits; it is our own product, so weigh that.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Does a solo lawyer need a CRM?">
A spreadsheet works while you remember every open inquiry. It stops working when you are in court and three inquiries wait, or when you cannot say which colleague sent you work this year. A solo lawyer pays [[commercial.price.starter.monthly]] per month on Starter, or [[commercial.price.pro.monthly]] on Pro with one mailbox in the inbox.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can Customermates run conflict checks?">
It can search, not check. Global search finds records by name, by contact details and by the text of attached files, so every adverse party you record as its own contact or organization shows up. It does not flag conflicts, only shows what your role may read, and does not replace your firm's conflict procedure.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can I connect Customermates to my practice management or billing software?">
Not natively. Customermates has REST, webhooks and a community node for n8n, which you run separately. If your practice management tool has an API, a workflow can create the client there when a deal reaches Engaged, or write the matter number back to the deal.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Will AI replace the law firm CRM?">
AI changes who types into the CRM more than whether you need one. Mate or an MCP client such as Claude can log an inquiry, move a deal and draft a reply, but it needs client history, stages and referral data to work from, and those live in the CRM. Mate sends a message without asking when you tell it to send, so say in each request or routine whether it should only save a draft.
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
