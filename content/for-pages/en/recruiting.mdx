A recruiting CRM keeps the relationships a recruitment business lives on: the client companies that give you mandates, the hiring managers inside them, and the candidates you place. Customermates fits the business-development side of that work for small agencies, executive search boutiques and independent recruiters. Clients, hiring managers and candidates sit in one contact base, each search mandate is a deal on a board, and from the Pro plan the email, LinkedIn and WhatsApp conversations around them land in one inbox. It is not an applicant tracking system: no job postings, no career page, no application forms and no per-candidate hiring pipeline. If those are what you need, the comparison further down names tools built for them.

## CRM or ATS: decide which job you are buying for

Most products sold as a "recruiting CRM" are an applicant tracking system with a CRM attached. The ATS half handles open roles: it posts the job, collects applications, parses CVs and moves each applicant through screening, interviews and offer. The CRM half handles relationships that outlast any one role: winning clients, staying in touch with passive candidates and knowing who spoke to whom last.

| Your situation | What to look for |
|---|---|
| You publish jobs and receive many applications per role | An ATS or an ATS plus CRM suite |
| You are an in-house HR team hiring for your own company | An ATS for companies hiring directly, such as [JOIN](https://join.com/de/preise) |
| You run a staffing desk with many placements a month | A staffing suite with job orders, submissions and placements built in |
| You win retained or contingent mandates through relationships and headhunt rather than advertise | A relationship CRM like Customermates, or a light ATS next to it |

## What a recruitment agency needs from a CRM

- **Two kinds of people in one network.** A hiring manager at a client today is a candidate in two years, and a placed candidate becomes a client contact. Keeping them in separate systems loses that history.
- **Mandates move like deals.** A search goes from first conversation to briefing, signed mandate, shortlist, interviews and placement or loss. The fee depends on how far it gets.
- **Candidates go quiet for months.** The person who turned you down in spring may be ready in autumn. Your talent pool needs a list sorted by last contact, not memory.
- **Conversations happen on LinkedIn.** Sourcing and first contact often run through LinkedIn messages or InMail, follow-ups by email and phone, and a few candidates prefer WhatsApp.
- **Off-limits rules.** If you promise clients not to approach their employees, you need to see whether a candidate's current employer is a client before you write to them.

## A recruiting setup in Customermates

Customermates has five record types. Under **My Company › Settings** you can rename some of them from a preset list, for example Deals to Jobs and Organizations to Companies. Every field name below is an example; custom fields can be text, single select, date, currency, email, phone or link.

| Record type | Used for | Example custom fields |
|---|---|---|
| Organizations | Client companies and the companies candidates work at | Relationship (single select: Prospect, Active client, Past client), Off-limits (single select: Yes, No), Industry |
| Contacts | Hiring managers and candidates | Contact type (single select: Candidate, Hiring manager, Referee), Availability (Open to move, Passive, Not looking, Placed), Practice area, Notice period, Salary expectation (currency), Retention until (date) |
| Deals, renamed Jobs | Each search mandate | Stage, Search type (Retained, Contingent, Interim), Mandate signed on, Shortlist due, Target salary (currency) |
| Services | Your fee types | Retained search fee, Contingent fee, Interim day rate |
| Tasks | Next steps, assigned to a recruiter | A "Due on" date field you add, because tasks have no built-in due date |

A candidate is linked to their current employer as an organization and to every job they are shortlisted for. The job is linked to the client and the hiring manager. Open a candidate and you see every mandate they were part of; open a client and you see every job and every person you know there. The assigned users on a contact show which recruiter owns the relationship.

### Mandate stages and fee value

The stage is a single-select field you define, and each option gets a win probability. A starting point: Conversation 10%, Briefing 25%, Mandate signed 50%, Shortlist sent 65%, Final interviews 80%, Placed 100%, Lost 0%.

A deal's value is price times quantity of its linked services. Fees vary per search, so one workable pattern is a service "Placement fee" priced at €1,000 per unit, with the quantity set to the expected fee in thousands. A €27,000 fee is quantity 27, and at Shortlist sent the weighted value is €17,550. That is arithmetic from your own probabilities, not a prediction.

### Saved views for shortlists and the Monday call list

1. **Shortlist for one job:** contacts with Contact type Candidate linked to that job, grouped by Availability.
2. **Warm candidates:** candidates with Availability Open to move whose record has not been updated for 60 days, oldest first.
3. **Mandates by shortlist date:** jobs in Mandate signed, sorted by Shortlist due.
4. **Retention review:** contacts whose Retention until date has passed.

Saved views are personal, so each colleague sets the same filters and saves their own view. More on filters and views is in the [records guide](/docs/app-records).

### Candidate and client conversations in one inbox (Pro and above)

In Customermates Cloud on Pro, Business and Enterprise, the [unified inbox](/features/unified-inbox) brings together email from Gmail, Outlook or IMAP, LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Instagram and Telegram. For LinkedIn you can connect a Classic account, or Classic with Recruiter if you hold a LinkedIn Recruiter seat; when you write from a contact, **Send as** then offers Recruiter InMail. Link a participant to a contact once and the conversations show on that contact's timeline. The [LinkedIn integration page](/features/linkedin-integration) has the details.

Two details decide the plan. Pro includes [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedAccountsPerUser]] connected account per user, so a recruiter who wants a mailbox and LinkedIn in the inbox needs Business, which includes [[commercial.entitlement.business.includedAccountsPerUser]]. And conversations from your own accounts stay private: colleagues see a thread only after you share it, or after you share the whole account on Business and above. If two recruiters must see who last wrote to a candidate, agree to share those threads.

### Candidate briefings and a weekly check-in with Mate (Customermates Cloud)

Mate is the assistant in Customermates Cloud. It runs on hosted AI credits included per active user on every cloud plan; reading inbox conversations needs Pro or higher. Before a client call, ask in the chat:

> Summarize what we know about Anna Berger: her current role, the jobs we put her forward for, and our last three conversations on LinkedIn and email.

For a weekly check-in, set up a [routine](/docs/app-routines), a saved instruction Mate runs on a schedule or when a record changes. Choose **Every week** under **Repeats**, Monday at 08:00, with instructions like these. Each run has a credit cap, so keep the list of candidates per run short; the [routine limits](/docs/app-routines#what-are-the-limits) are in the docs.

```text
Find contacts with Contact type Candidate and Availability Open to move
that were last updated more than 60 days ago. For each one, read the latest
inbox conversation. If it exists, save a short, personal check-in as a
draft reply in that conversation. Do not send anything and do not delete
anything. End with a list: name, practice area, days since the last update,
draft saved yes or no.
```

<AcquisitionCallout title="Ask for drafts, not sends" variant="boundary">
  Mate and routines can send email and chat messages without asking first,
  so tell a candidate routine to save drafts only. Filter the inbox by Draft:
  has some, read each draft and send it yourself. A new LinkedIn InMail cannot
  be saved as a draft, so a routine can only draft replies in existing
  conversations.
</AcquisitionCallout>

A second routine can react to an event: when a job's Stage changes to Shortlist sent, create a task "Collect interview feedback" linked to that job and assigned to the same recruiter. Starter includes [[commercial.entitlement.starter.includedRoutinesPerUser]] routine per user and Pro includes [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedRoutinesPerUser]].

Prefer your own AI client? Connect Claude, ChatGPT or another client through [MCP](/docs/mcp). It works on every plan and on self-hosted installations, with model use billed by your AI provider.

## What Customermates does not do for recruiting

- No job postings, job board distribution, career page or application forms.
- No CV parsing. A CV that arrives by email stays as an attachment in the inbox thread; records have no file attachments. Store CVs in your file storage and put the link in a link field on the contact.
- No per-candidate stage inside a job. A job has one stage; whether a candidate is submitted, interviewing or rejected for that job is not a field on the link. Note it in the job's notes, or use an ATS when you run many candidates per role.
- No interview scheduling or calendar booking. Pro adds read-only calendar access.
- No email sequences or bulk outreach, no candidate scoring or data enrichment, no mobile app.
- Tasks have no due dates or reminders. Add a date field and a saved view.

## Candidate data and GDPR

Candidate records are personal data, and in the EU you need a legal basis, a retention period and a way to delete on request. Customermates gives you the building blocks, not a compliance process.

- In Customermates Cloud the application database is configured in the AWS Frankfurt region. Hosting and other subprocessors, US providers among them, are on the [subprocessors page](/subprocessors), and a [data processing agreement](/dpa) is available.
- A Retention until date and the retention view above show whom to review. Deleting is a manual step: Mate asks for approval before each deletion, and unattended routines cannot delete.
- A custom role with Assigned read access limits a researcher or freelance recruiter to the records assigned to them.
- Customermates holds no certification such as ISO 27001 or SOC 2.

## Frequently asked questions

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="What is the difference between a recruiting CRM and an ATS?">
An ATS manages applicants for open roles: job posting, applications, CV parsing and the interview funnel. A recruiting CRM manages relationships over time: clients, hiring managers and passive candidates you stay in touch with. Many recruitment suites bundle both. Customermates covers the CRM side only.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can one CRM manage candidates and clients at the same time?">
Yes. In Customermates both are contacts, told apart by a single-select field such as Contact type, and both link to companies and jobs. A person who moves from candidate to hiring manager keeps one record with their full history.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Which CRM do recruitment agencies use?">
Agencies that advertise roles and handle many applications usually pick an ATS plus CRM suite such as Bullhorn, Recruit CRM, Crelate, Loxo or Manatal. Small search firms that headhunt and live on client relationships can use a general CRM configured for recruiting. Customermates is one such CRM and our own product, so weigh that.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How do I keep shortlists of candidates?">
Link the candidates to the job, then save a view of contacts filtered by that job and Contact type Candidate. Saved views are personal, so each colleague saves their own. A per-candidate status inside the job is not available.
</FaqItem>

</FaqItem>
