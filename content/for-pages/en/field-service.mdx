## CRM and field-service management solve different jobs

Search results for field-service CRM frequently describe full FSM products. That category may include appointment booking, skill-based dispatch, work orders, mobile technician workflows, route optimization, equipment history, parts, time capture, estimates, invoices, and payments.

A relationship CRM addresses the commercial layer: Who is the customer? Which stakeholders matter? What service opportunity is being considered? Which contract or upgrade conversation needs follow-up? Customermates addresses that layer.

| Field-service need | Customermates | FSM or operational system |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer and stakeholder context | Contacts, organizations, relations, notes, and activity | Operational account and site file |
| Enquiry or contract opportunity | Deals, custom stages, filters, and kanban | Booking, work order, and service execution |
| Next relationship action | Task linked to a CRM record | Technician calendar, dispatch, route, and SLA |
| Reference to operational work | User-defined external identifier | Equipment, service history, parts, photos, and time |
| Interfaces | REST, webhooks, MCP, and separate n8n option | Scheduling, FSM, inventory, accounting, and payment |

## A supported field-service relationship workflow

Create a deal for a new-service enquiry, contract opportunity, upgrade, replacement, or renewal. Use the CRM to preserve the commercial context and hand won work to the operational system.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the customer account">
    Add the customer organization and the people involved in buying,
    coordinating, or approving the service relationship.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the commercial opportunity">
    Represent the enquiry or contract conversation as a deal rather than a work
    order or service visit.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Configure relationship stages">
    Use stages such as new, qualified, site review completed externally,
    proposal sent, follow-up, won, or closed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep an external reference">
    Add a validated site, contract, estimate, or FSM identifier in a custom
    field when the two systems need a bounded handoff.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Record the next action and hand off">
    Attach a task for manual follow-up. Run appointment, dispatch, job,
    equipment, inventory, time, and billing activity in the FSM.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Account, site, and equipment context

Organizations and contacts can represent the customer account and its people. A custom field can hold a concise site label or external identifier for conversation context. That does not create a site hierarchy, asset register, work-order history, warranty record, or preventive-maintenance schedule.

Keep equipment models, serial numbers, installations, access instructions, service notes, photos, parts, technician time, and completion evidence in the operational system. A link or identifier may be enough for the CRM user to reach that authoritative record when needed.

For multi-location or enterprise service customers, Customermates can preserve the buying and relationship context. It does not coordinate regional technician capacity, enforce service-level agreements, or run a high-volume field workforce.

## Tasks, mobile expectations, and automation

Tasks hold next steps but have no due dates or statuses. Customermates has no technician calendar, availability engine, route optimization, offline mobile application, or double-booking prevention. The web app adapts to phone screens but does not replace a native FSM app for technicians.

A customer-operated scheduler or separate [n8n workflow](/features/workflow-automation) can create a task from an approved external date. The separate workflow owns its schedule, provider calls, mapping, retries, and monitoring. Customermates does not include a dispatch or recurring-maintenance automation engine.

On Pro and above, the [unified inbox](/features/unified-inbox) on Customermates Cloud brings in conversations from email, LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Instagram, and Telegram. You link conversations to contacts; messages are not attached to a site, piece of equipment, work order, or deal. Self-hosted deployments do not include the inbox.

## AI and integrations

An external AI client connected through [MCP](/docs/mcp) can read and edit customer and opportunity data within your permissions. It does not create a verified service report, infer parts used, optimize a schedule, dispatch a technician, or analyze equipment failures.

Where an FSM exposes a suitable interface, a customer-built connection can exchange minimal account or opportunity context through the [REST API and webhooks](/features/api), MCP, or a separate n8n integration. You own source authority, permissions, mapping, validation, retries, and duplicate handling.

There is no native Jobber, ServiceTitan, Salesforce Field Service, GPS, routing, inventory, accounting, quote, invoice, or payment connector, and appointments cannot be written to a calendar. To bring existing accounts in, Customermates imports and exports Excel workbooks (XLSX), one record type at a time; there is no scheduled sync with an FSM.
