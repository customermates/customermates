## Where CRM fits inside an insurance company

Insurance carriers and MGAs depend on policy-administration, claims, underwriting, billing, actuarial, document, and regulatory systems. A CRM has a narrower role: managing the people and organizations around distribution, partnerships, and commercial relationships.

Customermates can give a partnership team a structured view of agencies, brokers, service partners, their contacts, and a relationship opportunity. It is not a lighter replacement for a core insurance platform, and it should not hold sensitive insurance operations merely because its fields are configurable.

| Need | Customermates | Core insurance system |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agency and broker context | Organizations, contacts, relations, notes, and activity | Authoritative appointment and distribution record |
| Partner-development opportunity | Deals, custom stages, filters, and kanban | Formal due diligence and appointment controls |
| Next relationship action | Tasks linked to CRM records | Regulated workflow, service levels, and escalations |
| Connected conversation | Managed unified inbox from Pro | Official communication and document archive |
| Integration surface | REST, webhooks, MCP, and separate n8n option | Policy, claims, underwriting, billing, and data platforms |

## A practical broker-relationship workflow

Create an organization for a brokerage or agency and connect the relevant business contacts. A deal can represent a commercial relationship such as partner evaluation, onboarding, or a joint distribution opportunity. The deal should not represent a policy, claim, underwriting submission, or commission statement.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the partner organization">
    Add the agency, brokerage, service provider, or other distribution partner
    and keep its business context in one place.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Associate the people involved">
    Connect relationship owners, decision-makers, and operational contacts
    without importing policyholder or claims records.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Configure the commercial stages">
    Use stages such as identified, contacted, due diligence handled externally,
    commercial review, active relationship, or closed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add bounded partner fields">
    Store non-authoritative values such as partner type, territory, internal
    owner, relationship status, or an external-system reference.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Hand formal controls to the core system">
    Appointments, eligibility, contracts, production data, commissions, and
    regulatory evidence continue in their authoritative platforms.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What belongs outside the CRM

Customermates does not administer policies, accept first notice of loss, assign claims, calculate reserves, adjudicate coverage, perform underwriting, price risk, calculate commissions, collect premiums, issue documents, or maintain actuarial models.

Written premium, loss ratios, risk classifications, policyholder health or financial information, claims evidence, and formal appointment status should not be copied into generic CRM fields unless the insurer has separately designed and approved that data flow. Resource permissions do not provide field- or record-level isolation, and Customermates carries no insurance compliance certification.

For partner-performance analysis, keep the authoritative measures in the insurance data platform. A bounded reference or a manually reviewed summary can support a conversation, but a CRM view is not the regulatory or financial report.

## Messaging, AI, and system connections

The managed [unified inbox](/features/unified-inbox) is available from Pro upward for supported individual conversations. Participants can be associated with contact context. The inbox is not a policyholder campaign engine, and a message is not automatically linked to a policy, claim, underwriting file, or deal. Self-hosted deployments do not include messaging.

An authorized external AI client can use supported [MCP](/docs/mcp) actions on general CRM records. It does not become an underwriting, claims, actuarial, or regulatory agent. Sensitive insurance data should not enter an external client or model path without independent approval of the complete data flow.

A customer-built connection can use the [REST API and webhooks](/features/api), MCP, or the separate [n8n integration](/features/workflow-automation). The insurer owns source authority, field mapping, permissions, validation, retries, duplicate handling, and operational controls. No native policy-administration, claims, underwriting, carrier, or actuarial integration is claimed.

## Deployment and commercial fit

The managed Customermates database runs in an EU region; this is not a GDPR or insurance certification. Self-hosting lets the operator choose application and database infrastructure, but connected services and the surrounding control environment remain separate.

Starter includes the core CRM. Pro adds the managed unified inbox and one connected account per user. Self-hosted deployments use Starter entitlements and do not include the unified inbox. See the [pricing page](/pricing) for current plan details.

Choose Customermates when a distribution or partnership team needs a configurable relationship CRM beside established insurance systems. Choose a specialist platform when the product itself must run policy, claims, underwriting, rating, billing, commission, or regulatory operations.
