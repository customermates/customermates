An architecture firm needs a CRM for the part of the work that happens before a contract: deciding which competitions and RFQs to pursue, meeting submission deadlines, following up after an interview, and staying in touch with past clients and the consultants you team with. Customermates is an open-source, AI-native CRM built in Germany that does that part. It does not track hours, invoice fees or plan staff, so if you want one system for the whole practice, the limits section below names practice management tools built for architects.

## What an architecture firm needs from a CRM

Most CRMs assume one buyer and a short sales cycle. Architecture works differently:

- **Several parties on one project.** A client or developer, an owner's representative, a project manager, and your structural, MEP and landscape consultants all belong to the same pursuit.
- **Fixed deadlines.** A competition or RFQ has a submission date that does not move. Missing it ends the pursuit.
- **Different rules per procurement route.** After presenting to a private client you follow up. In a public procurement, communication usually goes through the channels the procedure defines, so you document rather than chase.
- **Long gaps between commissions.** A client may come back with the next building years later. The firm that kept in touch is on the shortlist.
- **References on demand.** An RFQ asks for comparable projects and named consultants, and you need them in minutes, not after a search through old folders.

## An architect setup in Customermates

Customermates has five record types. Under **My Company › Settings** you can rename Deals to **Projects** and Services to **Offerings**, so the app speaks your language. Every field name below is an example.

| Record type | Used for | Example custom fields |
|---|---|---|
| Organizations | Clients, developers, public authorities and consultant firms | Organization type (Single Select: Private client, Developer, Public authority, Consultant, Contractor) |
| Contacts | People at those organizations | Role (Single Select: Client representative, Project manager, Structural engineer, MEP engineer, Landscape architect), Referred by |
| Projects (Deals) | Each pursuit, from first inquiry to contract | Stage, Procurement route (Direct commission, Invited competition, Open competition, Public RFQ), Submission deadline (Date & Time), Interview date (Date), Construction value (Currency), Building type, Design phase, Project folder (Link) |
| Offerings (Services) | Fee packages with a price | Feasibility study, Planning application package, Fee estimate per €1,000 |
| Tasks | Next steps, assigned to a person | A "Due on" date field you add, because tasks have no built-in due date |

A project's value comes from its linked offerings, price times quantity. For a bespoke fee, an offering "Fee estimate per €1,000" priced at €1,000 with a quantity of 85 gives a project worth €85,000.

### Pursuit stages and weighted values

The stage is a Single Select field you define, and each option gets a win probability. A starting point: Lead 10%, Go or no-go 20%, Preparing submission 30%, Submitted 40%, Shortlisted 60%, Negotiating fee 80%, Won 100%, Lost 0%.

A project worth €85,000 in Shortlisted has a weighted value of €51,000. That is arithmetic from your own probabilities, not a forecast. On the [pipeline board](/features/pipeline) each project is a card you drag between stages.

Track the design phase in its own Single Select field (Schematic design, Design development, Construction documents, Bidding, Construction administration) rather than in the stage. A board grouped by Design phase and filtered to won projects shows where every active job stands, and dragging a card there changes its phase.

### Saved views for the week

1. **Submissions due:** projects in Preparing submission, sorted by Submission deadline.
2. **Waiting on a decision:** projects in Submitted or Shortlisted, sorted by Interview date.
3. **Active projects by phase:** won projects on a board grouped by Design phase.
4. **Consultants by discipline:** contacts with a consultant role, grouped by Role, for the team list in your next RFQ.
5. **Past clients gone quiet:** organizations of type Private client or Developer, sorted by last update, oldest first. This works if you update the organization's notes after each contact.

Saved views are personal, so each colleague sets the same filters and saves their own view. The [records guide](/docs/app-records) explains grouping, filters and import.

### Win rates by procurement route

Filter projects to Won and Lost and group them by Procurement route. Customermates shows the count and value per group, so you can compare how direct commissions, invited competitions and public RFQs turn out for your firm. It does not calculate a win rate or conversion statistics for you; the division is yours.

### Client and consultant conversations (Pro and above)

In Customermates Cloud on Pro, Business and Enterprise, the [unified inbox](/features/unified-inbox) brings email from Gmail, Outlook or IMAP together with LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Instagram and Telegram. You reply on the original channel. Link a participant to a contact once, and every conversation with that address appears on the contact's timeline.

Pro includes [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedAccountsPerUser]] connected account per user and Business [[commercial.entitlement.business.includedAccountsPerUser]]. Conversations from your own accounts stay private: colleagues, admins included, see a thread only after you share it, or after you share the whole account on Business and above.

### A weekly pursuit review with Mate (Customermates Cloud)

Mate is the assistant inside Customermates Cloud and runs on hosted AI credits; reading inbox conversations needs Pro or higher. Before an interview, ask it in the chat for a summary of the client's history: past projects, recent emails and open tasks.

For the weekly review, set up a [routine](/docs/app-routines), a saved instruction that Mate runs on a schedule or when a record changes. Choose **Every week** under **Repeats**, Monday at 08:00, with instructions like these:

```text
Find projects in the stages Submitted and Shortlisted that have not been
updated for 14 days. If the procurement route is Direct commission or
Invited competition, read the latest inbox conversation with the client
contact and, if my last message has no answer, save a short follow-up as a
draft reply. If the route is Open competition or Public RFQ, do not draft
anything. Do not send anything. End with a list: project, client, stage,
route, days since the last update, draft saved yes or no.
```

<AcquisitionCallout title="Ask for drafts, not sends" variant="boundary">
  Mate and routines can send email and chat messages without asking first, so
  tell a review routine to save drafts only. Filter the inbox by Draft: has
  some, read each draft and send it yourself.
</AcquisitionCallout>

A second routine can react to an event: trigger it when a project is updated, with Stage under **Changed fields**, and instruct it to create a task "Confirm consultant team" linked to that project and assigned to the same people, but only if the new stage is Won. Starter includes [[commercial.entitlement.starter.includedRoutinesPerUser]] routine per user and Pro [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedRoutinesPerUser]].

Prefer your own AI client? Connect Claude, ChatGPT or another client through [MCP](/docs/mcp). It works on every plan and on self-hosted installations, with model use billed by your AI provider.

## What Customermates does not do for architects

- No time tracking, fee invoicing, project controlling or resource planning.
- No proposal or SOQ templates and no document generation. Keep submissions in your file storage and the link on the project.
- No file storage on records and no CAD or BIM integration. Files sent in messages stay in the inbox thread.
- Tasks have no due dates, reminders or notifications. Use a date field and a saved view, or a routine.
- No mobile app and no calendar booking.

If you want one system for pursuits, hours and fees, look at practice management software. Customermates publishes this page; vendor details were checked on 8 October 2026 on each vendor's own website.

| If you need | Look at | What the vendor's site lists |
|---|---|---|
| Pipeline, project planning, time tracking and invoicing for A&E firms | [Monograph](https://www.monograph.com/) | Pipeline management, proposal builder, time tracking, invoicing, payments and project accounting |
| CRM with time, billing and accounting | [BQE CORE](https://www.bqe.com/core) | CRM, project management, time and expense tracking, billing and accounting; names architecture, engineering and consulting |
| An ERP for larger project-based firms | [Deltek Vantagepoint](https://www.deltek.com/en/products/project-erp/vantagepoint) | CRM and pipeline, project management, accounting and resource planning for architecture, engineering and consulting firms |
| A business development CRM for AEC with proposal tools | [TrebleHook](https://treblehook.com/) | Pursuit management, proposal templates, file storage, eSignatures and win-rate dashboards; runs on Salesforce |
| Resourcing, timesheets and project finance | [CMap](https://www.cmap.io/) | Pipeline and quoting, resource planning, timesheets, job costing and invoicing for architects, engineers and consultants |

A workable split is two systems: Customermates for pursuits and relationships, and your practice management tool for hours and fees. A webhook fires when a project changes, so an [n8n workflow](/docs/n8n) you run separately can pass a won project to the other tool, provided that tool has an API.

## Client data and confidentiality

In Customermates Cloud, the primary production database is configured in the AWS Frankfurt region. Hosting and the other subprocessors, US providers among them, are on the [subprocessors page](/subprocessors), and a data processing agreement is available. A custom role with Assigned read access limits a freelancer to the projects assigned to them. Customermates holds no certification such as ISO 27001 or SOC 2; if a public client requires one, check that before you move their data.

## Frequently asked questions

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="What is a CRM used for in architecture?">
It holds the business side before and between projects: who your clients and consultants are, which competitions and RFQs you are pursuing, when each submission is due, and what was said in the last conversation. Design work, hours and fees live in other software.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can a CRM help an architecture firm organize leads and track proposal win rates?">
Yes, if every pursuit is a record with a stage and a procurement route. In Customermates you group won and lost projects by route and compare counts and values. The win rate itself you calculate from those numbers; Customermates does not compute it.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Do I need a CRM or practice management software?">
If hours, fees and staffing are your problem, practice management software such as Monograph, BQE CORE or Deltek Vantagepoint covers them along with a pipeline. If those already work and your problem is follow-up, deadlines and relationships, a CRM such as Customermates is the smaller step. It is our own product, so weigh that.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can I track design phases in Customermates?">
Yes, with a Single Select field for the phase and date fields for milestones such as a client sign-off or permit submission. A board grouped by that field shows which projects are in which phase. There are no automatic milestone reminders.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Does a small studio need a CRM?">
A spreadsheet works while you can keep every open pursuit in your head. It stops working when two competitions overlap with a live project and a past client calls. A sole practitioner pays [[commercial.price.starter.monthly]] per month on Starter.
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
