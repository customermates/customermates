## Why architects need a CRM

A CRM for architects is not optional overhead, it is the system that turns scattered project pursuits into a repeatable pipeline. Architecture firms juggle long project timelines, complex client relationships, RFP responses, design phase transitions, consultant coordination, and portfolio references simultaneously. When that information lives in spreadsheets, email threads, and the heads of individual partners, projects slip through the cracks.

The problems compound as architecture firms grow:

- **RFP responses get lost**: Your firm spends days preparing a detailed RFP response for a public library project. The submission goes out, nobody follows up, and the selection committee awards the contract to a firm that stayed in contact throughout the evaluation period.
- **Design phase transitions stall**: A residential project moves from schematic design to design development. Without a central record of project phases, the principal architect has to chase updates from every team member to understand where things stand.
- **Past clients are forgotten**: You designed a corporate headquarters five years ago. The same client is now planning a campus expansion. Without a system to maintain the relationship, they shortlist firms they found through new searches instead of calling you.
- **Consultant coordination falls apart**: The structural engineer needs updated drawings. The landscape architect is waiting on site survey data. The MEP consultant has questions about the program. Without a central contact record, nobody knows who talked to whom.
- **Referral networks go cold**: A commercial developer referred three projects to your firm last year. You never tracked the source, never thanked them, and the referrals stop coming.
- **Portfolio opportunities are missed**: You completed a mixed-use project, but nobody linked it to the client record. When a similar RFQ comes in, you cannot quickly find relevant past work.

Architecture firm CRM software solves these problems by giving every team member, from principals to project architects to marketing coordinators, a single source of truth for relationships, pursuits, and projects.

## How Customermates works as architect CRM software

Customermates adapts to how architecture firms operate, from initial project pursuit through design phases and into long-term client relationships. You configure stages and custom fields to match your practice; it is a CRM, not a project management or accounting system.

### Project pipeline management

Every project pursuit becomes a deal in your [pipeline](/features/pipeline). Track each opportunity from initial inquiry through qualification, RFP/RFQ response, interview, selection, contract negotiation, and project kickoff. The Kanban board gives principals and business development managers a visual overview of the pursuit pipeline: drag opportunities between stages, filter by project type or value, and spot stalled pursuits early.

Customermates does not send follow-up reminders. Keep the submission date in a custom date column and use a saved view of the "RFP submitted" stage sorted by that date, so no pursuit sits unanswered. On Customermates Cloud, a [routine](/docs/app-routines) can have the Mate assistant list stalled pursuits every week and draft follow-ups for you to review.

### Design phase tracking

Architecture projects move through distinct phases: programming, schematic design, design development, construction documents, bidding, and construction administration. The deal stage is one configurable column, so use it for the pursuit and track the design phase in a custom single-select field. Group or filter by that field to show the principal architect which projects are in SD, which are in DD, and which are in CA.

For firms working under fee structures tied to design phases, this gives a clear overview of where fees are pending.

### RFP and proposal management

Track every RFP, RFQ, and SOQ your firm pursues. Store submission dates, required team members, selection committee contacts, interview dates, and decision timelines as custom fields and linked contacts on the deal record. Customermates does not generate proposal documents; keep those in your document system and store the link on the deal.

For automated notifications, send [webhooks](/features/workflow-automation) to n8n (operated separately) or another tool, which can notify the team when a pursuit reaches the interview stage.

Over time, filter won and lost pursuits by project type, size, and client to see where your firm wins most consistently.

### Client relationship management

Every client has a profile with [contact management](/features/contact-management): organization details, key decision-makers, past projects, active pursuits, notes, and activity history. When a past client reaches out about a new project, you have the context of your relationship at hand.

This matters in architecture because relationships span decades. The developer you designed a retail center for in 2019 may come back with a mixed-use tower in 2028.

### Contractor and consultant coordination

Architecture projects involve a network of consultants: structural engineers, MEP engineers, landscape architects, interior designers, lighting consultants, and specialty contractors. Maintain records for every collaborator with specialties, past project experience, contact details, and notes. Link consultants to the projects they have worked on so you can assemble the right team for each new pursuit.

When an RFP requires you to name your consulting team, filter your network by specialty to find the right partners.

### Portfolio and project history

Link completed projects to client records, team members, and consultant partners. Classify projects with custom single-select fields by type (residential, commercial, institutional, healthcare, hospitality, mixed-use), size, and location. When preparing for a new pursuit, filter for relevant past work that demonstrates your firm's experience in that project type.

## Key features for architecture firms

### Pipeline management with Kanban view

Visualize your pursuit pipeline on a Kanban board. Define stages that match your workflow: Lead, Qualification, RFP Response, Interview, Selection, Contract, Active Project, Complete. Drag pursuits between stages as they progress. Filter by project type, estimated fee, or assigned principal. Stage probabilities give you weighted pipeline totals.

### Custom fields for architecture data

Track the data points that matter to your practice: project type, estimated construction value, estimated fee, project size in square meters, design phase, building permit status, sustainability targets, and milestone dates. Typed custom columns live directly on deal and contact records.

### Organization and contact relationships

Architecture projects involve multiple stakeholders. Link contacts to organizations and deals, and use a custom field to record each contact's role: client, developer, owner's representative, general contractor, structural engineer, MEP consultant, landscape architect. Track which developers, contractors, and consultants you have worked with repeatedly and identify your strongest referral sources.

### Automation with webhooks and n8n

Customermates has no built-in workflow builder. It emits [webhooks](/features/workflow-automation) on record changes and exposes a REST API, and n8n (operated separately) connects through the Customermates community node. With these you can:

- Notify the team when a project advances to the interview stage
- Create a task checklist when a project's design phase changes from SD to DD
- Flag pursuits that have sat in "RFP submitted" for more than a week
- Alert leadership when high-value pursuits reach the selection stage

### Unified inbox for client and consultant communication

On Pro and above, email, LinkedIn, and WhatsApp come together in one [unified inbox](/features/email-integration) inside the CRM, and you link each conversation to the client or consultant. RFP questions, consultant coordination, and client updates stop bouncing between personal inboxes. The Mate assistant can draft replies that you confirm before they are sent.

### AI clients that operate your CRM

Connect Claude, ChatGPT, or another AI client through the MCP server ([[derived.mcp.tools.total]] tools) and let it do the data entry: log pursuit updates, move deals between stages, and draft project narratives for RFP responses. You choose and pay for the AI client. It can also summarize lengthy RFP requirements. Learn more in our guide on [using ChatGPT to summarize meeting notes](/blog/chatgpt-summarize-notes).

### Data protection and self-hosting

Client financials, fee structures, consultant agreements, and design information are sensitive. In the managed cloud, the application database runs in the AWS Frankfurt region; hosting and other subprocessors, including US providers, are listed on the [subprocessors page](/subprocessors), and a [DPA](/dpa) is available. If you need full control over where data lives, for example for government projects or competitions, self-host the open-source core.

### Open source and transparent

Customermates is [open source](https://github.com/customermates/customermates). You can inspect the code, contribute improvements, or self-host and choose where your data lives. No vendor lock-in and no forced migrations.

## CRM for architecture firms vs. generic CRM

Generic CRMs like [Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/) or [HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/) are built for standard B2B sales cycles. Architecture does not work that way. Projects involve selection committees, months-long RFP cycles, multi-phase design workflows, and client relationships that span decades.

Adapting a large CRM to architecture often means customization and ongoing maintenance. Customermates is smaller: configurable stages, typed custom fields, and saved views that you set up yourself, with the flexibility of open source.

Compared to AEC-oriented platforms such as [TrebleHook](https://treblehook.com/), BQE CORE, or Scoro, which combine CRM with project management, time tracking, or billing to different degrees, Customermates focuses on CRM and relationship management, without bundling project management and accounting features you may already have elsewhere. It is open source and self-hostable. If your firm also handles construction projects, see our [CRM for construction](/for/construction) page. For individual practitioners, our [CRM for freelancers](/for/freelancers) may also be relevant.
