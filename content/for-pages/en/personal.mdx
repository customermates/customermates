A personal CRM keeps one record per person you know, with notes on what you discussed and a date for the next conversation. Customermates works as one when most of your network is professional (clients, prospects, former colleagues, recruiters, collaborators) and you want your email, LinkedIn or WhatsApp conversations next to each person. If you mainly want an app on your phone that alerts you to birthdays, syncs your phone contacts or keeps a private journal about friends and family, a dedicated personal CRM such as Monica, Dex or Queue fits better.

Customermates is an open-source, AI-native CRM built in Germany for small client-facing teams. Everything on this page works for a single user.

## What a personal CRM has to do

An address book knows phone numbers, a spreadsheet goes stale, and neither tells you who to call this week. A personal CRM earns its place with four jobs:

- **Context:** how you met, what the person is working on, what you promised to send.
- **A due list:** one date per person and one list sorted by it. Without that, it is an archive.
- **The last conversation:** it usually sits in an inbox or on LinkedIn, not in your notes.
- **Your existing contacts:** Google Contacts and LinkedIn export files, so the CRM has to import them.

Dex and Queue add phone apps and keep-in-touch alerts on top. Customermates does the four jobs and leaves the phone side out.

## A personal CRM setup in Customermates

Customermates has five record types, and a personal setup uses four of them. Under **My Company › Settings**, the **Data model** section lets you rename them from a preset list: Contacts to People, Deals to Opportunities, Tasks to Follow-ups. The field names below are examples.

| Record type | Used for | Example custom fields |
|---|---|---|
| Contacts | Everyone you want to stay in touch with | Circle (Single Select: Clients, Prospects, Former colleagues, Mentors, Friends), Cadence (Single Select: Every 2 weeks, Monthly, Quarterly, Yearly), Next check-in (Date), How we met (Text), Phone (Phone), Birthday (Date) |
| Organizations | Employers and client companies | Relationship (Single Select: Client, Former employer, Target company) |
| Deals, renamed Opportunities | Job applications, proposals and introductions in progress | Stage (Single Select: Idea, In conversation, Interview or proposal, Won, Dropped) |
| Tasks, renamed Follow-ups | Things you promised someone | A "Due on" Date field you add, because tasks have no built-in due date |

A contact stores its channels (email address, LinkedIn, WhatsApp number, Instagram, Telegram) and one notes field; a plain phone number goes into a Phone field. A former colleague who changed jobs can stay linked to both employers.

### Two saved views do the reminding

Customermates sends no reminders, so the list you open is the system:

1. **Due for a check-in:** contacts sorted by Next check-in, ascending. The earliest dates come first, so anyone whose date has passed is at the top.
2. **Gone quiet:** contacts whose **Updated at** date is **Older than…** 90 days. This filter stays relative, so the list is current whenever you open it. It reads the contact record's own update date, so update Next check-in or the notes after each conversation.

On a custom Date field, filters compare against a fixed date; only the built-in created and updated dates offer relative choices. The [records guide](/docs/app-records) covers filters, sorting and views.

### Conversations next to each person (Pro and above)

In Customermates Cloud from Pro, the [unified inbox](/features/unified-inbox) brings in email from Gmail, Outlook or IMAP, plus LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Instagram and Telegram, and you reply on the original channel. Once a participant is linked to a contact, every conversation with that address or handle appears on the contact's timeline; an email address from your import is enough for the match.

Pro includes [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedAccountsPerUser]] connected account per user, so pick the one where your professional conversations happen, usually your mailbox or LinkedIn. Mailbox, LinkedIn and WhatsApp together need Business, which includes [[commercial.entitlement.business.includedAccountsPerUser]]. Phone calls and SMS are not channels.

### Meeting prep and a Monday list with Mate (Customermates Cloud)

Mate, the assistant in Customermates Cloud, uses hosted AI credits that every cloud plan includes per active user; reading the inbox needs Pro or higher. Before a coffee meeting, ask in the chat:

> I'm meeting Jana Weber tomorrow. Summarize my notes on her, our last emails and anything I promised her.

For the weekly list, set up a [routine](/docs/app-routines): choose **On a schedule**, **Every week**, Monday at 08:00, and give it instructions like these:

```text
Find contacts whose Next check-in is today or earlier, earliest first,
at most 10. For each one, read my notes and the latest inbox
conversation, then save a short, personal check-in message as a draft
reply in that conversation. Do not send anything and do not change
any field. End with a list: name, Circle, Next check-in, draft saved
yes or no.
```

## Getting your contacts in

<Steps>
  <Step title="Export your contacts">
    Google Contacts exports a Google CSV or a vCard file. LinkedIn's "Get a copy of your data" includes your connections as a CSV file, with email addresses only for connections who allow it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the file as .xlsx">
    The import reads Excel workbooks of up to 10,000 rows and does not accept CSV, so open the file in a spreadsheet program and save it as .xlsx. A vCard file needs converting first.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Import">
    Create Circle, Cadence and Next check-in first. Then open Import and export on the Contacts list, choose Add from file and map the columns, with email addresses and LinkedIn profile links going to the contact's channels. New contacts need a first and a last name.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect one channel">
    On Pro or higher, connect your mailbox or LinkedIn under My Profile › Channels.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The import is a one-time copy; nothing syncs afterwards. Export works the same way, one record type at a time as an Excel workbook.

## What Customermates does not do as a personal CRM

- No native mobile app and no push notifications. Pages rearrange on narrow screens, so you can look someone up in your phone's browser.
- No sync with phone contacts, Google Contacts or LinkedIn, only one-time Excel imports.
- No reminders or birthday alerts. A Birthday date field stores the date and stays silent.
- No contact enrichment: it does not look up job changes, photos or company data.
- No business-card scanning, no calendar booking and no email sequences.
- No free plan. The self-hosted open-source core costs no licence fee but has no inbox, no Mate and no routines.

If one of these is why you want a personal CRM, pick Monica, Dex or Queue from the table above.

## Where your data lives

In Customermates Cloud, the primary production database is configured in the AWS Frankfurt region, a product setting rather than a contractual residency promise. Hosting and the other subprocessors, US providers among them, are on the [subprocessors page](/subprocessors), and requests to Mate are processed by external AI providers. To keep notes about people on your own hardware, self-host the core. Customermates holds no security certification.

## Frequently asked questions

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="What is a personal CRM?">
A contact manager for one person that stores context about each relationship and when to get in touch again. A business CRM adds what a team shares, such as pipelines, assigned users and reports; Customermates is a business CRM that one person can set up for personal use.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What is the best CRM for personal use?">
It depends on what you need first: Monica for an open-source app for friends and family, Dex for reminders on every device and LinkedIn sync, Queue for a native Apple app. Customermates fits a mostly professional network; it is our own product, so weigh that.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can I build my own personal CRM for free?">
Yes. A spreadsheet with one row per person and a "next check-in" column covers the basics; our [Excel CRM template](/blog/excel-crm-template) shows a layout. Notion's gallery has free templates, and Monica can be self-hosted at no cost from Monica. The Customermates core is AGPL open source and self-hostable with no licence fee; you run the server, backups and updates, and the inbox and Mate are not included.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Does Customermates sync with my phone, and is there an app?">
No to both. There is no sync with phone contacts, Google Contacts, Outlook contacts or LinkedIn, and no app for iOS or Android. Import your contacts once from an .xlsx file, then add new people by hand, from an inbox conversation or by asking Mate. Customermates runs in the browser on your phone too.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can I track a job search or freelance leads in it?">
Yes. Rename Deals to Opportunities and create one per application or proposal, linked to the company and the recruiter or client. A Stage field with options such as Applied, Interview, Offer and Closed lets you drag each one across a board, and a Date field holds the day you promised to follow up.
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
