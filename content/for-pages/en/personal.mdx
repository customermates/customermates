Customermates is a business CRM that one person can set up this way, and it fits when your network is mostly professional (clients, prospects, former colleagues, recruiters, collaborators): in Customermates Cloud from Pro, email, LinkedIn or WhatsApp conversations sit on each record, and Mate or your own AI agent (Claude, ChatGPT or any client that speaks MCP) drafts the message for you to review, edit and send. ## What is a personal CRM, and how is it different from a business CRM? [#what-is-a-personal-crm]

A personal CRM stores context about the people you want to stay in touch with, plus a date for the next check-in. A business CRM adds what a team shares: pipelines, assigned owners, roles and reports. The same idea is also called a personal relationship manager or a people CRM.

- **Address book:** names and numbers, with no context and no due date.
- **Personal CRM:** one record per person with notes, a next check-in date and, in the better tools, the last conversation.
- **Business CRM:** the same, plus deals, teammates and reporting. One person can use it alone, as in the setup below.

## What does a personal CRM have to do?

An address book knows phone numbers, a spreadsheet goes stale, and neither tells you who to call this week. A personal CRM earns its place with four jobs:

- **Context:** how you met, what the person is working on, what you promised to send.
- **A due list:** one date per person and one list sorted by it. Without that, it is an archive.
- **The last conversation:** it usually sits in an inbox or on LinkedIn, not in your notes.
- **Your existing contacts:** Google Contacts and LinkedIn export files, so the CRM has to import them.

Dex, Queue and Mesh add apps and keep-in-touch alerts on top. Customermates does the four jobs and leaves the phone side out.

## When is a spreadsheet enough for a personal CRM? [#spreadsheet-or-personal-crm]

A spreadsheet or a Notion page is enough while you know everyone on the list, your conversations sit in one place and you open the sheet every week anyway. As a rule of thumb, that holds for a few dozen people. A dedicated tool starts to pay off when:

- **Conversations live in several inboxes:** email, LinkedIn and WhatsApp threads that you paste into notes so you do not lose the thread.
- **Dates slip:** there is a "next check-in" column, but nobody sorts by it.
- **The list outgrows your memory:** you cannot say who you have not spoken to in three months without scrolling.
- **You import from elsewhere:** a LinkedIn export or a Google Contacts file needs mapping, not retyping.

If in doubt, start with the sheet: our [Excel CRM template](/blog/excel-crm-template) shows a layout, and the [digital Rolodex guide](/blog/digital-rolodex) compares the tools that come after it. Moving later is an import, because Customermates reads Excel and CSV files.

## How do you set up Customermates as a personal CRM? [#set-up-customermates-personal-crm]

For one person, the setup comes down to five steps, and the rest of this section and the next cover them:

1. Create about four lists: contacts, organizations, opportunities and follow-ups.
2. Add the custom fields on contacts: Circle, Cadence and Next check-in.
3. Save two views: Due for a check-in and Gone quiet.
4. Import your contacts from a Google Contacts or LinkedIn export.
5. On Pro or higher, connect one mailbox or LinkedIn account, so conversations show up on each contact.

A new workspace starts without lists: you create the lists you need (contacts, organizations, deals or your own) in **Data model**, or let AI setup build them in the cloud. A personal setup needs about four. You can name them as you like (People instead of Contacts, Opportunities instead of Deals, Follow-ups instead of Tasks) and rename any list later in **Data model** under **General**. The field names below are examples.

| Record type | Used for | Example custom fields |
|---|---|---|
| Contacts | Everyone you want to stay in touch with | Circle (Single choice: Clients, Prospects, Former colleagues, Mentors, Friends), Cadence (Single choice: Every 2 weeks, Monthly, Quarterly, Yearly), Next check-in (Date), How we met (Text), Birthday (Date) |
| Organizations | Employers and client companies | Relationship (Single choice: Client, Former employer, Target company) |
| Deals, named Opportunities | Job applications, proposals and introductions in progress | Stage (Single choice: Idea, In conversation, Interview or proposal, Won, Dropped) |
| Tasks, named Follow-ups | Things you promised someone | A "Due on" Date field you add, because tasks have no built-in due date |

A contact stores its contact details (email address, phone number, LinkedIn, Instagram, Telegram) and one notes field; a phone number there can be called and also matches WhatsApp chats from the same number. A former colleague who changed jobs can stay linked to both employers.

### Two saved views do the reminding

Customermates sends no reminders, so the list you open is the system:

1. **Due for a check-in:** contacts sorted by Next check-in, ascending. The earliest dates come first, so anyone whose date has passed is at the top.
2. **Gone quiet:** contacts whose **Updated at** date is **Older than…** 90 days. This filter stays relative, so the list is current whenever you open it. It reads the contact record's own update date, so update Next check-in or the notes after each conversation.

On a custom Date field, filters compare against a fixed date; only the built-in created and updated dates offer relative choices. The [records guide](/docs/app-records) covers filters, sorting and views.

### Conversations next to each person (Pro and above)

In Customermates Cloud from Pro, the [unified inbox](/features/unified-inbox) brings in email from Gmail, Outlook or IMAP, plus LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Instagram and Telegram, and you reply on the original channel. Once a participant's address or handle is on a contact, every conversation with it appears on the contact; an email address from your import is enough for the match.

Pro includes [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedAccountsPerUser]] connected account per user, so pick the one where your professional conversations happen, usually your mailbox or LinkedIn. Mailbox, LinkedIn and WhatsApp together need Business, which includes [[commercial.entitlement.business.includedAccountsPerUser]]. Phone calls and SMS are not channels.

### Meeting prep and a Monday list with Mate (Customermates Cloud)

Mate, the assistant in Customermates Cloud, uses hosted AI credits that every paid cloud plan includes per active user; reading the inbox needs Pro or higher. Before a coffee meeting, ask in the chat:

> I'm meeting Jana Weber tomorrow. Summarize my notes on her, our last emails and anything I promised her.

For the weekly list, set up a [routine](/docs/app-routines): choose **On a schedule**, **Every week**, Monday at 08:00, and give it instructions like these:

```text
Find contacts whose Next check-in is today or earlier, earliest first,
at most 10. For each one, read my notes and the latest inbox
conversation, then save a short, personal check-in message as a draft
reply in that conversation. Do not send anything and do not change
any field. End with a list: name, Circle, Next check-in, draft saved
yes or no.
```

## Getting your contacts in

<Steps>
  <Step title="Export your contacts">
    Google Contacts exports a Google CSV or a vCard file. LinkedIn's "Get a copy of your data" includes your connections as a CSV file, with email addresses only for connections who allow it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the file">
    The import reads Excel workbooks and CSV files of up to 5,000 rows, so a CSV does not need saving as .xlsx first. Make sure the first row holds the column names. A vCard file needs converting first.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Import">
    Create Circle, Cadence and Next check-in first. Then open More actions on the Contacts list, choose Add from file and map the columns, with email addresses and LinkedIn profile links going to the contact's Contact details field. New contacts need a first and a last name.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect one channel">
    On Pro or higher, connect your mailbox or LinkedIn under Settings › Channels & calendars.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The import is a one-time copy; nothing syncs afterwards. Export works the same way, one record type at a time as an Excel workbook.

## What Customermates does not do as a personal CRM

- No native mobile app and no push notifications. Pages rearrange on narrow screens, so you can look someone up in your phone's browser.
- No sync with phone contacts, Google Contacts or LinkedIn, only one-time Excel or CSV imports.
- No reminders or birthday alerts. A Birthday date field stores the date and stays silent.
- No contact enrichment: it does not look up job changes, photos or company data.
- No business-card scanning, no calendar booking and no email sequences.
- The free plan has no inbox, no Mate and no routines, and neither does the self-hosted open-source core, which costs no licence fee.

If one of these is why you want a personal CRM, pick Monica, Dex, Queue or Mesh from the table above.

In Customermates Cloud, the primary production database is configured in the AWS Frankfurt region, a product setting rather than a contractual residency promise. Hosting and the other subprocessors, US providers among them, are on the [subprocessors page](/subprocessors), and requests to Mate are processed by external AI providers.
