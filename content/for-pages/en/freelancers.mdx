A freelancer needs a CRM once there are more open proposals and past clients than you can keep in your head, usually somewhere around the second busy project. Customermates is an open-source, AI-native CRM built in Germany that fits a one-person business: inquiries, proposals, clients and, from the Pro plan, the conversations around them sit in one place, and a date field tells you when your current work runs out. It handles winning work. Invoices, contracts and time tracking stay in other tools, and the limits section below names tools that bundle them.

## What freelancers need from a CRM

Many CRMs assume a sales team that sells full time. A freelancer sells in the gaps between delivery, so the CRM has to work in ten minutes a week. Four things matter most:

- **Knowing when the current work ends.** Feast or famine starts when nobody looks at the end date of the project in hand. The CRM should show it next to the pipeline, so you start selling four weeks before the gap, not after.
- **Proposals that wait for an answer.** You send three, get one reply and forget the other two while you deliver. You need a list sorted by how long each proposal has been quiet.
- **Inquiries on whatever channel the client likes.** A designer gets briefs on Instagram, a developer on LinkedIn, a translator by email, and the returning client writes on WhatsApp.
- **Past clients and who referred whom.** Repeat work and referrals usually cost the least effort to win, and they are the first thing to slip in a busy month.

If you are a solopreneur who sells packages or retainers rather than projects, the same setup works. Rename the stages and keep the end date.

## A freelancer setup in Customermates

Customermates has five record types. Here is one way to map them to freelance work; every field name is an example you can change.

| Record type | Used for | Example custom fields |
|---|---|---|
| Contacts | Clients and the people who refer them | Source (single select: Referral, Repeat client, LinkedIn, Website, Marketplace), Referred by |
| Organizations | Client companies, if you work for businesses | Client status (single select: Prospect, Active, Past) |
| Deals | Each inquiry, proposal or project | Stage, Proposal sent on, Project starts, Project ends, Proposal link |
| Services | What you sell, each with a price | Day rate, Logo package, Monthly retainer |
| Tasks | Next steps | A "Do by" date field you add, because tasks have no built-in due date |

### Five stages and the weighted value

The deal stage is a single-select field you define, and each option can carry a win probability. A lean freelance pipeline: Inquiry 10%, Call held 30%, Proposal sent 50%, Won 100%, Lost 0%. On the [pipeline board](/features/pipeline) each deal is a card you drag to the next stage.

A deal's value comes from the services linked to it, price times quantity. With a day rate of €800 and a proposal for ten days, the deal is worth €8,000, and in Proposal sent its weighted value is €4,000. That is arithmetic from your own probabilities, not a forecast, but adding up the column tells you whether enough work is coming.

### Three saved views for Monday

1. **Next gap:** Won deals sorted by Project ends. The top row is the date you need new work.
2. **Waiting proposals:** deals in Proposal sent, sorted by last update, oldest first.
3. **Quiet past clients:** contacts with Source Repeat client or organizations with Client status Past, sorted by last update, oldest first. Update a client's notes after each conversation so the sort stays meaningful.

Saved views are personal and save filters, sort and columns together. For more on fields and imports, see the [records documentation](/docs/app-records).

### Inquiries from every channel (Pro and above)

In Customermates Cloud on Pro and above, the [unified inbox](/features/unified-inbox) collects email from Gmail, Outlook or IMAP, plus LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Instagram and Telegram. You reply on the original channel and link the sender to a contact once; later conversations with that address then show on the contact's timeline.

Check the account count before you pick a plan. Pro includes [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedAccountsPerUser]] connected account per user, so a freelancer who wants a mailbox and Instagram in the inbox needs Business with [[commercial.entitlement.business.includedAccountsPerUser]]. Messages inside freelance marketplaces such as Upwork or Malt are not an inbox channel. Once a client moves to email or LinkedIn, those conversations can come in.

### A Monday routine with Mate (Customermates Cloud)

Mate is the assistant inside Customermates Cloud and uses hosted AI credits that every cloud plan includes per active user. A [routine](/docs/app-routines) is a saved instruction that Mate runs on a schedule or when a record changes. Under **Repeats**, choose **Every week**, Monday at 08:00, and write instructions like these:

```text
List deals in Proposal sent that have not been updated for 7 days, with
client, value and days waiting. List Won deals whose Project ends date is
within the next 30 days. For each waiting proposal, read the latest inbox
conversation with its contact and, if my message is the last one, save a
short follow-up as a draft reply. Do not send anything.
```

On Starter there is no inbox, so leave out the draft step and the routine returns the two lists. Starter includes [[commercial.entitlement.starter.includedRoutinesPerUser]] routine per user and Pro includes [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedRoutinesPerUser]], so on Pro a second routine can react to an event. Under **Events** pick the deal update, under **Changed fields** pick Stage and add a record filter for Stage Won, with the instruction to create a task "Ask for a referral" linked to that deal.

## What Customermates does not do for freelancers

- No invoices, payments, contracts or proposal documents. Keep the proposal PDF in your file storage and paste its link into a field on the deal.
- No time tracking. If you want hours on the deal, write them into a text field through the [REST API](/features/api) or an n8n workflow you run separately.
- Tasks have no due dates, reminders or notifications. A date field plus a saved view or a routine does the checking.
- No email sequences or bulk outreach, no booking page and no mobile app.
- No free plan. There is a [[commercial.trial.days]]-day trial, and the self-hosted core has no licence fee.

If one of these is why you are looking, a different tool fits better. Customermates publishes this page; vendor details were checked on 8 October 2026 on each vendor's own website.

| A freelance suite with time tracking and invoicing | [Bonsai](https://www.hellobonsai.com/pricing) | CRM, time tracking and iOS and Android apps from the Basic plan; invoices, proposals, contracts and a client portal from Essentials |

One workable split is two systems: Customermates for clients and pipeline, your invoicing tool for money. A webhook fires when a deal changes, so an n8n workflow you run can create an invoice draft in your accounting tool when a deal moves to Won, provided that tool has an API.

## Frequently asked questions

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="Do freelancers really need a CRM?">
Not on day one. A spreadsheet works while you have a handful of clients and can remember every open proposal. It stops working when proposals wait for weeks while you deliver, or when a past client writes on a channel you forgot they used. At that point, a list of waiting proposals and a visible project end date pay off.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is there a completely free CRM for freelancers?">
Yes. HubSpot's free CRM covers up to two users and 1,000 contacts, and CentralStationCRM has a free plan for 3 users and 200 contacts. Customermates has no free plan: it has a [[commercial.trial.days]]-day trial, Starter at [[commercial.price.starter.monthly]] per month, and an open-source core you can host yourself without a licence fee. Our [free CRM guide](/blog/free-crm) compares the options.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Which CRM suits a beginner or a one-person business?">
One you can set up in an afternoon and keep current in ten minutes a week. Choose a freelance suite such as HoneyBook or Bonsai if you want invoices and contracts in the same tool. Choose Customermates if your work comes from conversations across email, LinkedIn or Instagram and you invoice elsewhere; it is our own product, so weigh that.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can I build my own CRM in Excel or Notion?">
You can, and with a handful of clients it is a sensible start. A sheet or a Notion database holds clients and proposals well. It does not show which proposal has been quiet longest unless you update it by hand, and it does not hold your client conversations. Moving later is simple: Customermates imports Excel workbooks.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can Customermates handle invoicing?">
No. It does not create invoices, track payments or send payment reminders. Keep your accounting tool and connect it through webhooks and the REST API, for example with an n8n workflow you run that creates an invoice draft when a deal is won.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Will AI replace the CRM?">
AI changes who types into the CRM more than whether you need one. Mate or an MCP client such as Claude can log a call, move a deal and save a reply draft, but it needs the clients, stages and dates to work from, and those live in the CRM.
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
