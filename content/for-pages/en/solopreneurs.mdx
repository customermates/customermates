## Why solopreneurs need a CRM

When you run a one-person business, you are the sales team, the account manager, the support desk, and the marketing department, all at once. Every inquiry, every follow-up, every proposal lands on your desk. A CRM for solopreneurs keeps warm leads from going cold and revenue from slipping through the cracks.

A solopreneur CRM replaces scattered tools with one system that tracks every contact, every conversation, and every opportunity.

Common challenges a one-person CRM solves:

- **No single source of truth**: Contacts live in your email, notes in your phone, deal info in a spreadsheet. Context is always missing when you need it most.
- **Forgotten follow-ups**: A warm lead messaged you two weeks ago and you never replied. Without a list of open follow-ups, these opportunities disappear silently.
- **Hours lost to admin**: Copying data between tools, updating spreadsheets, writing the same emails: time you should spend on billable work.
- **No [pipeline visibility](/features/pipeline)**: How much revenue is in play? Which deals are about to close? Without a visual pipeline, you are guessing.
- **Scaling feels impossible**: Moving from 20 contacts to 200 means your informal methods collapse. A CRM for solo business owners gives you the structure to grow without hiring.

If you are a [freelancer](/for/freelancers), consultant, coach, or any type of solopreneur, a lightweight CRM is the difference between reactive chaos and a repeatable sales process.

## How solopreneurs actually use Customermates

### The consultant workflow

You meet a prospect at a networking event and add them to Customermates from your phone browser. You send a proposal and move the deal to "Proposal Sent". Your Monday routine on Customermates Cloud lists proposals without an answer after a week and drafts a follow-up for each. The client signs, and you record the next check-in date on the contact. Three months later, that contact shows up in your saved check-in view so you can ask about additional projects. Every step is tracked and nothing depends on memory.

### The online service provider workflow

Leads come in through your website contact form. A separately operated n8n workflow or your own script sends them to Customermates through the API and creates a task to respond. You qualify them using custom fields (budget, timeline, scope). Qualified leads move through your pipeline. After project delivery, your own automation or a routine drafts the request for a testimonial or referral. Your CRM becomes a client acquisition system that runs next to your service delivery.

### The coach or creator workflow

You manage a mix of one-on-one clients, group programs, and digital products. Custom fields track which program each contact is in, their renewal date, and engagement level. Your own n8n instance or another external service can handle onboarding emails, session reminders, and re-enrollment campaigns through the API. In Customermates, configured stage probabilities produce deterministic weighted deal values and pipeline totals across service lines; the product does not predict future revenue.

## Data protection and data ownership

As a solopreneur handling client data, you are responsible for GDPR compliance, especially if you work with European clients. Customermates gives you what you need to assess it:

- **Cloud data location**: the application database runs in the AWS Frankfurt region; hosting and other subprocessors, including US providers, are listed on the [subprocessors page](/subprocessors)
- **DPA**: a [data processing agreement](/dpa) under Art. 28 GDPR is available
- **Open source**: full transparency; audit the code yourself
- **Self-hostable**: run it on your own infrastructure and choose where the data lives
- **Open data access**: export contacts, organizations, or deals as an Excel workbook (XLSX), one record type at a time, or read records through REST
- **Consent fields**: record consent status and date in custom fields on the contact

Customermates holds no compliance certification. Read more about [CRM best practices](/blog/crm-best-practices) to get the most from your setup.
