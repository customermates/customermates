Contact management software keeps every person and company you deal with in one shared list, together with what has happened with them. In Customermates, a contact is linked to the organizations they work for, their deals and tasks, the teammates assigned to them, and the email addresses and messaging handles you reach them on.

These records and links work on every plan, self-hosted included. The conversations themselves need the [unified inbox](/features/unified-inbox), which starts on Pro in Customermates Cloud.

## What a contact and an organization hold

| | Contact | Organization |
|---|---|---|
| Built-in fields | First name, last name, notes | Name, notes |
| Channels | Email addresses, WhatsApp number, LinkedIn, Instagram and Telegram handles | None |
| Links | Organizations (several allowed), deals, tasks, assigned users | Contacts, deals, tasks, assigned users |
| Your own fields | Custom fields of the ten types | Custom fields of the ten types |

The field types are Text, Single Select, Date, Date Range, Date & Time, Date & Time Range, Currency, Email, Phone and Link. A landline or a mobile number without WhatsApp goes into a Phone field. Notes are one rich-text field per record and can hold tables and images.

An email address or handle belongs to exactly one contact in the workspace, so Customermates refuses a second contact with an address already in use. Global search (Cmd/Ctrl+K) finds contacts by name, channel value or linked organization; custom fields are reached through filters.

## Organizations as accounts

If you manage accounts rather than single people, the organization is the account. Under **My Company › Settings › Data model** you can rename record types for the whole workspace from a list of presets: Organizations to Accounts or Companies, Contacts to Clients, People or Leads.

An account setup needs three pieces. A Single Select field **Role** on contacts says who decides, who handles the day-to-day work and who sits in accounting. A Date field **Contract end** on organizations holds the renewal date. And the deals board, grouped by organization, shows each account's number of deals and their total value. Customermates calculates no account health, churn risk or score; if your team rates accounts, keep the rating in a Single Select field.

## A worked setup for a five-person consultancy

<Steps>
  <Step title="Name the record types">In **Data model**, switch Contacts to Clients and Organizations to Accounts if that is how your team talks.</Step>
  <Step title="Add four fields">On contacts: **Relationship** (Single Select: Prospect, Active client, Past client, Referral partner) and **Role** (Decision maker, Day-to-day, Accounting). On organizations: **Contract end** (Date) and **Source** (Single Select: Referral, Event, Website, Outbound).</Step>
  <Step title="Save three personal views">"My active clients": **Assigned** set to yourself and **Relationship** is Active client. "No organization yet": **Organizations** has none, for cleaning up after an import. "Gone quiet": **Updated at** older than 90 days.</Step>
  <Step title="Switch one view to the board">Group contacts by **Relationship** and drag a card from Prospect to Active client when someone signs.</Step>
  <Step title="Decide who sees what">In the role editor, set **Contacts** read access to **Assigned** for people who should only see their own clients, or **All** for everyone.</Step>
</Steps>

"Gone quiet" looks at the last edit, not the last message. Saved views are personal, so each colleague sets the same filters and saves their own view. The [records guide](/docs/app-records) documents every control.

## Moving contacts in from Excel, Outlook or Google

<Steps>
  <Step title="Export and convert">Outlook and Google Contacts deliver their download as a comma-separated text file (Google also as vCard). Open it in Excel and save it as an .xlsx workbook, because Customermates imports XLSX workbooks only.</Step>
  <Step title="Clean up first">Remove duplicate rows and split the list into one workbook for organizations and one for contacts. An import cannot be undone.</Step>
  <Step title="Import organizations">On **Organizations**, open **Import and export › Add from file**, map the columns and import.</Step>
  <Step title="Import contacts">Map first and last name, which are required, the email column to the Email channel, the company column to **Organizations** (matched by name, ignoring case) and the rest to custom fields. Rows with problems can be skipped and fixed later.</Step>
  <Step title="Correct in bulk">Export the list, edit it in Excel and import it again: the **Record ID** column updates existing records instead of adding new ones.</Step>
</Steps>

One file currently takes up to 10,000 rows and 10 MB. The import adds channels to new contacts only. There is no ongoing sync, so after the import your Outlook or Google address book and Customermates are separate lists.

## Conversations on the contact (Pro and higher)

In Customermates Cloud on Pro or higher, you connect Gmail, Outlook, IMAP, LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Instagram or Telegram, and conversations are matched to contacts through their channels. When an unknown address writes in, click **Link** on that participant in the inbox and pick or create the contact. Every conversation with that address, not only this one, then shows on the contact's timeline.

You see conversations from your own channels and those teammates share with the CRM. On Business a whole channel can be shared, so several colleagues' conversations with one client sit on one timeline. On Starter and when self-hosting, channels are stored and searchable, but there is no inbox.

## AI on your contacts: Mate and MCP

In Customermates Cloud, Mate, the hosted assistant, works on contacts in plain language and uses hosted AI credits:

```text
Create contacts for everyone in this email signature and link them to the organization Hartmann Consulting. Set Role to Decision maker for the managing director and Day-to-day for the others. Check first whether any of them already exist.
```

Mate creates and updates records without asking for approval; deleting a contact asks every time. A [routine](/docs/app-routines), also Cloud only, can run an instruction every Monday, such as "List every account whose Contract end falls in the next 60 days. Do not change any records."

On every plan, self-hosted included, your own AI client such as Claude or ChatGPT can search, create and update contacts through MCP, with [[derived.mcp.tools.total]] tools, billed by your AI provider. Webhooks fire on `contact.created`, `contact.updated` and `contact.deleted`.

## Who sees which contacts, and where the data lives

Each role sets read access per record type to **All**, **Assigned** or **None**, and **Manage** to Yes or No. Export needs read access, import needs **Manage**. A record's change history shows for roles with read access **All** on **Audit Log**, such as Admin. Permissions apply to whole record types, not to single fields.

In Customermates Cloud, the production database is configured in the AWS Frankfurt region. Service providers, including US companies, are listed on the [subprocessors page](/subprocessors), and a [data processing agreement](/dpa) is available. Customermates holds no certification. If contact data must stay on your own servers, run the AGPL core [self-hosted](/features/self-hosted), without the inbox and without Mate.

## What it does not do

- No sync with Outlook, Google or phone address books in either direction.
- No merge function. Clean duplicates before importing.
- No tags and no multi-select. Single Select fields hold one value each.
- No file attachments on records. Message attachments stay in their inbox thread, not on the contact.
- No reminders, due dates or birthday alerts. Store dates in custom fields and let a Cloud routine list the ones that have passed.
- No newsletters, mass email, sequences or data enrichment.
- No mobile app. Customermates runs in the browser.

Client files in therapy, care or social work that hold health data belong in specialised practice software, not in a general CRM.

## Frequently asked questions

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="What is the difference between contact management and a CRM?">
Contact management stores people, companies and what happened with them. A CRM adds the work around them: deals in a pipeline, tasks, reporting and integrations. In Customermates, contacts are the base that deals, tasks and the inbox attach to.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Does Microsoft have a contact management tool?">
Yes. Outlook keeps contacts and contact lists under People, in Microsoft 365 and Outlook.com, as an address book for one person. Microsoft's CRM is Dynamics 365 Sales. Customermates connects an Outlook mailbox on Pro and higher but does not sync Outlook contacts.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can I import contacts from Outlook, Google Contacts or Excel?">
Yes, as an Excel workbook. Download your contacts from Outlook or Google Contacts, open the file in Excel and save it as .xlsx first, because Customermates imports XLSX workbooks only. Import organizations before contacts so the company column links up.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How many contacts can I manage in Customermates?">
The plans set no contact limit, so 300 or 30,000 contacts cost the same. One import file currently takes up to 10,000 rows, one export the first 50,000 records of the current view.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What is account management in a CRM?">
Managing each client company as a whole: its people, open deals and contract dates. In Customermates the organization is the account and can be renamed to Accounts. Health scores and renewal alerts are not built in.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is there free contact management software?">
Outlook and Google Contacts come with the account. HubSpot's free CRM tools allow 2 users and 1,000 contacts, Zoho CRM's free edition 3 users and 5,000 records, CentralStationCRM's free Starter 3 users and 200 contacts. Customermates has no free Cloud plan, but its AGPL core runs self-hosted without a licence fee.
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
