CRM workflow automation means a recurring CRM step happens without anyone having to remember it: a deal is won and the onboarding tasks exist, a client emails and a reply draft is waiting. In Customermates you build it with **routines** (Mate, the hosted assistant, runs a saved instruction on a schedule or when a record changes, Cloud only), with **webhooks, the REST API and n8n** for rules that must behave the same every time, or with your own AI client over **MCP**.

The boundary is easy to state. Customermates automates work on single records. It has no email sequences, drip campaigns or mass outreach, no lead scoring, no web forms and no visual workflow builder with branches inside the CRM; that part lives in n8n, a separate product. If "CRM automation" means marketing automation to you, the next section shows where the line runs.

## What is CRM automation, and which kind does Customermates cover?

CRM automation covers two different jobs. **Record workflows** act on one deal, contact or conversation when something happens; this is usually what CRM workflow management refers to. **Marketing automation** sends timed messages to lists of contacts. Customermates is built for the first.

| Kind of automation | Typical examples | In Customermates |
|---|---|---|
| Record workflows | Create tasks when a deal is won, post to a team channel when a deal is created, list deals nobody touched | Yes: routines, webhooks, REST, n8n and MCP |
| AI work on records | Update a deal from call notes, draft a reply to a client | Yes: Mate in chat, routines (Cloud), your own AI client over MCP |
| Marketing automation | Email sequences, drip campaigns, mass outreach, lead scoring, web forms | No. Use a separate tool and bring its results in through REST or n8n |

A reply draft is one message in one conversation, not a sequence. If you need the third row, keep a dedicated tool for it and use Customermates as the system of record behind it.

## Which CRM workflows suit a routine, a webhook or an AI client?

| Job | Use | Plan |
|---|---|---|
| Follow-up reminder: a task for every deal whose next-contact date has passed | Routine on a schedule | Any paid Cloud plan |
| Reply draft when a client emails | Routine on **Email Received** | Cloud, Pro and higher |
| Onboarding tasks when a deal is won | Routine on **Record updated** for Deals, or a webhook into n8n | Routine: paid Cloud. Webhook: paid plans |
| Message in a team chat when a deal is created | Webhook into n8n, or a webhook with a body template | Paid plans |
| Flow with branches, waits and other apps | n8n on webhooks and REST | Paid plans, plus n8n |
| Update a deal from pasted call notes | Mate chat, or your own AI client over MCP | Mate: paid Cloud. MCP: every plan |

Our rule of thumb: if a step must behave identically every time, such as creating the same tasks for every won deal, use a webhook and n8n. A routine is an AI run that reads, decides and writes, so its output varies a little and every run costs credits. Use it where the reading is the point.

Stage changes show both routes. A routine on **Record updated** for Deals, with **Changed fields** set to **Stage** and a record filter for Won, can create onboarding tasks, assign them to the same people as the deal and link them to the deal. In n8n the same job costs no credits and never varies: the webhook delivers a `record.updated` event with the `before` and `after` value of each changed field, an IF node checks the new stage, and an HTTP Request node creates the tasks. Choose the routine when the tasks depend on what was agreed in the emails, and n8n when they are always the same.

## How do routines automate CRM work on a schedule or record event?

Routines exist in Customermates Cloud only. A routine is a name, an instruction of up to 5,000 characters and a trigger. When the trigger fires, Mate starts a new conversation with that instruction and works on its own, as the routine's owner: it sees what the owner sees and spends the owner's credits. Changes a routine makes never trigger other routines, so they cannot loop. The [routines guide](/docs/app-routines) covers every option.

- **On a schedule:** every 15 or 30 minutes, hourly, daily, weekly or monthly. Mate can also set a custom cron schedule, for example weekdays at 09:00.
- **When something changes:** any record event on any list, from contacts, organizations, deals, services and tasks to your own lists, plus inbox events except deleted emails and chats and permanent deletions. **Changed fields** narrows an update trigger to fields such as the deal **Stage**, and **Record filters** to records such as deals assigned to you.

| Limit | Current value |
|---|---|
| Runs per routine and hour | 4 |
| Credits per run | 10, then the run stops as Partial |
| Shortest schedule interval | 15 minutes |
| Failed runs in a row before the routine pauses | 3 |
| Debounce after a run starts | 300 seconds by default; Mate or MCP can set 0 to 86,400 |
| Run history kept | 90 days |

An unattended run may create and update records, append notes, create tasks and save drafts without asking. It may also send emails and chat messages. Everything that asks for approval in a chat is refused, because nobody is there to approve it: deletions, renaming lists, team invitations, webhook resends and support requests. If you want drafts and nothing else, write it into the instruction.

## How do I build a follow-up reminder routine?

Customermates sends no reminder notifications and tasks have no due date, so here a reminder is a task that a routine creates from a date you keep yourself. The routine runs every morning, finds deals whose date has passed and puts one task per deal in the task list. This needs a **Date** field such as "Next contact" on Deals; the [follow-up guide](/features/follow-up) shows how to add it and how to save the matching views.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add the date field">In **Data model**, add a field of type **Date** called "Next contact" to **Deals**, and set it on the deals you want to follow up.</Step>
  <Step title="Add the routine">Open **Routines**, click **Add** and name it "Follow-up reminders".</Step>
  <Step title="Set the trigger">Choose **On a schedule**, then **Repeats**: **Every day**, 08:00. For weekdays only, ask Mate to set a custom schedule.</Step>
  <Step title="Paste the instruction">Use the text below with your own stage names, and save.</Step>
  <Step title="Test it once">Click **Test trigger**. It is a real run and creates real tasks, so check the task list afterwards. **Run details** shows who the run acted as and the share of the monthly allowance it used; the owner can also open the full transcript.</Step>
</Steps>

```text
Find every deal that is not in Won or Lost and whose Next contact date is today or earlier. For each one, create a task named "Follow up: <deal name>, <Next contact date>", link it to the deal, assign it to the deal's assigned users and put the deal's latest note in the task notes. If a task with that exact name already exists, skip the deal. Do not send anything and do not change any deal. End with one line per deal: name, stage, date.
```

The date in the task name stops the routine from creating the same task twice. Once the person moves the Next contact date, the next due date gets its own task.

Credit use: a daily schedule is 30 or 31 runs a month and each run stops at 10 credits, so the ceiling is about 300 credits a month. Check your plan's monthly allowance on the [pricing page](/pricing) before running it daily; a weekly schedule has a ceiling of 50. One credit is one US cent of measured provider cost. Nothing pings anyone: the tasks wait in the task list. If you need a push message in your team chat when a date passes, run that check from n8n's schedule trigger against the REST API.

## How do I draft replies when a client emails?

This needs the [unified inbox](/features/unified-inbox), which starts on Pro.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Connect a mailbox">Connect Gmail, Outlook or IMAP under **Settings › Channels & calendars**.</Step>
  <Step title="Add the routine">Choose **When something changes** and the event **Email Received**. For WhatsApp or LinkedIn, pick **Message Received**.</Step>
  <Step title="Paste the instruction">Use the text below and save.</Step>
  <Step title="Review the drafts">In the inbox, set the **Draft** filter to **has some**. Edit a draft and click **Send draft**, or discard it.</Step>
</Steps>

```text
Read the email thread from this event. If it is a newsletter, an automated notice or spam, stop and do nothing. Otherwise find the sender's contact, organization and open deals. Save a reply draft in the same thread that answers the question and proposes one next step. Never send the email. Append one line to the contact's notes with what the client asked.
```

Credit use: every run that starts uses credits, including runs where Mate decides there is nothing to do. With four runs an hour at no more than 10 credits each, the ceiling is 40 credits an hour. Because of the debounce, a second email within five minutes of a run starting gets no run of its own, an email that arrives while a run is still going is skipped too, and after four runs in an hour, further emails that hour are dropped. You can ask Mate to shorten the debounce, down to zero, but the cap of four runs an hour stays. Treat the drafts as help with most emails, not a promise for every one.

## How do I send a new deal to n8n, Slack or another tool?

Customermates sends a JSON POST for each of [[derived.webhooks.events.total]] event types: [[derived.webhooks.events.records]] record events and [[derived.webhooks.events.messaging]] inbox events. Record events cover every list, your own included. Deal **Stage** is a configurable single choice field, so a stage change arrives as `record.updated` for the Deals list, and a new deal as `record.created`. The [webhook reference](/docs/webhooks) lists every payload.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add the trigger in n8n">Add a Webhook node and copy its URL. With the community node, version 2 or later, a Customermates Trigger node creates the subscription for you when the workflow is activated, and you can skip the next step.</Step>
  <Step title="Subscribe in Customermates">Under **Settings › Webhooks**, click **Add**, paste the URL, pick **Record created**, choose **Deals** under **Records from** and set a **Secret**.</Step>
  <Step title="Verify and deduplicate">Check the `X-Webhook-Signature` header, an HMAC-SHA256 of the raw body. Deliveries can arrive out of order or twice, so deduplicate on the event `id`.</Step>
  <Step title="Read the deal">Create an API key under **Settings › API keys & connectors** and store it in n8n as a Header Auth credential named `x-api-key`. An HTTP Request node then calls `POST /api/v1/records/read` with the `typeId` and `recordId` from `data.record.ref`.</Step>
  <Step title="Post to the channel">Use n8n's Slack node, or an HTTP Request node to the channel's incoming webhook URL, with the deal's name, value and owner. Customermates has no native Slack integration, so n8n is the route.</Step>
  <Step title="Activate the workflow">Waits, schedules, logs and execution limits belong to n8n. Customermates keeps the records and the delivery log.</Step>
</Steps>

To create the first task as well, add an HTTP Request node that calls `POST /api/v1/records/mutate` and links the new task to the deal. Reuse the same idempotency key when you retry the identical payload. The [n8n guide](/docs/n8n) has the endpoints and common mistakes, and the [n8n tutorial](/blog/n8n-for-beginners-build-your-first-workflow) walks through a first webhook workflow.

If you only need the chat message, skip n8n: a webhook can post straight to a Slack or Discord incoming webhook URL with a body template such as `{ "text": "{{event}} for {{data.record.ref.recordId}}" }`. That sends the event name and record ID; composing a message from record values is easier in n8n.

Deliveries are retried automatically on timeouts and server errors, up to 10 attempts in total, and logged under **Webhook log**, where a failed one can be resent. The [REST API](/features/api) under `/api/v1` covers every list you create, and `updateMany`, `deleteMany` and import take up to 100 records per request.

## How can an AI client update CRM records over MCP?

The MCP server gives AI clients such as Claude or ChatGPT [[derived.mcp.tools.total]] tools on the same data, on every plan and when self-hosting. Your own AI provider bills the model use. This is the route for updates you ask for in plain language, such as writing up a call.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Connect the client">Claude and ChatGPT connect through a custom connector with OAuth, so there is no key to manage. CLI and editor clients use an API key from **Settings › API keys & connectors**. Setup per client is in the [MCP reference](/docs/mcp).</Step>
  <Step title="Narrow the tools">Optionally append `?toolsets=records` to the endpoint URL so the client sees only the record tools plus `search` and `fetch`.</Step>
  <Step title="Ask for the update">Tell the client what happened, for example the prompt below. It looks up the list and field IDs, finds the deal, reads its current version and writes the change with `mutate_crm_record`.</Step>
  <Step title="Check the result">Open the deal's activity history. Changes over MCP count like changes in the app: a subscribed webhook receives a `record.updated` event, and event routines can trigger on it.</Step>
</Steps>

```text
Find the deal "Acme renewal". Append these call notes to its notes: the client wants a revised quote with two seats fewer. Set Next contact to 14 days from today and create a task "Send revised quote" linked to the deal. Show me what you changed.
```

Appending to a notes field never overwrites what is there, and it does not conflict with a colleague's newer edit. In Customermates Cloud, Mate does the same from a chat, and its policy asks for your approval before gated actions such as deleting records; sending an email or chat message is not gated, so ask for a draft when the wording needs review.

External MCP calls work differently. The server checks authorization and validation but does not ask for confirmation; the advice to confirm before deleting or sending goes to the client. An API key carries the permissions of the user who created it, with no per-key scoping. Create it under a user whose role fits the job, and check how your client handles approvals before you grant write access.

## Is there an open-source CRM with a workflow builder?

Customermates is open source and has no workflow builder inside the CRM. [n8n](https://n8n.io/) fills that role. Its features that matter here are a visual builder, JavaScript or Python in any step and ready-made nodes for apps such as Slack, and it connects to Customermates through webhooks and the REST API. You can self-host its Community Edition under the [Sustainable Use License](https://github.com/n8n-io/n8n/blob/master/LICENSE.md), which permits use only for your own internal business purposes or for non-commercial or personal use, or subscribe to n8n Cloud, [priced by monthly workflow executions](https://n8n.io/pricing/). Customermates includes no n8n runtime or subscription.

The community node [customermates/n8n-nodes-crm](https://github.com/customermates/n8n-nodes-crm) offers dedicated CRM nodes if you prefer them to HTTP Request nodes. Use version 2 or later: version 1.x targets endpoints the API no longer has, so its workflows fail until you update them.

## What updates itself, and what still needs a person?

- **Without AI:** every change lands in the record's activity history and reaches every webhook subscribed to that event, and weighted pipeline values follow your stage weights. On Pro and higher, once you link an email address or handle to a contact, every conversation with it appears on that contact's timeline.
- **With AI, when you ask:** paste call notes into Mate, or into your own AI client over MCP, and ask it to update the deal, append the notes and create follow-up tasks.
- **With AI, unattended:** routines, as above.

Our advice: let a routine propose a stage move in a note and let the person assigned to the deal make it. Stages drive the weighted pipeline, and a wrong move made by an unattended run is easy to miss.

## What does Customermates not automate?

- No visual workflow builder, branching or waits inside the CRM. Use n8n or a similar tool.
- No email sequences, drip campaigns or mass email. A reply draft is one message in one conversation.
- No task due dates, reminders or notification centre. A custom date field on the deal plus the follow-up routine above gives you a list to work from, as in [follow-ups](/features/follow-up).
- No lead scoring, data enrichment or web forms. Send form entries in through REST or n8n.
- No routines, Mate or inbox in [self-hosted Customermates](/features/self-hosted). Webhooks, REST and MCP work there.
- No guarantee a routine runs: the hourly cap, the debounce or an owner without credits can skip a run. Steps that must run for every event belong on a webhook, where failed deliveries are retried and can be re-sent.

<FaqItem question="Can a CRM update itself automatically?">
Partly. Activity history, webhook events and weighted pipeline values update on their own, and on Pro and higher a linked email address brings later conversations onto the contact's timeline. Writing notes or changing deals based on what was said takes AI: Mate when you ask, a routine on a trigger, or your own AI client through MCP.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What is the difference between CRM workflow automation and marketing automation?">
Marketing automation sends campaigns to lists of contacts on a timetable. CRM workflow automation acts on single records, such as one deal or one conversation. Customermates has no email sequences, drip campaigns or mass email, so keep a marketing tool if you need those.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What happens to deals when follow-ups after meetings are forgotten?">
They stay in their stage until someone stumbles on them, and they keep counting in the weighted pipeline. Customermates does not nudge anyone: there are no task due dates and no notifications. What helps is a custom Date field "Next contact" on the deal plus the follow-up routine above, which creates a task for every deal whose date has passed.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Do routines work when self-hosting?">
No. Routines need Mate and hosted AI credits, which exist only in Customermates Cloud. Self-hosted, you build comparable jobs with webhooks, REST and n8n, or with your own AI client over MCP.
</FaqItem>
