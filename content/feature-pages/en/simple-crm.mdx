A simple CRM is one your team keeps updating because there is little to learn and nothing to configure before the first deal. Customermates gets there with a small data model: contacts, organizations, deals, services and tasks, plus a deal pipeline that exists the moment you create the workspace. It suits small client-facing teams such as consultants, agencies and founders, and it costs [[commercial.price.starter.monthly]] per user per month on Starter.

Simple also means some things are missing on purpose. The limits are listed further down, so you can rule Customermates out quickly if you need them.

## What a new workspace already contains

Most "simple CRM" pages promise a short setup. Here is what you actually find after sign-up, before you change anything. Each item is a Single Select custom column, so you can rename options, add new ones or delete the column. None of them is a built-in field: tasks, for example, have no fixed status of their own, only this preset column.

| Record type | Column | Preset options |
|---|---|---|
| Deals | Stage | Prospecting 10%, Qualification 20%, Demo 40%, Proposal 60%, Negotiation 80%, Won 100%, Lost 0% |
| Contacts | Sales Pipeline | New, Contact, Qualified, In Progress, Won, Lost |
| Tasks | Status | Open, In Progress, Blocked, On Hold, Done, Archived |

The percentages are win probabilities. They drive a deterministic weighted value per deal and a weighted pipeline total; they are stage weights you set, not a prediction. You adjust them under **My Company › Settings**, which needs the Admin role or **Manage** on the Company row.

What else keeps the setup short:

- **Your own words for records.** Under **Data model** you pick preset names, for example Clients instead of Contacts, Projects or Jobs instead of Deals, Follow-ups instead of Tasks. The relationships stay the same.
- **One list surface for everything.** Every record type opens as a table or a board, with search, filters, grouping and sorting under the same toolbar. See [records in the app](/docs/app-records).
- **Views save themselves.** Filters, sort, layout, grouping and columns save together as a named view that appears as a tab. Every change autosaves. Views are personal to their creator, so each person keeps their own.

## Set it up in one sitting

<Steps>
<Step title="Create the workspace">
Sign up and enter your name and country in the profile step, the only required step of the onboarding wizard. The Knowledge Base, team invite and AI client steps can be skipped and done later.
</Step>
<Step title="Rename to match your business">
Open **My Company › Settings**, choose the names your team uses under **Data model**, and edit the Stage options and probabilities if your sales steps differ. A consultancy might use Projects with the stages Enquiry, Scoping, Proposal sent, Won and Lost.
</Step>
<Step title="Import one record type">
Use **Import and export › Add from file** on the contacts list and map the spreadsheet columns to fields. The wizard reads `.xlsx` workbooks only, so save other spreadsheet formats as `.xlsx` first. Import organizations before contacts and deals, so the links can match by name. Our [Excel CRM template guide](/blog/excel-crm-template) shows a sheet layout that maps cleanly.
</Step>
<Step title="Add the one column you will miss">
Tasks have no built-in due date. If you work with follow-up dates, add a Date custom column such as "Next step" to deals or tasks with **Customize › Add Field**, then save a view sorted by it.
</Step>
<Step title="Invite the team">
Send invitations from **My Company › Members**. New members wait for approval until an Admin, or a member whose role can manage users, assigns a role and sets them to active.
</Step>
</Steps>

## What this simple CRM leaves out

<AcquisitionCallout title="Check these before you commit" variant="boundary">
No reminders or notifications for tasks, and no recurring tasks. No mobile app; the web app runs in the browser. No email sequences or bulk sending. No file attachments on records, only images and tables inside notes. No shared team views. No built-in visual workflow builder.
</AcquisitionCallout>

Automation exists, but it lives in two separate places. In Customermates Cloud, [Routines](/docs/app-routines) run a saved instruction through Mate on a schedule or when a record changes, using hosted AI credits. A routine runs as its owner and sees what that owner sees. For rule-based flows you connect an n8n instance you run or subscribe to separately, through webhooks, the REST API or the community node.

If your team depends on push reminders on a phone, or wants a free plan for good, one of the tools below is likely a better start.

## How it connects when you need more

The simple start does not lock anything away. The REST API and webhooks are on every plan, and your own AI client (Claude, ChatGPT and others) can read and update records through [MCP](/docs/mcp). In Customermates Cloud, Mate prepares summaries and reply drafts and uses hosted AI credits. The cloud database is configured in the AWS Frankfurt region; the [subprocessors](/subprocessors) page lists the providers involved, including US ones, and a [DPA](/dpa) is available.

## Frequently asked questions

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="What is the easiest CRM to use?">
The easiest one is the one whose structure already matches your work. Look for a ready pipeline, few required fields and import from the spreadsheet you have. Customermates starts with deal stages plus default single-select status fields for contacts and tasks, which you can edit like any custom field, and lets you rename record types to your own terms. Test any CRM with your real data during the trial rather than judging the demo.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is there a free version of Customermates?">
There is no free cloud plan. The cloud has a [[commercial.trial.days]]-day trial without a credit card. The open-source core under AGPL-3.0 can be self-hosted without licence fees, with the Starter feature set and without the inbox or Mate. If you need free hosting as well, HubSpot and CentralStationCRM offer free plans with user and contact limits.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can I switch from Excel to Customermates?">
Yes. Import each record type from an `.xlsx` workbook, matching your spreadsheet columns to fields and custom fields in the import wizard. Exported workbooks carry a Record ID column, so you can edit them and import them back as updates. CSV files and Google Sheets need to be saved as `.xlsx` first, and attachments do not transfer.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How much does a simple CRM cost per month?">
For Customermates, a three-person team pays [[commercial.price.starter.monthly.seats.3]] per month on Starter, or [[commercial.price.pro.monthly.seats.3]] on Pro with the unified inbox. Other simple CRMs range from free plans with limits to a flat per-user price, as in the comparison table above.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Does a simple CRM make sense for one person?">
Yes, if you have more than a handful of open deals or clients and lose track of who you talked to last. A solo consultant on Starter gets the full record model and a pipeline. If you mostly need a contact list, a spreadsheet may still be enough.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Do I need technical skills?">
No. Adding a field, renaming stages and importing a workbook happen in the app. Technical skills only come in if you connect n8n, use the REST API or self-host.
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
