CRM reporting means counting and summing what is already in your CRM, so questions like "what is in the pipeline?" or "which clients brought the most revenue?" get an answer without a spreadsheet. In Customermates, that happens in three places: dashboard charts, lists grouped by a field, and the weighted value on every deal. Anything that needs real arithmetic over many records, such as a median deal size or a ranking, goes to Mate in Customermates Cloud, to your own AI client through MCP, or to Excel after an export.

It is deliberately small. If you need conversion tracking over time, time in stage, quotas or revenue forecasts, the section on limits names tools that do that.

## What a chart widget can calculate

Every chart takes one list as its source, an aggregation and a grouping. The aggregation is a count of records, or the sum, average, minimum or maximum of a number or money field, such as **Value** or **Weighted value** on deals.

**Grouping** is **No grouping** (one total), a field such as Stage or Lead source, each record, **Assigned to**, or a field reached through a relationship. That gives you "deal value by stage", "deal value per organization" or "line item amounts by service". Group by a date field, **Created at** or **Updated at** and pick a **Time interval** to get one value per day, week, month, quarter or year.

**Weighted value** is a calculated field on deals: the deal's value times the **Probability %** of its stage, which you set on each option of the Stage field in **Configure**. A new workspace already has probabilities filled in, so it works from the first deal.

**Filters** narrow what a chart counts, using any field, including dates; linked fields appear as **List › Field**. That is how you chart "won deal value per organization": deals as the source, a filter on Stage is Won, grouped by organization.

**Chart styles** are Number, Bar chart, Horizontal bars, Area over time, Ranked table, Doughnut, Funnel and Radar. A live preview shows the result before you save.

The second widget kind, the **Activity timeline**, is a newest-first feed of record changes, and on Pro and higher also messages and calendar events from connected accounts. You filter it by category, account or related records, for example "everything that happened on deals of my three largest clients".

## Reports a small team uses, and how to build each

These recipes assume the default Stage field plus two single choice fields you add to deals: "Lead source" and "Lost reason".

| Question | Source | Measure | Grouping | Filters |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What is in the pipeline, by stage? | Deals | Sum of Value | Stage | Stage is not Won or Lost |
| What is it worth if stage probabilities hold? | Deals | Sum of Weighted value | Stage | Stage is not Won or Lost |
| Where does won business come from? | Deals | Sum of Value | Lead source | Stage is Won, custom "Closed on" date between the first and last day of the quarter |
| Which clients bring the most revenue? | Deals | Sum of Value | Organization | Stage is Won |
| Which services sell? | Line items | Sum of Amount | Service | Deals › Stage is Won |
| Why do we lose? | Deals | Count | Lost reason | Stage is Lost |
| How much open work is there? | Tasks | Count | Status | Status is not Done or Archived |

Tasks have no built-in due date. The Status field in the last row is a single choice field that a new workspace starts with, and you can rename, change or delete it like any other field.

Two questions are better answered by a list than by a chart. **Who is working on which deals?** Switch the deals list to the board and group it by **Assigned**: each person gets a column with their deal count and totals. **How many deals did we open per month?** Group the deals table by created date, bucketed by month. Saved as views, both stay one click away; views are personal to the person who saves them.

## Set up a weekly pipeline dashboard

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check the stage probabilities">In **Configure**, open Deals and the **Stage** field, and adjust the **Probability %** of each option. A preview shows the effect on deals and widgets.</Step>
  <Step title="Add the fields your questions need">In **Configure**, open Deals and use **Add › Field** for a Single choice "Lead source" (Referral, LinkedIn, Website, Event), a Single choice "Lost reason" and a Date field "Closed on". A chart can only group by what you record.</Step>
  <Step title="Create the first chart">On the **Dashboard**, press **Add widget** and pick **Bar chart**. Under **Data**, choose Deals as the source, sum **Weighted value** and group by Stage. Exclude Won and Lost in the filters, then press **Save**.</Step>
  <Step title="Add three more">Won deal value by Lead source, won deal value per organization, and a count of lost deals by Lost reason. Drag and resize them; the layout is saved for you.</Step>
  <Step title="Share the setup, not the dashboard">Switch on **Share as a template** for each chart. A teammate picks it under **Start from a template** and gets their own copy, which counts only the records their role can read.</Step>
</Steps>

The [dashboard guide](/docs/app-dashboard) documents every option in the widget editor.

## How reporting connects to Mate, MCP and the API

**Mate (Customermates Cloud).** Mate has a read-only analysis tool for questions that need arithmetic across many records: a median, a ranking, a win rate, or a join between deals and organizations. It reads the records your role can see, computes in an isolated sandbox and changes nothing. Each question uses hosted AI credits. An example:

```text
For deals that reached Won or Lost since 1 July: what is the win rate per lead source, what is the median value of won deals, and which three organizations brought the most won value?
```

The answer is a chat message, not a saved chart. Mate can also create or change dashboard widgets for you when you describe them.

**Routines (Customermates Cloud).** A routine runs a saved instruction on a schedule, such as "Every Monday at 8:00, summarize the open pipeline by stage and list deals not updated in 30 days." It runs as you, its owner, with your access and credits, and the run summary stays on the routine. On Pro and higher it can also email that summary to you from your connected mailbox; Mate sends without asking again, so say "draft" in the instruction if you want to read it first. See [workflow automation](/features/workflow-automation) for triggers and limits.

**Your own AI client through MCP.** Claude, ChatGPT and other MCP clients use [[derived.mcp.tools.total]] tools on every plan and when self-hosting. With `query_crm_measure` a client calculates totals across all matching records, so "what is our weighted pipeline for organizations in Bavaria?" has an exact answer. The [MCP documentation](/docs/mcp) has the setup.

**REST and webhooks.** If reports belong in another system, read records through the [REST API](/features/api) or receive a webhook on every change and keep your own reporting table up to date. [n8n](/docs/n8n) can do either on a schedule; it runs separately and is not part of Customermates.

## Frequently asked questions

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="What are typical CRM reports?">
For a small team, five cover most decisions: pipeline value by stage, weighted pipeline, won revenue by lead source, revenue per client and lost deals by reason. All five are chart widgets in Customermates. Activity reports, conversion funnels and sales forecasts are common in larger CRMs; Customermates has an activity timeline and a funnel chart of current stages, but no forecast.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can Customermates forecast revenue?">
Not in the predictive sense. It calculates a weighted pipeline: each deal's value times the win probability you set for its stage. Nothing learns from past deals or predicts close dates. If you need that, look at HubSpot Sales Hub Professional or Pipedrive's forecast view.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can my team share one dashboard?">
No. Dashboards and saved views are personal. You can share a chart with **Share as a template**, and each teammate starts their own copy from it. Their copy counts only the records their role can read, so two people can see different numbers in the same chart.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can I export CRM reports to Excel?">
You export records, not charts. Any list exports to an Excel workbook with its current filters, sort and visible columns, one record type at a time. CSV is not offered, so build the report with a PivotTable in Excel.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Which reports are included on the Starter plan?">
All chart widgets, the weighted pipeline, grouped lists, the activity timeline of record changes and XLSX export are on Starter. Pro adds messages and calendar events to the timeline. Mate's analysis needs Customermates Cloud and hosted AI credits, which every Cloud plan includes.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can I build reports with ChatGPT or Claude instead?">
Yes. Connect the client through MCP, and it can list and filter records, calculate totals across matching deals and create dashboard widgets. The model, its subscription and what it does with your data are governed by your AI provider, not by Customermates.
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
