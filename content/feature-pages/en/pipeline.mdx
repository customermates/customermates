Sales pipeline software puts your open deals on a board, one column per stage, so you can see what is in play, what it is worth and which deal has gone quiet. In Customermates the deals list turns into that board with one switch. Every column header shows the number of deals, their total value and their weighted value, and you move a deal by dragging its card.

Customermates is an open-source, AI-native CRM for small client-facing teams: consultancies, agencies, other service businesses and founders who sell themselves. Customermates does not predict revenue or flag idle deals on its own. If your high-volume outbound team needs that, the comparison further down names tools built for it.

## What the pipeline board does

**Stages are a column you own.** The stage of a deal is a single-select column called Stage. A new workspace starts with seven options and win probabilities already set:

| Stage | Probability |
|---|---|
| Prospecting | 10% |
| Qualification | 20% |
| Demo | 40% |
| Proposal | 60% |
| Negotiation | 80% |
| Won | 100% |
| Lost | 0% |

Rename them, reorder them, add your own or delete the ones you never use. A consultancy might run Enquiry, Discovery call, Proposal sent, Verbal yes, Won and Lost.

**The board.** Open **Deals**, choose **Appearance** and switch **Layout** to **Board**, grouped by Stage. Drag a card to another column and the stage changes; drop it on **No value** and the stage is cleared. Each card shows the fields you make visible in **Appearance**, for example value, assigned users, organization and an Expected close date.

**Column headers do the arithmetic.** Each header shows the deal count, the stage probability, the total value and the weighted value. A deal's weighted value is its value times the probability of its stage. Three open deals make the idea concrete:

| Deal | Stage | Value | Weighted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Website relaunch | Prospecting | 5,000 | 500 |
| Brand workshop | Proposal | 8,000 | 4,800 |
| Annual retainer | Negotiation | 12,000 | 9,600 |
| **Pipeline** | | **25,000** | **14,900** |

The same two totals, **Total pipeline** and **Weighted pipeline**, appear under **My Company › Settings**, next to the probabilities.

**Deal value comes from services.** You do not type a deal's value. It is the price of each linked service times its quantity on that deal, so a deal with two workshop days at 1,500 and ten coaching hours at 150 is worth 4,500 in your workspace currency.

**The same list as a table.** Switch **Layout** back to **Table** and keep the grouping: stages become collapsible row groups with their own counts and sums. Tables sort by **Deal Value** and **Weighted Value** as well as by any custom column.

## Set up your pipeline

<Steps>
  <Step title="Rename the stages">Open any deal, press **Customize** and edit the Stage column with the **Edit column** pencil. On a board grouped by Stage you can also click a column header to open the same dialog.</Step>
  <Step title="Check the probabilities">Under **My Company › Settings**, **Deal stage field** shows Stage. Enter a win probability from 0 to 100 next to each option and save. Every deal's weighted value is recalculated.</Step>
  <Step title="Add your services">Create what you sell under **Services**, each with a price (**Amount**). Deal values are built from these.</Step>
  <Step title="Add two custom fields to deals">A Date field "Expected close" and a single-select "Lost reason" with Price, Timing, Chose a competitor and No decision.</Step>
  <Step title="Import your open deals">Use **Add from file** with an Excel workbook, organizations first, then contacts, then deals, so the deal rows can link to records that already exist. CSV files are not accepted, so save them as XLSX first.</Step>
  <Step title="Save your pipeline view">With the board grouped by Stage and a filter on Stage that leaves out Won and Lost, press **New view** and name it "Open pipeline". The view keeps filters, grouping, layout and visible fields.</Step>
</Steps>

The [records guide](/docs/app-records) covers boards, views and import in more detail.

## Running more than one pipeline

Customermates has no separate pipeline object, so a second pipeline is a second single-select column on deals. A team that sells new projects and renews retainers adds a column "Renewal stage" with Upcoming, In discussion, Renewal sent and Renewed, then saves a board view grouped by it and filtered to renewal deals.

Two limits apply. Only one column, set under **Deal stage field**, carries probabilities and weighted values, so the second board shows counts and total values but no weighted figures. And a board shows at most 50 columns, which no sensible pipeline reaches.

If you would rather keep one stage list, add a single-select "Pipeline" field with New business and Renewal, and save one view per value of that filter.

## Keeping the pipeline current

A board is only useful while it is true. Three ways to keep it that way:

**A stale-deals view.** Filter deals on **Updated at** with **Older than…** 14 days and Stage not Won or Lost. The filter stays relative, so the saved view always shows the deals nobody has touched in two weeks. Any edit counts as an update, not only a stage change.

**A weekly routine (Customermates Cloud).** Routines run a saved instruction for Mate on a schedule or when a record changes, using the owner's hosted AI credits. Starter includes [[commercial.entitlement.starter.includedRoutinesPerUser]] routine per user, Pro [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedRoutinesPerUser]]. Set one to **Every week** on Monday morning:

```text
Find deals in Proposal or Negotiation that were not updated in the last 14 days. For each one, create a task "Check in: [deal name]", link it to the deal and assign it to the deal's assigned users. Do not send any messages.
```

The routine runs as its owner and sees only the deals that person can see. Creating tasks needs no approval; deletions would, so an unattended run refuses them. More patterns are on the [workflow automation](/features/workflow-automation) page.

**Your own AI client through MCP.** Claude, ChatGPT and other MCP clients can move deals between stages and update fields with the [[derived.mcp.tools.total]] tools described in the [MCP documentation](/docs/mcp), on every plan and when self-hosting. A stage change from any source fires a `deal.updated` webhook with the changed fields, which an n8n workflow you run separately can pick up.

## Limits

<AcquisitionCallout title="What the pipeline does not do" variant="boundary">
  The weighted pipeline is your value times your probabilities. There is no model that predicts revenue or close dates, and no report of time in stage, stage conversion or sales velocity. Cards are not highlighted when a deal goes idle; use the stale-deals view instead. Saved views and dashboards are personal and cannot be shared, so each teammate saves their own; a dashboard chart shared as a template can be copied. Deals have no file attachments, and there is no mobile app.
</AcquisitionCallout>

For metrics like win rate per person, Mate in Customermates Cloud can calculate them from your records with a read-only analysis tool, or you export the deals as XLSX. Chart options are on the [reporting](/features/reporting) page.

## Frequently asked questions

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="What is the best sales pipeline software for a small team?">
The one your team keeps up to date. For outbound volume, look at a tool with several pipelines and idle-deal alerts, such as Pipedrive. For a team whose deals move through email and LinkedIn conversations, the deal and the conversation should sit in one place, which is the case Customermates is built for.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is there a CRM called Pipeline?">
Yes. Pipeline CRM, renamed from PipelineDeals in 2020, is sales pipeline software for small and mid-size B2B teams. Its Start plan is $25 per user per month billed annually or $29 billed monthly, with one pipeline (prices checked on 8 October 2026 on the [Pipeline CRM pricing page](https://pipelinecrm.com/pricing/)).
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can I have several pipelines in Customermates?">
Yes, as separate single-select columns on deals, each with its own saved board view. Only the column set as the deal stage field has probabilities and weighted values.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Does Customermates forecast revenue?">
No, not in the predictive sense. The weighted pipeline multiplies each deal's value by the probability you set for its stage. That is arithmetic on your own numbers, not a prediction.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can my whole team see the same board?">
Everyone whose role can read all deals sees the same deals and can build the same board; with **Assigned** read access, a person sees only the deals assigned to them. Saved views are personal, so each colleague saves their own board view.
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
