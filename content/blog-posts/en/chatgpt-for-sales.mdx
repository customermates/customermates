# ChatGPT for Sales: Prompts, Call Notes and the CRM Connection

ChatGPT is good at the writing and thinking work around a sale: account research, first drafts of emails, discovery questions, objection answers and turning messy call notes into a clean summary. It is only as good as the facts you give it, and on its own it does not keep your pipeline, remember what a colleague promised a client, or send anything. Teams get the most out of it with saved prompts in a Project, a business plan for anything that contains customer data, and, if ChatGPT should also update records, a CRM connected through MCP.

This guide covers prompts by deal stage, a call-note workflow, the data rules per ChatGPT plan, and what OpenAI currently requires before ChatGPT can write to a CRM. Customermates publishes it and appears in one labelled section near the end.

## Where ChatGPT helps in sales, and where it does not

| Task | ChatGPT does well | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Account research before a call | Structures what you paste or what web search finds into a one-page brief | Wrong or outdated details about people. Check names, roles and dates yourself |
| First-touch email or LinkedIn message | A specific first draft in seconds when you give it a real trigger | With only a name and a URL, it writes a generic email that sounds personal |
| Discovery questions | Open questions tailored to role and industry | Questions you would never ask in your own voice |
| Objection answers | Several angles on one objection, from your own arguments | Invented proof points. Tell it to use only the cases you provide |
| Call notes to CRM entry | Summary, decisions and next steps in a fixed format | Owners or dates it guessed. Ask it to write "not stated" instead |
| Pipeline review | Patterns across a deal list you paste or connect | It sees only the fields you give it, not the relationship |
| Sending and follow-up | Not at all on its own | Sending and cadences need your inbox or a dedicated outbound tool |

The rule that follows from the table: ChatGPT prepares, you decide and send. A wrong fact in an internal note costs a minute. A wrong fact in an email to a buyer can cost the deal.

## Set it up once: a sales Project

Before the prompts, give ChatGPT the context it would otherwise ask for every time. In a Project or a custom GPT, add:

- your offer in five sentences, with prices if you name them;
- your ideal client: industry, size, the role that buys, the problem that triggers a purchase;
- two or three short client stories you are allowed to mention;
- the objections you hear most and how you answer them;
- tone rules, for example: "Short sentences. No 'I hope this finds you well'. One question per email."

OpenAI's pricing page lists Projects on every personal plan, Free included, and custom GPTs among the Plus features. Keep one Project for outbound and one for account work, because a Project stuffed with everything gives vaguer answers.

## Prompts by deal stage

Replace the brackets with real facts. The more specific the input, the less the output sounds like a template.

**Research brief before a first call**

```
I have a first call with [name], [role] at [company], [size], [industry].
Here is what I found: [paste LinkedIn about section, company page, recent news].
Give me: 3 likely problems they have that our offer solves, 3 open questions
for the first 10 minutes, and the one client story from this Project that fits best.
Mark anything you are guessing as a guess.
```

**First-touch email**

```
Write a first email to [name] at [company]. Trigger: [new hire, funding, post, event].
What they care about: [paste]. Our relevance in one line: [line].
90 words at most, plain language, one specific question at the end.
No compliments, no "quick call", no claims about results we have not given you.
```

**Objection answer**

```
The prospect wrote: "[exact words]".
Our usual answer: [paste]. Facts we can use: [paste].
Give me three replies under 80 words: one that asks what is behind the objection,
one that answers it directly, one that offers a smaller first step.
Use only the facts above.
```

**Follow-up after a meeting**

```
Here are my notes from today's meeting with [name] at [company]: [paste].
Draft a follow-up under 150 words: thank them in one line, restate the two or three
points we agreed, list next steps with owner and date as stated in the notes,
and end with the next appointment. If an owner or date is missing, write [missing].
```

**Pipeline review**

```
Here are my open deals with stage, value, last activity date and next step: [paste].
List the deals with no activity for more than 14 days, the deals without a next step,
and for each one a single question I should answer before Monday's review.
Do not estimate close probabilities.
```

The [sales follow-up email guide](/blog/sales-follow-up-email) has more follow-up structures if that is the step where your deals stall.

## From call notes to a CRM entry

Summarizing call notes is the most common daily use, and it is where a fixed output format pays off. Paste a transcript or your own notes and ask for fields that match your CRM:

```
Summarize these call notes for the CRM. Return exactly these fields:
- Summary: 3 sentences (context, what was decided, what happens next)
- Pain points: bullet list
- Decision makers mentioned: names and roles
- Next steps: action, owner, date
- Stage suggestion: one of [Prospecting, Qualification, Demo, Proposal, Negotiation]
If something is not in the notes, write "not stated". Do not add facts.

Notes:
[paste]
```

**Before:** "Call with Hanna Roth, Roth Steuerberatung. 6 people, client list in Excel, getting messy. Wants WhatsApp and email with clients in one place. Must clear it with her partner next week. Send proposal by Tuesday."

**After:**

> - Summary: Hanna Roth (Roth Steuerberatung, 6 people) is replacing an Excel client list and wants email and WhatsApp conversations next to client records. She will discuss it with her partner next week. We send a proposal by Tuesday.
> - Pain points: Excel list hard to keep current; client conversations spread across channels.
> - Decision makers mentioned: Hanna Roth; her partner (name not stated).
> - Next steps: send proposal, us, Tuesday; internal discussion, Hanna Roth, next week.
> - Stage suggestion: Proposal.

Three habits make the summaries reliable. Label speakers in transcripts ("Rep:", "Client:"), because attribution of action items depends on it. Put numbers and dates on their own line in your notes. And read the result before you paste it into the record, because a model fills gaps when it is not told otherwise.

If a meeting tool already records your calls, its own summary is usually generic. Paste its transcript into the prompt above to get the fields your CRM uses.

## Customer data: which ChatGPT plan to use

Most sales prompts contain personal data: names, roles, what someone said on a call. What happens to it depends on the plan.

| Plan type | Training on your conversations | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free, Go, Plus, Pro (personal) | Controlled by the setting "Improve the model for everyone" | Turn it off under Settings, Data controls, before you paste client details |
| Business, Enterprise | OpenAI does not train on business data by default, including data accessed through apps | Sign OpenAI's Data Processing Addendum; admins set retention |

OpenAI offers the DPA for ChatGPT Business, ChatGPT Enterprise and the API. Its consumer terms for Europe include a separate addendum for business use, so using ChatGPT commercially is allowed; the question for a company is which terms and which data you put into it.

Under GDPR, that leaves a short checklist for a sales team:

- Use a business plan with a signed DPA for anything that contains client data.
- Paste the conversation, not the whole client record. Leave out phone numbers and addresses the task does not need.
- Tell your team which plan and which Projects are approved, and which are not.
- Mention AI processing in your privacy notice if you process prospects' data this way.

This is a working checklist, not legal advice. Your data protection officer decides the details.

## ChatGPT alone or a dedicated sales tool?

| Your situation | What fits |
| --- | --- |
| One person, a handful of active deals, notes in a document | ChatGPT with a Project is enough. Copy results where you keep your deals |
| A team that shares clients and needs to know who said what | A CRM as the record, with ChatGPT or another AI client connected to it |
| Outbound at volume with multi-step cadences | A sales engagement tool with sequences and deliverability controls. ChatGPT drafts, it does not send cadences |
| Calls recorded and transcribed automatically | A meeting recorder plus the call-note prompt above, or an automation that does both |

ChatGPT does not replace a system that stores the deal history. Its memory belongs to one user, and a chat history is not something a colleague can pick up when you are on holiday.

## Connecting ChatGPT to a CRM

There are three ways, from simplest to most direct.

1. **Copy and paste.** Works on every plan. Slow, but nothing changes in the CRM unless you change it.
2. **An automation tool.** n8n, Zapier or Make send a transcript or a new record to OpenAI's API and write the result back. You pay for the automation tool and the API separately, and you maintain the workflow. The [n8n beginner guide](/blog/n8n-for-beginners-build-your-first-workflow) shows the basics.
3. **An MCP app.** ChatGPT calls the CRM's tools directly: search contacts, update a deal, add a note.

For the third route, OpenAI's help center (checked 8 October 2026) says:

- Full MCP support, including write actions, is rolling out in beta to ChatGPT Business, Enterprise and Edu.
- It runs on ChatGPT web only, not on mobile.
- On Business, only admins and owners can turn on developer mode and create an app, under **Workspace settings › Apps › Create**. Enterprise admins can grant it to selected members.
- Pro users can connect MCP servers with read and fetch permissions in developer mode, without write actions.
- ChatGPT may ask for confirmation before write actions, depending on the app's permissions and the action.

So a small team that wants ChatGPT to update the CRM needs ChatGPT Business, and an admin who adds the app once. Claude's connector rules differ; the [Claude CRM guide](/blog/claude-crm) covers them.

Two risks matter once it works. A connected AI acts with the permissions of the person who connected it. And if your CRM can send email or chat messages, a send call sends. Ask for drafts and send them yourself.

## Frequently asked questions

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="Can ChatGPT do sales on its own?">
No. It can research, draft, summarize and suggest, but it does not hold your pipeline or a relationship. In a plain chat it sends nothing. Connected to tools, it can act, and that is exactly why a person should approve what goes to a buyer.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can ChatGPT summarize sales calls?">
Yes, from a transcript or your notes. Give it a fixed format with summary, decisions, next steps with owner and date, and tell it to write "not stated" for anything missing. Review the result before it goes into the CRM.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is it allowed to use ChatGPT for business?">
Yes. OpenAI's terms for individuals include an addendum for commercial and business use, and ChatGPT Business, Enterprise and the API run under OpenAI's business terms. For client data, use a business plan and sign OpenAI's Data Processing Addendum.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How do I connect ChatGPT to my CRM?">
Through an MCP app, an automation tool such as n8n, or copy and paste. MCP apps with write actions currently need ChatGPT Business, Enterprise or Edu, on the web, and an admin who creates the app. Your CRM must offer an MCP server; Customermates' address ends in /api/v1/mcp.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Should I use ChatGPT for outbound or get a dedicated sales tool?">
Use ChatGPT to write better first messages from real research. If you send more than a few dozen emails a week with follow-up steps, you need a tool built for sending, with sequences and deliverability controls. Many teams use both.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What is the best AI tool for sales?">
It depends on the job. A general assistant such as ChatGPT or Claude fits research and drafting. A meeting recorder fits transcripts. A CRM with an AI assistant or an MCP connection fits keeping records current. Start with the task that costs your team the most time.
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
