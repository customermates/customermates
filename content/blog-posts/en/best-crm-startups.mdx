# Best CRM for Startups: Which One Fits Your Stage

There is no single best CRM for startups. Two founders with no budget can start on HubSpot's free tools (up to 2 users) or the Zoho CRM free edition (up to 3 users). For founder-led B2B sales, look at Pipedrive or Attio; for phone-heavy outbound, Close; for high-touch work with partners and investors, folk. Customermates, which publishes this guide, fits service startups whose deals move through email, LinkedIn and WhatsApp conversations.

Below: a decision table by stage, sourced notes on each vendor, the arithmetic behind the startup programs, and a worked setup for customer and investor pipelines side by side. Vendor facts were checked on 6 November 2026 against each vendor's own pages, listed at the end.

## Which CRM fits your stage and sales motion?

Most "best CRM" lists rank tools on features a two-person team will never open. A better filter is where you are and how you sell.

| Your situation | Look at first | Why | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Two founders, pre-revenue, no budget | HubSpot Free Tools, Zoho CRM free edition, Attio Free | $0 entry for 2, 3 and 3 users | The user cap, and the paid tier you need after the first hire |
| VC-backed, pre-seed to Series A | HubSpot for Startups, Attio startup program, Salesforce Launchpad | Large first-year discounts on paid tiers | Annual commitment, eligibility windows, discounts that step down |
| Founder-led B2B sales, one to five sellers | Pipedrive, Attio | Pipedrive calls itself a sales-pipeline CRM; Attio has a free plan for 3 seats | Pipedrive's sequences start on Growth |
| Outbound by phone | Close | Calling and SMS on every plan | Power Dialer and automated workflows start on Growth |
| Investors, partners, community | folk | Focus on high-touch sales, LinkedIn extension on every plan | Deals and email sequences start on Premium |
| Service startup (agency, consultancy, studio) with deals in email, LinkedIn and WhatsApp threads | Customermates (publisher) | Unified inbox on Pro and higher; Mate in Customermates Cloud (hosted AI credits) or your own AI client through MCP; open-source core | No telephony, no sequences, no mobile app, free hosted plan only for [[commercial.limit.free.maxMembers]] person |

If two rows describe you, pick the one that describes how your next ten deals will close, not the one you hope will be true in two years.

## Which vendors belong on your startup CRM shortlist?

### HubSpot

HubSpot suits founders who want a free start and expect to grow into a larger paid suite later. The free tier, called Free Tools, costs $0 for up to 2 users. Sales Hub then runs through Starter, Professional and Enterprise. On 6 November 2026 the [Sales Hub pricing page](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/sales) listed Professional at $90 per seat per month billed annually, or $100 billed monthly, plus a required one-time onboarding fee of $1,500.

[HubSpot for Startups](https://www.hubspot.com/startups) gives 90% off in year one, 50% in year two and 25% in year three to startups that raised pre-seed, seed or Series A (not Series B or later) and are affiliated with an approved partner or have verified funding. A second tier gives 30% and then 15% through approved entrepreneurial organizations. Both apply to net-new Professional or Enterprise products only and require an annual commitment.

### Pipedrive

Pipedrive [describes itself](https://www.pipedrive.com/en) as a sales-pipeline CRM and fits founder-led sales with clear, repeatable stages. The [current plans](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/new-pipedrive-plans) are Lite, Growth, Premium and Ultimate. Sequences, which Pipedrive describes as lead-nurturing workflows built from manual emails and follow-up tasks, start on Growth. LeadBooster, with chatbot, live chat, prospector and web forms, is included on Premium and Ultimate. Pipedrive [charges per seat](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/how-does-pricing-work-in-pipedrive), whether or not the seat is assigned to a user, monthly or annually with a discount for annual payment.

### Attio

Attio has a Free plan for up to 3 seats, then Plus, Pro and Enterprise. On 6 November 2026 its [pricing page](https://attio.com/pricing) displayed Pro at US$79 per user per month billed annually or US$99 billed monthly. Email and calendar sync covers 1 account per user on Free and Plus and 2 on Pro. The [Attio startup program](https://attio.com/startups) gives 80% off the annual Pro plan and credits in year one and 30% off in year two. It is open to VC-backed companies incorporated less than 10 years ago that signed up within the last 90 days, for workspaces of up to 10 seats.

### folk

folk calls itself an AI CRM for the entire sales motion and stresses high-touch sales, with team conversations in one place. The [pricing page](https://www.folk.app/pricing) lists Standard, Premium and Enterprise with a 2-week free trial. Standard includes pipeline management, email campaigns, the LinkedIn extension and email, calendar and WhatsApp sync. Custom objects and deals, email sequences and API access come with Premium.

### Close

Close fits a startup that sells on the phone. [Every plan](https://close.com/pricing) (Solo, Essentials, Growth, Scale) includes calling and SMS, while the Power Dialer and automated workflows start on Growth. The trial runs 14 days with no credit card.

### Zoho CRM and Salesforce

The [Zoho CRM free edition](https://www.zoho.com/crm/zohocrm-pricing.html) is free for 3 users with no time limit; paid editions are Standard, Professional, Enterprise and Ultimate, and annual plans save up to 34%. Salesforce points startups to [Starter Suite](https://www.salesforce.com/crm/startup-crm/), which on 6 November 2026 it said starts at $25 per user per month with no implementation fees and a 30-day free trial, and to Salesforce Launchpad, which adds discounts and implementation support for venture-backed startups.

Formula: seats × monthly per-seat rate × 12 × (1 − discount). HubSpot's required US$1,500 onboarding is separate: do not assume the startup discount waives it. For the 30% startup tier, apply 70% of list in year one and 85% in year two. Eligibility and a written quote decide the actual bill. Customermates' twelve-month total is twelve monthly bills, not a yearly price or commitment. Sources and rates checked on 6 November 2026.

Before choosing, fill in an exit worksheet for **each** finalist: renewal date and cancellation notice from the quote; export format; contacts, organizations, deals and their relationships; conversation history; attachments; and the work needed to recreate fields and views. Mark each item exported and re-imported, manual work, or not verified. Leave migration cost blank until someone has measured the work. A low first-year bill does not answer these questions.

Test with one contact, one organization, one deal, a linked task and an attachment before committing. For Customermates, the [record export guide](/docs/app-records#how-do-i-import-or-export-records) describes list exports in Excel or JSON with active filters applied. This is no whole-account export or attachment transfer; do not treat the record export as an inbox backup. ### Worked setup: a customer pipeline and an investor pipeline from your inbox

This is how a two-founder team could run sales and a seed round in one workspace. A new workspace starts without lists: create the customer, organization, deal and task lists and their relationships first. Task dates and statuses are ordinary configured Date and Single choice fields, not built-in task features.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Connect the channels where deals happen">
    Each user connects accounts under **Settings › Channels**: [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedAccountsPerUser]] per user on Pro, [[commercial.entitlement.business.includedAccountsPerUser]] on Business. On Pro, one founder might connect the Gmail or Outlook mailbox and the other LinkedIn.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Turn conversation participants into contacts">
    In a thread, open **Thread settings** from the header chip. Under **People**, click **Link** next to each participant to attach an existing contact or create one. Every conversation with that address, not only this one, then appears on the contact's timeline. Model each fund as an organization and each partner as a contact.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the investor fields on Deals">
    Add a **Stage** field to Deals (for example Prospecting, Qualification, Demo, Proposal, Negotiation, Won, Lost); stages are not preset, and this field drives the weighted pipeline. Keep it for customers. In **Configure**, open Deals and use **Add**, then **Field**, for four custom fields: Deal type (Single choice: Customer, Investor), Round stage (Single choice: Researching, Intro requested, First meeting, Partner meeting, Due diligence, Committed, Passed), Ticket size (Money) and Next touch (Date).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep investors out of the sales numbers">
    New deals start in the first stage, Prospecting. On investor deals, set Deal type to Investor, clear **Stage** so they do not count as Prospecting, and put the amount in Ticket size. A deal's value comes from its line items, so investor deals without line items add nothing to the total or weighted sales pipeline, and a sales board filtered to Deal type Customer does not show them.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save two views">
    On **Deals**, create a view with **New view**, filter Deal type to Investor, open **Appearance**, choose **Board** and group by Round stage: dragging a card now moves an investor to the next stage. A second view sorts all deals by Next touch, ascending. Share them with your co-founder so you both work from the same filters.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Track commitments as linked tasks">
    For "send the updated deck to the partner", create a task linked to the deal and the contact, and assign it. Tasks have no built-in due date or reminder, so add a Date field on tasks if you want to sort them.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Optional, Customermates Cloud only:** a [routine](/docs/app-routines) set to repeat **Every week**, on Monday at 08:00, can do the review for you. It uses hosted AI credits from its owner's allowance, and reading conversations needs a plan with the inbox. Example instruction:

> Find deals where Deal type is Investor and Next touch is today or earlier. For each one, read the latest conversation with the linked contact, append a two-line status to the deal notes, and save a reply draft in that conversation. Do not send anything.

Keep the last sentence as an instruction, not a technical approval gate. Mate and routines can send emails and chat messages immediately without asking. Review saved drafts in the inbox before sending; draft-first behaviour depends on following your instruction. Each run also has a credit cap; a run that reaches it stops with the status **Partial**, so keep the list short. Starter includes [[commercial.entitlement.starter.includedRoutinesPerUser]] routine per user and Pro includes [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedRoutinesPerUser]].

## What should you check before committing?

1. **List the channels where deals actually move.** If half your pipeline lives in LinkedIn and WhatsApp, a CRM that only syncs email will stay half empty.
2. **Price month 25, not month 1.** The program table above shows why.
3. **Count seats after the next hire.** Free tiers stop at 2 or 3 users, and Attio's startup program covers workspaces of up to 10 seats.
4. **Check how data comes out.** Ask which file format, whether export is per object or for the whole account, and whether there is an API. Customermates exports one list at a time as an Excel workbook or JSON and has a REST API; there is no whole-account export.
5. **Run twenty real deals through the trial** before importing your whole spreadsheet.

Moving from a spreadsheet into Customermates works one list at a time. Import the sheet as an Excel workbook or a CSV file, organizations first, then contacts, then deals, so the link columns can match existing records by name. Each file is row-capped; the [import guide](/docs/app-records) has the limits.

## FAQ

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="What is the best free CRM for startups?">
For two founders, HubSpot Free Tools covers up to 2 users at $0. Zoho CRM's free edition and Attio Free each cover up to 3. Check the upgrade path before you build habits around one: the paid tier you need after the first hire is the real price. Customermates Cloud has a free plan for [[commercial.limit.free.maxMembers]] person and [[commercial.limit.free.maxRecords]] records, without the inbox, Mate or routines; its AGPL core can be self-hosted without a licence fee, without the inbox and Mate.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What CRM do startups use?">
I know of no reliable public census, so be careful with any list that claims one. What is public is who courts startups: HubSpot, Attio and Salesforce each run a startup program with discounts for venture-backed companies. If you are bootstrapped, a free tier or a low per-seat plan is enough until the second or third hire.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can I manage fundraising and investors in a CRM?">
Yes, as a pipeline: one organization per fund, one contact per partner and one deal per fund per round, with a separate stage field, a ticket-size field and a next-touch date. The worked setup above shows this in Customermates. A CRM does not replace a cap table, a data room or deck analytics; use dedicated tools for those.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="When should a startup start using a CRM?">
When a second person talks to customers or investors, or when you miss a follow-up because it lived in someone's inbox. Before that, a spreadsheet works. After that, the cleanup gets harder every month, and importing 50 organizations is easier than reconstructing 500 threads.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Will AI replace the CRM?">
No. AI changes who types into the CRM, not whether you need a shared record of who said what and where each deal stands. In Customermates Cloud, Mate can update records and draft replies using hosted AI credits, and external clients such as Claude or ChatGPT do the same through MCP. The records still live in the CRM, where your next hire can read them.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How much should a startup spend on a CRM?">
Compare the per-seat cost of the tier you will need in a year, which is rarely the entry tier. Two founders on Customermates Pro pay [[commercial.price.pro.monthly.seats.2]] per month; five people on Starter pay [[commercial.price.starter.monthly.seats.5]]. For HubSpot Professional or Attio Pro, run the year-three numbers from the program table above before you sign an annual contract.
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
