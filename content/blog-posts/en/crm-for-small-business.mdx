# CRM for Small Business: Which One Fits and What Five Users Pay

Pick a small-business CRM by how your deals move, then price it at your real headcount. Two or three people with no budget can start on HubSpot's free tools (up to 2 users) or the Zoho CRM free edition (up to 3 users). A sales team that works a pipeline stage by stage should look at Pipedrive and HubSpot first, and a company that runs on Outlook and Teams should price Microsoft Dynamics 365 Sales. If your clients write by email, LinkedIn and WhatsApp and the deal is decided in those threads, you need a CRM that puts the conversations on the deal. That is the case Customermates, the publisher of this guide, was built for.

Below: a decision table, what five users pay per month on each vendor's entry tier, a worked setup for a five-person service team, and the limits that should rule a product out. Vendor prices were checked on 8 October 2026 on each vendor's own pricing page, linked at the end.

## What a team of five needs from a CRM

Enterprise checklists ask about sales territories and approval chains. A team of three to twenty people has fewer questions, and they are sharper. Ask them in the demo, with your own data on screen.

| Need | Ask the vendor | Why it matters at five people |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Records that stay current without nagging | Which channels sync into a contact: email only, or LinkedIn and WhatsApp too? Can an AI assistant update records? | Nobody in a five-person company retypes conversations. The CRM is current only if updates come from where the work happens. |
| A deal value that matches how you bill | Can the value come from line items, such as a workshop day or a monthly retainer? | The pipeline total should be the number you would actually bill, not a guess typed into a field. |
| Access per person | Can a freelancer see only the clients assigned to them? | Small teams mix employees, freelancers and partners. |
| A price that survives the next hire | What does the tier you need cost per user, monthly and annually? Is there a one-time fee? | Free plans stop at 2 or 3 users, and some paid tiers come with onboarding fees or annual contracts. |
| A way out | Which file format does export use? Per record type or the whole account? Is there an API? | Leaving is cheap only if you checked this before you arrived. |

| Your situation | Look at first | Why | Watch for |

If two rows describe you, go by where last month's deals were actually won: on calls, in a pipeline review or in a message thread. For the most common two-vendor shortlist, see [HubSpot vs Pipedrive](/compare/hubspot-vs-pipedrive).

Three notes on the numbers:

- **Zoho CRM** is free for 3 users with no time limit. Five users need one of the paid editions (Standard, Professional, Enterprise, Ultimate). Zoho shows prices by region, and annual billing costs less than monthly billing.
- **One-time fees** matter more for a small team than the seat price. HubSpot's $1,500 Professional onboarding fee costs more than three months of five Professional seats at the annual-billing price ($1,350).
- **Customermates** bills per user and month, with no annual commitment. Starter has no inbox. Every cloud plan includes monthly hosted AI credits per active user; see [pricing](/pricing).

### Worked setup: a five-person consultancy on Pro

<Steps>
  <Step title="Price what you sell">
    On **Services**, add each offering with an **Amount**, for example a workshop day and a monthly retainer. A deal's value is the sum of each linked service's amount times its quantity, so a deal with one workshop day and six retainer months is worth exactly that. If "deals" is not your word, rename the record type to Projects or Jobs under **My Company › Settings › Data model**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the stage probabilities">
    A new workspace starts with a **Stage** column on deals: Prospecting 10%, Qualification 20%, Demo 40%, Proposal 60%, Negotiation 80%, Won 100%, Lost 0%. Adjust the percentages under **My Company › Settings**. The weighted pipeline is each deal's value times the probability of its stage, a fixed weight you chose and not a prediction.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Import the spreadsheet, one record type at a time">
    Save the sheet as an Excel workbook (`.xlsx`); CSV files are not accepted. On each list, **Import and export › Add from file** lets you match spreadsheet columns to fields. Import organizations first, then contacts, then deals, because link columns match existing records by name. New contacts need a first and a last name. Row and size limits are in the [records guide](/docs/app-records).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect the channels where clients write">
    Each person connects accounts under **My Profile › Channels**: [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedAccountsPerUser]] per user on Pro, [[commercial.entitlement.business.includedAccountsPerUser]] on Business. In a conversation, open **Thread settings** from the header chip and click **Link** next to a participant to attach the address to a contact. Every conversation with that address then appears on the contact's timeline.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Limit what freelancers see">
    Under **My Company › Roles**, set **Read access** to **Assigned** for a freelancer role. People with that role see only the records they are assigned to. Deals have assigned users rather than one owner, so a senior consultant and a freelancer can share a deal.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save one view per person">
    On **Deals**, click **New view**, filter **Assigned** to your name, and under **Appearance** choose **Board** grouped by Stage. Views are personal, so each teammate saves their own.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Customermates Cloud only:** an event-driven [routine](/docs/app-routines) can take over the handoff after a win. Under **Routines**, click **Add**, choose **When something changes**, pick the event **Deal Updated**, set **Changed fields** to Stage and add a record filter for Stage is Won. Example instruction:

> This deal just moved to Won. Create a task "Kickoff: deal name" linked to the deal, its organization and its contacts, and assign it to the deal's assigned users. Then read the latest conversation with the main contact and add a three-line summary of what was agreed to the deal notes. Do not send any messages.

The run uses hosted AI credits from the routine owner's allowance and works with that owner's access and channels. Mate can send emails and chat messages without asking first, so keep the last sentence: it limits the routine to a task and a note. Reading conversations needs a plan with the inbox. Starter includes [[commercial.entitlement.starter.includedRoutinesPerUser]] routine per user and Pro includes [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedRoutinesPerUser]].

Tasks in Customermates have no built-in due date and no reminder. If you want to sort kickoffs by date, add a custom Date column to tasks.

## FAQ

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="Which CRM is best for a small business?">
The one your team will keep current, at a price that still works after the next hire. Be wary of any list that names one winner for everyone. Start from the decision table above, shortlist two products, and run twenty real deals through each trial before you import everything.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How much does a CRM cost for a small business?">
On 8 October 2026, other vendors' entry paid tiers at list price ran from €14 per user per month (Pipedrive Lite, billed annually) to $65 (Dynamics 365 Sales Professional, paid yearly), so five users pay between €70 and $325 a month before any one-time fees. HubSpot Sales Hub Starter lists at $20 per seat; HubSpot was also showing a time-limited offer of $7 on annual billing. Customermates costs [[commercial.price.starter.monthly]] per user per month on Starter and [[commercial.price.pro.monthly]] on Pro.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is a free CRM enough for a small business?">
For two or three people tracking contacts and a simple pipeline, often yes. The limit arrives with the next hire, because HubSpot's free tools stop at 2 users and Zoho's free edition at 3. Customermates has no free hosted plan; its AGPL core can be self-hosted without a licence fee, without the inbox and without Mate.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is an enterprise CRM too complex for a smaller team?">
Often the terms are a bigger hurdle than the software. Salesforce Pro Suite is billed annually with a contract, HubSpot's Enterprise tier starts at $150 per seat per month plus a required $3,500 onboarding fee, and Dynamics 365 Sales is paid yearly. Those terms fit a company with an admin who configures the system. A team without one is better off with a tier it can set up itself and leave without a contract.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can ChatGPT build or replace a CRM?">
ChatGPT can suggest spreadsheet columns or write code for a simple contact app. It does not give you a shared, permissioned record that the whole team updates and that outlives a chat. The useful split runs the other way: keep the records in a CRM and let an AI client work on them. Claude, ChatGPT and other clients can read and update Customermates through MCP, and in Customermates Cloud, Mate does the same with hosted AI credits.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How long does it take to set up a CRM?">
If your data sits in one clean spreadsheet, plan one working day for the steps in the worked setup above: stages, the import, connected channels and one view per person. The habit takes longer than the setup. Connecting the channels where clients already write shortens it, because the CRM then fills from conversations instead of typing.
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
