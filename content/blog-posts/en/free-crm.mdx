# Free CRM software: what is actually free?

**Free CRM software** usually means one of three things: a permanent but limited cloud plan, source code you operate yourself, or a temporary trial of a paid product. The right choice depends less on the word “free” than on users, records, workflow limits, data movement, support, and who operates the system.

<ArticleSummary title="The short answer">
  <SummaryItem label="Permanent free cloud plan">
    Best when a small team wants a hosted CRM quickly and can work within the
    provider’s current user, record, automation, and support limits.
  </SummaryItem>
  <SummaryItem label="Open-source self-hosting">
    Best when you can operate the application, database, upgrades, backups, and
    security yourself. The software license can be free while operations are not.
  </SummaryItem>
  <SummaryItem label="Free trial">
    Best for testing a paid product with a representative workflow. A trial is
    not a long-term free plan.
  </SummaryItem>
  <SummaryItem label="What to verify">
    Check official pricing and limit pages on the day you decide. Vendor plans,
    AI allowances, and included integrations change.
  </SummaryItem>
</ArticleSummary>

## Three free CRM models

| Model | What is free | What still costs | Good first fit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Permanent cloud plan | A provider-hosted account within published limits | Higher tiers, add-ons, extra capacity, and migration later | Solo operators and very small teams |
| Open-source self-hosting | Access to source code under its license | Infrastructure, setup, maintenance, security, backups, and support | Technical teams that value control |
| Free trial | Temporary access to a paid product | The subscription after the trial | Teams comparing real workflows before buying |

A free plan can be the sensible answer. It can also be the expensive answer if a team discovers too late that the missing capability is data export, permissions, automation, or the channel where customer conversations happen.

## Current shortlist from official provider sources

The following is a starting point, not a universal ranking. The links go to the provider’s own current page so you can verify the plan before signup.

### HubSpot Free CRM

[HubSpot’s official free CRM page](https://www.hubspot.com/products/crm) currently describes a no-expiration free account. Its [pricing page](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/crm) publishes the current user, contact, and feature limits. It is a strong candidate when you want a hosted CRM and expect to add HubSpot products later.

Check the exact limits, which tools are “lite” versions, what carries HubSpot branding, and the cost of the first paid tier your workflow would require.

### Zoho CRM Free Edition

[Zoho’s official free CRM page](https://www.zoho.com/crm/free-crm.html) positions the free edition for a small sales team and publishes its current user, record, workflow, storage, and integration limits. It is worth evaluating when your team already uses the Zoho ecosystem.

Check whether the free edition supports the precise automation, email, reporting, and integration path you need rather than assuming every Zoho capability is included.

### Bitrix24 Free

[Bitrix24’s pricing page](https://www.bitrix24.com/prices/) lists a permanent free tier beside flat-price paid plans. It combines CRM with broader collaboration and work-management features.

Check the current free-plan user policy, storage, pipeline, email, telephony, automation, and app limits. The breadth can be useful, but it also makes a representative workflow test important.

### Brevo Free

[Brevo’s current plan guide](https://help.brevo.com/hc/en-us/articles/208589409-About-Brevo-s-pricing-plans) describes a free plan with sales tools alongside marketing and messaging limits. It is relevant when email campaigns and a lightweight sales pipeline belong in one platform.

Check seats, open-deal and pipeline limits, connected mailbox and calendar allowances, automation, and whether you need a paid sales package.

### CentralStationCRM Starter

[CentralStationCRM’s plan page](https://centralstationcrm.de/plans) publishes a permanent Starter tier and its current users, contacts, and storage allowances. It is especially relevant for German-language teams seeking a deliberately simple hosted CRM.

Check whether the limits and optional extensions fit the next twelve months, not only today’s contact count.

### SuiteCRM and other open-source options

[SuiteCRM](https://suitecrm.com/) publishes an open-source CRM that you can operate yourself. Open-source access changes the cost model; it does not remove hosting, security, upgrade, backup, integration, or support work.

The same evaluation rule applies to every self-hosted option: inspect the license, release cadence, deployment documentation, upgrade path, security process, and the exact edition you plan to run.

### A free trial is not a free tier

Some products offer a trial rather than a permanent free CRM. For example, [Freshsales’ current pricing page](https://www.freshworks.com/crm/pricing/) presents paid plans with a trial. That can still be useful for evaluation, but it belongs in a different row of the decision.

## How to evaluate a free CRM in one afternoon

<Steps>
  <Step title="Define one representative workflow">
    Use a real but non-sensitive example: create an organization and contact,
    open a deal, assign a task, move the deal, and find the full history again.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write down the hard limits">
    Record users, records, fields, pipelines, permissions, automation, files,
    email, API access, support, import, and export from the official plan page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Test the exit before the entry">
    Confirm how you would export records and attachments. A free start is less
    valuable when leaving later requires manual reconstruction.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Price the first realistic upgrade">
    Model the plan you would need after adding the next teammate, pipeline,
    automation, or channel. Do not compare only the zero-price row.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assign operational ownership">
    For self-hosting, name who owns deployment, upgrades, backups, monitoring,
    security response, and recovery. If nobody owns them, they are not free.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Where Customermates fits

Customermates has an AGPL-licensed community core that can be self-hosted, and a separate managed cloud product starting at [[commercial.price.starter.monthly]] per user per month. Those are different operating models.

- The self-hosted edition gives you the community core under its license; you operate the stack and receive Starter entitlements.
- The managed cloud product operates the application and database service. Supported unified-inbox channels are cloud-plan capabilities from Pro.
- MCP exposes supported CRM tools to compatible external AI clients. The client and model provider remain separate.
- Hosted Mate is a cloud capability with plan allowances and environment-dependent availability; it is not a self-hosted feature.
- n8n is a separate product you run or subscribe to. Customermates publishes open interfaces and a community node rather than bundling n8n.
- A general import/export workflow is not currently promised, so include data entry and migration in your evaluation.

## Decision checklist

Choose a permanent free cloud plan when all of the following are true:

- the current published limits cover your next twelve months;
- the required workflow works without a paid add-on;
- permissions and support match your risk;
- export and migration are acceptable.

Choose open-source self-hosting when:

- control of deployment and source matters;
- someone owns operations and security;
- infrastructure and maintenance cost are budgeted;
- the edition provides the exact CRM workflow you need.

Choose a paid plan or trial when the missing capability—permissions, automation, support, data movement, or a customer channel—costs more to work around than the subscription.

## Frequently asked questions

<Faq>
  <FaqItem question="Is there a genuinely free CRM?">
    Yes. Some providers publish permanent free cloud tiers, and open-source CRM
    projects publish code you can operate yourself. The two models have different
    limits and costs, so verify the current official plan and operating burden.
  </FaqItem>

  <FaqItem question="Is open-source CRM free?">
    The software can be available without a license fee, depending on its license.
    Hosting, setup, upgrades, backups, security, integrations, and support still
    require time or money.
  </FaqItem>

  <FaqItem question="Which free CRM is best for a small team?">
    The best fit is the one whose current limits cover your representative
    workflow and whose first paid upgrade remains acceptable. Test users,
    records, permissions, automation, import/export, and support before deciding.
  </FaqItem>

  <FaqItem question="Does Customermates offer a free hosted CRM plan?">
    This page does not claim a permanent free cloud plan. Customermates offers a
    self-hostable AGPL community core and paid managed-cloud plans with a trial.
