A sales CRM is the system where every open deal lives: who the buyer is, which company they work for, what the deal is worth, which stage it is in and what happens next. Which one fits depends less on feature counts than on how your team sells. Phone-heavy outbound teams need a dialer, pipeline-driven teams need fast stage updates and automated follow-ups, and small service teams whose deals are decided in email, LinkedIn or WhatsApp threads need those conversations on the deal.

This guide gives you the checks to run in a demo, a decision table by sales motion, entry prices taken from each vendor's pricing page on 8 October 2026, and a worked pipeline setup. Customermates publishes this guide and appears in its own labelled section; nothing below is ranked.

## What a sales CRM holds

A general CRM may also run marketing campaigns or support tickets. A sales CRM is built around the deal. The core record model is the same almost everywhere:

| Record | What it holds | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contact | A person, their channels and the conversation history | Anna Weber, head of operations |
| Organization | The company, with all its contacts and deals | Weber Logistik GmbH |
| Deal | One opportunity: value, stage, assigned reps, linked people | "Weber: warehouse process audit", 18,000 |
| Stage | Where the deal sits in your process | Proposal |
| Activity | Emails, calls, meetings and changes on the record | Proposal sent on Tuesday |

If your team can open one deal and see all five without asking a colleague, the CRM is doing its job. If someone still has to search their inbox to know what was promised, it is not.

## Features to check in a demo

Vendor feature pages list everything. These are the questions that separate a CRM your reps will keep current from one they will avoid. Bring your own deals to the demo and ask the vendor to show each answer live.

| Feature | Ask the vendor | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pipeline board | Can I drag a deal to the next stage, and can I edit the stages myself? | Stage changes happen dozens of times a week. Each one should take a second. |
| Deal value | Is the value typed in, or calculated from products or services and quantities? | A calculated value is the amount you will actually charge. |
| Conversation capture | Which channels land on the contact automatically: email only, or LinkedIn and WhatsApp too? | Whatever is not captured has to be retyped, and mostly it won't be. |
| Follow-up automation | Can the CRM send follow-up sequences, or only create tasks? On which plan? | Sequences are often gated to a higher tier. |
| Pipeline value | Does the pipeline total weight deals by stage probability? Is there a separate forecasting module, and on which plan? | A weighted total and a revenue forecast are different things. |
| Reporting | Can a sales lead build a chart of pipeline value by stage or by rep without an analyst? | If the weekly review needs a spreadsheet export, the CRM is not the source of truth. |
| AI | Can an AI assistant update records and draft replies? Does that cost extra credits or a higher plan? | AI is useful when it removes data entry. Check what it costs per month. |
| Leaving | Which export format? One record type at a time or the whole account? Is there an API? | Switching costs are decided before you move in. |

### Weighted pipeline is not a forecast

A common question is which CRM features predict revenue. Many CRMs offer a weighted pipeline: each stage gets a win probability, and every deal's value is multiplied by it.

| Deal | Value | Stage | Probability you set | Weighted value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Process audit | 18,000 | Proposal | 50% | 9,000 |
| Training days | 6,000 | Qualification | 20% | 1,200 |
| Retainer renewal | 24,000 | Negotiation | 75% | 18,000 |
| **Total** | **48,000** | | | **28,200** |

That 28,200 is arithmetic on probabilities you chose, not a prediction. It tells you whether the pipeline can carry next quarter if your stage probabilities are honest. Dedicated forecasting features sit in paid tiers at the vendors checked for this guide: Salesforce from Pro Suite, HubSpot from Sales Hub Professional, Pipedrive's forecast view from Growth and Zoho CRM's sales forecasting from Standard. For many small teams, a weighted pipeline reviewed every week is enough.

## The Monday-morning test

Before comparing features, run one thought experiment with every CRM on your shortlist. Picture your busiest rep on Monday morning, behind on last week. They have 14 emails to file, 3 calls from Friday, 2 meetings from Thursday and 5 deals with new amounts or stages.

How long will that take in this CRM? If the answer is more than 15 minutes, the CRM will be accurate for a few weeks and then quietly drift. The products that pass are the ones where updates come from where the work already happens: synced conversations, one-click stage changes, and increasingly an AI assistant that writes the notes and moves the stage for the rep. The ones that fail have long forms with required fields.

## Where Customermates fits

Customermates is an open-source, AI-native CRM built in Germany for small client-facing teams: consultants, agencies, service businesses and founders who sell. Its case is a team whose deals move in conversations rather than in a call queue. Here is how a sales pipeline is set up in it, verified against the product.

**Deals and stages.** A new workspace starts with a **Stage** field on deals, with the options Prospecting, Qualification, Demo, Proposal, Negotiation, Won and Lost. It is a single-select field, so you rename, add or remove stages yourself. On the **Deals** board grouped by **Stage**, you drag a card to change its stage.

**Deal value from services.** You keep your offers as services with a price, then link them to a deal with a quantity. The deal value is the sum, so "3 workshop days plus a monthly retainer" gives the amount you will charge, not a guess.

**Weighted pipeline.** Under **My Company › Settings**, the **Deal stage field** names the stage field, with a win probability from 0 to 100 percent per stage. A new workspace starts with Stage selected and probabilities filled in, so you adjust them to your own win rates. Every deal then gets a **Weighted Value**, and the settings page shows the **Total pipeline** and **Weighted pipeline**. Details are in the [core concepts docs](/docs/concepts).

**Assignment and segments.** Deals have **Assigned** users, so two people can share a deal. Add a single-select field such as "Lead source" with Referral, LinkedIn, Website and Event to segment deals.

**Views for the weekly review.** A saved view stores filters, sort, layout and grouping. Example: "My open deals", filtered to deals assigned to you and Stage not Won or Lost, shown as a board grouped by Stage. Saved views are personal, so each teammate sets the same filters and saves their own view. On the dashboard, a chart of deals with the metric weighted deal value, grouped by Stage, gives the review its one number.

**Conversations on the deal.** On Pro and higher, the [unified inbox](/features/unified-inbox) brings email (Gmail, Outlook, IMAP), LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Instagram and Telegram into the CRM, linked to contacts and deals. Pro includes [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedAccountsPerUser]] connected account per user, Business [[commercial.entitlement.business.includedAccountsPerUser]]. Starter has no inbox.

**AI that does the data entry (Customermates Cloud).** Mate, the assistant in Customermates Cloud, uses hosted AI credits. It can summarize a thread, draft a reply and update the deal. Routines run a saved instruction on a schedule or when a record changes. An example routine, triggered when a deal is updated and limited to the changed field Stage:

> When a deal moves to Proposal, read the latest conversation with its contacts, write a three-line summary of what was agreed into the deal notes, and draft a follow-up reply. Do not send anything.

The last sentence matters: Mate sends messages when told to, so a routine that should only draft has to say so. See [how routines work](/docs/app-routines). If you prefer your own AI client, Claude, ChatGPT and others can work with the CRM through [MCP](/docs/mcp) with [[derived.mcp.tools.total]] tools, using your own AI provider instead of hosted credits.

## Rolling out a sales CRM in a small team

<Steps>
  <Step title="Write down your stages">Five to seven stages, each with an exit rule, for example "Proposal: a written offer has been sent". If you cannot name the rule, merge the stage with its neighbour.</Step>
  <Step title="Decide the minimum fields">Deal name, value, stage, assigned rep and the main contact. Everything else is optional until the team asks for it.</Step>
  <Step title="Import one record type at a time">Organizations first, then contacts, then deals, so the links resolve. In Customermates, import and export use XLSX workbooks, one record type per file; the [Excel CRM template guide](/blog/excel-crm-template) shows how to clean a sheet first.</Step>
  <Step title="Connect conversations">On Pro or Business, each rep connects the channel where most deals are discussed, for example the mailbox or LinkedIn, so new messages land on the right contact without retyping. Pro includes [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedAccountsPerUser]] connected account per user, Business [[commercial.entitlement.business.includedAccountsPerUser]].</Step>
  <Step title="Run the weekly review in the CRM">Open the pipeline board in the meeting instead of a slide or a spreadsheet. Deals without a next step get one before the meeting ends. The [sales pipeline guide](/blog/sales-pipeline) covers stage design in more depth.</Step>
</Steps>

## FAQ

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="What is a sales CRM?">
A sales CRM is software that stores contacts, companies and deals in one place and shows each deal in a pipeline stage with a value and the reps assigned to it. Its job is to make the next step on every deal visible to the whole team, so nothing depends on one person's inbox or memory.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Which is the best CRM for sales?">
There is no single best one, because sales motions differ. Close is built for teams that make many calls. Pipedrive suits teams that run a clear stage-based pipeline. HubSpot fits companies that want marketing and sales on one platform. Salesforce fits larger teams with a dedicated admin. Customermates, which publishes this guide, fits small service teams whose deals are decided in email, LinkedIn and WhatsApp threads.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How much does a sales CRM cost per month?">
Free tools exist for very small teams: HubSpot's free tools cover up to 2 users and the Zoho CRM free edition up to 3. On 8 October 2026, Salesforce Starter Suite listed $25 per user per month and Close Essentials $49 billed monthly; the plans with sequences or forecasting cost more. Customermates Pro, with the unified inbox, is [[commercial.price.pro.monthly]] per user per month.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Which CRM features help predict revenue?">
Most small-team CRMs give you a weighted pipeline: deal value times the win probability you assign to each stage. That is useful for planning, but it is arithmetic, not a prediction. Dedicated forecasting features come in higher tiers, for example Salesforce Pro Suite or HubSpot Sales Hub Professional. Customermates has the weighted pipeline only.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is a CRM hard to learn?">
The basics take an afternoon: a pipeline board, a contact list and a deal page. What takes longer is agreeing on stages and the minimum fields, and keeping the data current. Choose a CRM where updates come from synced conversations or an assistant, and keep required fields to a handful.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What is the difference between a sales CRM and a CRM?">
Every sales CRM is a CRM, but not every CRM is built for sales. General platforms add marketing automation, support tickets or ERP functions. A sales CRM puts the deal pipeline first: stages, deal values, assigned reps and next steps. If you also need invoicing or stock management, read about [CRM and ERP](/blog/crm-and-erp).
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
