# Sales Follow Up Email Template: Examples for Every Situation, With Timing and Context

A useful sales follow up email template names what the buyer asked for, supplies one checked answer and asks for one next step. Choose when to send from their reply and agreed dates; stop immediately after rejection or an opt-out. Customermates can help keep the conversation and your chosen next step together.

Below you find templates sorted by situation: after a sales call, after a proposal, after no response, closing the loop and after the sale. Each one says when to send it and which piece of context to pull from your notes, because a follow-up that quotes the buyer's own words is the one that gets answered.

<ArticleSummary title="At a glance">
  <SummaryItem label="After a sales call">A recap the same day, ideally within a few hours: their goal, their blocker, who decides, and the next step with a date.</SummaryItem>
  <SummaryItem label="After no response">Check the agreed date and permission first. Consider one useful question only if it adds information.</SummaryItem>
  <SummaryItem label="Closing the loop">Close internally when there is no active next step. Rejection or an opt-out means stop immediately.</SummaryItem>
  <SummaryItem label="Context">Every template starts from a fact you wrote down: the last call note, the date you agreed, or the person who owns the decision.</SummaryItem>
</ArticleSummary>


For a first commercial contact, choose a relevant, permission-checked draft from the [cold email templates](/blog/cold-email-templates) before planning any follow-up.
## What every sales follow up email needs

Weak follow-ups usually fail for one of two reasons: they repeat the pitch, or they ask nothing that can be answered in one line. Four parts fix both.

| Part | What it does | Weak | Better |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Subject line | Tells the buyer which conversation this is | "Following up" | "Thursday's call: the import question" |
| First sentence | Picks up where you left off | "I hope this finds you well." | "You asked whether your old deal history can come across. It can, here is how." |
| Body | Delivers one thing: an answer, a resource, a decision aid | A recap of everything you sell | Two sentences on the one problem they named |
| Ask | One step, with a date | "Let me know your thoughts." | "Could you send me the export by Friday so I can test it?" |

### Where the context comes from

The difference between a template and a good follow-up is one specific detail. Before you write, look up three things:

| Context | Where you find it | How it shows up in the email |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The last call note | Your notes on the deal or contact, written right after the call | "You said the blocker is the data migration." |
| The agreed date | The next step you both said out loud: a decision date, a demo, a budget meeting | "You wanted to decide by 15 November." |
| The owner | Who on their side decides, and who on your side owns the deal | "Is Maria still the right person for the security review?" |

If you cannot fill a bracket in the templates below from one of these three, cut the sentence. A follow-up with a made-up personal detail is worse than a short, plain one.

### Subject lines that get opened

Specific beats clever. These work because they point at something only the two of you discussed:

- "Recap and next steps: [topic] for [company]"
- "The answer on [question they asked]"
- "[Colleague's name] asked about [concern], here is the detail"
- "Proposal for [project]: two points to check"
- "Still on your list for [month]?"

Avoid fake reply prefixes such as "Re:" on a first email. The buyer notices, and it costs you the trust the follow-up is meant to build.

The intervals in the template labels are examples only. The decision tree overrides them: an agreed date, rejection or opt-out takes priority.

## Follow up email after a sales call

The first follow-up is the easiest one to get right, because the conversation is fresh. Send it from the same address, in the same thread as the meeting invitation if there was one.

### Template 1: Same-day recap after a sales call

**When:** the same day, within a few hours. **Context:** your call note and the next step you agreed.

**Subject:** Recap: [topic] and next steps

Hi [first name],

Thanks for the time today. My notes, so we are working from the same list:

- You want to [goal] before [date or event].
- The blocker right now is [problem in their words].
- [Person] needs to sign off on [budget, security, scope].

Next steps: I send [document] by [day]. You check [item] with [person]. We speak again on [day and time].

Does that match your notes?

[Your name]

### Template 2: After a demo with a new stakeholder

**When:** the same day, addressed to the person who joined for the first time, copying your main contact. **Context:** the question the new person asked.

**Subject:** [Colleague's name] asked about [concern], here is the detail

Hi [first name],

Good to meet you on today's call. You asked how [concern]. Short answer: [direct answer]. The details are in [document or page], section [name].

[Main contact] mentioned that you decide on [topic] together by [date]. If a 20-minute session on [concern] alone would help before then, I can do [day] or [day].

[Your name]

## Follow up after a proposal

Proposals go quiet because the buyer has to explain them to someone else. Make that easy. If proposals keep stalling because the buyer was never a fit, [client qualification](/blog/client-qualification) helps you spot that before you write one.

### Template 3: Two days after sending a proposal

**When:** two working days after the proposal. **Context:** the scope you agreed and the date they want to start.

**Subject:** Proposal for [project]: two points to check

Hi [first name],

The proposal went out on [date]. Two points I would look at first:

- **[Scope or option]:** this covers [need they named], and leaves out [item] on purpose.
- **[Price or timeline]:** based on [team size, start date] as we discussed.

Would a 20-minute call on [day] help to go through it with [stakeholder]?

[Your name]

## Follow up email after no response

Silence does not explain the buyer’s decision and does not authorize another message. Each follow-up after silence needs one thing the previous one did not have.

### Template 4: Second follow-up with something new

**When:** two to three working days after the last email. **Context:** the challenge they described on the call.

**Subject:** [Resource] on [their challenge]

Hi [first name],

After our call I remembered [article, checklist, short example] on [challenge they mentioned]. The part about [specific section] is close to what you described about [their situation].

No need to reply if it is not useful. If it is, I can walk you through how it would look for [company] on [day].

[Your name]

### Template 5: Answering an objection you heard

**When:** about a week after the call, if they raised a concern. **Context:** the objection in their words.

**Subject:** Your question about [concern]

Hi [first name],

You asked whether [objection]. Fair question. Here is the honest answer: [direct answer, including what does not work].

If you want to check it yourself, [trial step, document, reference] would show it in about [time].

Which would help you more?

[Your name]

### Template 6: No reply after a week or two

**When:** about two weeks after the call. **Context:** the decision date they gave you.

**Subject:** Still on your list for [month]?

Hi [first name],

When we spoke on [date], you were aiming to decide by [agreed date]. Has the timing moved?

A one-word answer is fine: "yes", "later" or "no".

[Your name]

## Closing the loop politely

### Template 7: The closing email

**When:** only when a final message is appropriate within the permitted exchange; never after rejection or an opt-out. **Context:** the problem they described.

**Subject:** Shall I close this for now?

Hi [first name],

I have not heard back since [date], so I will assume [project] is not a priority at the moment, and I will stop writing about it.

If [problem they described] comes back, reply to this email and we pick up where we left off.

All the best,
[Your name]

A closing email is optional, not a tactic to provoke a reply. Mark the deal as lost or paused in your [sales pipeline](/blog/sales-pipeline) when you send it, so nobody follows up again by accident.

## After the sale

### Template 8: Checking in after the purchase

**When:** two days, two weeks or two months after the purchase. **Context:** the result they bought it for.

**Subject:** How is [product or service] working out?

Hi [first name],

[Product or service] has been live for [time]. Is [result they wanted] happening the way you expected?

If anything feels harder than it should, reply with one line and I will look at it this week.

[Your name]

If the reply turns out to be a complaint, stop the sales follow-ups and handle it first through your [complaint management process](/blog/complaint-management): one owner, a quick acknowledgement, then a fix.

## When should you send, wait or stop? A buyer-response decision tree

Choose the branch before choosing a sales follow up email template. These are editorial working rules for an existing, permission-checked conversation, not legal permission or an automatic sequence. Templates 1 to 8 are alternatives, not eight messages to send in order.

| Check in this order | Action and timing | Stop condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Did they opt out or reject the offer? | Stop sales follow-ups immediately. Record the request so another colleague does not restart contact. | No closing pitch, different channel or new salesperson to bypass the refusal. |
| Is permission or the requested scope unclear? | Hold the draft and check the applicable national rules and original request. | No send while that question is unresolved. |
| Did they ask for a later date, or send an out-of-office reply? | Wait for the requested date or their return; do not run a parallel reminder. | A later date replaces the earlier plan. An auto-reply is not buying interest. |
| Did they ask a question or raise an objection? | Answer that point once you have checked the answer, using template 5. | Do not add an unrelated pitch; pause if you cannot substantiate the answer. |
| Is there an agreed decision or delivery date? | Deliver what you promised; ask about the next step on the agreed date. | Do not chase before the buyer's review meeting. |
| No answer and no agreed date? | If the scope permits, consider one useful check after two or three working days, using template 3 or 4. | Without new information or an active reason to continue, close internally. Silence is not consent. |
| Still silent after that useful check? | Close internally, or use template 7 once only if a closing message remains appropriate and permitted. | No restart unless the buyer reopens the topic or agrees a new next step. |

### Worked draft: the proposal is waiting for a review meeting

Illustrative notes: Maya requested a scoped onboarding proposal, said finance meets on 12 November, and asked for the training line to be separated. It is 6 November. The right action today is to deliver the revision she requested, then wait until the agreed review date, rather than chase a decision two days after the proposal.

**Subject:** Revised onboarding proposal for your 12 November review

Hi Maya,

You asked for training to be separate so finance can compare the onboarding scope at the 12 November meeting. The revised proposal separates those two lines; the scope we discussed is unchanged.

Shall I check back on 13 November after your review?

Alex

If she confirms, record 13 November. If she says the project is cancelled, stop immediately; do not send the closing template.

### Worked draft: an unanswered request with one useful answer

Illustrative notes: Jordan asked for a migration checklist after a demo but gave no decision date. You sent it three working days ago. A question from the call remains unanswered: whether attachments are included. You have now checked that your proposed service excludes them. This draft discloses the limitation rather than inventing urgency.

**Subject:** One scope point on the migration checklist

Hi Jordan,

You asked whether attachments are included in the migration scope. I checked: the proposed scope covers the agreed record data, but excludes attachments. I should have made that explicit in the checklist.

Does that exclusion rule this option out for your team?

Alex

If there is still no reply, close internally under this decision rule. If Jordan says “not this quarter”, pause and agree a date only if Jordan wants one. If Jordan says “no” or asks you to stop, stop immediately.

### What is the 2-2-2 rule in sales?

The 2-2-2 rule is a follow-up rhythm for existing customers: contact them two days, two weeks and two months after a purchase. Sales trainer Mark Hunter described it in a [2013 post](https://thesaleshunter.com/securing-repeat-sales-from-existing-customers-with-222/) and recommended phone calls for all three, because a call gives you a conversation rather than a one-way message. Many teams use the same intervals for email when a call would be too much.

## How to follow up politely

Politeness in a follow-up is mostly about removing pressure while still asking a clear question. Swap the stock phrases for ones that carry information:

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| "Just checking in." | "You mentioned deciding by [date]. Is that still the plan?" |
| "Did you get a chance to look at my last email?" | "One thing I left out last time: [new detail]." |
| "Per my last email…" | "To save you scrolling: the two open points are [A] and [B]." |
| "I will follow up." | "I will send [item] by Thursday and write again on Friday." |
| "Let me know your thoughts." | "Does Tuesday or Wednesday suit you better?" |
| "Sorry to bother you again." | Leave it out. A useful email does not need an apology. |

"I will follow up" sounds professional only when it says what and when. "I will send you the revised scope on Thursday" is a promise the buyer can hold you to, and that is what makes it credible.

## Common mistakes

- **The essay.** If a follow-up needs scrolling on a phone, cut it in half.
- **Several questions at once.** The buyer answers none. Ask one.
- **Same email, new date.** Each follow-up needs one thing the previous one did not have.
- **Following up on the wrong channel.** If the conversation started on LinkedIn or WhatsApp, a cold email from a different address can look like spam.
- **Losing track.** Many missed follow-ups are not a writing problem. Nobody noticed the thread went quiet, which is the job of [follow-up tracking in a CRM](/features/follow-up).

## Keeping track of who owes you a reply

For a handful of open conversations, your mail client is enough. Gmail can show [sent emails you may need to follow up on](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/6585?hl=en) at the top of the inbox, once you tick "Suggest emails to follow up on" under Nudges in the General settings. In Outlook you can [flag a message for follow up](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/outlook/flag-email-messages-for-follow-up) before you send it, with presets such as Tomorrow or Next Week.

Both work per mailbox. They stop helping when a deal involves several people, when half the conversation happens on LinkedIn or WhatsApp, or when you want to see which open deals have gone quiet rather than which emails. ## A note for follow-ups to recipients in Germany

German law treats advertising by email without the recipient's prior express consent as unreasonable harassment ([§ 7 (2) no. 2 UWG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/__7.html)). A follow-up on a conversation the buyer started, or on an offer they asked for, is a different situation from a "follow-up" to someone who never agreed to hear from you. The existing-customer exception in § 7 (3) UWG covers only an address you received in connection with a sale, used for your own similar products, when the customer has not objected and was told at collection and in every email that they can object. This is not legal advice; check your own case with a lawyer.

Timing never creates permission. A buyer's request covers its actual scope, not unrelated promotions; a CRM source field is evidence to inspect, not consent. For France, [CNIL guidance](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/la-prospection-commerciale-par-courrier-electronique-sms-mms-et-automate-dappel) distinguishes prior consumer consent (with limited exceptions) from profession-related B2B prospecting with information and an easy objection route. For US commercial email, the [FTC CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) requires accurate sender details, a non-deceptive subject, advertising identification, a postal address and an opt-out route, including B2B. Its ten-business-day opt-out deadline is not our follow-up policy: stop immediately. Check the rules applicable to the actual recipient; Germany's rule above cannot be replaced by the US standard.

Legal sources checked on 6 November 2026. These summaries do not determine permission for an individual message.

## FAQ

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="How do you write a follow up email after a sales call?">
Send it the same day. Thank them in one line, list what you heard (their goal, their blocker, who decides), name the next step on each side with a date, and ask whether that matches their notes. Template 1 above is a ready version; fill every bracket from your call note.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How long should you wait to follow up, and how many follow-ups?">
Send a requested recap promptly. Then use the agreed date, any reply and the decision tree above. Two or three working days can be a review point, not permission to send. There is no required number; stop immediately on rejection or an opt-out.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What is a good follow-up message when there is no response?">
A short question that can be answered in one word, tied to something the buyer told you: "When we spoke, you wanted to decide by the end of the month. Is that still the plan?" Avoid "just checking in", which gives the reader nothing to respond to.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What subject lines work for a sales follow-up?">
Lines that point at something only the two of you discussed: "Recap and next steps: [topic] for [company]", "The answer on [question they asked]" or "Still on your list for [month]?". Avoid a fake "Re:" on a first email.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How do I end a follow-up sequence politely?">
Send one short closing email that says you will stop writing, names the problem they described, and invites them to reply when it becomes a priority again. Template 7 is an example. Then mark the deal as lost or paused so nobody writes again by accident.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What is the 2-2-2 rule in sales?">
A rhythm for contacting a customer two days, two weeks and two months after a purchase: first to check that things arrived as promised, then for feedback, then to talk about the next need. Mark Hunter described it in 2013 with phone calls in mind; it works for email too.
</FaqItem>

Customermates does not run automated sequences, although some CRMs and sales engagement tools do. It shows which conversations wait for a reply, and on Customermates Cloud Mate can prepare drafts that you send yourself. For timed cadences, connect a separate tool or your own n8n workflow.
</FaqItem>
