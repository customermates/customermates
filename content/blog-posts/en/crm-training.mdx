# CRM Training: How to Get a Team Using the CRM, and Where to Learn It Yourself

CRM training means one of two things. Either you are teaching your team to use the CRM you just set up, or you are learning CRM as a skill through a course or certification. For a team, the method that works is short: train the daily workflow rather than the menus, run one 60 to 90 minute session per role close to go-live, use your own records, then hold a 15-minute review inside the CRM every week for a month. For yourself, start with the free academy of the CRM you will actually work in and pay for a certification only if you are heading into an admin or consulting role.

Customermates publishes this guide. The setup example near the end uses Customermates; the course section names other vendors' programs with links to their own pages.

## What a team actually needs to learn

Most teams fail at CRM adoption for a dull reason: people were shown every screen once and never practiced the four things they do daily. A good session cuts the scope down to what each person repeats every week and writes the rules down, so "log the call" means the same thing to everyone.

| Topic | Who needs it | Learned when the person can... |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Finding a contact or organization | Everyone | Open the right record in under ten seconds from search |
| Creating and moving a deal | Anyone who sells | Add a deal from an inquiry and move it to the next stage without help |
| Notes and next steps | Anyone who talks to clients | Leave a note a colleague could act on, and set the next step |
| Their own list view | Everyone | Filter to "my open deals" and save it |
| Pipeline review | Founder or manager | Run the weekly meeting from the board, not from a spreadsheet |
| Fields, stages, roles, import | Admin | Add a stage, change a field and import a workbook |

The written rules matter as much as the clicks. One page is enough: which stages exist and what has to be true to enter each one, which fields are required, where client emails belong, and what nobody should store in the CRM (private opinions about people, passwords, health data). If the rules are not written, each person invents their own and the data stops being comparable within weeks. The [CRM strategy guide](/blog/crm-strategy) covers how to decide those rules in the first place.

## A four-week CRM training plan for a small team

This plan assumes a team of two to fifteen people and a CRM that is already chosen. If you are still choosing or migrating, the [CRM implementation guide](/blog/crm-implementation) comes first.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Before go-live: configure and load real data">
  The admin sets up stages, custom fields and roles, imports the current clients and open deals, and writes the one-page rules. Pick one or two people who like the tool and give them a preview a week early; they answer the small questions later.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Go-live week: one session per role">
  Run the sessions no more than two or three days before people start using the CRM for real work. Groups of four to eight, so everyone has their hands on the keyboard. Each person works on their own deals, not on a fictional "Acme Corp".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Weeks 1 to 4: weekly 15-minute review inside the CRM">
  The manager or founder opens the pipeline board in the weekly meeting and goes through it deal by deal. Anything missing gets fixed on the spot. This is the training: people update the CRM because the meeting runs from it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="End of week 4: check and adjust">
  Look at the adoption checks below. Remove fields nobody fills, rename stages people misuse, and give the one or two people who are behind a 20-minute one-to-one rather than another group session.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### A 90-minute session for people who sell

| Minutes | Block | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 10 | Why | What each person gets back: no more asking around for the last email to a client, no lost follow-ups. Not "management wants reports". |
| 10 to 30 | Records | Find a contact, open their organization, read the history, add a note |
| 30 to 55 | Deals | Create a deal from a real inquiry, move it through the [pipeline stages](/features/pipeline), set the next step |
| 55 to 70 | Their own view | Each person filters the deal list to their own open deals and saves it |
| 70 to 90 | Practice | Everyone updates five of their own real deals while the trainer walks around |

Managers need a shorter session of about 60 minutes on reading the board and running the weekly review. Admins need their own 90 minutes on stages, fields, roles and import. People in a back-office or assistant role usually need only the first two blocks.

### What this costs in time

For a five-person team, a realistic first month looks like this: about six hours of preparation for the admin, five sessions of 90 minutes (7.5 person-hours), and four weekly reviews of 15 minutes with five people (5 person-hours). That is roughly 18 to 20 person-hours. The preparation is the part people skip, and it decides whether the sessions use real data or a demo nobody cares about.

## Five mistakes that undo the training

1. **One session for everyone, covering every feature.** People remember the parts they used that week. Teach by role and leave the rest for later.
2. **Training weeks before launch.** By go-live the details are gone. Keep the gap to a few days.
3. **Practice data.** A fictional pipeline teaches the clicks but not the habit. Import your real clients first.
4. **The manager never opens it.** If the weekly meeting still runs from a spreadsheet, the CRM is optional, and everyone knows it.
5. **Too many required fields.** Each required field is a reason to update the CRM later, which often means never. Start with the fields the weekly meeting actually reads.

## How to check whether the training worked

Logins and "activities per day" measure motion, not adoption. Three checks say more, and you can run them from a filtered list in most CRMs:

- **Every open deal has an assigned user and a next step.** A filter for empty values shows the gaps.
- **Nothing is untouched for too long.** Filter open deals by last update older than 14 days. A few is normal; a long list means the CRM is not where the work happens.
- **The weekly meeting runs from the board.** When this one holds, the other two usually follow.

If one person keeps showing up in the gaps, ask them what is in the way. Often it is a field that makes no sense for their work, and the fix is to remove it.

## Learning CRM yourself: courses and certifications

If you want CRM skills for your own career, learn in the system you will work in. A Salesforce admin role needs Salesforce knowledge; general CRM theory helps less than an afternoon in the actual product. The main vendor programs:

| Program | What you get | Cost | Certification | Fits if |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Salesforce Trailhead](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/what-is-trailhead/) | Self-paced modules and hands-on practice in Salesforce | Free learning platform | Salesforce certifications are separate exams; the fee is listed on each credential page | You aim for a Salesforce admin, developer or consultant role |
| [HubSpot Academy](https://academy.hubspot.com/courses) | Lessons and courses on the HubSpot CRM, sales and marketing | HubSpot says the account and its course library are free | [Free certifications](https://academy.hubspot.com/certification-overview) with an exam | Your company uses HubSpot, or you work in marketing |
| [Pipedrive Academy](https://learn.pipedrive.com/) | Courses, tutorials and webinars on Pipedrive | Free for all users, per Pipedrive | Completion certificates on some courses | You sell in a small team that uses Pipedrive |
| [Microsoft Dynamics 365 Fundamentals (CRM)](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/d365-fundamentals-customer-engagement-apps-crm/) | Exam on Dynamics 365 customer engagement apps | Microsoft marks this certification as retired | No longer available | Check Microsoft Learn for the current Dynamics 365 paths instead |

A certification is worth the time and any exam fee when you will configure a CRM for other people: as an admin, a consultant or in sales operations. For someone who sells or manages accounts, an hour of practice in your company's own setup does more than a certificate.

## Training a team on Customermates

If your team uses Customermates, a few parts of the product change how the plan above works in practice. Customermates is an open-source, AI-native CRM for small client-facing teams; this section describes what is in the product and what is not.

**Write the rules where the tools read them.** The one-page rules belong in the workspace's Knowledge Base. Put the standing rules in the Operating Guide and turn repeated tasks, such as "log a discovery call", into Procedures with a "When to use" line. Knowledge Base pages also work on self-hosted instances. Mate and connected AI clients read the Operating Guide first and follow a matching procedure before acting.

**Guided tours, in Customermates Cloud.** A new person can open **Ask Mate** and ask for a tour, for example "show me how to move a deal to the next stage and where my saved views are". Mate builds the tour on the spot, moves between pages and explains each stop. This uses hosted AI credits from the monthly allowance each active user gets; see [pricing](/pricing) for the allowances. The [assistant documentation](/docs/app-assistant) lists what Mate can point at and what it cannot click for you.

**Each person saves their own views.** Saved views in Customermates are personal, so the "my open deals" step in the session is necessary, not optional: there is no shared team view to hand out. The [records documentation](/docs/app-records) explains filters, grouping and the board.

**A next-step date instead of task reminders.** Customermates tasks have no due dates or reminders. For the "every deal has a next step" rule, add a custom Date column called "Next step on" to deals and sort the personal deal view by it. The adoption check for stale deals uses the built-in **Updated at** field with **Older than…** as described in the [filter reference](/docs/filter-syntax).

**A weekly check as a Routine, in Customermates Cloud.** A manager can create a routine that repeats every week on Monday at 8:00, with an instruction such as: "List the open deals assigned to me that have no Next step on date or were last updated more than 14 days ago. Write the list in the run summary. Do not send any messages." A routine runs as its owner and spends that person's hosted AI credits. Because Mate can send messages when an instruction asks it to, keep the "do not send" line in.

**Import before the session.** Records import as XLSX workbooks, one record type at a time: organizations first, then contacts, then deals.

What Customermates does not give you for training: there is no built-in course library or quiz, no native mobile app, and no shared views. Mate, tours and Routines need Customermates Cloud; self-hosting has the CRM, the Knowledge Base and MCP for your own AI client, but no Mate. ## FAQ

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="What is CRM training?">
It is teaching people to use a customer relationship management system for their own work: finding records, keeping deals current, writing notes others can use, and following the team's rules for what goes where. In career terms, it also means courses and certifications that teach a specific CRM, such as Salesforce or HubSpot.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How long does it take to learn a CRM?">
The daily basics take one session of 60 to 90 minutes for someone who sells. The habit takes longer: plan for about four weeks of weekly reviews before the team updates the CRM without being asked. Admin skills on a large platform take weeks of study; on a small-team CRM, an afternoon with stages, fields and import is usually enough.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can I learn CRM for free?">
Yes. Salesforce Trailhead, HubSpot Academy and Pipedrive Academy are free, and many CRMs offer a free plan or a trial you can practice in. Free courses teach the vendor's product; for general concepts, practice in one real system teaches more than theory.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How much does a CRM certification cost?">
It depends on the vendor. HubSpot Academy certifications are free. Salesforce certifications are separate exams, and the current fee is listed on each credential page on Trailhead. Microsoft has retired its Dynamics 365 Fundamentals (CRM) certification, so check Microsoft Learn for the current Dynamics 365 exams.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is CRM hard to learn?">
Using a CRM is not hard; the record types are contacts, organizations and deals, and most work is search, update and move. What is hard is the discipline of keeping it current. That is a team habit, built by running meetings from the CRM, not by more training.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What are the four types of CRM?">
The usual split is operational (daily sales and service work), analytical (reporting on customer data), collaborative (sharing customer context across teams) and strategic (customer-focused planning). Many sources list only the first three. The [overview of CRM systems](/blog/crm-systems) explains the types with examples.
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
