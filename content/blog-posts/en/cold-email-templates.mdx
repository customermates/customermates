# Cold email templates for a useful first conversation

Useful cold email templates identify the sender and their offer, connect one verified fact to a relevant task, and give the recipient a concrete reason to reply. The four examples below are starting points for individual drafts, with a permission check before any send.

Customermates publishes this guide and sells the CRM described below. It supports editable records and individual message drafts in its cloud Inbox from Pro; it has no built-in sequences, drip campaigns or mass sending workflow.

## Which cold email template should you choose?

A cold email is a first approach to someone with whom you have no established conversation. A reply after a call or proposal belongs in the [sales follow-up email guide](/blog/sales-follow-up-email). Choose the reason for writing before choosing the words.

| What you can verify | Choose | Reason to reply | Do not use it when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A public change relevant to your offer | 1. Observed change | Get a concrete example for the new task | You are guessing at an internal problem |
| A question the person has publicly asked | 2. Useful resource | Get the specific resource that answers it | The resource does not exist yet |
| A service need tied to their work | 3. Bounded service | Check a deliverable and its scope | You cannot explain what you would deliver |
| Permission to name a mutual contact | 4. Genuine introduction | Confirm the right owner for a relevant question | Nobody actually suggested the introduction |

For choosing whom to approach and which channel fits, start with [B2B customer acquisition methods](/blog/customer-acquisition). A relevant recipient and a lawful route matter more than polishing a generic opening.

## What must you check before sending?

**Checked on 4 November 2026.** In the US, CAN-SPAM covers individual commercial emails and B2B messages too. Use accurate sender details and subjects, disclose advertising, include a valid physical postal address and a clear opt-out, and honor opt-outs within 10 business days. Keep the opt-out mechanism working for at least 30 days. The [FTC's CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) explains the requirements.

Country rules differ. In Germany, advertising email generally requires prior express consent, including B2B; the existing-customer exception has four cumulative conditions. A public work address or relevance alone does not establish permission. See [§ 7 UWG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/__7.html). These templates do not establish permission or guarantee compliance; check the rules that apply to your sender, recipient and use of personal data.

The examples use fictional situations and obvious placeholders. Replace every bracket before sending. Add the applicable sender identity, postal address, privacy information and opt-out details to each message. For US commercial email, make the advertising disclosure clear, for example “Advertisement from [company]. To stop marketing emails from us, reply ‘unsubscribe’.” An opt-out does not replace consent where consent is required.

## Four original cold email templates to adapt

### 1. A verified change and a small proof step

**Use when:** a real announcement makes your offer relevant. Record its source and date. Do not infer that expansion means the recipient has a broken process.

```text
Subject: [New initiative]: a [specific example]

Hi [first name],

Your [source and date] announcement says [verified change].
I build [product], which helps [specific role] do [supported task].
I have a [small example] showing [relevant workflow].

Would that example help you evaluate [task] for [initiative]?

Best,
[Name, role and company]
[Required sender, privacy and opt-out details]
```

**Fictional worked example:** Maya at Lark Studio publicly announces a new client-onboarding service. A sender selling a document-review service could write: “Your announcement describes an onboarding pack for each client. I run Paperlane, a document-review service. I have a one-page handover checklist separating client inputs from internal checks. Would that checklist help you plan the new pack?” Neither business nor the result is a customer claim.

### 2. A resource that answers a real question

**Use when:** the resource is ready, useful without a purchase and directly related to a question the recipient asked. Do not pretend to have read a post you have not checked.

```text
Subject: A checklist for [question they asked]

Hi [first name],

In [public source], you asked how to [specific task].
I work on [product/service] at [company]. Our [resource]
sets out [two relevant checks], including [useful limitation].

Would you like the [format] version to use with your team?

Best,
[Name, role and company]
[Required sender, privacy and opt-out details]
```

Name the resource, its contents and the actual choice. “Can I send you something valuable?” gives the reader no basis for answering. If an accessible link is more useful than asking permission to share it, include that one link and ask whether a particular section resolves the question.

### 3. A service with a clear deliverable

**Use when:** you can explain what you would inspect or produce without inventing a diagnosis, savings figure or customer result.

```text
Subject: [Deliverable] for [specific workflow]

Hi [first name],

Your [public page] describes [relevant workflow].
I provide [service] at [company]. For this task, the deliverable
would be [specific artifact], covering [scope] and excluding [limit].

Would a sample of [artifact] help you judge whether that fits?

Best,
[Name, role and company]
[Required sender, privacy and opt-out details]
```

A fictional example of a bounded deliverable is a field-mapping sheet for an agency's client handover, covering source fields and owners but excluding migration execution. Promise only a sample you can actually supply.

### 4. A genuine suggested introduction

**Use when:** the named person really suggested this contact and allows you to say so. Their suggestion does not by itself authorize marketing email.

```text
Subject: [Mutual contact] suggested I ask about [task]

Hi [first name],

[Mutual contact] suggested you handle [task] and said I could
mention their name. I build [product] at [company].
I can share [specific example] relevant to [task].

Are you the person who evaluates [specific question]?

Best,
[Name, role and company]
[Required sender, privacy and opt-out details]
```

If there was no introduction, choose another template. Never imply a recommendation, partnership or previous conversation that did not happen.

## How do you write the subject and opening?

Describe the actual subject: “[Initiative]: handover checklist”, “A sample field map for [workflow]” or “[Contact] suggested I ask about [task]”. Avoid fake “Re:” or “Fwd:”, invented deadlines and subjects suggesting an account problem.

Then use four parts: a verified reason for writing, your identity and commercial role, one useful offer, and one answerable question. Keep the draft short enough to read on a phone. A working target is 60 to 120 words before required footer information, not a performance benchmark. Cut generic praise and unsupported promises before cutting context.

**Review checklist:**

- Confirm the permission or other applicable basis for this specific message and check prior objections.
- Verify the recipient, company, source and date. Delete assumptions about private needs.
- Replace every placeholder and disclose who you are and what you sell.
- Check the promised resource, sample or supported product behavior.
- Keep one question with an obvious reason to answer.
- Add the legally required identity, address, privacy and opt-out information.
- Read the whole message aloud and check subject, links and signature in the saved draft.

## What should you do if nobody replies?

Silence is not consent and does not prove interest. Do not put a first-contact draft into an automatic sequence. Consider another message only if contact remains permitted and you have a genuinely useful new reason; stop after an objection. If the person responds and agrees a next step, record that agreement and its real date. Use the follow-up guide for the resulting conversation rather than treating every silent contact as an active deal.
