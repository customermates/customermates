# CRM Examples: Software, Use Cases by Business and One Setup You Can Copy

Well-known examples of CRM software are Salesforce Sales Cloud, HubSpot, Microsoft Dynamics 365 Sales, Zoho CRM and Pipedrive, plus open-source options such as SuiteCRM. Examples of CRM in use are a sales team moving deals through stages, an agency keeping every client email next to the client record, and a support desk opening a customer's history before answering. What all of them share is one record per person and company, every interaction attached to it, and a pipeline that shows what happens next.

This guide gives you all three kinds of example: the software, the use cases by type of business, and one setup worked out down to the columns. Customermates publishes it and appears below in its own labelled section. Vendor facts were checked on 8 October 2026 on each vendor's own pages. If you need the basics first, start with [what a CRM is](/blog/what-is-crm).

## The CRM types, each with one example

Textbooks split CRM into operational, analytical and collaborative CRM, and some add strategic CRM as a fourth. Most products cover several types at once, so the split helps you name a need, not pick a product. For the longer explanation, see [CRM systems and their types](/blog/crm-systems).

| Type | What it does | Concrete example |
|---|---|---|
| Operational | Runs the daily work: records, pipeline, tasks, messages | A rep moves a deal from Proposal to Negotiation and logs the call in the deal's notes |
| Analytical | Turns the records into numbers | A dashboard shows won deal value by lead source for the last quarter |
| Collaborative | Shares one customer history across people and teams | A colleague covering a holiday reads the full email thread before replying to the client |
| Strategic | Aligns the business around long-term customer value | A firm reviews which clients bring repeat work and plans account time around them |

## Real company examples, as the vendors report them

Most "real company" CRM examples online come from vendor case studies. Treat the numbers below as what the vendor reports about its own customer, not as independent results.

- **Siemens.** Salesforce writes that Siemens put 18,000 sellers, resellers and partners on a single CRM, unifying data across seven business units with Sales Cloud ([Salesforce CRM examples](https://www.salesforce.com/crm/examples/)).
- **FedEx.** According to the same Salesforce page, FedEx uses Marketing Cloud to send personalized reactivation emails and to flag quotes that are not followed by a shipment, and Salesforce reports a 13% improvement in customer activation.
- **Uber Eats.** Salesforce's German examples page says Uber Eats brought 30 systems into one platform, which made merchant disputes faster to settle ([Salesforce CRM-Beispiele](https://www.salesforce.com/de/crm/examples/)).

What transfers to a small team is the pattern, not the scale: one record per customer, and no switching between tools to answer a question.

## CRM use case examples by type of business

These are the record models that work for common businesses. The last column says plainly whether Customermates is a reasonable fit.

| Business | Main records | Example deal stages | What matters most | Customermates fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B2B sales team | Organizations, contacts, deals | Prospecting, Qualification, Demo, Proposal, Negotiation, Won, Lost | Stage discipline, deal value, who owns the next step | Good; these are the default deal stages |
| Consultancy or agency | Organizations, contacts, deals, services | Inquiry, Discovery call, Proposal, Won, Lost | Client email and LinkedIn next to the deal, scope and fee per engagement | Good, see the setup below and [CRM for consultants](/for/consultants) |
| Recruiting | Candidates and clients as contacts, roles as deals | Sourced, Screened, Interview, Offer, Placed | LinkedIn conversations, many people per role | Workable for small agencies; no job-board integration |
| Real estate | Buyers and sellers as contacts, properties or mandates as deals | Inquiry, Viewing, Offer, Notarization, Closed | Two sides per deal, phone-heavy work | Partial; there is no telephony, so calls are logged by hand |
| Support desk | Customers, conversation history | Open, Waiting, Solved | Ticket queues and SLAs | Weak; use a help-desk tool instead |
| Online shop retention | Customers, orders | None; purchase data drives everything | Shop integration and campaign emails | Weak; use a CRM built into your shop or email-marketing platform |
| Association or club | Members, events | Applied, Approved, Renewal due, Lapsed | Member lists, renewals | Workable for small lists; no campaign sending |

Two rules hold across all rows. Model the thing you sell as the deal, even when you call it a mandate, a role or an engagement. And keep the stage list short enough that everyone agrees where a deal sits.

## A worked setup: a four-person consultancy in Customermates

Here is one example taken all the way down to the columns. It uses the real record types and labels of Customermates; you can rebuild it in any CRM that has custom fields and saved filters.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Set up the record types">Clients go in **Organizations**, people in **Contacts**, and each engagement you are trying to win in **Deals**. Your standard offers go in **Services** with a price each (for example "Strategy workshop" and "Monthly retainer"), and a deal's value follows from the services and quantities linked to it.</Step>
  <Step title="Rename the deal stages">The deal **Stage** is a single-select column. Replace the defaults with Inquiry, Discovery call, Proposal sent, Won and Lost. Each option carries a weight, so the pipeline shows a weighted value next to the total.</Step>
  <Step title="Add three custom columns">On deals: "Engagement type" (single select: Project, Retainer, Workshop), "Lead source" (single select: Referral, LinkedIn, Website, Event) and "Expected start" (date). Customermates has no tags; single-select columns do that job and can be filtered and grouped.</Step>
  <Step title="Save a personal view">On **Deals**, filter Stage = Proposal sent and assigned users = you, sort by updated date and save it with **New view** as "My open proposals". Saved views are personal, so each colleague sets the same filters and saves their own view.</Step>
  <Step title="Import existing clients">Export your client list to an Excel workbook (.xlsx) and import organizations first, then contacts, one record type at a time. See [records, import and views](/docs/app-records) for the column matching.</Step>
  <Step title="Connect the inbox (Pro or higher)">Each consultant connects their mailbox or LinkedIn account in the [unified inbox](/features/unified-inbox). Pro includes [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedAccountsPerUser]] connected account per user and Business [[commercial.entitlement.business.includedAccountsPerUser]], so a consultant who wants email, LinkedIn and WhatsApp side by side needs Business. Starter has no inbox.</Step>
</Steps>

**An example routine (Customermates Cloud, uses hosted AI credits).** A routine is a saved instruction for Mate, the assistant, with a trigger. Set it to repeat every week on Monday at 08:00 with this instruction:

> List my deals in stage "Proposal sent" whose last update is older than ten days. For each one, write a short follow-up email in the existing thread with the main contact and save it as a draft. Do not send anything.

The last sentence matters. A routine runs as its owner and may send messages without asking, so an instruction that should only draft has to say so. Deletions and other actions that need approval are refused in unattended runs. Limits and run statuses are on the [routines documentation](/docs/app-routines).

If you prefer your own AI client, Claude, ChatGPT and other MCP clients can read and update the same records through the Customermates MCP server, using your AI provider instead of hosted credits.

## Where Customermates does not fit

Some CRM examples above need things Customermates does not do. Be clear about them before you pick it:

- **No email campaigns or automated sequences.** Messages go out one at a time, drafted or sent from the inbox. For newsletters or multi-step outreach, use an email-marketing tool next to the CRM.
- **No support for phone-heavy sales.** There is no telephony or call logging integration, and no mobile app.
- **No ticket queues.** There is no help-desk module with SLAs.
- **No built-in task due dates.** Tasks have relations, assignees and notes; add a date custom column if you need one, and filter on it.
- **No quotes, invoices or client portal.** Keep those in your accounting or proposal tool.

If most of your work sits in those areas, look for a CRM suite whose edition includes a help desk, calling or quoting, and check on the vendor's pricing page which edition contains the module you need.

## Frequently asked questions

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="What is a CRM and what are examples?">
A CRM (customer relationship management) system stores your contacts, companies, deals and conversations in one place, so everyone sees the same history and the next step. Examples of CRM software are Salesforce Sales Cloud, HubSpot, Microsoft Dynamics 365 Sales, Zoho CRM, Pipedrive, SuiteCRM and Customermates.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What is the most popular CRM?">
There is no neutral public count of users across all vendors. Salesforce, HubSpot, Microsoft Dynamics 365, Zoho CRM and Pipedrive are the names that appear in nearly every comparison. Popularity says little about fit: a three-person consultancy and a 500-seat sales floor need different tools.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What are the four types of CRM?">
Operational CRM runs daily sales and service work, analytical CRM turns records into reports, collaborative CRM shares one customer history across teams, and strategic CRM aligns the business around long-term customer value. Most products combine the first three.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is there a free CRM, and which is best for beginners?">
HubSpot's free CRM covers up to two users and 1,000 contacts, Zoho CRM has a free edition for up to three users, and Salesforce lists a Free Suite. For beginners, the best choice is the one whose default pipeline already looks like your process, so you change little. More options are in [free CRM software](/blog/free-crm).
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Where is CRM used?">
Anywhere a business has repeat contact with the same people: B2B sales, agencies and consultancies, recruiting, real estate, financial advice, support teams and membership organizations. The record model changes by business, the principle does not.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is SAP a CRM?">
SAP is a software vendor, not a type of software. CRM manages customer relationships and sales, while ERP runs finance, purchasing and operations, and large vendors sell products in both categories. The differences are explained in [CRM and ERP](/blog/crm-and-erp).
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
