# CRM Cost: What You Pay for Licences, Setup and Running a CRM

A CRM costs between nothing and several hundred dollars per user per month. On 8 October 2026, free plans covered 2 users (HubSpot) or 3 users (Zoho CRM), the entry paid tiers of the other vendors below started between €14 and $65 per user per month at list price, mid tiers cost between €39 and $105, and the top editions reached $550 at Salesforce. For a five-person team on an entry or mid tier, that is roughly $800 to $6,300 a year in licences alone.

The licence is the cost you can read off a pricing page. The rest comes from how you are billed, one-time onboarding fees, usage credits for AI features, and the hours your own team spends on setup and upkeep. This guide gives you the list prices, the extra costs with a way to estimate each, a three-year example, and the ROI formula. Customermates publishes this guide; its prices sit in the tables as one entry and in a separate, labelled section.

## The costs that come on top of the licence

Published industry averages for implementation costs vary so widely that they say little about your case. A better estimate is your own hours times your own hourly rate, plus whatever a partner charges. Go through this list once and write a number next to each line.

| Cost item | What drives it | How to estimate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Setup and configuration | Number of pipelines, custom fields, permission roles | Hours for one person to configure and test, times their rate |
| Onboarding fee or partner | Vendor requirement or complexity | The vendor's listed fee or the partner's price |
| Data migration | How clean your spreadsheets and old CRM are | One to two hours per record type for mapping and checking, more if duplicates are rife |
| Integrations | Email, calendar, accounting, website | Native connectors cost nothing extra; an automation tool or a developer does |
| Training | Team size and how different the new tool is | Hours per person times team size times rate |
| Ongoing admin | Field changes, new users, cleanup | A few hours a month, every month |
| AI usage | Credits included in the plan versus actual use | Check what one month of real use consumes during the trial |
| Exit | Export format and completeness | The hours to move your data out again later |

The line most teams leave out is ongoing admin. Two hours a month at $80 an hour is $1,920 a year, which is more than the licences for five people on several entry tiers. The [CRM implementation guide](/blog/crm-implementation) covers the setup work step by step.

## Building your own CRM

A custom build has no licence fee and a large up-front cost. Take an illustrative case: if a developer at $90 an hour needs 400 hours for contacts, deals, permissions, email sync and an export, that is $36,000 before hosting. Then the system needs updates, security fixes and someone who still understands the code in two years. For most small teams, a self-hosted open-source CRM is the cheaper way to get control over the code and the data. The [open-source CRM guide](/blog/open-source-crm) compares those options.

## How to calculate CRM ROI

The formula is simple: **ROI = (gain minus cost) / cost × 100**. The hard part is choosing honest inputs. Three rules keep the number defensible:

- Count gross margin from additional deals, not revenue. A $10,000 project with a 40% margin adds $4,000.
- Count saved time only if it turns into billable or selling time. An hour saved on searching emails is worth nothing if nobody uses it.
- Put the full first-year cost on the cost side: licences, onboarding, setup hours and training.

**Worked example (all inputs are assumptions you should replace with your own).** A five-person team pays $2,000 a year in licences and spends 20 setup hours at $80, so the first-year cost is $3,600. Each person saves one hour a week across 46 working weeks, 230 hours in total; the team turns half of that into billable work, which is worth 115 × $80 = $9,200. Better follow-up wins one extra project a year with $6,000 gross margin. The gain is $15,200.

ROI = ($15,200 − $3,600) / $3,600 × 100 = **322%**. The payback period is the first-year cost divided by the monthly gain: $3,600 / $1,267 ≈ 2.8 months.

Measure before you buy. Note the number of open deals, how many go quiet without a reply, and the hours spent looking for information. Measure the same things after 90 days. If the CRM is not used, every one of these numbers stays flat, so adoption is the first metric to watch.

## Budget a CRM in six steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Count seats for the next twelve months">
    Include planned hires and anyone who only needs to look things up. On per-seat pricing every one of them costs the full price.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find the tier that holds all must-haves">
    List what you cannot work without, such as email in the CRM, more than one pipeline or roles and permissions. The first tier that has everything is your real price.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Price both billing options">
    Seats times price times twelve, once for yearly and once for monthly payment. Pay yearly only if your headcount is stable.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add one-time fees and internal hours">
    Onboarding fees, migration, setup and training go into year one. Use the cost table above.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add running costs">
    Admin hours, AI credits beyond the included amount, and integration tools for every following year.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the way out">
    Find out which format the export uses and whether an API exists. Moving away later is also a cost.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## FAQ

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="How much does a CRM cost per month?">
For one user, between $0 and about $550 at list price. Entry tiers of the vendors in this guide cost $7 to $65 per user per month, mid tiers €39 to $105 and enterprise editions $150 to $550. A five-person team on one of the mid tiers in the table pays about $200 to $525 a month (Pipedrive Growth at €39 comes to €195) before setup and internal time.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is there a 100% free CRM?">
Yes, within user limits. HubSpot's free tools cover up to 2 users, the Zoho CRM free edition up to 3 users, and Salesforce lists a Free Suite. Self-hosted open-source CRMs have no licence fee, but the server and upkeep cost money or time. The [free CRM guide](/blog/free-crm) compares the free options.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How much does Salesforce CRM cost?">
On 8 October 2026, Salesforce listed the Starter Suite at $25 per user per month, billed monthly or yearly, and the Pro Suite at $100, billed annually. The Sales Cloud editions Core, Advanced and Max cost $195, $395 and $550 per user per month, billed annually.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What hidden costs does a CRM have?">
The usual ones are one-time onboarding fees, data migration, integration tools, training, ongoing admin hours and AI usage beyond the included credits. Contract terms count too: a yearly commitment means you keep paying for seats you no longer need until the term ends.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How do I calculate the ROI of a CRM?">
ROI = (gain minus cost) / cost × 100. Use gross margin from additional deals and only the saved time that becomes billable or selling time as the gain, and the full first-year cost including setup hours as the cost. Measure open deals, unanswered conversations and search time before you start and again after 90 days.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How much does it cost to build my own CRM?">
That depends on the scope and your developer rate. As an illustration, 400 hours at $90 is $36,000 before hosting, and maintenance continues every year after. A self-hosted open-source CRM gives you control over code and data for much less, and a [customizable CRM](/blog/customizable-crm) covers many needs that lead teams to consider a build.
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
