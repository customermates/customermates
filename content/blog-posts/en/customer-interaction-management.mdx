# Customer Interaction Management: Every Conversation on the Right Record

Customer interaction management (CIM) means tying every customer conversation to the right customer record: the channel a message arrived on, the customer and deal it concerns, the person who replies, and whether a reply is still owed. Done well, anyone on the team can open a customer and read the whole history before replying. For a team under twenty people, that usually means a CRM that holds the conversations, not a separate CIM product.

The rest of this guide separates CIM from the terms it gets confused with, shows which kind of software fits which situation, and walks through a concrete setup. Customermates publishes this guide and the worked example uses Customermates; the steps work in any CRM with an inbox.

## CIM, CRM, CCM and CSM: four terms, four jobs

Search results for "customer interaction management" mix contact-center software, CRM guides and enterprise document platforms. Wikipedia defines CIM as enterprise software that manages the interaction between an organization and its customers across email, SMS, telephone, instant messaging, scanned paper mail and social media ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customer_interaction_management)). In a small company the same idea is a set of habits plus whatever tool holds the conversations.

| Term | What it manages | Typical buyer | Typical tools |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Customer interaction management (CIM) | Two-way conversations and who answers them | Any team that talks to customers | CRM with inbox, helpdesk, contact center |
| Customer relationship management (CRM) | Contacts, companies, deals and their history | Sales, account management, founders | CRM |
| Customer communication management (CCM) | Templated, often regulated outbound documents | Insurers, banks, utilities, public bodies | Quadient Inspire, OpenText Communications (Exstream), SmartCOMM |
| Customer service management (CSM) | Support cases, routing to agents, service levels | Support teams with ticket volume | Zendesk, Freshdesk, ServiceNow CSM |

The CCM row is the one that causes wrong purchases. Quadient describes Inspire as software to "create, personalize, deliver and govern customer communications across print and digital channels" ([Quadient](https://www.quadient.com/en-us/customer-communications)). OpenText describes OpenText Communications, formerly Exstream, as CCM software for personalized, compliant communications across a variety of channels ([OpenText](https://www.opentext.com/products/customer-communications-management)), and Smart Communications calls its platform a CCM platform built for regulated enterprises ([Smart Communications](https://www.smartcommunications.com/)). These products produce one-to-many output. If your problem is "nobody knows what the client last wrote on WhatsApp", a CCM platform does not solve it.

CSM is the other neighbor. In ServiceNow, the CSM interaction record lets agents accept and answer live chats, calls, email and SMS ([ServiceNow docs](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/xanadu/customer-service-management/csm-interaction-record-page.html)). That is CIM for a support desk with queues and agents. A consultancy or agency with a few hundred client conversations a month rarely needs queues; it needs the conversations next to the client and the deal.

## What a working CIM setup answers

Test any setup with four questions. Pick a customer at random and try to answer them within a minute:

1. **What was said last, on any channel?** The email from Tuesday and the WhatsApp message from Thursday sit in one history.
2. **Who answers next?** One named person, not "whoever saw it".
3. **Is anything waiting for us?** A list of conversations where the customer wrote last and nobody has replied.
4. **What is this about commercially?** The open deal, its stage and who is assigned to it.

If one answer requires logging into a second tool or asking a colleague, that is the gap to close first. Most small teams fail on the third question, because unanswered messages live in personal inboxes and phones.

## Which software fits which situation

| Your situation | Look at | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under about 20 people, conversations are one-to-one with clients and prospects | A CRM with a unified inbox | Conversations sit on the customer record next to deals; no second system |
| A support team answers many similar requests and needs routing by agent capacity | A helpdesk or contact center | Zendesk's omnichannel routing assigns email, messaging and call tickets by agent availability and capacity ([Zendesk help](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4409149119514)) |
| You send thousands of regulated documents on a schedule | A CCM platform | Template governance, print and audit of what went to whom |
| You run newsletters or campaigns | An email marketing tool connected to the CRM | Bulk sending is a different job from answering individual people |

Many companies combine two rows. The connection matters more than the brand: a helpdesk that never writes back to the customer record rebuilds the fragmentation CIM was meant to remove. Freshdesk, for example, offers email, chat, phone and messaging in its Omni product and lists integrations with tools such as HubSpot ([Freshworks](https://www.freshworks.com/freshdesk/omni/)); check that the integration carries the conversation, not only the contact.

## How to set up customer interaction management

<Steps>
  <Step title="List every channel and who owns it">
    Write down each inbox and messaging account customers use: shared mailboxes, personal Gmail or Outlook, LinkedIn, WhatsApp on someone's phone. Note who owns each account and whether colleagues should see it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Bring the channels into one list">
    Connect them to one tool so new messages appear in one place, newest first, and replies go out on the original channel.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Link every sender to a contact">
    A message from an unknown address is the one that gets lost. Link the sender to an existing contact or create one, so the conversation appears on the contact's history and, through the contact, next to the organization and its deals.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Agree on a few shared states">
    Unread, open, closed is enough. Agree what "closed" means, for example "answered and nothing promised".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name an owner per customer">
    Assign each client and open deal to a person. That person answers or hands over explicitly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the waiting list once a week">
    Thirty minutes: conversations where the customer wrote last, conversations with unlinked senders, deals with no recent contact.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Worked example: a five-person agency in Customermates

The agency has two founders, two project leads and one sales lead. Clients write by email, WhatsApp and LinkedIn. The setup below runs on Customermates Cloud on the Pro plan, because the [unified inbox](/features/unified-inbox) needs Pro or higher; Starter has no inbox.

**Channels.** Each person connects their own account under **My Profile › Channels**. The inbox takes Gmail, Outlook or IMAP for email, plus LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Instagram and Telegram. Pro includes [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedAccountsPerUser]] connected account per user, so the agency splits them: the two project leads and one founder connect their mailboxes, the other founder connects the WhatsApp number clients use, and the sales lead connects LinkedIn. Anyone who needs a mailbox and a messaging account at the same time needs Business, which includes [[commercial.entitlement.business.includedAccountsPerUser]] per user. A new channel is private to its owner. A single conversation can be shared with the team from **Thread settings**; sharing a whole account with everyone needs Business.

**Linking.** In **Thread settings**, each participant is linked to a contact or created as a new one. The address or handle is then added to the contact, so every later conversation with that person lands on the same contact. The contact's **Channels** block has a link that opens all conversations with that contact in the inbox. Contacts link to organizations and deals, so the project lead sees the deal and its assigned users from the same record. The basics of the record model are on the [contact management](/features/contact-management) page.

**States.** Every conversation is **Unread**, **Open**, **Closed** or **Spam**. The state is shared, not per person, and a new incoming message sets it back to **Unread**, so a closed thread cannot hide a fresh question.

**Two saved views in the inbox.** Inbox filters save as personal views, so each person creates their own. A view only lists conversations that person can see: their own channels plus whatever teammates shared. On Pro, a team-wide waiting list therefore depends on sharing conversations one by one; on Business, shared accounts make it automatic.

| View | Filters | Answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Waiting for us | State is Open, Last message is Received, Last message date older than 2 days | Is anything waiting for us? |
| Unlinked people | Participants not fully linked | Which conversations are not on a customer record yet? |

**One routine (Customermates Cloud only, uses hosted AI credits).** A routine runs Mate, the in-app assistant, on a schedule or on an event. The sales lead creates one with an email-received event and this instruction:

> When a new email arrives from a contact linked to an open deal, read the thread and the deal, then save a reply as a draft in the thread. Do not send anything. Add one line to the deal notes summarizing what the client asked.

The instruction says "draft" on purpose. Mate can send messages without a fresh approval when asked to, and a routine runs as its owner with that owner's channels, so it only sees emails that reach the sales lead's own or shared channels. Routines also have run and credit caps, and emails that arrive while a run is in progress or after the hourly cap start no run, so the routine helps but does not replace the weekly check. The [routines documentation](/docs/app-routines) lists the limits.

**What it costs.** Five users on Pro come to [[commercial.price.pro.monthly.seats.5]] per month; on Business, with shared accounts and more connected accounts, [[commercial.price.business.monthly.seats.5]]. Both include monthly hosted AI credits per active user, see [pricing](/pricing). Billing is monthly.

If you prefer your own AI client, Claude, ChatGPT and other MCP clients can read threads, save drafts and update records through the [MCP server](/docs/mcp). The MCP server does not enforce a confirmation before sending; whether the client asks first depends on the client.

## Measuring interaction quality without a contact center

Contact centers report handle time and service levels. A small team needs fewer numbers, read every week:

| Metric | How to get it in a small team |
| --- | --- |
| Conversations waiting longer than two days | Count of the "Waiting for us" view |
| Unlinked conversations | Count of the "Unlinked people" view |
| Open deals nobody has touched lately | A saved deal view sorted by last updated, oldest first (a rough proxy, since any edit counts) |
| Customer view of service | Ask in the quarterly review; CSAT or NPS surveys need a separate survey tool |

Customermates does not calculate first response time or resolution time, and it has no ticket queue with service-level timers. If those numbers matter contractually, a helpdesk is the better fit.

## Privacy: what to settle before you centralize conversations

Pulling every conversation into one system concentrates personal data. Under the GDPR you need a legal basis for storing it, a data processing agreement with each provider, and a rule for how long you keep conversations of former clients. Decide who may see which accounts before connecting personal ones. In Customermates Cloud the primary production database is configured in the AWS Frankfurt region, a selected setting rather than a contractual residency guarantee; providers, including US companies, are listed on the [subprocessors page](/subprocessors), and the [DPA](/dpa) is public. Teams that need the data on their own servers can self-host the core CRM, without the inbox.

## FAQ

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="What is customer interaction management in simple terms?">
It is keeping every customer conversation, on every channel, attached to the right customer, with a named person responsible for the next reply. The test: anyone on the team can open a customer and see what was said last and whether something is waiting.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What is the difference between CIM and CRM?">
A CRM stores who the customer is, which company they belong to and which deals are open. CIM covers the conversations with them and who answers. In small teams a CRM with a built-in inbox does both; large organizations often run a contact center next to the CRM and connect the two.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What is CSM vs. CRM?">
CSM usually means customer service management: support cases, queues and routing to agents, as in ServiceNow CSM or a helpdesk. Some teams use CSM for customer success management, the work of keeping clients after the sale. CRM is the record of customers and deals that both build on.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is customer communication management the same thing?">
No. Customer communication management (CCM) software such as Quadient Inspire or OpenText Communications (Exstream) composes and delivers templated, often regulated documents across print and digital channels. It suits insurers, banks and utilities. Teams whose problem is answering individual clients across email and chat need a CRM with an inbox or a helpdesk instead.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What software do small businesses use for customer interaction management?">
Most use a CRM with an inbox that connects their email and messaging accounts. Support-heavy businesses add a helpdesk such as Zendesk or Freshdesk. Customermates brings email, LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Instagram and Telegram into one inbox next to CRM records on Customermates Cloud from Pro.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can AI handle customer interactions?">
AI can read a thread, summarize it and draft a reply. For client conversations, keep a person who checks the draft and presses send, unless the case is routine. In Customermates Cloud, Mate drafts replies and runs routines using hosted AI credits; external AI clients connect through MCP.
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
