# Best CRM Software: Which One Fits Your Team

The best CRM software is the one built for how your team sells, so the useful answer is a shortlist by use case, not a ranking. Salesforce and Microsoft Dynamics 365 fit large organizations with admins and complex processes. HubSpot fits a free start, Pipedrive a small team that works its pipeline stage by stage, and Close a team that sells by phone. Customermates, which publishes this guide, fits small service teams whose deals move through email, LinkedIn and WhatsApp.

Vendor facts below were checked on 8 October 2026 on each vendor's own pages. For a full selection method with weighted criteria, read [how to choose a CRM](/blog/how-to-choose-crm).

## Choose by use case

Most "best CRM" lists sort tools by feature count. A more useful filter is how your next twenty deals will close and who will keep the records current.

| Your situation | Look at first | Why | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Many teams, an admin, approval processes | Salesforce Sales Cloud | Editions from Starter Suite to Max; Pro Suite adds quoting and forecasting | Pro Suite and up are billed annually |
| The company runs on Outlook and Teams | Microsoft Dynamics 365 Sales | Paid plans list Copilot capabilities in Outlook and Teams | Every listed price is paid yearly |
| Free start, marketing tools later | HubSpot | Free Tools for up to 2 users; Marketing Hub and Service Hub from the same vendor | One-time onboarding fee on Professional and Enterprise |
| Small B2B sales team with clear stages | Pipedrive | Automations and sequences from Growth | No free plan |
| Tight budget, up to three users | Zoho CRM | Free edition for 3 users | Prices differ by region |
| Outbound by phone | Close | Calling and SMS on every plan | Power Dialer and automated workflows start on Growth |
| Deals next to project work on boards | monday CRM | Four plans from Basic to Ultimate | Three-seat minimum, no free plan |
| Phone and email in one low-cost plan | Freshsales | Growth includes chat, email and phone | Listed prices are billed annually |
| A first CRM with one price | Less Annoying CRM | One plan, no tiers, 30-day trial | No higher tier to grow into |
| Service business whose deals live in email, LinkedIn and WhatsApp threads | Customermates (publisher) | Unified inbox from Pro; Mate in Customermates Cloud; MCP for your own AI client; open-source core | No telephony, no sequences, no mobile app, no free hosted plan |

If two rows describe you, take the one that matches how you sell today. A CRM chosen for the company you hope to be in three years mostly buys features nobody opens.

## The shortlist, vendor by vendor

Listed alphabetically. The order is not a ranking.

### Close

Close is built for teams that sell on the phone: every plan (Solo, Essentials, Growth, Scale) includes calling and SMS, according to the [Close pricing page](https://www.close.com/pricing). The Power Dialer and automated workflows start on Growth. If your deals close over email and LinkedIn rather than calls, the main reason to pick Close falls away.

### Freshsales

Freshsales, from Freshworks, includes chat, email and phone in its entry plan, Growth, according to its [pricing page](https://www.freshworks.com/crm/pricing/). Pro adds contact scoring and deal insights, and Enterprise adds forecasting insights and audit logs. It suits a small team that wants calling and email in one inexpensive plan.

### HubSpot

HubSpot suits teams that want to start free and may later add marketing or service software: the [Sales Hub pricing page](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/sales) lists Marketing Hub and Service Hub next to Sales Hub. Free Tools cost $0 for up to 2 users. Customizable workflows (up to 300) and sequences come with Professional, which carries a one-time onboarding fee of $1,500; Enterprise carries $3,500. For five Professional seats, that fee adds more than a quarter to the first-year bill.

### Less Annoying CRM

Less Annoying CRM sells one plan. "No tiers. No contracts," says its [pricing page](https://www.lessannoyingcrm.com/pricing), and the plan covers unlimited contacts, pipelines and custom fields, task management, email logging and mobile access. There is also an API. It fits a small team buying its first CRM. Because there are no tiers, the 30-day trial shows you everything you will ever get.

### Microsoft Dynamics 365 Sales

Dynamics 365 Sales fits companies that already work in Outlook and Teams. Its [pricing page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics-365/products/sales/pricing) lists Copilot capabilities in Outlook and Teams on Sales Professional, Enterprise and Premium, and Sales Enterprise adds Copilot in Dynamics 365 with agentic capabilities. Microsoft Relationship Sales, which includes LinkedIn Sales Navigator, needs at least 10 seats.

### monday CRM

monday CRM, from monday.com, fits teams that already plan their work on monday boards. Its [pricing page](https://monday.com/crm/pricing) lists Basic, Standard and Pro, each with a minimum of three seats, plus Ultimate on request. A solo user therefore pays for three seats, and there is no free plan.

### Pipedrive

Pipedrive suits small B2B sales teams that work through clear, repeatable stages. Its [pricing page](https://www.pipedrive.com/pricing) shows four plans and no free plan. According to [Pipedrive's plan overview](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/new-pipedrive-plans), automations and sequences start on Growth, and LeadBooster, with chatbot, live chat, prospector and web forms, is included from Premium.

### Salesforce Sales Cloud

Salesforce is the largest CRM vendor by market share. In a June 2026 [announcement](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/idc-crm-market-share-ranking-2026/), Salesforce reported that IDC's Worldwide Semiannual Software Tracker gave it 20.0% of the CRM market in 2025, its 13th year in first place. The [Sales Cloud pricing page](https://www.salesforce.com/sales/pricing/) runs from a Free Suite through Starter Suite and Pro Suite to the Core, Advanced and Max editions; Pro Suite adds quoting, forecasting and more customization, and Max includes Agentforce. Salesforce fits organizations with complex processes and people to configure them. A small team can start on Starter Suite and move up once it needs quoting.

### Zoho CRM

Zoho CRM has a free edition for 3 users with no time limit, then Standard, Professional, Enterprise and Ultimate. The [Zoho CRM pricing page](https://www.zoho.com/crm/zohocrm-pricing.html) sets prices by region and says plans paid yearly save up to 34%. It fits teams on a tight budget: three people can work in the free edition before anyone pays.

## Where Customermates fits

*Disclosure: Customermates publishes this guide.* Customermates is an open-source, AI-native CRM built in Germany for small client-facing teams: consultancies, agencies, other relationship-driven service businesses and founders. Contacts, organizations, deals, services and tasks share one workspace with conversations from connected email (Gmail, Outlook or IMAP), LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Instagram and Telegram accounts, which come together in the [unified inbox](/features/unified-inbox) on Pro and higher. Starter has no inbox. In Customermates Cloud, the assistant Mate prepares summaries and reply drafts using hosted AI credits, and Routines run saved instructions on a schedule or when a record changes. If you would rather use Claude, ChatGPT or another AI client, it works with the CRM through [MCP](/docs/mcp) on every plan, with model use billed by your own AI provider.

### A first week in Customermates

<Steps>
  <Step title="Import one record type at a time">
    Save your spreadsheet as an Excel workbook (CSV files are not accepted) and use **Import and export › Add from file** on each list. Import organizations first, then contacts, then deals, so link columns can match existing records by name. Each workbook is row-capped; the [import guide](/docs/app-records) has the limits.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set your stages and probabilities">
    A new workspace starts with a **Stage** field on deals: Prospecting, Qualification, Demo, Proposal, Negotiation, Won and Lost. Rename the options to match how you sell, then give each a win probability under **My Company**. A deal's value comes from its linked services, price times quantity, and its weighted value is that amount times the stage probability. That is arithmetic from your own numbers, not a prediction.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect the channel where deals happen">
    Each user connects accounts under **My Profile › Channels**. Pro includes [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedAccountsPerUser]] connected account per user and Business [[commercial.entitlement.business.includedAccountsPerUser]]; Business also lets you share a whole account with the team. Customermates matches conversation participants to contacts by their email address, phone number or channel identifier, so keep those fields filled in on each contact.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the view you check every week">
    On **Deals**, use **New view** to save, for example, deals in Proposal and Negotiation sorted by last update, oldest first. Views are personal and not shared, so each colleague saves their own.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Customermates Cloud only:** a [routine](/docs/app-routines) set to **Every week**, Monday at 08:00, can do that review. It uses hosted AI credits from its owner's allowance, and reading conversations needs a plan with the inbox. Example instruction:

> Find deals in Proposal or Negotiation that have not been updated for 14 days. For each one, read the latest conversation with the linked contacts and save a short follow-up as a draft reply in that conversation. Do not send anything.

Keep the last sentence. A routine may send email and chat messages without asking, so the instruction is what keeps it to drafts. Starter includes [[commercial.entitlement.starter.includedRoutinesPerUser]] routine per user, Pro [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedRoutinesPerUser]].

## Test your shortlist before you commit

A demo shows the vendor's best path. Your own data shows yours.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Write down where deals move">
    List the channels your last ten won deals went through. If half ran over LinkedIn or WhatsApp, a CRM that only syncs email stays half empty.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick two tools, not five">
    Take the two rows from the decision table that describe you. Five parallel trials means none gets real use.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Import twenty open deals">
    Bring in a real slice with contacts and organizations, not the whole spreadsheet. Trials are short: 14 days at Close, monday CRM and Pipedrive, 21 at Freshsales, 30 at Less Annoying CRM and [[commercial.trial.days]] at Customermates.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Price month thirteen">
    Add annual commitments, onboarding fees and seat minimums from the tables above. The first month is rarely the expensive one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Test the exit">
    Export one record type and check the file format, whether export works per object or for the whole account, and whether there is an API. Customermates exports one record type at a time as an Excel workbook and has a REST API; there is no whole-account export.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## FAQ

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="What is the number one CRM?">
By market share, Salesforce. Salesforce reported in June 2026 that IDC's Worldwide Semiannual Software Tracker ranked it first for the 13th year in a row, with 20.0% of the CRM market in 2025. Market share measures the vendor, not the fit: it says nothing about whether a five-person team will keep its records current.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What are the top 10 CRM platforms?">
There is no neutral top 10. Every list reflects its publisher's criteria and often its commercial interest, this one included, which is why it is sorted by use case. The ten products covered here are Close, Freshsales, HubSpot, Less Annoying CRM, Microsoft Dynamics 365 Sales, monday CRM, Pipedrive, Salesforce, Zoho CRM and Customermates.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Which CRM is best for beginners?">
One with a single clear plan and a trial long enough to run real deals. Less Annoying CRM has one plan and a 30-day trial, HubSpot Free Tools cost nothing for up to 2 users, and Freshsales gives full access for 21 days. Whatever you test, import a slice of your own deals instead of clicking through demo data.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is there a CRM that is completely free?">
Yes, with limits. HubSpot Free Tools cover up to 2 users, the Zoho CRM free edition 3 users, and Salesforce lists a Free Suite at $0. Pipedrive and monday CRM have no free plan. Customermates has no free hosted plan; its AGPL core can be self-hosted without a licence fee, without the inbox and Mate. The [free CRM guide](/blog/free-crm) compares the options.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How long does it take to implement a CRM?">
I know of no reliable figure that holds across team sizes, so be wary of any single number. The software is rarely the slow part; cleaning the spreadsheet and agreeing on stages and required fields take longer. Some plans include vendor-led setup: HubSpot charges a one-time onboarding fee of $1,500 on Sales Hub Professional and $3,500 on Enterprise.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Will AI replace the CRM?">
No. AI changes who types into the CRM, not whether a team needs one shared record of contacts, deals and conversations. Salesforce's Max edition includes Agentforce, Dynamics 365 Sales Enterprise lists agentic capabilities, and in Customermates Cloud Mate updates records and drafts replies using hosted AI credits. Each of these agents works on the CRM's records.
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
