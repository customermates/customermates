# Cold Calling Tips: How to Make B2B Cold Calls That Book Meetings

The cold calling tips that make the biggest difference are simple: call a short list of people who plausibly have the problem you solve, say who you are and why you are calling in the first 30 seconds, aim for one next step (usually a meeting) instead of a sale, and write down the outcome of every call before you dial the next number. Everything else, from scripts to call times, is fine-tuning on top of those four habits.

This guide covers B2B cold calling from the call list to the follow-up: an opener you can adapt, answers to the objections you will hear most, when to call and how often, the legal rules in the US, UK, Germany and France, and a call log that keeps the next step from getting lost. Customermates, the CRM that publishes this guide, has no phone system or dialer; it is where you keep the call notes, contacts, next steps and pipeline, and the last section shows that setup.

## What is cold calling, and does it still work in 2026?

Cold calling means phoning someone who has had no previous contact with you to start a sales conversation. In B2B, that is usually a decision-maker or a person who feels the problem your product solves.

It still works when three conditions hold: the buyer is reachable by phone (owners of small firms, operations and finance roles, trades and local businesses often are), the deal is worth a personal conversation, and the caller has a reason to call this person rather than anyone. It works badly for low-value products, for buyers who never answer unknown numbers, and as a volume game where nobody researched the list.

| Cold calling fits when | Look at other channels when |
| --- | --- |
| A single customer is worth hours of your time | The product sells for a few euros or dollars a month |
| Buyers are owners or managers who take calls | Buyers sit behind switchboards and only answer known numbers |
| You can say in one sentence why you call this company | You would read the same pitch to everyone on the list |
| The law in the buyer's country allows the call (see below) | The list is consumers, and you have no consent to call them |

## Before the call: build a short, researched list

Most bad cold calls go wrong before anyone dials. A list of 30 well-chosen companies beats 300 names pulled from a directory, because every call needs a reason.

| Column in your call list | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Company and person | Name the person you want, not just "the IT department" |
| Role | Tells you which problem to open with |
| Reason to call | A trigger: hiring, a new location, a new product, a post they wrote, a tool they use |
| Phone number and source | Where the number came from, which matters for the legal check |
| Best time to reach | Filled in after the first attempts |
| Attempts and outcome | No answer, gatekeeper, call back, not interested, meeting booked |
| Next step and date | What happens next and when, so nothing depends on memory |

Before each call, spend two or three minutes on the company website and the person's public profile. You are looking for one fact you can use in your opener, not a biography. If you need a checklist for deciding whether a company is worth calling at all, the guide to [client qualification with BANT, CHAMP and MEDDIC](/blog/client-qualification) covers the criteria.

Set one goal per call. For a first call it is almost always "agree a time for a proper conversation". A goal that small is easy to ask for and easy to say yes to.

## What to say in the first 30 seconds

The person you call is in the middle of something else. In the first half minute they decide whether to listen. Say who you are, why you are calling them in particular, and ask a question they can answer.

**Opener you can adapt:**

> Hi [first name], this is [your name] from [company]. I am calling because [reason: you are hiring three field technicians / you opened a second location / you mentioned on LinkedIn that quotes take too long]. We help [type of company] with [one problem in their words]. Is that something you are dealing with at the moment, or is it already solved?

Why this works:

- **Name and company first.** Hiding who you are costs trust, and in several countries the law requires you to say it (see the legal section).
- **A reason that is about them.** "I saw you are hiring" is better than "We are a leading provider of".
- **One problem, not a product tour.** Pick the problem most likely to matter for this role.
- **A question with an easy way out.** "Or is it already solved?" lowers the pressure and often gets a more honest answer.

Some callers ask for permission first ("Did I catch you at a bad time?"). That can work, but it hands the person an easy no. A more neutral version is "Do you have two minutes, or should I call back at a better time?", which offers a real choice.

### Questions that keep the conversation going

When the person engages, ask before you pitch. Three or four questions are enough:

- "How are you handling [problem] today?"
- "What happens when [problem] goes wrong?"
- "Who else is involved when you change how you do this?"
- "If you were to look at this, when would that be realistic?"

Write the answers down during the call. They are the raw material for your follow-up email and for the meeting.

### Closing the call

Ask for the next step with a specific time: "Would Tuesday at 10 or Thursday at 2 work for a 20-minute call with a screen share?" If they agree, confirm the time, the email address and who else should join, and send the invitation while you are still on the phone or right after.

## How to handle common objections

Objections in the first minute are usually reflexes, not decisions. Acknowledge them, ask one question, and accept a real no.

| Objection | What it often means | A response to try |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "I'm not interested." | "I don't know yet why I should be." | "Fair enough. Most people I call say that before they hear why I called. Is [problem] not a topic for you, or just not right now?" |
| "Send me an email." | A polite exit, or a real request | "Happy to. So I send something useful: is the bigger issue [A] or [B]?" Then send it the same day. |
| "We already have a provider." | They are not looking | "Good to hear. What would make you look at an alternative, if anything?" |
| "No budget." | No priority yet, or wrong timing | "Understood. When do you usually plan this kind of spend?" Note the month and call then. |
| "Call me later." | Busy right now | "Of course. Is Thursday morning better, or early next week?" Put the exact time in your list. |

If the answer is a clear no, thank them and end the call. Record "not interested" with the reason, so nobody on your team calls them again next week by accident.

## When to call and how many attempts

There is no universal best time to cold call. Studies from dialer vendors disagree with each other, and your market matters more than any average. Use these starting points and then look at your own call log:

- **Call in blocks.** One or two focused blocks of 45 to 90 minutes beat calls spread across the day.
- **Avoid the obvious bad slots.** Monday morning planning, lunch and late Friday afternoon tend to be hard.
- **Try different times.** If someone never answers at 10, try 8:30 or 16:30. Owners and trades are often easier to reach early.
- **Spread your attempts.** Several tries over two to three weeks, at different times, before you mark someone as unreachable. Combine with one short email or LinkedIn message between calls.
- **Stop at a clear no.** Record it and respect it.

Track three numbers per block: dials, conversations and meetings booked. After a few weeks you will know your own connect rate and best hours, which is worth more than any benchmark.

### Voicemail and gatekeepers

Keep a voicemail under 30 seconds: your name, your company, the one-sentence reason, your number spoken slowly twice. Do not expect a call back; the voicemail mainly makes your next email or call familiar.

With an assistant or receptionist, be open about who you are and why you call. Ask for help rather than trying to trick your way through: "I'm trying to reach whoever handles [topic]. Is that [name], and when is a good time to reach them?"

## Is cold calling legal? The rules by country

Rules for sales calls differ by country, and almost everywhere calls to consumers are stricter than calls to businesses. The overview below was checked on 31 October 2026 against the official sources linked in each row. It is a summary, not legal advice; check your own case, especially before you buy a list or call consumers.

| Country | Consumers (B2C) | Businesses (B2B) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| United States | The [National Do Not Call Registry](https://www.donotcall.gov/) and the federal Telemarketing Sales Rule cover sales calls to consumers. The TCPA adds limits on autodialed and prerecorded calls, especially to mobile phones. Several states have their own rules. | Most business-to-business calls fall outside the federal Do Not Call rules, but the TCPA limits on autodialed and prerecorded calls to mobile numbers still apply, and state rules can add requirements. |
| United Kingdom | You must not make marketing calls to numbers registered with the Telephone Preference Service (TPS) unless the person specifically consented to your calls, or to anyone who told you not to call ([ICO guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/telephone-marketing/)). | Companies can register with the [Corporate TPS](https://www.tpsonline.org.uk/ctps). Screen your list against both TPS and CTPS, and display your number. |
| Germany | Advertising calls to consumers need prior express consent ([§ 7 (2) no. 1 UWG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/__7.html)), which must be documented and kept for five years. Fines up to 300,000 euros. | Calls to businesses need at least presumed consent: a concrete reason to believe this company wants the call, such as a clear link between your offer and its business. |
| France | Since 11 August 2026, calls to consumers require prior consent, unless the call concerns an existing contract. Bloctel, the old opt-out list, has ended ([Bloctel](https://www.bloctel.gouv.fr/)). Calls with consent are limited to weekdays 10:00 to 13:00 and 14:00 to 20:00. | Allowed on the basis of legitimate interest when the call relates to the person's job; the person must be informed and able to object easily ([CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/prospection-commerciale-par-telephone-hors-automate-dappel-quelles-sont-les-regles)). |

Two rules that hold almost everywhere: say who you are and that the call is commercial at the start, and stop calling someone who asks you to. Record that request in your CRM where everyone on the team can see it.

## Track every call so the follow-up happens

A good call with no follow-up is a wasted call. The habit that separates people who book meetings from people who just dial: write the outcome down before the next call.

| Field in your call log | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Date and time | 2026-11-03, 10:40 |
| Contact and company | Laura Weber, Weber Haustechnik |
| Outcome | Conversation, meeting booked |
| What they said | Quotes take two days, wants to cut it to same day; owner decides |
| Next step and date | Demo on Thursday 10:00, send agenda today |
| Objection, if any | "We tried software before and nobody used it" |

The next step is where most deals stall. If the person said "send me something", the email goes out the same day, and the [sales follow-up email templates](/blog/sales-follow-up-email) cover what to write after a call, after no response and to close the loop. Once a meeting is booked, the deal belongs in your [sales pipeline](/blog/sales-pipeline) with a stage and a next step. How a [CRM for sales teams](/blog/crm-for-sales) holds calls, deals and next steps together is covered in its own guide.

## FAQ

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="What is the best opening line for a cold call?">
Your name and company, then the reason you are calling this specific person, then a question. For example: "Hi Laura, this is Jonas from Northwind. I am calling because you mentioned that quotes take two days. Is that still a problem, or already solved?" A line that is about them beats any clever hook.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What is a good time to cold call?">
There is no single best time. Calling in focused blocks, avoiding Monday morning, lunch and late Friday, and trying early morning or late afternoon for hard-to-reach people are good starting points. After a few weeks, your own call log shows when your buyers actually pick up.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What is the 3-3-3 rule in sales?">
There is no single definition. Sales trainers use the name for different rules of thumb, for example spending three minutes finding three facts about a prospect before you call. Treat it as a reminder to prepare, not as a standard.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Does cold calling still work in 2026?">
Yes, for B2B deals where a customer is worth a personal conversation and buyers take calls, such as owners of small firms, trades and operations roles. It works poorly as a high-volume game with unresearched lists, and for consumers it is now restricted in many countries.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is B2B cold calling legal?">
In most countries, calls to businesses are allowed under looser conditions than calls to consumers, but the conditions differ: Germany requires at least presumed consent, the UK requires screening against the Corporate TPS, France requires that the call relates to the person's job and that they can object. See the table above and check your own case.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How many times should you call a prospect?">
Several attempts spread over two to three weeks and at different times of day, combined with a short email or LinkedIn message between calls. Stop at a clear no and record it, so nobody on the team calls again.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What to do instead of cold calling?">
Warm outreach usually converts better: referrals from customers, people who engaged with your content or posts, event contacts, and a short personal email or LinkedIn message before a call. Many teams combine both, calling people who already know their name.
</FaqItem>

</FaqItem>
