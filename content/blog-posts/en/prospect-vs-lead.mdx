# Prospect vs Lead: What Is the Difference, and What Is a Prospect?

Prospect vs lead comes down to one question: has anyone checked this person or company against your criteria yet? A lead has shown interest or been identified but is still unchecked, while a prospect is a lead that passed that check and is worth active sales effort; the word prospect itself simply means a possible future customer.

Companies use both words loosely, and the same word can mean different things in two sales teams. This guide gives one consistent set of definitions for contact, lead, MQL, prospect, opportunity and customer, with the entry criterion, the owner and the next action for each stage. Customermates, the open-source CRM that publishes it, has no lead scoring and no data enrichment, so the last section shows the stages as a field, a board and tasks that you set up yourself.

## What is a prospect, and what is a lead?

A **prospect** is a possible future customer. The [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/prospect) gives exactly that as the marketing sense of the word. In sales it carries a check on top: the person or company fits the kind of customer you sell to and has a problem you can solve.

A **lead** comes earlier. It is a person or company that has shown interest in what you sell, for example by sending an enquiry, replying to your message, asking for a demo or arriving through a referral, and that nobody has evaluated yet. Salesforce describes a lead in the same way, as a potential sales prospect who expressed interest in a product or service.

Two side notes on the words. In everyday English, "prospects" also means chances or outlook, as in career prospects; in sales, "the prospects of a company" are its potential customers. And "to prospect" is a verb: it means searching for and approaching potential customers, which is the work that produces leads and prospects.

| | Lead | Prospect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | A contact that showed interest or was identified | A lead that passed your qualification check |
| What you know | A name, a way to reach them, usually the source and what they asked | Need, decision maker, budget range and timing, or the reasons one is missing |
| Who works it | Whoever replies first | The person who sells |
| Goal | Reply and find out whether it fits | Agree a scoped proposal |
| If it fails | Disqualify with a reason, or park with a date | Park with a date to look again, or lose it |

## What is the difference between a lead and an opportunity?

A lead is a person or company. An opportunity is a specific potential deal: there is something to sell, a value, and an expected decision date. One customer can have several opportunities over the years, and many leads never produce one.

Salesforce's lead conversion shows the order. According to its [Trailhead module on leads](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/leads_opportunities_lightning_experience/create-and-convert-leads-lightning), converting a lead uses the lead record to create a business account, a contact and an opportunity. The lead always comes first. The stages an opportunity then moves through, from first demo to won or lost, are covered in the guide to the [sales pipeline](/blog/sales-pipeline).

## Contact, lead, prospect, opportunity, customer: what are the stages?

This is one set of definitions you can copy. Each stage has a test someone else could run in your CRM, a single owner and an obvious next action.

| Stage | What it means | It enters when | Owner | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Contact | A person or company you know exists and might fit. No sign of interest yet. Some teams call these suspects. | Name, a way to reach them and a recorded source | Whoever found them | Check the fit against your target profile, then reach out or drop |
| Lead | A contact that showed interest or replied | An enquiry, a reply, a referral, a demo request or a conversation at an event | Marketing, or whoever replies first | Reply, then qualify |
| MQL (optional) | A lead that marketing judges ready for sales, by rules you agreed | It matches the target profile and took an agreed action, such as asking for prices | Marketing | Hand over to sales, who accept it or send it back with a reason |
| Prospect (the same checkpoint as SQL) | A qualified potential customer | The qualification answers are in and the verdict is yes | The seller | Book the next conversation and work towards a scoped proposal |
| Opportunity | A specific potential deal | A deal exists with a value, a stage and an expected decision date | The seller | Move the deal through the pipeline stages |
| Customer | A person or company with at least one won deal | The first deal is won | Account management, or the seller in a small team | Onboard, check in, look for the next opportunity |

Two exits sit outside the table: **Disqualified**, always with a reason from a fixed list, and **Parked**, with a date to look again. A team of three without a marketing department can drop the MQL row, because every enquiry goes straight to the person who sells.

The criteria behind "the qualification answers are in" (need, decision maker, budget range, timing) are in the guide to [lead qualification with BANT, CHAMP and MEDDIC](/blog/client-qualification). For how the stages relate to what the buyer experiences, see the [customer journey guide](/blog/customer-journey).

## Why does every company define these terms differently?

Because the words come from tools and teams, not from a standard. Checked on 2 November 2026 against the sources named in the table:

| Source | Lead | Prospect | Opportunity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [HubSpot lifecycle stages](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/use-lifecycle-stages) | A contact or company that converted on your website or through another interaction | No stage of its own; the closest is the sales qualified lead, qualified by sales as a potential customer | A contact or company associated with a deal |
| [Salesforce Trailhead](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/leads_opportunities_lightning_experience/create-and-convert-leads-lightning) | A potential sales prospect who expressed interest | Used inside the definition of a lead | Created when a lead is converted |
| [Superhuman Prospecting glossary](https://superhumanprospecting.com/glossary/prospect/) | Becomes a prospect when it matches the ideal customer profile and meets agreed criteria | A potential customer that fits the ideal customer profile | A prospect with a clearly identified deal, with scope and value |

A second split runs through outbound and inbound sales. Some outbound teams call a researched target a prospect before it has said a word and keep "lead" for people who responded; inbound teams usually reserve "lead" for the first sign of interest and "prospect" for the qualified stage. Both are in use, so ask what a colleague or a vendor means before you compare numbers.

What matters is that your team uses one set. Four habits keep it that way:

- **One sentence per stage**, written down where the team can read it.
- **An entry criterion that is a field value**, not a feeling: "Prospect means Need, Decision maker and Timing are filled in and the verdict is yes."
- **One owner per stage**, so a record is never everyone's job.
- **A reason whenever a record leaves**, from a fixed list, so you can count why leads fail.

## How do you define a prospect in your own company?

Use four tests, and call a lead a prospect only when it passes all of them:

1. **Fit.** It matches your target profile: the kind of company, size, region and role you sell to.
2. **Need.** There is a problem you can solve, in the person's own words, not only in your assumption.
3. **Ability to buy.** You know who decides and that the budget range is realistic.
4. **Timing.** There is a reason to act in a period that suits you, or a date to look again.

Everything that fails a test stays a lead, becomes Parked with a date, or is disqualified with a reason. Keep these tests in one place and apply them in the same order every time. The qualification guide linked above lists the questions that fill them in.

## How do you create a prospect list?

A prospect list is a short, researched list of companies and people who fit your target profile. A list of 30 well-chosen names beats 300 pulled from a directory, because every outreach needs a reason.

1. **Write the target profile.** Three to five criteria: industry, company size, region, the role you sell to and a trigger such as hiring or a new location. [Customer segmentation](/blog/customer-segmentation) turns these criteria into groups you can filter and report on.
2. **Choose the sources.** Referrals from customers, companies that resemble your best customers, LinkedIn searches, events, industry associations, directories and your own old enquiries.
3. **Use fixed columns.** Everyone who edits the list keeps the same ones (see the table below).
4. **Qualify before you invest effort.** Unchecked names stay Contacts. Only names that pass your tests become prospects.
5. **Check the contact rules.** The rules for calls and email differ by country and between consumers and businesses. The guide to [cold calling](/blog/cold-calling) lists the phone rules for the US, the UK, Germany and France, and the guide to [customer acquisition](/blog/customer-acquisition) covers the email rules.

Suggested columns for step 3:

| Column | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Company, person and role | Name the person you want, not only the department |
| Source | You cannot judge a channel you did not record |
| Reason to contact | The trigger, which becomes your opening line |
| Stage | Contact until someone has replied or been qualified |
| Next step and date | What happens next, and when |

## How do you track these stages in Customermates?

Customermates is our product. It has no separate lead or prospect record and no automatic conversion: one person stays one contact record from the first message to the signed deal, and the stage is a field that you, Mate or your AI client change. An opportunity is a deal linked to that contact.

The stage matters because it decides the next follow-up. With LinkedIn and email connected on Customermates Cloud from Pro, the conversation sits on that same contact, Mate or your own AI agent drafts the reply and the next step, you review and send, and the next steps show up on your dashboard.

## FAQ

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="What is the difference between a prospect and a lead?">
A lead has shown interest or been identified but is unchecked. A prospect is a lead that passed your qualification check, so it fits your target profile, has a need and can buy. A lead is a first signal; a prospect is a decision to spend sales effort.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What does prospect mean in sales?">
A possible future customer, usually one that fits your target profile and has been qualified. Prospecting is the activity of finding and approaching such people or companies.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What are prospects in business?">
They are the people and companies a business hopes to sell to but who have not bought yet. The prospects of a company are therefore its potential customers, ranging from cold names on a list to qualified buyers in conversation.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is a prospect the same as an opportunity?">
No. A prospect is a person or company. An opportunity is a specific potential deal with a scope, a value and an expected decision date. A prospect can have no opportunity yet, or several over time.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Which comes first, a lead or a prospect?">
In the HubSpot and Salesforce models the lead comes first and the opportunity after it. Where the word prospect names a qualified stage, as in the Superhuman glossary, it sits between the two. Some outbound teams reverse the words and call a researched target a prospect before any reply. Decide on one order and write it down.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How do you turn a lead into a prospect?">
Qualify it. Ask the questions behind your criteria (need, decision maker, budget range, timing), record the answers in fields and decide. If the answers are yes, the lead becomes a prospect. If not, disqualify it with a reason or park it with a date.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can Customermates score leads or find prospects for me?">
No. Customermates has no lead scoring, no data enrichment and no lead database. It keeps the contacts, stages, tasks, conversations and deals you give it, and Mate or your AI client can help you review them.
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
