# Connecting Outlook to a CRM: add-in, server-side sync or the CRM's own inbox

An Outlook CRM integration does one of two jobs, and good ones do both. It shows CRM records next to the email you are reading in Outlook, and it copies emails and meetings from your Microsoft 365 mailbox onto the right contact and deal. The first job is done by an Outlook add-in. The second runs on the CRM's servers, whether Outlook is open or not.

So the real question is where your team wants to work. If people live in Outlook all day, pick a CRM with a solid add-in plus server-side sync. If they would rather answer email from the CRM, next to the deal and the other channels, pick a CRM that brings the mailbox in. One technical check applies to everyone: the new Outlook for Windows runs only web add-ins, so older COM or VSTO plug-ins stop working there.

Customermates publishes this guide. Customermates, an AI-native CRM, takes the third approach: it has no Outlook add-in and brings Outlook mail into its own inbox instead. Vendor details below were checked on 8 October 2026 on the vendors' own pages, linked at the end.

## The three integration types

| Type | What it does | Where you work | Check before you buy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Add-in in Outlook | Shows the matching contact, deals and history beside an email; logs that email or meeting to a record when you click | Outlook | Runs in your Outlook clients (classic and new Windows, Mac, web, mobile); works with shared mailboxes if you use them |
| Server-side sync | Connects the mailbox to the CRM through Exchange or Microsoft 365 and copies sent and received mail, and often meetings, to matching records | Outlook, with the CRM filling itself | Which folders sync, how fast, what happens to private mail, whether contacts and calendar sync in both directions |
| Mailbox inside the CRM | Imports the mailbox into the CRM's inbox, where you read, reply and file mail next to the record | The CRM | Whether you can still use Outlook in parallel, whether replies go out from your real address, what happens to the calendar |

Most CRMs in the next table combine the first two: an add-in for looking things up and server-side sync for completeness. The third type suits teams whose customer conversations are spread across email and chat channels, because the CRM then shows all of them in one list.

## The new Outlook for Windows check

Microsoft's developer documentation is explicit: VSTO and COM add-ins aren't supported in the new Outlook on Windows, and vendors must migrate to an Outlook web add-in. Classic Outlook on Windows still runs them. When a user switches to the new client, Outlook installs web counterparts of existing COM add-ins where they exist.

Two details from the same page matter for CRM buyers. Microsoft lists "CRM and tracking services" as a supported scenario for web add-ins. It lists "data sync service", bidirectional sync of mail items, as only partially supported. And the new client currently needs a Microsoft 365 work or school account on Exchange Online; on-premises, hybrid and sovereign Exchange accounts are not supported yet.

In practice: if your team is moving to the new Outlook, ask each vendor whether its add-in is a web add-in, and test it in the new client before you migrate. Server-side sync and a mailbox inside the CRM do not depend on the Outlook client at all.

## Which approach fits

| Your situation | Look for |
|---|---|
| The team works in Outlook all day and does not want a second window | An add-in that runs in all your Outlook clients, plus server-side sync |
| You are on Microsoft 365 with an IT admin, Teams and Dataverse | Dynamics 365 Sales; it is Microsoft's own CRM and its App for Outlook sits on server-side synchronization |
| You need Outlook contacts and the CRM's contacts to stay identical | A CRM with two-way contact sync, tested with your address book first |
| Most customer conversations happen by email plus LinkedIn or WhatsApp | A CRM that imports the mailbox into a shared inbox with those channels |
| A team mailbox such as sales@ must be visible to several people | Check shared mailbox support explicitly; the Dynamics 365 App for Outlook, for example, does not support Microsoft 365 shared mailboxes |
| You are moving to the new Outlook for Windows | Web add-ins only, or an approach that does not need an add-in |

### Let Mate or your own AI client do the follow-up

In Customermates Cloud, Mate can summarise a thread or draft a reply, and it uses hosted AI credits. A routine runs a saved instruction when something happens. For Outlook mail, create a routine with the trigger **When something changes**, pick the event **Email Received**, and write instructions like these:

> When an email arrives from a contact who has an open deal assigned to me, read the thread and the deal. Save a reply draft in the thread that answers the open question. Do not send anything. If the email asks for a meeting, say so in the draft instead of proposing times.

The "do not send" sentence matters. A routine runs as its owner with the owner's channels, and Mate sends email without asking again when an instruction tells it to send. ## Test any Outlook integration before you roll it out

1. Send an email from Outlook to a known contact. It should appear on that contact within the vendor's stated sync time.
2. Reply from a phone. The reply should join the same thread, not create a new contact.
3. Email someone who is not in the CRM. Check whether the CRM creates a contact on its own, asks you, or ignores the message, and which of these you want.
4. Put a private email in a folder you excluded. It must not appear in the CRM.
5. Create a meeting in Outlook with a customer. Check where it shows up and whether editing it in the CRM changes Outlook.
6. Open the add-in, if there is one, in every Outlook client your team uses, including the new Outlook for Windows.

## Can you use Outlook as a CRM?

For a handful of clients, partly. Outlook has contacts, categories, tasks and a calendar, and a folder per client gets you surprisingly far. What it lacks is a deal with a stage and a value, a list of open deals per person, and a history that a colleague can read when you are away. Microsoft's CRM is Dynamics 365 Sales, which is licensed separately from Microsoft 365. Once you track more than a few deals at once, a CRM connected to Outlook is less work than folders and categories.

## FAQ

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="How do I integrate a CRM with Outlook?">
Most CRMs offer two parts. First connect your mailbox in the CRM's settings so emails sync on the server. Then install the CRM's add-in from Outlook's add-in store, or let your Microsoft 365 admin deploy it for everyone. In Customermates there is no add-in: you connect the Outlook account under **My Profile › Channels** on Pro or higher, and the mail appears in the CRM inbox.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is Microsoft Outlook a CRM, and does Microsoft 365 include one?">
No. Outlook is an email and calendar client with contacts and tasks, but no deals, stages or pipeline. Microsoft's CRM is Dynamics 365 Sales, a separate subscription; Microsoft lists Sales Professional at $65 per user per month, paid yearly.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Do CRM add-ins work in the new Outlook for Windows?">
Only web add-ins do. Microsoft states that COM and VSTO add-ins aren't supported in the new Outlook on Windows, while classic Outlook still runs them. The new client also needs a Microsoft 365 work or school account on Exchange Online for now.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is Salesforce for Outlook being discontinued?">
Yes. Salesforce is retiring Salesforce for Outlook, its older desktop product, and recommends the Outlook integration (a side panel in Outlook) and Einstein Activity Capture for email and event sync. Check Salesforce's release notes for the date that applies to your org.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Does HubSpot integrate with Outlook, and is it free?">
Yes. The HubSpot Sales Office 365 add-in tracks and logs emails and brings HubSpot sales tools into Outlook. HubSpot lists it as available with all products and plans, and its help article names no separate charge for the add-in. Your inbox must be connected to HubSpot first.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can CRM contacts sync with Outlook contacts?">
Some CRMs do this. Dynamics 365 synchronizes contacts between Exchange and the app, for example. Customermates does not: it matches conversations to contacts by email address, and you link or create contacts in the CRM.
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
