# AI CRM: What an AI-Native CRM Does and How to Judge One

An AI CRM is a CRM in which a language model works with your customer data: it reads contacts, deals and conversations, answers questions about them, changes records and drafts the next message. Products differ less in whether they have AI than in what the AI may do. In some, it writes text in a side panel and a person still makes every change. In an AI-native CRM, the AI uses the same actions and permissions as a user, and it also works when nobody types a prompt: on a schedule, or when a record changes.

Customermates, an AI-native CRM, publishes this guide and appears in its own labelled section below. Facts about other vendors come from their own pages, checked on 8 October 2026.

## What AI does in a CRM today

Most of what gets sold as AI in customer relationship management falls into seven jobs. The first five are language-model work and pay off in the first week, even with a few hundred records. The last two depend on data volume and on outside sources.

| Job | Example | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Summarise | "What happened with Acme since June?" across notes, emails and LinkedIn messages | Does it read the conversations, or only the notes field? |
| Draft | A reply in the thread, in the contact's language, that refers to the open deal | Does it save a draft, or can it also send? |
| Update records | Move a deal to the next stage, add a note, link a new contact to its organization | Is every change logged, and does it respect user roles? |
| Answer questions over data | "Which open proposals have the highest value and no message from us in two weeks?" | Does it compute over all records, or guess from the first page of results? |
| Run unattended | A daily pass over open deals, or a reaction when a deal is won | Are there caps on runs and spending, and what may it do while nobody watches? |
| Score | A likelihood-to-close number per lead or deal, or an expected revenue figure | Which history does the model learn from, and is there enough of it? |
| Fill in data | Company size or job titles taken from outside sources | Which data provider supplies it, and on what legal basis? |

A scoring model learns from your won and lost deals. A consultancy that closes 30 deals a year gives it very little to learn from, so for small teams the first five jobs save the most time.

## AI-native, add-on AI and smart CRM: what the labels mean

Vendors use these labels loosely. Here is what each one usually means, and the question that tells them apart.

| Label | What it usually means | The question that exposes it |
|---|---|---|
| CRM with AI features | An assistant panel that summarises records and writes text; a person still makes each change | "Can it change the record itself?" |
| Smart CRM | An older marketing term for a CRM with automation and scoring. HubSpot also uses Smart CRM as the name of its CRM | "Which of these features are AI, and which are rules someone configured?" |
| AI-native CRM | The AI operates the CRM through the same actions and permissions as a user, inside the app and from outside AI clients, including unattended runs | "Which actions need my approval, and which run straight away?" |
| Agentic CRM | AI agents finish multi-step tasks on their own. The term overlaps with AI-native | "What can an agent do while nobody is watching?" |

A quick test for "AI-native": list what a person can do in the CRM, then check how much of it the AI can do through a tool. If the AI can only read, or only writes text that you paste somewhere, it is an add-on. The [agentic CRM guide](/blog/agentic-crm) goes deeper into agent actions and the approvals around them.

Ask these in a demo or look them up in the documentation. The third column shows how Customermates answers, so you can see what a concrete answer looks like.

MCP, the [Model Context Protocol](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/docs/getting-started/intro), is an open-source standard for connecting AI applications such as Claude or ChatGPT to external systems. With an MCP server, the AI client you already pay for works in the CRM directly.

Run the same test in every trial, with your own data instead of demo records. The Customermates trial runs [[commercial.trial.days]] days with Pro features, so the inbox is part of it.

## Customermates as an AI-native CRM

Customermates is an open-source CRM built in Germany for small client-facing teams: consultants, agencies, other service businesses and founders. Contacts, organizations, deals, services and tasks live in one workspace; on Pro and higher, the email, LinkedIn and WhatsApp conversations about them sit alongside. AI reaches that data in two ways, and self-hosting changes which of them you get.

| | Mate | Your own AI client through MCP | Self-hosted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where it runs | Customermates Cloud, every plan | Customermates Cloud on every plan, and self-hosted | Your server, with the AGPL core |
| Who pays for the model | Monthly hosted AI credits per active user | Your own AI subscription or provider; clients include Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor, Codex and Gemini CLI | Your own AI provider, through MCP |
| Inbox | Pro and higher | Messaging tools on Pro and higher, cloud only | No inbox |
| Unattended runs | Routines | Only if your client schedules them | No Mate and no routines |

Mate is the assistant inside Customermates Cloud. It reads your workspace Knowledge Base, where you keep company facts, processes and tone of voice, and cites the pages it used. For questions that need arithmetic across many records, such as the median deal value per lead source, it runs a read-only analysis over the matching records instead of reading one page of results.

Your own AI client connects to the [MCP server](/docs/mcp): Claude and ChatGPT through a custom connector with OAuth sign-in, Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and Gemini CLI with an API key. The server asks clients to confirm before deleting or sending, but whether you see a confirmation depends on the client.

### A worked setup for a five-person consultancy

- **Deal stages:** Lead (10%), Qualified (30%), Proposal sent (60%), Won (100%), Lost (0%). The percentages are stage probabilities and drive the weighted pipeline.
- **Custom field on deals:** Source, a single-select field with Referral, LinkedIn, Website and Event.
- **Saved view:** "My open proposals", filtered to the stage Proposal sent and to deals assigned to me, sorted by value. Saved views are personal, so each consultant saves their own.
- **Inbox:** each consultant connects [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedAccountsPerUser]] account on Pro, usually the work email or LinkedIn.

Each consultant then adds two [routines](/docs/app-routines). Both run in Customermates Cloud and use hosted AI credits.

The first, "Proposal follow-ups", runs **On a schedule**, **Every day** at 09:00:

```text
Look at the open deals assigned to me in the stage "Proposal sent".
For each deal, read the latest conversation with its contacts.
If our message was the last one and is more than five days old,
save a short follow-up as a draft in that thread, in the language
of the conversation. Do not send anything.
End with a list of the drafts you saved.
```

The second, "Won handover", runs **When something changes**, on the event Deal Updated, with **Changed fields** set to the stage:

```text
If this deal is now in the stage "Won", create a task
"Kick-off: [deal name]", link it to the deal and assign it to the
deal's assigned users. Then add a five-line summary of the latest
conversation to the deal's notes. If the stage is anything else,
do nothing.
```

A routine runs as its owner, with the owner's channels and permissions. An unattended run refuses everything that would need approval, and changes made by a routine never trigger another routine. Mate sends messages when asked to, which is why the first instruction says "Do not send anything".

**What it costs:** on Pro, this team pays [[commercial.price.pro.monthly.seats.5]] per month for the inbox, [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedAccountsPerUser]] connected account and up to [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedRoutinesPerUser]] routines per person, with monthly hosted AI credits per active user for Mate and the routines (see [pricing](/pricing)). Starter costs the same team [[commercial.price.starter.monthly.seats.5]] per month and includes Mate, MCP and [[commercial.entitlement.starter.includedRoutinesPerUser]] routine per person, but no inbox, so the follow-up routine would have no threads to read. Billing is monthly.

### What Customermates does not do

- No lead scoring, no data enrichment and no revenue forecasting. Weighted pipeline values come from the stage probabilities you set.
- No email sequences or bulk sending. Routines have no recipient lists or sequence steps, and Mate sends individual messages.
- No calendar booking. Pro and higher read connected calendars without writing to them.
- No native mobile app.
- No inbox, Mate or routines when self-hosted. Self-hosting gives you the AGPL core with REST, webhooks and MCP.

If predictive scoring for a large sales team is why you want AI, Customermates is not the fit: look at Zoho CRM with Zia or at Salesforce, whose own pages describe predictive features.

## Frequently asked questions

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="What is an AI CRM?">
A customer relationship management system in which an AI model reads and acts on your customer data: it summarises relationships, answers questions over records, updates deals and drafts messages. In an AI-native CRM, the AI also uses the same actions and permissions as a person and can run on a schedule.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is a smart CRM the same as an AI CRM?">
Mostly a difference in wording. Smart CRM is the older term, and HubSpot also uses it as the name of its CRM product. Judge any product, whatever it calls itself, by what the AI may change, what it asks first and who pays for the model.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Which AI CRM is the best?">
There is no single answer, and Customermates, which publishes this guide, does not rank itself. If you already run Salesforce, HubSpot, Zoho CRM or Dynamics 365, start with your vendor's AI. If your team works through email, LinkedIn and WhatsApp and wants AI to keep the CRM current from those conversations, a CRM with a unified inbox and an MCP server, such as Customermates, is worth a trial. Run the ten-minute test in each one.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can ChatGPT make a CRM?">
ChatGPT or Claude can generate the code for a simple contact database, but then you own its hosting, permissions, backups and every fix. The more common route is to connect the AI client to an existing CRM through MCP, so it reads and updates real records under your permissions. Customermates offers this on every plan; the guide to [using Claude with a CRM](/blog/claude-crm) shows the setup.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Will AI replace the CRM?">
Not the record itself. An AI model needs a place that stores who the client is, what was agreed and which deal is at which stage, with permissions around that data. What AI replaces is much of the typing: logging conversations, moving stages and writing first drafts. The CRM becomes the system the AI reads from and writes to.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is AI in a CRM safe for customer data under GDPR?">
That depends on where the model runs and who processes the data, so ask every vendor for its subprocessor list and a data processing agreement. In Customermates Cloud, the primary database is configured in the AWS Frankfurt region. The subprocessors page names Google Cloud Vertex AI and Microsoft Azure as the providers behind Mate's models, and the privacy policy explains that using Mate can involve processing in the United States. With your own AI client through MCP, the data the client reads goes to the AI provider you chose. Self-hosting the open-source core keeps the CRM data on your own servers.
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
