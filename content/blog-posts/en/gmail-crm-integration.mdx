# Gmail CRM Integration: How It Works and What to Check

A **Gmail CRM integration** connects a Gmail or Google Workspace mailbox to the system that holds your contacts and deals, so an email thread and the customer record it belongs to stop sitting in two places. Every product in this category promises that. They deliver it in very different ways, and the difference decides what your team can actually do: read the CRM while writing an email, see every customer email from inside the CRM, or let an automation or AI client act on both.

This guide explains the three integration architectures, the Google rules every vendor has to follow, the questions worth asking before you connect a mailbox, and how a Gmail account works in [Customermates](/features/email-integration) step by step. Disclosure: Customermates publishes this guide.

## The three ways a CRM connects to Gmail

### 1. A sidebar or add-on inside Gmail

The CRM appears as a panel next to the open email. You look up the sender, log the message, or create a record without leaving Gmail. Streak goes furthest and runs its whole pipeline inside the Gmail interface; HubSpot, Copper and Pipedrive offer Gmail extensions or add-ons next to their own web apps.

- **Good for:** people who spend their day in Gmail and want CRM context while they write.
- **Watch for:** what gets logged automatically versus one message at a time, and whether the panel works in the Gmail mobile app or only on the web.

### 2. Mailbox sync into the CRM

You authorize the mailbox once, and the CRM reads threads from it on the server. Conversations appear in the CRM next to the contact, and replies go out through the same Gmail account, so they sit in your Sent folder as usual. This is the model Customermates uses.

- **Good for:** teams that work from the customer record and want every conversation there, including messages nobody remembered to log.
- **Watch for:** which folders sync, who else can read a synced thread, and how the CRM decides which contact a thread belongs to.

### 3. An API or workflow integration you build

You connect the Gmail API to the CRM's API or webhooks with a workflow tool such as n8n, or with your own code. A deal stage change can send a Gmail message; an incoming email can create a record. You decide exactly what moves.

- **Good for:** specific, repeatable automations that the product does not offer.
- **Watch for:** you own the OAuth app, the error handling and the maintenance. Google's verification rules below apply to your app, not to the CRM vendor.

## The Google rules behind every Gmail integration

Two things on Google's side shape what any vendor can offer.

**Gmail scopes.** Google classifies the scopes that read, modify or fully access a mailbox (for example `gmail.readonly`, `gmail.modify` and full `mail.google.com` access) as [restricted scopes](https://developers.google.com/gmail/api/auth/scopes). An app that uses them must pass Google's restricted-scope verification, and one that stores or transmits that data on its servers must also complete a security assessment. Sending only (`gmail.send`) is a sensitive scope with lighter requirements. If you build your own integration, budget for this; if you buy one, the vendor or its provider carries it.

**Workspace admin controls.** In Google Workspace, an administrator can restrict which third-party apps may access Google data. If a connection fails for one user but works for another, check the organization's API access controls before debugging the CRM.

**Sending limits.** Google Workspace allows up to 2,000 messages per user per day on paid accounts, 1,500 for mail merge and 500 during a trial, with a maximum of 500 external recipients per message, according to [Google's sending-limits page](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/gmail/gmail-sending-limits-in-google-workspace) (checked 27 September 2026). Mail a CRM sends through your Gmail account counts against those limits.

## What to check before you connect a mailbox

1. **Which folders sync?** Archive, spam and all-mail folders behave differently across products.
2. **Who can read a synced thread?** Private by default, shared per thread, or visible to the whole workspace?
3. **How are threads matched to contacts?** By email address, by domain, or by hand?
4. **Where do replies come from?** Your real Gmail address and signature, or a relay address?
5. **What happens to calendar data?** Read-only display, or can the CRM create and change events?
6. **What can automation and AI do?** Read only, draft for review, or send?
7. **What happens when you disconnect?** Which data stays in the CRM and which disappears?

## How Gmail works in Customermates

Customermates connects Gmail as a channel of its [unified inbox](/features/unified-inbox). The inbox runs on Customermates Cloud and needs a plan with messaging: Pro, Business, Enterprise or a trial. Each member can connect [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedAccountsPerUser]] connected account on Pro and [[commercial.entitlement.business.includedAccountsPerUser]] connected accounts on Business; see [pricing](/pricing).

<Steps>
  <Step title="Connect the account">
    Open **My Profile › Channels**, choose **Connect channel** and pick
    **Gmail**. You authorize through the hosted sign-in flow; the channel card
    then shows whether email, calendar or both are connected.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the folders">
    On the account's **Folders** tab, every folder is on by default except
    trash, junk or spam, drafts and the all-mail folder. Turn a folder on if
    mail from it, for example an archive, should appear in the inbox.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Link participants to contacts">
    Open **Thread settings** on a conversation and **Link** each participant
    to an existing contact or create a new one. The address is added to the
    contact, so every conversation with it appears on that contact's
    timeline.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Decide who sees what">
    Conversations from your Gmail account are private by default. Share a
    single conversation with the team from its header, or, on Business and
    Enterprise, share the whole account.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reply and write new email">
    Reply from the conversation, or start a new email from the **New message**
    icon next to a contact's address. Mail goes out from your Gmail account,
    with the signature you set on the account's **Email** tab.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A few Gmail-specific details from the [inbox guide](/docs/app-inbox):

- **Folders are read-only for Gmail.** Outlook and IMAP conversations can be moved to another folder from the CRM; a Gmail conversation shows its folder as a read-only chip.
- **Thread state is shared.** **Unread**, **Open**, **Closed** and **Spam** are one value for everyone who can see the conversation, and setting a state never sends anything to the other side.
- **Drafts are one per conversation.** A saved draft is visible in the inbox and can be sent, edited or discarded later. Drafts cannot keep attachments.

<AcquisitionCallout title="What this integration does not do" variant="boundary">
  There is no Gmail sidebar or Chrome extension. Connected calendar data is
  read-only: Customermates shows events but does not create, change or book
  them. There are no sequences or bulk sends, because messaging is one-to-one:
  each message goes to one conversation. Self-hosted installations have no inbox at all; there you
  would use the REST API, webhooks or your own workflow instead.
</AcquisitionCallout>

## AI on top of the Gmail inbox

Because Gmail conversations sit next to CRM records, an AI client can use both. External clients such as Claude or ChatGPT connect through [MCP](/docs/mcp) and use tools like `get_messaging_threads` to read conversations, `save_message_draft` to prepare a reply for review, and `send_email` to send one. Customermates registers [[derived.mcp.tools.total]] MCP tools in total; the messaging tools need a plan with messaging.

A practical, reversible first workflow: ask the client to read the latest thread with a contact, summarize it into a note on the deal, and save a reply as a draft. You read the draft in the inbox and press **Send** yourself. The MCP server instructions tell clients to ask before sending, but that confirmation happens in the client, not as a second approval on the server, so check how your client handles it. Mate, the assistant inside Customermates Cloud, requires a fresh approval for every send.

## Gmail integration for automation

If a workflow must react to CRM changes, use [webhooks](/docs/webhooks): Customermates emits [[derived.webhooks.events.total]] event types for records and messaging, which a separately operated n8n instance or your own service can consume. The [n8n community node](/docs/n8n) and the REST API cover the other direction. That is architecture 3 from above, running next to the synced inbox rather than replacing it.

## Can you use Gmail as a CRM?

For a handful of conversations, labels, stars and Google Contacts go a long way. They stop working when several people share customers, when you need deal stages and values, or when conversations happen outside email. Gmail has no pipeline, no deal values and no shared history per customer across channels. At that point, keep Gmail as the place mail is sent and received, and let a CRM hold the record. If your customers also write on WhatsApp or LinkedIn, see how a [customer communication management](/blog/customer-communication-management) setup brings those channels together.

## Frequently asked questions

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="How do I connect Gmail to Customermates?">
On Customermates Cloud with Pro, Business, Enterprise or a trial, open **My Profile › Channels**, choose **Connect channel**, pick **Gmail** and complete the sign-in. Conversations from the folders switched on for that account then appear in the Inbox.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Does Customermates have a Gmail sidebar or Chrome extension?">
No. Customermates brings Gmail conversations into the CRM instead of putting the CRM inside Gmail. You read, reply and link threads to contacts in the Customermates inbox.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can other team members read my Gmail conversations?">
Not by default. Conversations from your account are private until you share a single conversation or, on Business and Enterprise, share the whole account. A teammate's private conversations never appear for anyone else, admins included.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Does the integration sync Google Calendar?">
Calendar data from a connected Google account can be read into Customermates, but only for display. Customermates does not create, change or book calendar events.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Does the Gmail integration work in self-hosted Customermates?">
No. The inbox and connected channels are part of Customermates Cloud. A self-hosted installation includes the core CRM, REST, webhooks and MCP, so any Gmail connection there is a workflow you build and operate.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can I send email campaigns or sequences from Gmail through the CRM?">
No. Messaging in Customermates is one-to-one, with no bulk sends. Campaigns and sequences belong in a dedicated email provider, which you can connect to CRM events through webhooks.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How many emails can I send through Gmail per day?">
Google limits paid Workspace accounts to 2,000 messages per user per day and trial accounts to 500, according to Google's sending-limits page. Mail you send through Customermates from your Gmail account counts against those limits.
</FaqItem>
</Faq>

For Microsoft 365 mailboxes, the same questions apply; see the [Outlook CRM integration guide](/blog/outlook-crm-integration).
