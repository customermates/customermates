# Gmail CRM Integration: The Three Types and How to Pick a CRM for Gmail

A Gmail CRM integration connects your Gmail account to a CRM in one of three ways: a sidebar extension inside Gmail, a mailbox sync that brings your mail into the CRM's inbox, or a BCC or forwarding address that copies single emails. The best CRM for Gmail is the one whose type matches where your team works, so this guide compares the types, shows how common CRMs for Gmail connect and gives you a checklist to choose.

Two things Google controls apply to every option that connects to your Google account: reading mail requires a restricted Gmail scope that the vendor must have had verified, and a Workspace admin can block the app outright. Whichever type you pick, mail sent from your account counts against Gmail's daily sending limit.

Customermates, an AI-native CRM, publishes this guide. It uses the mailbox-sync route and has no Gmail sidebar, which the Customermates section below spells out. Every vendor and Google fact in this guide was checked on 3 November 2026 on the primary pages linked in the text.

## What are the three types of Gmail CRM integration?

| | Sidebar or extension in Gmail | Mailbox sync with the CRM inbox | BCC or forwarding address |
|---|---|---|---|
| Setup | Install a Chrome extension or Gmail add-on, sign in to the CRM and to Google | Authorize the Gmail account once; the CRM reads it on its server | No install: add the CRM's address to Bcc when you send, forward replies by hand |
| What syncs | What the extension logs or what you add by clicking, often mail you send or open | Sent and received threads from the synced folders or labels, matched to contacts; replies go out through Gmail | Only the emails you copy, one by one; replies arrive only if you forward them |
| Privacy and permissions | Browser extension permissions or a Google grant; the Pipedrive Gmail add-on asks you to grant it all access to your Google account | The CRM reads your mailbox on its server, which needs a restricted Google scope the vendor has had verified; sharing rules differ per product | The CRM sees only what you send to its address; the cost is manual work and gaps |
| Typical tools | Streak, HubSpot Sales extension, Copper, Pipedrive add-on, Zoho CRM for Gmail, Nutshell, folkX | Pipedrive email sync, Nutshell, Copper, folk, Keap, Customermates | HubSpot BCC and forwarding addresses, Pipedrive Smart Bcc |

**Sidebar.** You look up the sender, log the message or create a record without leaving Gmail. Check whether logging is automatic or one message at a time, and which browser the extension needs: HubSpot's extension is built for Chrome, Streak supports Chrome, Safari and Edge, and Zoho's runs in Chrome.

**Mailbox sync.** The CRM reads threads from the account on its server, so mail nobody logged still shows up on the contact. Replies go out through the same Gmail account and appear in your Sent folder. "Two-way" means mail comes in and replies go out; whether a label or a state change travels back to Gmail differs per product. Check which folders or labels sync, who else can read a synced thread, and how threads are matched to contacts.

**BCC or forwarding.** HubSpot logs an email when you add its BCC address to the BCC line, creates the contact if none matches, and logs a reply when you forward it to its forwarding address; the combined size is capped at 40 MB. Pipedrive's Smart Bcc works on every plan, gives each deal its own address, and needs customer replies forwarded to the same address; messages must stay under 20 MB. It works with any Gmail account and asks Google for nothing, but it records only what you remember to copy.

## How do I choose a CRM for Gmail?

Work through these questions with the people who will use the CRM every day:

1. **Where does the team work?** People who live in Gmail are happiest with a sidebar. People who start from the customer record want the mailbox sync.
2. **How many people share clients?** One person can live with BCC or a free sidebar. Once two or more share clients, a sync keeps the history complete without anyone clicking "log".
3. **Do you need mail nobody logged?** Only a sync (or a sidebar that logs automatically) captures it. BCC never does.
4. **Which folders or labels sync?** Archive, spam and the all-mail folder behave differently across products.
5. **Who can read a synced thread?** Private by default, shared per thread, or open to the whole workspace?
6. **How are threads matched to contacts?** By address, by domain, or by hand?
7. **Where do replies come from?** Your real Gmail address and signature, or a relay?
8. **What does it ask Google for?** Read the consent screen. A Workspace admin may block the app, so ask IT before you buy.
9. **What does it do with Google Calendar and Contacts?** Show events, or also create them?
10. **What happens when you disconnect?** Does the imported mail stay in the CRM or get deleted?
11. **Which plan unlocks the Gmail part, for how many seats?** Several vendors put sync or templates on a higher plan than the sidebar.
12. **Do customers also write on WhatsApp or LinkedIn?** Then a CRM with one inbox for all channels saves a second tool.

A short way to read the answers: all day in Gmail with one team, pick a sidebar CRM such as Streak or Copper. An existing HubSpot account or a free start, use its extension or the BCC address. Pipeline first with email in the CRM, look at Pipedrive's email sync. Several people, several channels and the record as the home screen, pick a sync-based CRM with an inbox, which is where Customermates fits.

## What rules does Google set for every Gmail integration?

**Scopes.** Google lists `gmail.readonly`, `gmail.modify`, full `https://mail.google.com/` access, `gmail.compose`, `gmail.metadata` and `gmail.insert` as [restricted scopes](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/auth/scopes). Send-only access (`gmail.send`) and the Gmail add-on scopes for the message you have open are sensitive, and `gmail.labels` is non-sensitive. An app that stores or transmits restricted-scope data on servers must, per Google's [restricted scope verification](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/restricted-scope-verification), also complete a security assessment, renewed at least every 12 months. A mailbox sync lives on that restricted side, so ask a vendor whether it has been verified. If you build your own integration, plan for this before you write code.

**Admin controls.** In Google Workspace, an admin decides per app whether it is [trusted, limited or blocked](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/apps/control-which-apps-access-google-workspace-data). A limited app can use only unrestricted Google services, and admins can also restrict a predefined list of high-risk Gmail scopes. When a connection works for one colleague and fails for another, check these settings before you debug the CRM.

**Sending limits.** Google Workspace allows 2,000 messages per user per day and 1,500 for mail merge, and trial accounts 500 a day, with up to 500 external recipients per message, according to [Google's sending-limits page](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/gmail/gmail-sending-limits-in-google-workspace). The limits apply over a rolling 24 hours. A personal Gmail account hits its limit at more than 500 emails a day, per [Gmail Help](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/22839?hl=en). Mail your CRM sends through the account counts too.

## How does the Gmail integration work in Customermates?

Gmail is one channel of the [unified inbox](/features/unified-inbox) in Customermates Cloud. It needs a plan with messaging: Pro, Business, Max, Enterprise or a trial. Each member connects [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedAccountsPerUser]] account on Pro, up to [[commercial.entitlement.business.includedAccountsPerUser]] on Business and up to [[commercial.entitlement.max.includedAccountsPerUser]] on Max; Starter has no inbox. Details are on the [pricing page](/pricing). The reason to connect Gmail here is follow-up: every client email lands on the client record, Mate or your own AI agent drafts the reply and the next step, you review and send, and the next step appears on your dashboard.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Connect the account">Open **Settings › Channels**, click **Connect channel** and pick **Gmail**. You sign in to Google on a secure connection page and return to **Channels**. The card shows whether email, calendar or both are connected, and your history imports in the background. Use the **IMAP** entry only for mail accounts that are not Gmail or Outlook.</Step>
  <Step title="Choose the folders">On the account's **Folders** tab, every folder is on except trash, junk or spam, drafts and the all-mail folder. Switch on an archive folder if those conversations should appear.</Step>
  <Step title="Link participants to contacts">Open **Thread settings** from the chip in a conversation's header and click **Link** next to each participant. Pick an existing contact or create one. The address is added to the contact, so every conversation with it lands on that contact's timeline.</Step>
  <Step title="Decide who sees what">Your conversations are private by default; admins cannot read them either. Share a single conversation with the switch in **Thread settings**, or, on Business, Max and Enterprise, share the whole account under **Visibility** on its **Details** tab.</Step>
  <Step title="Set the signature and reply">Set the signature on the account's **Email** tab; it is added to every email from that account. Reply in the conversation, or start a new email with the **New message** icon next to a contact's address.</Step>
</Steps>

A few Gmail details from the [inbox guide](/docs/app-inbox):

- **Labels are read-only.** Outlook and IMAP conversations can be moved to another folder from the CRM. A Gmail conversation shows its labels but has no move option.
- **State is shared.** **Unread**, **Open**, **Closed** and **Spam** are one value for everyone who sees the conversation. Setting **Spam** does not report anything to Google.
- **One draft per conversation.** **Save as draft** under **More send options** keeps a message for later. Drafts cannot keep files, so attach them when you send.
- **A shared address is connected once.** If a colleague already connected `sales@` in your workspace, a second connection is refused with **Account already connected**. The owner shares it instead.
- **Sending is capped.** Customermates allows 300 sent emails a day per connected Gmail account, as listed in the [messaging rate limits](/docs/messaging-rate-limits). That is below Google's own limits.
- **Disconnecting deletes the import.** **Disconnect** removes the account and permanently deletes the conversations, messages and calendar events Customermates imported from it. A shared account disappears for your teammates too.

### Worked example: a three-person consultancy

Three consultants each connect their Google Workspace address on Pro. That costs [[commercial.price.pro.monthly.seats.3]] per month for the team. Each consultant keeps client mail private and shares the conversations that a colleague needs to see. Every client contact is linked once, so the contact page shows the full email history next to open deals.

In Customermates Cloud, each consultant can add a routine that runs when a client writes. Routines spend hosted AI credits from the owner's monthly allowance, and Pro includes [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedRoutinesPerUser]] routines per user. Under **Routines**, click **Add**, choose **When something changes** and select the **Email Received** event. Then write the instruction:

> When an email arrives from a contact linked to an open deal, add a two-sentence summary to that deal's notes. Save a reply as a draft and do not send it. Ignore newsletters and automated mail.

The last two sentences matter. A routine runs as its owner with the owner's channels, and Mate may send email without asking. If you want only drafts, the instruction has to say so. The draft waits in the conversation until the consultant sends it. Limits on runs and credits are in the [routines guide](/docs/app-routines).

To try this with your own Gmail, [start your trial](/auth/signup?job=followup): [[commercial.trial.days]] days of Pro with no credit card, enough to connect one account and link your first clients.

## How can I build my own Gmail CRM integration?

If no product fits, you connect the Gmail API to the CRM's API or webhooks with a tool such as n8n or your own code. A stage change can send a message; an incoming email can create a record; a row in Google Sheets can start either. You own the OAuth app, the error handling and Google's verification, and a workflow that fails quietly is the typical failure, so alert on errors. If your leads still live in a spreadsheet, [Google Sheets as a CRM](/blog/google-sheets-crm) covers when that is enough.

With Customermates, if you use Claude, ChatGPT or another AI client, connect it through [MCP](/docs/mcp). It can read Gmail conversations, save a draft for review and send email, using the same permissions as your account. The server tells clients to confirm before sending, but that check happens in the client, not on the server. Test how your client handles it with a draft first. External clients use your own AI provider, not hosted credits.

For deterministic automation, [webhooks](/docs/webhooks) report [[derived.webhooks.events.total]] event types, including [[derived.webhooks.events.messaging]] messaging events such as an ingested email. A separately run n8n instance can consume them through the [n8n community node](/docs/n8n), for example to post a message elsewhere when a client replies. n8n is not included with Customermates.

## Can you use Gmail as a CRM?

For one person and a few dozen clients, labels, stars and Google Contacts can carry you. Gmail has no deal stages, no deal values and no record that a colleague can open to see the [full history with a client](/blog/customer-interaction-management). The [Google Workspace Marketplace](https://workspace.google.com/marketplace/category/business-tools/sales-and-crm) lists CRM apps from other vendors under Sales & CRM, for example Streak, Zoho CRM and Insightly. Once a second person shares clients with you, keep Gmail for sending and receiving and let a CRM hold the [contact and deal records](/features/contact-management). For a wider shortlist beyond Gmail, see [how to choose a CRM](/blog/how-to-choose-crm).

<FaqItem question="Does a CRM log my Gmail emails automatically?">
A mailbox sync does: it reads the connected account on the CRM's server and matches threads to contacts. A sidebar logs what the extension tracks or what you click, depending on the product. BCC or forwarding logs only the emails you copy by hand.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How do I connect Gmail to Customermates?">
On Customermates Cloud with Pro, Business, Max, Enterprise or a trial, open **Settings › Channels**, click **Connect channel**, pick **Gmail** and sign in to Google. Conversations from the folders switched on for that account then appear in the Inbox.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Do I need IMAP or POP3 to connect Gmail?">
No. In Customermates you pick the **Gmail** entry and sign in to Google. The **IMAP** entry is for other mail accounts, and Customermates does not offer POP3.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Does Customermates have a Gmail sidebar or Chrome extension?">
No. Customermates brings Gmail conversations into the CRM instead of putting the CRM inside Gmail. You read, reply and link threads to contacts in the Customermates inbox.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can my team read my Gmail conversations?">
Not by default. Your conversations stay private until you share a single conversation or, on Business, Max and Enterprise, the whole account. A teammate's private conversations never appear for anyone else, admins included.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Does the integration sync Google Calendar?">
A connected Google account can also bring calendar events into Customermates on Pro and higher, for display only. Customermates does not create, change or book events.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How many emails can I send through Gmail per day?">
Google Workspace allows 2,000 messages per user per day on paid accounts and 500 on trial accounts. Personal Gmail accounts hit a limit above 500 a day. Mail you send through any CRM from your Gmail account counts against those limits, and Customermates adds its own cap of 300 sent emails a day per Gmail account.
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
