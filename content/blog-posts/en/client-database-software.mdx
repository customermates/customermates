# Customer and client database software: which kind fits your team

Customer database software, also called client database software, keeps one record per client: who they are, which company they belong to, what you agreed and what work is still open, findable by everyone on the team. For a [small service business](/for/professional-services) the practical answer is usually a light CRM, because it links people, companies and ongoing work without you building anything. A no-code database such as Airtable or Ninox fits when your records don't look like clients and deals. And a spreadsheet or an address book is still fine if you work alone with a short list.

Below you'll find how the terms differ, the five kinds of software that show up for this search, how a client card turns into linked records, what each free plan allows before it stops, a 30-minute test to run on any trial, and a client database set up in Customermates. Customermates publishes this guide and appears in its own labelled section. Vendor details were checked on 11 November 2026 against each vendor's own pages, linked at the end.

Client database management also needs an operating owner: decide who corrects duplicates, checks access and removes information that is no longer needed. Buying software does not assign that responsibility for you.

## Customer database, CRM database, client management: which term means what

These words overlap, and vendors use them loosely. Here is how this guide uses them, and where to go for the neighbouring topics.

| Term | What it means here | Read more |
|---|---|---|
| Customer or client database | The stored records about your clients: who they are, how to reach them, which company they belong to, what was agreed. This page. | You are here |
| CRM database | The linked data model behind a CRM: people, companies, deals, tasks and the history on each record. | [CRM database guide](/blog/crm-database) |
| Client management | Working with clients day to day: owners, next steps, conversations. As software it is called client or contact management. | [Client management software](/features/contact-management) |
| Client list or card file | A flat list or card index with one line or card per client and no links between them. | [Contact list template](/blog/contact-list-template) |

A client database is the shared place where every client has exactly one record. The question this guide answers is how much structure you need around that place.

## Five kinds of software sold as a client database

Search for customer database software and you get CRMs, database builders, address books, accounting tools with a customer list and industry suites for studios or practices. They solve different problems, so pick the kind first and the product second.

| Kind | Examples | Strong at | Where it stops | Fits when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spreadsheet | Excel, Google Sheets | Free, familiar, ready in minutes | No link between a person and their company, no history per client, copies drift once two people edit | You work alone and the list fits on one screen |
| Address book or contacts app | Google Contacts, Outlook contacts, phone contacts | Free, already on your phone and in your mail, fast to search | Built for looking a person up, not for tracking what was agreed or what is still open; no deals, no history per client | You work alone and need to reach people, not manage work with them |
| No-code database | Airtable, Ninox, Notion databases | You design the tables, fields and relations yourself | You also build and maintain the client logic; free plans cap records | Your records are properties, equipment or events rather than clients and deals |
| CRM | HubSpot, Zoho CRM, Pipedrive, Customermates | People, companies, deals and conversation history are linked from day one | Fixed core record types; paid plans are priced per user | Clients bring ongoing work, proposals or repeat business |
| Accounting or industry software | Invoicing tools, booking systems for studios, practice software | The client list is tied to invoicing, bookings or appointments | Little room for relationship history and conversations | Your client work is mostly billing or scheduling |

A common trap for small businesses: they want a simple list of clients and only find full industry suites with bookings and payments. If you need bookings, that suite is the right category. If you need to know who your clients are and what you agreed with them, a CRM or a database is the lighter tool. And if you want to see how far a well-built sheet goes first, the [Excel CRM template](/blog/excel-crm-template) and the [Google Sheets CRM guide](/blog/google-sheets-crm) are reasonable starting points. If one person only needs to look up names and numbers, the [digital rolodex guide](/blog/digital-rolodex) compares address books.

## From client card to client records: what goes where

If your clients live on index cards, in a rolodex or on one spreadsheet row each, the move to a database changes one thing: the card splits into linked records. Everything on the card keeps a home.

| On the card or row | In the database | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Company name and contact person | Two linked records: a company and a person | One company can have several people, and its history stays when a contact leaves |
| Postal address | An address field on the company or the person | Billing and site addresses can differ, and a field can be filtered where free text on a card cannot |
| Several phone numbers and emails, crossed out and rewritten | Several entries in a person's contact details | The old and the new number sit side by side instead of one overwriting the other |
| Client since, last contact | Date fields | You can sort and filter by them, for example clients not contacted for 90 days |
| Client type in the margin or a coloured tab | A single-choice field such as Segment | One spelling everywhere, so the filter works |
| Handwritten notes with dates | Notes on the record, plus the conversation history where the tool has it | Searchable and visible to the whole team |
| Quote or contract stapled to the card | A file attached to the record | You open it from the client, not from a filing cabinet |
| "Call back in March" | A date field for the next step and a view sorted by it | The list reminds you only when you open it; a date field on its own sends no reminder |

Tidy the cards before you move them: decide which spelling of each client counts, and give each person their own row. Then the import runs smoothly, companies first and people second.

## The 30-minute trial test

Feature lists look alike. These checks separate tools in half an hour, using your own data on any trial or free plan.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Import 50 real rows">Take 50 rows from your current list, including a messy one with a missing name. Watch how the importer maps your columns and what it does with the bad row: reject it, skip it, or import it broken without telling you.</Step>
  <Step title="Add a duplicate on purpose">Create a person who already exists, with the same email address. A good tool stops you or warns you. A weak one creates a second record and splits that client's history in two.</Step>
  <Step title="Link a person to a company">Open the company afterwards. It should list all its people and their open work without you building a lookup.</Step>
  <Step title="Add the fields you really filter on">For example "Client since" as a date, "Segment" as a single choice and the billing address as text. Then filter on each one. A field you can't filter on is a note, not data.</Step>
  <Step title="Search like you will in a hurry">Search by part of a surname, by an email address and by a company name. Each search should find the client.</Step>
  <Step title="Export everything and open the file">If the export drops custom fields or the link between person and company, your exit will drop them too.</Step>
</Steps>

If the team needs it, add a sixth check: give a colleague access to their own clients only and see what they can still open.

## A client database in Customermates, step by step

Customermates is an open-source, AI-native CRM built in Germany for small client-facing teams such as consultants, agencies and founders. Here is a client database for a small consultancy, set up the way the product works today. A new workspace starts without lists: on Customermates Cloud, describe your business in one sentence and Mate builds the lists, pipeline and dashboard, and on a self-hosted instance you create the lists in Data model. Then adjust them like this.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Name the records the way you talk">In Data model, open a list and change its **Name** and **Plural name** under **General**: rename Contacts to Clients and Organizations to Companies. Deals can become Projects, and Tasks can become Follow-ups. Any name works, and it applies to the whole workspace.</Step>
  <Step title="Add the fields you will filter on">On Companies, add "Segment" as a Single choice field with Retainer, Project and Former, and "Client since" as a Date field. On Clients, add "Billing address" as an Address field. Phone numbers and email addresses go into the Contact details field, which the people list from AI setup has from the start.</Step>
  <Step title="Import companies first, then people">Use More actions › Add from file with an Excel workbook (.xlsx or .xls) or a CSV file, one list per import. Import the companies, then the people sheet with a column that holds each person's company name: the wizard matches link columns by record name, and email and phone columns can be matched to the client's Contact details field. A spreadsheet can be up to 10 MB and each sheet up to 5,000 data rows.</Step>
  <Step title="Save three views">On Companies, "Retainer clients" filters Segment to Retainer. On Clients, "Untouched for 90 days" filters Updated at with Older than… 90 days. "Clients without a company" filters for clients with no linked company. Share "Untouched for 90 days" with the whole team or with selected roles, so everyone cleans up the same list.</Step>
  <Step title="Connect conversations (Pro)">On Pro, each user connects [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedAccountsPerUser]] account, such as Gmail, Outlook, LinkedIn or WhatsApp, and the [unified inbox](/features/unified-inbox) shows those threads next to the client. Starter has no inbox.</Step>
</Steps>

Clean the source files before you import. The importer is strict about a few things:

- Keep one person per row, and spell each company exactly the same way in both files: the wizard links people to companies by the company's record name.
- Write phone numbers with the country code, for example +49 or 0049. A national number such as 0621 123456 has no implicit country and is refused.
- Put the column names in the first row. A CSV file may use a comma, semicolon, tab or pipe as its delimiter, and Excel in many European regional settings writes semicolons.
- After the import, open the list of failed rows, fix the reason it names in your source file and import those rows again.

Two things the trial test above will show. Customermates lets one email address belong to several records, so it does not stop that duplicate: search for the address before you add a client. And search finds a client by name or by any email address or phone number in their contact details. The import rules are documented on the [records page of the docs](/docs/app-records).

**In Customermates Cloud, a routine for new clients.** Mate, the assistant in Customermates Cloud, uses hosted AI credits. A routine runs saved instructions on a schedule or on a record event, as the person who owns it, and Starter includes [[commercial.entitlement.starter.includedRoutinesPerUser]] routine per user. A routine triggered by **Record created** on Clients could carry this instruction:

> When a new client is added, check whether they are linked to a company. If not, find the company whose name matches the domain of their email address and link it. Never create a company and never send a message. If nothing matches, add one line to the client's notes saying so.

The last two sentences matter. Mate can send messages when an instruction asks for it, so a routine that should only tidy records has to say so. If you'd rather use your own AI client, Claude, ChatGPT and other MCP clients can read and update the same records through MCP, and that works when you self-host too.

A client database earns its keep when every client has a next step. On Pro, LinkedIn and email threads join the client record, Mate or your own AI client drafts the reply and the next step, and you review, edit and send. To try that on your real clients, [start your [[commercial.trial.days]]-day trial](/auth/signup?job=followup). No credit card.

## When Customermates is the wrong client database

- **You only need names and addresses for invoicing.** The customer list in your accounting software is enough, and Customermates has no quotes or invoicing.
- **You send newsletters.** There is no mass email in Customermates. Use an email marketing tool.
- **You take bookings or appointments.** Customermates has no booking. Look at industry software for your trade.
- **You mostly work from your phone.** There is no native mobile app.
- **Your data model isn't people and companies.** Customermates starts without lists and lets you create your own, but it is built around people, companies and deals. Equipment, properties or events with nothing to sell may fit better in Airtable or Ninox.
- **You want to self-host and keep the inbox.** Self-hosting is the AGPL core with REST, webhooks and MCP. The unified inbox and Mate are not part of it.

## Where the client data lives

A client database holds personal data. In the EU, a cloud provider that stores it for you is a processor, and [Article 28 GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) requires a contract with it, the data processing agreement. Before you import, check where the database runs, who the subprocessors are, and whether you can get all your data back out.

Ninox lists Germany as the server region on its pricing page. Customermates Cloud has its primary database configured in the AWS Frankfurt region; that is a product setting, not a contractual residency guarantee, and the subprocessors, US providers included, are on the [subprocessors page](/subprocessors). The [data processing agreement](/dpa) is public. On the way out, Customermates exports each list to an Excel workbook or JSON, up to 5,000 records per export in the app; there is no single whole-account export. Self-hosting puts the database on your own server.

## Frequently asked questions

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="What is a client database?">
A client database is the shared place where every client has one record: name, contact details, company, the work you do together and notes on what was agreed. Unlike a plain client list, its records can link to each other, so a company shows all its people and their open work. A [CRM database](/blog/crm-database) adds deals, tasks and conversation history on top.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What should a client database contain?">
Start with what you look up and filter on: name, company, phone numbers and email addresses, client since, a segment, who owns the client and the next step with a real date. Add a field only when you will filter or report on it; everything else belongs in the notes. The table in the client card section above shows where each item of a paper card goes.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What is the best free client database software?">
It depends on how many people use it and how many records you keep. HubSpot's free tools cover 2 users and 1,000 contacts, Zoho CRM's free edition 3 users, Airtable Free 1,000 records per base and Ninox Free 5 users with 5,000 records. If you can run a server, EspoCRM, SuiteCRM and the Customermates core are open source with no licence fee. Customermates Cloud has a free plan for [[commercial.limit.free.maxMembers]] person and [[commercial.limit.free.maxRecords]] records, without the inbox, Mate or routines, and a [[commercial.trial.days]]-day trial.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How do I create a client database?">
Start with two record types: people and the companies they work for. Write down the fields you will actually filter on, then clean your current list so every row is one person and every column holds one kind of value. Remove duplicates, import the companies first and the people second, and save a few views. The guide to [building a CRM database](/blog/crm-database) covers the data model in more depth.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can I use Excel as a client database?">
Yes, while one person maintains it and the list stays short. It breaks down when two people edit different copies, when one company has several contacts, and when you need the history of what was agreed. A clean sheet with one kind of value per column also makes the later move to a CRM much easier.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What is the difference between a client database and a CRM?">
A client database stores who your clients are. A CRM adds what is happening with them: deals or projects moving through stages, follow-up tasks and the history of your conversations. If you track proposals or ongoing work, you need the CRM part.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What is the best software to record customers' names and addresses?">
If names and addresses are all you need, the customer list in your invoicing software or a shared spreadsheet is enough. Choose a CRM when you also track conversations and work per client. In Customermates the address goes into an Address field, which holds one postal address and opens it in maps.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can I host a client database on my own server?">
Yes, with an open-source CRM. EspoCRM and SuiteCRM are licensed under AGPLv3, and EspoCRM runs on PHP with MySQL, MariaDB or PostgreSQL. The Customermates core is AGPL-3.0 and runs with Docker Compose and PostgreSQL 16. Self-hosted Customermates includes REST, webhooks and MCP for your own AI client, but no unified inbox and no Mate.
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
