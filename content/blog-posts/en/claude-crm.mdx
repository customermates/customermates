# Claude CRM: Connect Claude to Your CRM and Keep Control

Claude has no CRM of its own, but it can work inside yours. Through a connector based on the [Model Context Protocol (MCP)](/blog/what-is-an-mcp-server), Claude finds, creates and updates records in the CRM and acts with the permissions of the person who connected it. Close, HubSpot, Attio, folk, Breakcold, Pipedrive, Zoho CRM, Capsule and Customermates offer such a connection today; Copper has announced a read-only beta for November 2026, and Salesforce is piloting its own Claude plugin. What separates them is the plan you need on each side, what Claude can reach (records only, or the email and chat history too), whether it can delete, and where you decide which actions it may take without asking.

Below you will find a ranked table of seven CRMs with sources checked on 10 November 2026, the setup in Claude and Claude Code, the permission settings that matter and the cost for a small team. Customermates publishes this guide and appears in it as one option, in its own labelled section.

## What do people mean by a Claude CRM?

| What you mean | How it works | Fits | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Claude operates your existing CRM | The CRM vendor's MCP server or connector, added in Claude | Teams that already keep customers in a CRM | Plan requirements on both sides, missing actions such as delete |
| Claude works in the CRM's web interface | Claude in Chrome uses your logged-in browser session. [Anthropic's own example](https://academy.claude.com/use-cases/log-sales-calls-to-your-crm) matches calendar attendees to Salesforce contacts and drafts call logs | One person, occasional logging, no API setup | Slower than a connector, and it depends on the page layout |
| Claude is the CRM | Notes in a Claude project, or a Notion database that Claude updates through Notion's MCP server | One person with a short list of relationships | No shared permissions or audit history, and fields drift unless you define them |
| You build a CRM with Claude Code | Claude Code writes a custom application for you | Unusual data models, learning projects | You maintain login, backups, permissions and integrations yourself ([build or customize](/blog/customizable-crm)) |

Most search results for "Claude CRM" are about the first row, so the rest of this guide is about connectors.

## What can Claude do once it is connected to a CRM?

The tools come from the CRM, not from Claude, so the exact list depends on the vendor. Across the connectors below, the everyday work looks like this:

- Answer questions from live records: "Which deals are in the Proposal stage, and who is the contact on each?"
- Create and link records after a call: a contact, its organization, a deal and a task.
- Change fields and add notes from a transcript or from notes you paste in.
- Where the CRM holds conversations, read a thread and prepare a reply.

Claude picks the tools and the order on its own. That works well for a handful of records per request. For hundreds of rows, vendor limits apply (HubSpot's connector creates or updates 10 records at a time in bulk), and an import is the better tool.

For a week-by-week routine with prompts for research, call prep, follow-up drafts and pipeline review, see [Claude for sales](/blog/claude-for-sales).

### When is another row the better pick?

- **Close** if your team sells by phone, or if you want the CRM itself to offer a read-only scope. Customermates has no telephony, and its connection carries your role's permissions with no per-key scope.
- **Breakcold** is the closest alternative for the same job, with the inbox in MCP. It publishes a packaged Cowork Skill for Claude on its product page, which is a reason to pick it.
- **Attio** if you want a custom data model with lists and reports that Claude can query.
- **HubSpot** if your team already runs it, or needs marketing and service objects such as tickets, marketing emails and landing pages next to sales records.
- **folk** if you manage relationships in groups and want Claude to create and launch email campaigns; folk's tool descriptions tell Claude to ask for your approval before a launch.
- **Copper** if your team already runs it: watch for the beta, which is read only at first.

## How do I connect Claude to a CRM?

The steps are the same for any CRM with a remote MCP server; the ranked table gives each vendor's own wording. The [Claude connectors guide](/blog/claude-connectors) explains directory and custom connectors and which Claude plans allow them.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check who can add the connector">
    On Free, Pro and Max you add it yourself; Free allows one custom connector. On Team and Enterprise, an Owner adds it under **Organization settings › Connectors**, and then each member connects individually.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find the connector or add the server address">
    In Claude, open **Customize › Connectors**. A connector that Claude lists, such as HubSpot's, Attio's or Pipedrive's, you find by search and click **Connect**. For a server that is not listed, such as Breakcold's `https://mcp.breakcold.com/mcp/v1`, click **+**, choose **Add custom connector** and paste the vendor's MCP URL.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sign in and approve">
    The CRM opens a sign-in window. Read the access it asks for, then approve. From then on, Claude acts as you, with your role's permissions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set tool permissions">
    Select the connector under **Customize › Connectors** to see **Tool permissions**. Set read tools to **Always allow**, and leave anything that writes, sends or deletes on **Needs approval**. On Team and Enterprise, Owners can restrict a connector's actions for the whole organization, and members cannot override that.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Prove it with one read and one update">
    Ask a read-only question first, such as your open deals in one stage. Then change one field on a test record and add a note. Open the record in the CRM itself and check that the change is there.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Claude Code, including Pipedrive

Claude Code adds remote MCP servers from the terminal. For Customermates, create a key under **Settings › API keys & connectors** (**Add**, then **Standard API key**; the key is shown once) and run:

```bash
claude mcp add --transport http customermates https://<your-customermates-address>/api/v1/mcp \
  --header "x-api-key: YOUR_KEY"
```

A server that signs in with OAuth instead of a key, such as Pipedrive's or folk's, is added without the header. You then complete the sign-in with `/mcp` inside Claude Code, as [Anthropic's Claude Code documentation](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/mcp) describes:

```bash
claude mcp add --transport http pipedrive https://mcp.pipedrive.ai/mcp
```

Pipedrive's server URL comes from its [ChatGPT guide](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/mcp-chatgpt), and its Claude guide covers the Claude app only, so try a read before you rely on Claude Code with it. folk documents the Claude Code command itself, `claude mcp add --transport http folk https://mcp.folk.app/mcp`. The default scope is the current project. `--scope user` makes a server available in all your projects, and `--scope project` writes it to a `.mcp.json` file meant to be shared with your team, so keep API keys out of a file you commit. Codex, Cursor and the Gemini CLI are covered in the [CLI guide](/docs/connect-cli).

## How do I connect Claude to Customermates?

Customermates connects as a custom connector, which works on every Claude plan (Free allows one; Anthropic's help article, re-checked on 10 November 2026). Every workspace has its own URL, `<your address>/api/v1/mcp/w/<workspace id>`, with the same tools locked to that workspace. The address you open Customermates at is your own on a self-hosted install.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Copy the workspace URL">
    In Customermates, open **Settings › API keys & connectors**, click **Add**, choose **Claude** under **Quick connections**, then **Claude account**. The dialog shows the URL of your current workspace with a copy button. Connecting needs **Read access All** on the **API & Webhooks** row of your role, as the [API keys](/docs/api-keys) page does; the built-in Admin role has it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the connector in Claude">
    Open **Customize › Connectors**, click **+**, choose **Add custom connector**, name it and paste the URL. Leave authentication on OAuth: there is no key to copy. The **Add to Claude** button in the same dialog opens Claude's form with the URL filled in (Claude web and Team only).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sign in and approve">
    A Customermates window opens. Sign in, check that the consent screen names the connecting app and the host it sends access to, then click **Approve**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm the tools and test one change">
    Back in Claude, Customermates appears with its tools. Ask a read question first; `get_workspace_context` is the natural first call, because it returns your user, company, roles, connected accounts and the Knowledge Base. Then change one field on a test record. The record's **History** panel lists it, provided your role can read the audit log.
  </Step>
</Steps>

To give Claude fewer tools, append `?toolsets=records,messaging` to the endpoint URL; the [MCP reference](/docs/mcp) lists the toolsets, and the [connector guide](/docs/connect-custom-connector) has a troubleshooting table. On the Free plan, REST and MCP requests together are limited to [[commercial.limit.free.apiRequestsPerMinute]] per minute and [[commercial.limit.free.apiRequestsPerDay]] per day per workspace, and a request over the limit gets HTTP 429; paid plans and self-hosted instances have no request limit.

## Who controls what Claude writes

This is where setups differ most, and a wrong assumption here costs data.

- **The client is usually the gate.** The Customermates MCP server tells connected clients to confirm with you before they delete or send, but it does not enforce that: once Claude calls an allowed tool, the action runs. What protects you is **Tool permissions** in Claude. Capsule describes the same choice: you decide whether Claude checks with you before it makes changes.
- **Claude gets your permissions, not fewer.** A Customermates connector or API key inherits the permissions of the user behind it; there is no per-key scope. If Claude should do less than you can, connect it as a separate user with a narrower role (which takes a seat), or expose fewer tools with the toolsets parameter in the [MCP reference](/docs/mcp).
- **Sending is immediate.** If your CRM can send email or chat messages, a send call sends. Ask Claude to save a draft and send it yourself from the inbox.
- **Vendor guardrails vary.** Close asks which of three scopes to grant (read only, read and write, or read, write and delete) when you connect. Attio asks for confirmation before writes, and lets Claude delete only tasks and comments. HubSpot's connector cannot delete and applies conditional property and pipeline stage validation rules, but not association label validations. Capsule lists only create and update actions. Zoho's Data Operations server can delete, also in bulk. Breakcold's OAuth calls run as the signed-in user, and a bearer token carries the scopes and workspaces you set.
- **What Claude reads goes to Anthropic.** Tool results become part of the conversation. Anthropic publishes separate [consumer and commercial terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms); check which ones cover your Claude plan, and whether you need a data processing agreement, before you connect customer data.

## Where Customermates fits

*Disclosure: Customermates publishes this guide.* Customermates is an open-source, AI-native CRM built in Germany for small client-facing teams: consultants, agencies, other service businesses and founders. Its MCP endpoint is part of the AGPL core, so it works on every cloud plan and on self-hosted installations, with [[derived.mcp.tools.total]] tools. Through it, Claude works with the lists you set up (for example contacts, organizations, deals, services and tasks), their notes and fields, and the workspace Knowledge Base. On Pro and higher in Customermates Cloud, the same connection reaches the [unified inbox](/features/unified-inbox) with email, LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Instagram and Telegram conversations.

Four prompts that test the setup. Customermates has no preset stages, so replace the stage, organization and deal names in brackets with ones from your workspace:

1. "Show my open deals in the [Proposal] stage with their organization and weighted value." (read)
2. "On the [organization] deal, set Stage to [Negotiation] and add to its notes: budget approved on today's call, contract draft next." (update)
3. "Create a contact for Sarah Lee, link her to the [organization] organization and its deal, and create a task assigned to me to send the contract draft." (create and link)
4. "Find my latest email thread with Sarah Lee, summarize it in two lines and save a reply as a draft. Do not send it." (Pro, inbox)

The fourth prompt is follow-up in one line: Claude reads the thread on the client record and drafts the reply, and you review, edit and send it. To run it on your own Claude account, [start your [[commercial.trial.days]]-day trial](/auth/signup?job=followup). No credit card.

If you would rather not run your own AI client, Mate is the assistant inside Customermates Cloud. It uses the monthly hosted AI credits per active user that come with each paid plan, prepares summaries and reply drafts, and runs Routines: saved instructions on a schedule or on record events. In Customermates Cloud, the primary production database is configured in the AWS Frankfurt region. That is a selected setting rather than a residency guarantee, and the subprocessors are listed publicly.

Customermates is not the right pick if you need a phone dialer, email sequences or a native mobile app, or if your team runs HubSpot or Salesforce and is happy with it. In that case, use the vendor's own connector from the ranked table above.

## Frequently asked questions

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="Can Claude be used as a CRM?">
For one person with a short list, yes. A Claude project or a Notion database that Claude updates can hold contacts and notes. A team needs more than a chat history: shared permissions, defined fields and a record of who changed what. The common setup is a CRM that holds the records, with Claude as the interface on top.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can I connect ChatGPT or Claude to my CRM?">
Yes, if your CRM offers an MCP server or a vendor connector. Claude adds it as a custom connector, and ChatGPT accepts custom MCP connectors as well. Which ChatGPT plans allow write actions is up to OpenAI, so check its help center. Customermates documents the setup for both in its connector guide.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How do I connect Pipedrive to Claude Code?">
Run `claude mcp add --transport http pipedrive https://mcp.pipedrive.ai/mcp`, then open `/mcp` in Claude Code and complete Pipedrive's sign-in. Pipedrive's Claude guide covers the Claude app only, and the server URL comes from its ChatGPT guide, so start with a read-only request.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can Claude connect to Salesforce?">
Yes, through "Salesforce in Claude", the plugin from the Claudeforce partnership between Salesforce and Anthropic. Salesforce's page says it is available to select pilot customers; on 10 November 2026 it still named September 2026 for an open beta and gave no general availability date, so check the page for the current status. Claude in Chrome can also work in the Salesforce web interface through your browser session.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Does Copper work with Claude?">
Not yet as a product you can switch on. Copper's page announces an official MCP server for Claude, built and hosted by Copper, as a read-only beta for November 2026, and on 10 November 2026 it still read "coming soon" with a waitlist. Write access is planned for a later release, and Copper has not announced a price. Third-party Copper connectors exist; Copper says most of them run on an admin API key that ignores each user's permissions.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Which CRM has the best Claude integration?">
It depends on what Claude must reach. For the client conversation itself, Customermates (the publisher of this guide) and Breakcold document email, LinkedIn and chat access through MCP. Close documents the clearest permission choice, with read only, read and write, or read, write and delete scopes. HubSpot is the pick if your team already runs it. The ranked table above lists what each vendor documents.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is AI replacing the CRM?">
The interface is moving into AI clients, the record is not (see the [AI CRM guide](/blog/ai-crm)). Every connector in this guide reads and writes the CRM's own database with the user's permissions. Salesforce describes Claudeforce as putting your data to work inside Claude, governed by the permissions and business rules your company already runs on.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Do I need a paid Claude plan?">
Not for one custom connector, because Free allows one. HubSpot's connector requires Pro, Max, Team or Enterprise, and Close's organization connector library needs a paid plan too. On Team and Enterprise, an Owner adds connectors for the organization and can restrict their actions for everyone.
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
