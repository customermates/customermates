# Digital rolodex: what it should hold and which tool fits

A digital rolodex is a searchable address book you keep in an app or a file instead of a card wheel: a name, a company, contact details and a few notes on each person. For most people the contacts app on their phone is enough; a spreadsheet adds columns of your own, and a CRM such as Customermates fits when the people are clients or prospects whose conversations and follow-ups you also have to track.

This guide lists what a digital rolodex should hold, compares six ways to keep one, and shows how to move from cards or a spreadsheet. Customermates publishes it and appears in its own labelled section, including where it is the wrong choice. Third-party details were checked on 7 November 2026; the sources are linked under the comparison and at the end.

## What is a digital rolodex, and what does Rolodex mean?

The Rolodex was a rotating card file: you spun to a card, read a name and a number, and slotted new cards in alphabetical order. The name is a portmanteau of "rolling" and "index", the original was patented in 1956, and Rolodex is still a trademark ([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/language_corner/rolodex-history.php)). In search, "digital rolodex" simply means the same job done in software: find a person fast and see why you know them.

Do people still use a Rolodex? Some keep a card file on the desk, but the list that matters usually lives in a phone, an email account or a spreadsheet today. What has lasted is the habit: one place for the people you may need, with enough context to remember why.

Software can do three things a card wheel could not. It finds a person by any detail (name, company, phone number, email address), it travels with you, and it can be backed up and moved to another tool when you outgrow it.

## What should a digital rolodex hold?

Start with what you would actually look up, and keep one row or card per person.

| Field | What to store | Why it earns its place |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Name and company | Full name, company, role | The role tells you why this person is in the list at all. |
| Contact details | Phone numbers in international format, email addresses, a LinkedIn or other profile | People have several of each; storing only one loses the rest. |
| How you know them | Where you met, who introduced you | The detail you will not remember in two years. |
| Last contact | The real date of the last conversation | Shows who has gone quiet. |
| Next step and date | An agreed action with a real date, or blank | Turns an archive into a list you act on. |
| Notes | What they work on, what you promised | Short and factual; you will read it before a call. |
| Group | One or more labels such as client, supplier, former colleague | Lets you pull up everyone in a group. |
| Address | A postal address, only if you actually post things | Optional for most people. |

Leave out what the work does not need. If your rolodex holds other people's personal data for business, the data protection principles apply: a specific purpose, only the data that purpose needs, accurate and current entries, and no longer than necessary ([European Commission, data protection principles](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/principles-gdpr_en)).

A phone address book holds the first two rows well and the rest only loosely. A spreadsheet or a CRM holds all of them. The [contact list template](/blog/contact-list-template) gives you these columns as a blank CSV.

## Is there a Rolodex app for iPhone?

Rolodex is a trademark of the card file, so the useful search is for a contacts or personal CRM app, not for that name. The simplest one is the Contacts app on the iPhone itself: with iCloud turned on for Contacts, it keeps your contacts up to date on every device signed in to the same Apple Account ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/icloud/set-up-contacts-mm2e5b1c6686/icloud)). Each contact has a notes field you open with Edit ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/edit-contacts-iph89a9c71d8/ios)).

If you want reminders to keep in touch, personal CRM apps go further. [Dex](https://getdex.com/) lists web, iOS, Android, macOS and Windows apps, keep-in-touch reminders and a timeline of past interactions. Customermates has no iPhone or Android app: it runs in the browser, and the pages rearrange on narrow screens, so you can look someone up in your phone's browser.

## Which digital rolodex should you use?

Six options cover almost every case. The Customermates row is our own product; the rest are described by what their vendors state.

| Option | Cost model | Sharing | Notes and history | Import and export |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Phone contacts (iPhone, Android) | Comes with the phone | Your own devices through an Apple or Google account | A notes field per person; no follow-up list | Built in |
| Google Contacts | Comes with a Google account | Your own devices and account | A notes field and labels to group people | Imports CSV or vCard, exports CSV or vCard |
| Spreadsheet (Excel, Google Sheets) | The software you already use | One shared file; copies drift when several people edit | Whatever columns you add; history is what you write in a cell | CSV and workbook files in and out |
| Notion template | Free for one person; a team on the Free plan gets a limited number of blocks and moves to a paid per-member plan | Share pages with other members | Notes and properties as the template defines them | Depends on the template; test an export first |
| Personal CRM app (Dex) | Paid subscription | Built around one person's network; Dex also lists a Team plan | Notes, custom fields, groups, reminders, interaction timeline | Pulls contacts from email, calendar, LinkedIn, WhatsApp and phone contacts |
| Business CRM (Customermates) | Cloud per user per month, billed monthly; self-hosted core without a licence fee | Everyone in the workspace works on the same records, roles decide who sees which list | Notes, linked companies, dates, files; conversations from Pro | Import from Excel, CSV or JSON; export to Excel or JSON |

Google's help pages name two limits for Google Contacts: do not import more than 3,000 contacts at once, and an account has a limit of 25,000 contacts. Notion's pricing page describes Free as the plan for individuals. Checked on 7 November 2026 against [Google Contacts import](https://support.google.com/contacts/answer/15147365), [Google Contacts export](https://support.google.com/contacts/answer/7199294), [Google Contacts labels](https://support.google.com/contacts/answer/30970), [Notion pricing](https://www.notion.com/pricing) and [Dex](https://getdex.com/).

How to choose:

- **One person, names and numbers:** use the phone contacts or Google Contacts. Adding a second system makes you maintain two lists.
- **You want your own columns:** a spreadsheet works while one person edits it. The [Excel CRM template](/blog/excel-crm-template) and the [Google Sheets CRM guide](/blog/google-sheets-crm) show how far a sheet goes before it strains.
- **You already live in Notion:** a template is a free start for one person. Check sharing and export before you rely on it.
- **You want keep-in-touch reminders on your phone:** a personal CRM app with native apps is built for that.
- **Clients, a team and conversations:** a business CRM. The [client database software guide](/blog/client-database-software) compares the kinds of tools you will meet.

## How do you move from cards or a spreadsheet to a digital rolodex?

<Steps>
  <Step title="Sort the pile first">Keep the people you could realistically contact again. Merge duplicates now; every duplicate you import is a duplicate you clean later.</Step>
  <Step title="Choose six to eight fields">Take them from the table above. One row per person, one kind of value per column, and the first row holds the column names.</Step>
  <Step title="Type or capture the cards">Type the details from your cards into the sheet. Customermates does not read business cards: a photo attached to a File field is stored and shown, but its text is not read and not searchable.</Step>
  <Step title="Write phone numbers with the country code">Write +44 and the number, or 0044 and the number. It keeps numbers callable from anywhere, and Customermates refuses a national number without a country code.</Step>
  <Step title="Import ten rows first">Google Contacts takes CSV or vCard files. Customermates takes Excel, CSV or JSON through More actions, Add from file, with a wizard to map columns, up to 5,000 data rows per sheet. An import cannot be undone, so check the ten rows, delete them if the mapping is wrong, then import the rest.</Step>
  <Step title="Export a backup on a schedule">Google Contacts exports CSV or vCard from More actions. Customermates exports each list as an Excel workbook or JSON, up to 5,000 records per export. Keep a dated copy somewhere you control.</Step>
</Steps>

The [records documentation](/docs/app-records) covers import mapping, the Contact details field and export in detail.

## When is a CRM overkill for a digital rolodex?

A CRM is more than you need when:

- you are the only user and the list is names, numbers and emails;
- you mostly look people up on your phone, because Customermates has no native app;
- you want birthday or keep-in-touch alerts, because Customermates sends no reminders;
- the list is a one-off, such as wedding guests or a club, and not an ongoing relationship.

You have outgrown the phone book when:

- you keep a second list of who to call this week;
- more than one person edits the same contacts;
- email, LinkedIn or WhatsApp conversations should sit next to the person;
- you need to filter by company, relationship or city.

If two or more of the second list ring true, try a CRM against a copy of your data before you commit. The [contact management features](/features/contact-management) show what a CRM adds on top of an address book.

## Customermates as a digital rolodex (our product)

Customermates is an open-source, AI-native CRM built in Germany. A new workspace starts without lists, so you create a People list in Data model (or, in the cloud, let the AI setup during onboarding build one from your description). Then:

- **Contact details:** this field holds several phone numbers, email addresses and social profiles per person. Search finds a person by any of them, and a phone number there can be called.
- **Notes, dates and groups:** add a Formatted text field for notes, a Date field such as Next check-in, and a Multiple choice field for groups. Customermates has no record tags; a multiple choice field is the equivalent.
- **Who is due:** save a view sorted by Next check-in, and one with Updated at older than 90 days for people who went quiet. A list with a Date field also shows as a calendar.
- **Addresses:** an Address field is checked against the country's address format when saved. The cloud has no address search, so you type the address.
- **Conversations (Pro and higher, cloud):** conversations from a connected email or LinkedIn account appear on the person's record in the [unified inbox](/features/unified-inbox) when their address or profile matches.
- **Moving in and out:** import Excel or CSV, export Excel or JSON. The [personal CRM setup](/for/personal) walks through a full network in Customermates.

<FaqItem question="Do people use Rolodex anymore?">

<FaqItem question="Is there a free digital address book?">

<FaqItem question="Can I share a digital rolodex with my team?">
Yes, but not every option is built for it. Phone contacts and personal CRM apps follow one person's account. A shared spreadsheet drifts into copies. A business CRM gives everyone the same records with roles, which is what Customermates does in a workspace.
</FaqItem>

No. A rolodex answers who someone is and how to reach them. A CRM adds what is happening with them: next steps, deals or projects, conversations and shared ownership. Start with the rolodex and add the CRM when you need those.
