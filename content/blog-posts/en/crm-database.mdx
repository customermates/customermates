# CRM database: what it is and how to build one that stays clean

A CRM database, short for customer relationship management database, is the structured store behind a CRM: one record per person, one per company, one per deal, the links between them, and the history of what happened. The links are what separate it from a contact list. Open a company and you see its people, its open deals and the last conversation; open a deal and you see who is involved and what was offered.

You rarely build that database from scratch. You pick a CRM, decide which record types and fields you need, clean your existing data and import it in the right order. This guide covers that design work, shows how four CRMs model their data, spells out how Customermates models the same lists, and ends with a worked example and the export path. Customermates publishes this guide; other vendors' details come from their own documentation, linked at the end.

## What a CRM database holds

Think of it as a handful of tables that point at each other. Most CRMs ship the same core set and differ in what they add around it.

| Record type | What one record is | Typically linked to | Fields worth having |
|---|---|---|---|
| Person (contact) | One human you talk to | Their company, their deals, tasks, messages | First and last name, email, phone, role, how you met |
| Company (organization, account) | One business or institution | Its people and deals | Name, segment, website, billing address |
| Deal (opportunity) | One piece of potential or ongoing business | The people and companies involved, products or services sold | Stage, value, expected start, source |
| Product or service | Something you sell at a price | The deals that include it | Name, price |
| Task or activity | Something someone has to do or did | Any of the above | Assignee, notes |

Two design questions decide whether the database holds up later. Can one person belong to several companies (a consultant who sits on two boards, a freelancer working for three agencies)? And can one deal involve several people? If your CRM forces one company per person or one contact per deal, you will start writing workarounds into notes. Read the documentation before you commit: Pipedrive's data article describes linking a person to one organization, while in Customermates a relationship can allow one or several records on each side, so a contact can belong to several organizations.

The other half of a CRM database is history. Every change to a record and every linked email or message forms a timeline. That timeline is what lets a colleague take over a client without a handover meeting, and it is the part a spreadsheet cannot give you.

## CRM database examples: how four CRMs structure their data

The record types tell you what a CRM was built for. This table compares the data model only, not price or quality.

| CRM | Standard record types | Your own record types |
|---|---|---|
| [HubSpot](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/understand-objects) | Contacts and companies as the foundational objects, plus deals, leads, tickets, products, projects, tasks and many more | Custom objects, which need an Enterprise subscription |
| [Salesforce](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/data_modeling/objects_intro) | Standard objects such as Account, Contact, Lead and Opportunity | Custom objects "to store information that's specific to your company or industry" |
| [Pipedrive](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/how-is-pipedrive-data-organized) | Leads, deals, people, organizations, activities, products, projects and mail | The linked article names no custom object type; Projects is an add-on on Growth and lower plans and standard on Premium and Ultimate |
| Customermates | Contacts, organizations, deals, services and tasks, each freely renamable | Your own lists with typed fields, relationships and calculated fields |

The pattern: HubSpot and Salesforce let you add your own object types and configure them, on HubSpot with an Enterprise subscription. Pipedrive's data items stay close to the sales process. Customermates starts without lists: you create the ones you need and name each one, so a contacts list can read Clients and a deals list Projects across the whole workspace, and you can rename them later.

## How Customermates models the core lists

Customermates keeps CRM data in configurable lists, and no list is built in. On Customermates Cloud the AI workspace setup designs the lists from your answers; a self-hosted workspace starts empty, and people who manage the data model create the lists in Data model. The setup always creates a people list and Tasks, usually Organizations and Deals, and a catalogue of services or products only when you sell them. A typical result looks like this, as described in the [data model documentation](/docs/concepts).

| List (default name) | One record is | Typically linked to | What to know |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contacts | One person | Organizations, deals, tasks | A Contact details field holds their email addresses, phone numbers and social profiles and matches incoming messages to the record |
| Organizations | One company or account | Contacts, deals | Any fields you add, such as segment or client since |
| Deals | One opportunity | Contacts, organizations, services | The stage is a Single choice field with a win probability per stage; the value is the sum of its line items |
| Services (or Products) | Something you sell | Deals, through line items | A unit price; a line amount is quantity times unit price |
| Line items | One line on one deal | Its deal | A sub-list: it belongs to one deal, is edited inside it and is removed with it |
| Tasks | One piece of work | The records you link it to | A due date or status is an ordinary field you add |
| Your own lists | Anything else: Projects, Applications, Properties | Whatever you link | The same field types, relationships and saved views as every other list |

A relationship connects two lists. Each side has its own label and allows one or several records, so one contact can belong to several organizations and a project can link several contacts and deals. Both records show the link. On Pro, where connected accounts bring in messages, a relationship can also show the messages of the people it links on the deal or organization.

Fields can be calculated. A deal's value adds up its line amounts, and the weighted value is the deal value times the stage probability divided by 100, which is pipeline weighting, not a forecast. Every record keeps a stable ID, so renaming a list or a field breaks no references, and a saved view shows records without copying them.

## CRM database, spreadsheet or plain database?

All three can hold customer data. The question is who maintains it and how much of the logic you want to build yourself.

| Choose | When | You give up |
|---|---|---|
| A spreadsheet | One person, a short list, no shared history needed | Links between people and companies, a per-client timeline, safe editing by several people |
| A plain database or no-code tool (Airtable, Notion, Access, PostgreSQL) | Your records are not people and deals, or you need a model no CRM offers | You build the forms, relations, permissions and history yourself |
| A CRM | Several people work with the same clients and you track deals, follow-ups or conversations | Some freedom in the data model; most cloud CRMs charge per user |

The same jobs look different in a sheet and in a CRM database:

| The job | In an Excel sheet | In a CRM database |
|---|---|---|
| Link one person to two companies | A second row or two names in one cell, so you search twice | One person record linked to both companies, where the CRM allows several; the link shows on both records |
| Keep a segment value consistent | A free-text column, unless you build and maintain a dropdown | A Single choice field with fixed options; rename an option in Customermates and every record follows |
| See what happened with one client | Whatever someone typed into a notes column | A history of changes and linked messages on the record |
| Let two people work at once | One shared file, a version to merge or a lock | Each person edits records in place, with access set per list and action |
| Report on the numbers | Formulas and pivot tables you build and keep up | Dashboard widgets that sum or count records and group them by a field, such as deal value by stage |

If a sheet is where you are now, the [Excel CRM template guide](/blog/excel-crm-template) shows how far one goes and how to lay it out so the later import is easy. For choosing between the kinds of client database tools, see [client database software](/blog/client-database-software).

## How to build a CRM database

These steps apply to any CRM. The order matters more than the tool.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Write down the record types you need">Start with people, companies and deals. Add a separate type only when it has its own lifecycle. A product catalogue does; a meeting location does not.</Step>
  <Step title="List the fields you will filter or report on">If nobody will ever filter on a field, it belongs in the notes. For each field pick a type: date, currency, email, phone, link, or a single choice from a fixed list. A "Segment" field as free text ends up with "Retainer", "retainer" and "Ret." within a month.</Step>
  <Step title="Define your deal stages and what moves a deal">Five to seven stages with a written exit rule each, for example "Proposal: a written proposal has been sent". Stages without exit rules turn into opinions.</Step>
  <Step title="Pick one unique key per person">Usually the email address. It is how you find duplicates before import and how the CRM matches incoming email to the right person. HubSpot, for example, deduplicates contacts by email address and companies by domain name.</Step>
  <Step title="Clean the source data">One person per row, one kind of value per column, split full names into first and last name, remove duplicates by email, and delete people you no longer have a reason to keep.</Step>
  <Step title="Import companies first, then people, then deals">Each later import can then link to records that already exist, matched by name. Test with 50 rows before the full file.</Step>
  <Step title="Save the views people work from">"My open deals", "Clients without a company", "Not touched in 90 days". A database nobody looks at decays quietly.</Step>
  <Step title="Name an owner for data quality">One person who runs the monthly check below. Without an owner, cleanup is everyone's job and therefore nobody's.</Step>
</Steps>

## When data does not fit contacts, companies or deals

This is the most common design problem, and there are four honest answers.

1. **It describes an existing record.** A contract end date, a client segment, a LinkedIn URL. Add a typed custom field to that record type.
2. **It is something you sell.** Put it in the product or service catalogue and link it to deals, so the deal value follows from what is in it.
3. **It is text you read but never filter.** Meeting notes, background, preferences. Use the notes field.
4. **It has its own lifecycle and relations.** Properties, vehicles, candidates, events. That is a custom object. HubSpot offers them on Enterprise, Salesforce on its platform, and no-code databases such as Airtable are built around them. In Customermates you add it as your own list in Data model, with its own fields and relationships.

## Worked example: a CRM database for a four-person consultancy

Here is the same design in Customermates, set up the way the product works today. Customermates is an open-source, AI-native CRM built in Germany for small client-facing teams such as consultants, agencies and founders. A new workspace starts without lists: on Customermates Cloud, describe your business in one sentence and Mate builds the lists, pipeline and dashboard, and the steps below also work by hand in Configure.

**Lists.** In Data model, open Contacts and change **Name** and **Plural name** under **General** to Client and Clients, then rename Deals to Projects the same way. Any name works. Organizations, Services and Tasks keep their names.

**Fields.** Each field has a type: Text, Formatted text, Number, Money, Yes or no, Date, Date and time, Date range, Date and time range, Single choice, Multiple choice, Web address, Member, Address, File or Phone, email & social.

| List | Field | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Organizations | Segment (Retainer, Project, Former) | Single choice |
| Organizations | Client since | Date |
| Clients | Role | Text |
| Clients | LinkedIn profile | Web address |
| Projects | Engagement period | Date range |
| Projects | Source (Referral, Inbound, Event) | Single choice |

**Relations.** A client can link to several organizations, and a project to several clients, organizations and assigned users. A project's value is the sum of its line items, each a service price multiplied by its quantity, so "Strategy workshop" at one price and "Monthly retainer" times six add up on their own.

**Stages.** Stages are not preset: you add a Stage field (for example Prospecting, Qualification, Demo, Proposal, Negotiation, Won, Lost) and set win probabilities per stage in Data model. The weighted pipeline is the plain arithmetic of those percentages, not a prediction.

**Unique key.** Email addresses go into the client's Contact details field, which matches incoming messages to the record. One address can belong to several records, so remove duplicates before you import. Global search (Cmd/Ctrl+K) finds a client by name or by any email address or phone number in their contact details.

**Import.** Use More actions › Add from file with an .xlsx workbook or a .csv file, one list at a time, in the order organizations, clients, projects. Link columns are matched by record name. A CSV file can be imported directly, with no conversion. The [records documentation](/docs/app-records) has the full import rules.

**Views.** Save "Not touched in 90 days" by filtering Updated at with Older than… 90 days, and "Clients without an organization". Share "Not touched in 90 days" with the whole team or with selected roles, so everyone works from the same list.

**Cost.** Four people on Starter pay [[commercial.price.starter.monthly.seats.4]] per month. On Pro, which adds the [unified inbox](/features/unified-inbox) so each user's connected email, LinkedIn or WhatsApp account shows its conversations next to the client, it is [[commercial.price.pro.monthly.seats.4]] per month. Billing is monthly, and your first workspace starts with Pro free for [[commercial.trial.days]] days without a credit card. Self-hosting the open-source core has no licence fee but no inbox and no Mate.

**A routine for data quality (Customermates Cloud).** Mate, the assistant in Customermates Cloud, uses hosted AI credits. A routine runs saved instructions on a schedule or on a record event, as the person who owns it; Pro includes [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedRoutinesPerUser]] routines per user. A weekly routine could carry this instruction:

> Every Monday, list clients created in the last 7 days that have no linked organization or no email address, and list organizations with no Segment value. Report the lists in the run summary. Do not change any record and do not send any message.

The last sentence matters. Mate can send messages when an instruction asks for it, so a routine meant only to report has to say so. If you prefer your own AI client, Claude, ChatGPT and other MCP clients can query the same records through [[derived.mcp.tools.total]] [MCP tools](/docs/mcp), on Cloud and self-hosted.

A database earns its keep when each record has a next step. On Pro, the threads join the client record, Mate or your own AI client drafts the reply and the next step, and you review, edit and send. To try that on your own records, [start your [[commercial.trial.days]]-day trial](/auth/signup?job=followup).

## Keeping a CRM database clean

Contact data goes stale as people change jobs and companies merge. A short routine with a named owner beats a yearly cleanup project.

| How often | Check | In Customermates |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | New people without a company, new deals without a stage | Saved views, or the routine above |
| Monthly | Records not updated in 90 days: still relevant? | Filter Updated at, Older than… 90 days |
| Monthly | Free-text values that should be choices | Add a Single choice field, then set it on up to 100 selected records at once with Edit |
| Quarterly | People you no longer have a reason to keep | Delete them from the list or the record page, then from Trash |
| Before any big change | A copy of what you have | Export each list as Excel or JSON; there is no single whole-account export |

An exported workbook can be edited and imported again as **Existing records**, which updates them instead of creating duplicates. That makes bulk corrections practical without an API.

## How to export a CRM database

Plan the way out while you design the way in. You need an export for backups, for bulk corrections in a spreadsheet and for the day you change tools, and you should test it on a small list before you rely on it. In Customermates, every list has the same options under **More actions**, and an export only contains what your role can read.

| You want to | Do this | Good to know |
|---|---|---|
| Edit many records in a spreadsheet | **Export as Excel (.xlsx)** on the list | One sheet per list and one column per field you can read; search, filters and sorting apply; up to 5,000 records |
| Keep a backup of a list, links included | **Export as JSON** | The file includes links for re-import with **Add from file**, as **Create with exported IDs** or **Update matching IDs** |
| Re-import a large JSON export | Use the REST API or MCP | The app rejects a JSON file with more than 100 records or 500 links; the API exports pages of up to 500 and imports 100 records per request |
| Move to another CRM | Export each list as Excel and import in the order organizations, people, deals | Customermates has no whole-account export; check the new tool's import rules (Pipedrive, for example, documents importing people, organizations and deals from CSV or XLS files) |

The [records documentation](/docs/app-records) lists every import and export limit.

## Personal data in a CRM database

In the EU, a CRM database of people is personal data under the [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj). Three principles from Article 5 translate directly into database design: keep only data that is "limited to what is necessary" (fewer fields), keep it accurate (the cleanup routine), and limit how long you store it (the quarterly deletion). Article 17 gives people the right to have their data erased, so you need to be able to find and delete one person completely. Article 28 requires a contract with any provider that stores the data for you.

For Customermates Cloud, the [data processing agreement](/dpa) is public, the application database is configured in the AWS Frankfurt region, and the [subprocessors](/subprocessors), US providers included, are listed. Customermates holds no compliance certification. If you need the data on your own server, [self-host the open-source core](/features/self-hosted).

## When Customermates is the wrong database

- **You need field-level permissions.** Permissions apply per list and action, not per field.
- **You need one-click export of everything.** Export runs one list at a time, as Excel or JSON.
- **You send newsletters from your customer list.** There is no mass email; use an email marketing tool.

## Frequently asked questions

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="What is a CRM database?">
It is the store behind a CRM: records for people, companies, deals and tasks, linked to each other, with typed fields and a history of changes and conversations. The links and the history are what make it more than a contact list.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What data does a CRM database store?">
Four kinds. Who a person or company is: names, email addresses, phone numbers, addresses, roles. What you sell and to whom: deals, stages, values, products or services. What happened: emails and messages, notes, tasks and changes to the record. And what you add yourself, such as a segment, a source or a contract end date. Typed fields keep the first and last kinds searchable and countable.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What is the difference between a database and a CRM?">
A database stores whatever tables you design and leaves the forms, relations, permissions and history to you. A CRM is a database with that customer logic already built: people linked to companies and deals, pipeline stages, assignees and a timeline per record. You trade some freedom in the data model for not having to build it.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is a customer database the same as a CRM database?">
They overlap. A customer database is the list of customers and what you know about them; a CRM database adds the links between people, companies and deals, the pipeline and the history behind a CRM. If you only need a client list and are choosing a tool for it, see [client database software](/blog/client-database-software).
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is Excel a CRM database?">
No. Excel can hold a customer list, and for one person with a short list that is enough. It has no links between people and companies, no history per client and no safe way for several people to edit at once. A clean sheet with one kind of value per column still makes a good starting point for an import.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What are the four types of CRM?">
The usual split is operational (running sales, service and marketing work), analytical (reporting on customer data), collaborative (sharing customer information across teams) and strategic (organizing the business around long-term customer relationships). All four use the same CRM database; they differ in what you do with it.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How do I store data that doesn't fit contacts, companies or deals?">
If it describes an existing record, add a typed custom field. If it is something you sell, use the product or service catalogue. If it is only read, use notes. If it has its own lifecycle and relations, you need custom objects, which HubSpot offers on Enterprise and Salesforce on its platform; in Customermates you add your own list for it.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How much does a CRM database cost?">
A spreadsheet or a self-hosted open-source CRM has no licence fee, but you pay in setup and maintenance time. Cloud CRMs mostly charge per user and month. In Customermates Cloud that is [[commercial.price.starter.monthly]] per user on Starter and [[commercial.price.pro.monthly]] on Pro with the unified inbox; see [pricing](/pricing).
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
