# Customizable CRM: What You Can Change, and When to Build Your Own

A customizable CRM is a ready-made CRM in which you change the data model, the pipeline and the views yourself, without writing code. For most small teams that is enough: custom fields, your own deal stages and saved views cover how a consultancy, agency or service business actually works. Building your own CRM only pays off when you need record types no product offers, and then the realistic options are a CRM with custom objects or a no-code database, rather than code written from scratch.

The deciding question is narrow. Do you need new kinds of records, or new fields on the usual ones? The rest of this page helps you answer that before you pay for a plan.

## What "customizable" covers, layer by layer

Vendors use the word for very different depths. Split it into layers and test each one in a trial:

| Layer | What it means | How to test it in a trial |
|---|---|---|
| Custom fields | New fields on existing records: a date, a currency amount, a dropdown | Add the five fields you would really fill in. Check that filters and sorting work on them. |
| Pipeline and stages | Your own stage names, more than one pipeline, stage probabilities | Rebuild your current sales steps. See whether a stage is a fixed system field or something you define. |
| Naming | Calling deals "Projects" or contacts "Clients" | Rename and check the new label appears in lists, forms and exports. |
| Views | Saved filters, sorting, grouping, board or table | Save three views. Find out whether colleagues can see them or only you. |
| Custom objects | New record types with their own fields and relations, such as "Property" or "Contract" | Ask which plan includes them and how many. This is the most common upsell. |
| Automation and access | Triggers on field changes, API, webhooks, roles | Fire a webhook on a changed field. Read and write one record over the API. |

Two layers decide most purchases: custom fields and custom objects. Field types matter more than field counts. A dropdown keeps data consistent; free text drifts as soon as several people type into it.

## Where vendors put custom objects

Custom objects are the clearest dividing line between plans. These are the vendors' own statements:

| Product | Custom objects | Source |
|---|---|---|
| HubSpot | Available with Enterprise subscriptions (Sales Hub, Marketing Hub, Service Hub and other Enterprise hubs) | [HubSpot knowledge base](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/object-settings/create-custom-objects) |
| Zoho CRM | Custom modules on Enterprise and Ultimate; not on Free, Starter, Standard or Professional | [Zoho CRM edition comparison](https://www.zoho.com/crm/comparison.html) |
| Attio | Free up to 3, Plus up to 5, Pro up to 12, Enterprise unlimited | [Attio pricing](https://attio.com/pricing) |
| Twenty | Unlimited on Pro, Organization and Enterprise; the open-source core can be self-hosted | [Twenty pricing](https://twenty.com/pricing) |
| EspoCRM | Entity Manager creates custom entities, fields and relationships; open source | [EspoCRM features](https://www.espocrm.com/features/) |
| Pipedrive | The cited help page covers custom fields on leads, deals, people, organizations, products and projects; the number of fields depends on the plan | [Pipedrive support](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/custom-fields) |
| Customermates | None. Five fixed record types with custom fields on each | [Customermates data model](/docs/concepts) |

Vendor details checked on 8 October 2026 against the linked official pages. Plans change, so confirm the tier you need on the vendor's site before you buy.

If you need a "Property" record that links to several owners and tenants, or a "Contract" record with its own lifecycle, choose a product from the upper rows. If your business is people, companies, opportunities and the services you sell, custom objects are usually a plan upgrade you don't need.

## Adapt a product, use a no-code builder, or build your own

| Choose | When | What you take on |
|---|---|---|
| A customizable CRM | Your work is people, companies, deals and follow-up, plus fields specific to your trade | Living with the vendor's record model and its plan boundaries |
| A CRM with custom objects | You need one or two extra record types with relations, such as properties or contracts | A higher plan with most vendors; more setup and more to keep tidy |
| A no-code database (Airtable, Knack, Caspio and similar) | Your data model is genuinely unusual and changes often | You design and maintain everything: permissions, email capture, import, the views your team uses |
| Code from scratch | The CRM is your product, or it must sit deep inside your own software | Authentication, roles, email and calendar sync, search, audit history, import and export, backups, security updates, for as long as it runs |

Nobody can give you an honest fixed price for a custom CRM without a specification. The cost that people underestimate is not the first version; it is the years of maintenance, because every feature a rented CRM ships becomes your backlog. Before you commission a build, write down the record types your current tool cannot represent. If the list is empty, you need a better-configured CRM, not a new one.

A middle path has opened up: start from a CRM with a full API and let an AI client do the custom work against it. With an open-source CRM that exposes REST, webhooks and an MCP server, a small script or an AI client can cover the one workflow that is specific to you, while the vendor maintains everything else.

## A worked setup in Customermates

Here is how a five-person consultancy would shape Customermates to its process. Every step is configuration, no code.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Rename the record types">
    Under **Data model** in company settings, choose preset names: Contacts become **Clients**, Deals become **Projects**, Services become **Packages**. Free-text names are not possible; the presets are listed in the [company settings docs](/docs/app-company#how-do-i-rename-record-types-in-the-data-model). The API and MCP keep the original names.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Define the stage field">
    A new workspace starts with a **Stage** column on deals. Edit its options to match your sales steps, for example Enquiry, Scoping call, Proposal sent, Signed, Lost. Give each option a weight, and every deal gets a weighted value from those fixed percentages, summed into a weighted pipeline total.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add custom fields where they drive a decision">
    On Projects: **Engagement type** (Single Select: retainer, fixed scope, workshop), **Day rate** (Currency), **Contract renewal** (Date). On Clients: **Source** (Single Select) and **Direct line** (Phone). Ten field types are available, listed in the [data model docs](/docs/concepts#what-are-custom-columns).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the views each person works from">
    A board of Projects grouped by Stage, a table of retainers sorted by **Contract renewal**, and a list of Clients grouped by **Source**. Saved views are personal, so each colleague sets the same filters and saves their own view.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Bring existing data in">
    Export each sheet from your old tool as an Excel workbook and import one record type at a time, Clients first, then Projects, mapping columns to your new custom fields. The [records docs](/docs/app-records#how-do-i-import-or-export-records) list the file limits.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Automate one step (Customermates Cloud)">
    A Routine with the trigger "deal updated", watching only the **Stage** field and filtered to Proposal sent: "Draft a short follow-up to the main client contact referencing the proposal. Save it as a draft, do not send." Routines run Mate, use hosted AI credits and run as their owner. Without the "do not send" line, a routine may send the message itself. See [Routines](/docs/app-routines).
  </Step>
</Steps>

Connected email, LinkedIn and WhatsApp conversations appear next to these records in the [unified inbox](/features/unified-inbox) on Pro and higher. Starter has no inbox. If you work with Claude, ChatGPT or another AI client, it reads and writes the same records, including your custom fields, through [MCP](/docs/mcp) with [[derived.mcp.tools.total]] tools.

## What Customermates does not let you customize

These limits matter if you are comparing customizable CRMs:

- **No custom objects.** You get contacts, organizations, deals, services and tasks. A "Property" or "Contract" record type is not possible. Choose Twenty, Attio, EspoCRM or an Enterprise plan elsewhere if you need one.
- **Ten field types, no number field.** Text, date, date range, date and time, date and time range, currency, single select, link, email and phone. There is no plain number, multi-select, checkbox or formula field.
- **Renaming uses presets.** Deals can become Opportunities, Projects or Jobs, not any word you type.
- **Permissions stop at the record type.** Roles control read and manage access per record type, including "only records assigned to me". There are no field-level permissions.
- **Saved views are personal.** There are no shared team views.
- **No tagging.** Use a Single Select field instead, which also keeps values consistent.
- **Tasks have no built-in due date.** Add a Date field and sort by it; nothing alerts you when that date passes.
- **Import and export are Excel workbooks**, one record type at a time. There is no whole-account export.

For self-hosting, the open-source core includes records, custom fields, views, roles, REST, [webhooks](/docs/webhooks) and MCP. The inbox, Mate and Routines are cloud-only. Details are on the [self-hosted page](/features/self-hosted).

## Keeping a customized CRM usable

Customization fails more often through too many fields than too few. Three rules help:

1. Add a field only when someone filters, sorts or decides on it. "Might be useful" fields stay empty.
2. Prefer Single Select over free text for anything you will group by later.
3. Every quarter, delete fields that nobody has filled in. In Customermates, deleting a field removes its values for good, so export first if in doubt.

## Frequently asked questions

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="What is the difference between a customizable CRM and a custom CRM?">
A customizable CRM is a finished product that you adapt with fields, stages, views and automation, and the vendor maintains it. A custom CRM is software built for you, with code or a no-code platform, and you maintain it. Customizable is faster and cheaper for most small teams; custom only pays off when no product can represent your records.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can I build my own CRM?">
Yes. A no-code database like Airtable, Knack or Caspio can give you a working first version quickly if your process is clear. Coding one from scratch is a long project, because you also build login, roles, email sync, search, import and backups. If your records are people, companies and deals, configuring an existing CRM gets you further for less.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How much does a custom CRM cost?">
It depends on the specification, so be wary of any fixed figure. Count the maintenance, not just the first build: security updates, hosting, backups and every feature you would otherwise get from a vendor. For comparison, five users on Customermates Pro cost [[commercial.price.pro.monthly.seats.5]] a month, or [[commercial.price.pro.monthly.seats.5.months.12]] over twelve months, and other vendors publish their prices on their pricing pages.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can I build a CRM in Excel?">
For one person and a few hundred contacts, yes. It breaks down when several people edit at once, when you need links between clients and projects, or when conversations should sit next to the record. Our [Excel CRM template guide](/blog/excel-crm-template) shows a workable layout and when to move on. Customermates imports Excel workbooks, one record type at a time.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Which CRM offers the most flexible data model for custom objects?">
Among the products checked here, Twenty includes unlimited custom objects on its Pro, Organization and Enterprise plans, Attio allows up to 12 on Pro and unlimited on Enterprise, EspoCRM creates custom entities in its Entity Manager, and HubSpot and Zoho CRM reserve custom objects for Enterprise tiers. Customermates has no custom objects.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can I keep my CRM data on my own server?">
Yes, with a self-hostable CRM. Customermates, Twenty and EspoCRM can all run on your own infrastructure. With Customermates, the self-hosted version runs with Docker Compose and includes custom fields, views, REST, webhooks and MCP, but not the inbox, Mate or Routines. See the [open-source CRM guide](/blog/open-source-crm) for the trade-offs.
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
