# CRM Comparison: Features, Prices and Real Cost Side by Side

Comparing CRMs by feature list alone misleads, because almost every CRM has contacts, a pipeline and some automation. What separates them is the plan on which a feature starts and what that plan costs your team. The charts below show both for eight CRMs, using each vendor's own pricing page, followed by a two-year cost calculation and a scorecard for your final two candidates.

Vendor facts were checked on 8 October 2026. Customermates publishes this comparison and is one of the eight; it is described in its own labelled section. For a shortlist sorted by use case, read [the best CRM software](/blog/best-crm-software).

### The cost formula for your own shortlist

List price is one of five lines. For each candidate, fill in:

1. **Subscription:** price of the plan you need × users × months.
2. **One-time fees:** onboarding or setup packages the vendor lists.
3. **Migration:** hours to clean and import your data × an internal hourly rate.
4. **Administration:** hours per month someone spends on fields, users and automations × rate × months.
5. **Add-ons:** AI credits, calling minutes, extra API volume or connectors you will buy.

An example of line 3: 20 hours of cleanup at €80 per hour adds €1,600 to whichever system you pick. That is why the cheapest subscription is not always the cheapest system. Simple systems need fewer hours on lines 3 and 4, and that difference is the honest answer to "ease of implementation". More on budgets in [what a CRM costs](/blog/crm-cost).

## The three types of CRM

Most guides sort CRMs into three types, and some add a fourth:

- **Operational CRM** runs daily sales and service work: contacts, deals, tasks, conversations. Every product in this comparison is operational at its core.
- **Analytical CRM** turns the records into reports, segments and forecasts. In small-team tools this is usually a reporting module, not a separate product.
- **Collaborative CRM** shares customer context across people and departments, such as a shared inbox or a timeline every colleague can see.
- **Strategic CRM**, the fourth type some sources name, is about long-term customer development rather than a software category.

For a small team the type matters less than the plan. You need an operational CRM, and you check which analytical and collaborative parts come with the plan you can pay for.

## Score your final two

A chart narrows the field. A scorecard settles it. Use it on two candidates, with your own data.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Write five to seven must-haves">
    Each one should be testable, for example "deals move by stage on a board", "LinkedIn messages appear on the contact" or "export a record type with one click".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Weight each criterion from 1 to 5">
    5 for the things that would make you leave, 1 for nice extras. Agree on the weights before anyone opens a trial.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Run both trials on the same twenty deals">
    Import a real slice of contacts, organizations and open deals, then work one normal week in each system.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Score 1 to 3 per criterion, separately">
    1 is missing, 2 partly works, 3 works. Each daily user scores alone; average afterwards.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Multiply, add, then check the cost">
    Weighted score next to the two-year cost from the formula above. If the scores are close, take the cheaper system or the one with the easier exit.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A worked example with made-up scores, so the arithmetic is clear:

| Criterion | Weight | System A | System B |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pipeline by stage | 5 | 3 (15) | 3 (15) |
| Email and LinkedIn on the contact | 4 | 1 (4) | 3 (12) |
| Workflow automation | 3 | 3 (9) | 2 (6) |
| Export and API | 2 | 2 (4) | 3 (6) |
| **Weighted total** | | **32** | **39** |

## FAQ

<Faq>
<FaqItem question="What is the number one CRM platform?">
By market share, Salesforce. On 4 June 2026, Salesforce reported that IDC's Worldwide Semiannual Software Tracker (April 2026) gave it 20.0% of the CRM market in 2025, its 13th consecutive year in first place. Market share describes the vendor, not whether its plans fit a team of five.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What are the top 5 CRM systems?">
There is no neutral top five. Every list reflects its publisher, this one included. The one measured ranking cited here is market share, where IDC puts Salesforce first. For a small team, a better question is which two systems have your must-haves on a plan you can pay for: use the feature chart to find them, then the scorecard to decide.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How do I compare CRMs on ease of implementation and total cost of ownership?">
Use the five-line cost formula above: subscription, one-time fees, migration hours, monthly administration hours and add-ons. Ease of implementation shows up in lines 3 and 4. Measure it in the trial by importing twenty real deals and timing the setup, instead of trusting a vendor's estimate.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What are the four types of CRM?">
Operational, analytical and collaborative are the three most guides name. Some add strategic CRM as a fourth, which describes long-term customer development more than a kind of software. Every product compared here is an operational CRM with reporting and sharing on top.
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Which CRM is free?">
HubSpot Free Tools cover up to 2 users, the Zoho CRM free edition 3 users, and Salesforce lists a Free Suite. Pipedrive and monday CRM have no free plan. Customermates has no free hosted plan; its AGPL core can be self-hosted without a licence fee, without the inbox and Mate. See the [free CRM guide](/blog/free-crm).
</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can I switch CRM later?">
Yes, if you test the exit before you sign. Export one record type in the trial and check the format, whether export works per object or for the whole account, and whether there is an API. Customermates exports one record type at a time as an Excel workbook and has a REST API; there is no whole-account export.
</FaqItem>
</Faq>
