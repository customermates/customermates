Customermates is an open-source, AI-native CRM. This page documents the **Dashboard** screen of the web app for humans and for AI agents that drive the UI. Route: `/dashboard`, locale-prefixed like `/en/dashboard`, sidebar id `#nav-dashboard`.

## What does the Dashboard show, and can I share widgets with my team? [#what-does-the-dashboard-show]

The Dashboard is the landing screen after sign-in. It shows configurable widgets over your CRM data: pipelines, task counts, recent activity, whatever you set up. Every widget belongs to you as a user; teammates see it only after you share it with **Share as a template**, which lets them start their own copy from it. Adding widgets needs no role permission, but a chart counts only the records your role can read (**Read access** **Assigned** or **All**), so a teammate's copy of your template can show other numbers. **Add widget** appears only if your role can read at least one record type; the **Activity timeline** kind needs read access to the **Audit Log** or, on a plan with messaging, to **Inbox messages**.

**Link:** the **Dashboard** page, `/dashboard`. **Mate:** `navigate` and `highlight_element` with `nav-dashboard` or `dashboard-add-widget`; inside the editor `widget-modal-kind` (the two kinds on step 1), `widget-modal-save` (**Create widget** on step 2, **Save changes** when editing) and `widget-modal-reset` (**Reset**, when editing with unsaved changes) are targets too, while the tabs, switches and widgets are not, so Mate names them.

## Which widget types exist, and which charts can they show? [#which-widget-types-exist]

Press **Add widget** (`#dashboard-add-widget`) to choose one of two widget types, a **Chart**, which aggregates one record type into bars, a doughnut or a radar, or an **Activity timeline**, or a template a teammate shared:

- **Chart**: choose a configured source type, an aggregation (count, sum, average, minimum or maximum), and a numeric value field when needed. This works for the five starting types and customer-created types. Group by a compatible field, by each record, or through a configured relationship path. For example, sum Deal Value by Stage, or sum line-item amounts by Service. The source defines the contribution grain: equal amounts on different records both count, and a deal linked to two organizations can contribute fully to both groups. Source filters limit contributions; group filters limit the displayed groups. Calculated values require access to their inputs unless an administrator has published the summary. To plot a time series, group by a date or date-and-time field, **Created at** or **Updated at** and choose a **Time interval**: day, week (starting Monday), month, quarter or year. Date-and-time values are bucketed in the time zone of the browser that saved the widget, while date-only values never shift; records without a date are counted below the chart instead of on the time axis. Group by **Assigned to** to compare assignees: a record with two assignees counts for both. The **Appearance** section offers bar, labelled bar, doughnut and radar styles plus **Number** (one overall value, which needs **No grouping**), **Area over time** (needs a time interval; empty periods show zero for counts and sums and a gap for averages, minimums and maximums), **Ranked table** (groups by value with their share of the total, the top ten plus a count of the rest) and **Funnel** (a single-choice field such as a stage, in option order, with the conversion from each step to the next; a stage with a probability of 0, such as Lost, is listed below the funnel instead of counting as a step). A style that does not fit the current grouping is disabled with a hint.
- **Activity timeline**: a newest-first feed of changes, messages and connected-account activity. Its filters belong to that widget, so several timeline widgets can show different views without changing each other or the timeline on a record page.

Over MCP, `manage_widgets` lists, reads, creates, updates and deletes charts and timelines. A chart uses `measure` (source, aggregation, value field, filters and grouping) and `displayOptions`; `isTemplate` controls sharing. A time series sets `groupBy.dateInterval` and an IANA `groupBy.timeZone`, and `displayOptions.displayType` must fit the grouping. Chart and timeline writes require the current `expectedRevision` and an `idempotencyKey`; updates also require the saved `expectedVersion`. Read the widget before updating: a supplied measure or display-options object replaces that complete object. Activity timelines use `activityQuery` and `showFilters`. The widget kind is immutable. Creates and updates accept an optional `layout` (`x`, `y`, `w`, `h` on the 12-column desktop grid) saved in the same call, so an assistant can build an arranged dashboard: without it a new widget takes the first free spot and an updated one stays where it is; a position that overlaps another widget is refused, and widgets float up into empty rows above them. `list` returns each widget's current layout. The tool supports the same configuration and appearance options as the app. See the [MCP catalog](/docs/mcp#tool-catalog) for exact input schemas.

**Link:** the **Dashboard** page, `/dashboard`. **Mate:** `navigate` and `highlight_element` with `dashboard-add-widget` for **Add widget**, then `widget-modal-kind` for the two kinds once the editor is open; the template choices are not highlight targets, so Mate names them.

### What can an activity timeline show, including calendar events? [#what-can-an-activity-timeline-show]

An activity timeline widget shows entries from three categories. The **Record changes** category contains audit-log changes. **Messages** contains Inbox messages. **Account & calendar** contains connected-account activity, such as accepted LinkedIn connections, and calendar events. Customermates has no calendar page of its own: on Customermates Cloud, events from the calendar of a connected Gmail or Outlook account appear in this category, and `get_calendars` reads them over MCP.

All accessible records are the implicit baseline. Filter a timeline by category, provider, connected account, conversation, or related contacts, organizations, deals, services, and tasks. Each field can be configured once. Relationship filters use the same operators as record lists: **in** and **not in** accept one or more records, while **has some** and **has none** need no value. Different filter fields are AND-combined, and multiple values within one **in** filter are OR-combined. Unlinked entries fail positive relationship filters and pass negative ones. Filters become persistent when you save the widget.

Entries are shown newest first. Use **Load older** for the next bounded page. A row can show one primary record, up to three related records, and a remaining-record count. Permitted record links open the relevant record, and message details can open the permitted Inbox thread. Rows without a CRM association remain visible without a record link.

**Link:** the **Dashboard** page, `/dashboard`. **Mate:** `navigate` and `highlight_element` with `dashboard-add-widget` for **Add widget**, then `widget-modal-kind` for the **Activity timeline** kind once the editor is open; the timeline filters and rows are not highlight targets, so Mate names them.

### Who sees what in a timeline?

| Access | Visible content |
| --- | --- |
| Audit log only | Permitted changes |
| Inbox messages only, on a plan with messaging | Permitted messages, connected-account activities, and calendar events |
| Both permissions, on a plan with messaging | One merged timeline containing all permitted sources |
| Neither | A non-sensitive unavailable state |

The two permissions are the **Audit Log** and **Inbox messages** rows of the member's role under **My Company › Roles**, each with **Read access** set to **All**. Audit-log changes are available independently of the messaging entitlement. Messages, connected-account activities, and calendar events require cloud mode, Inbox access, and a plan with messaging (Pro and above); on Starter, **Inbox** shows **Upgrade to unlock messaging** with an **Upgrade your plan** button that opens **My Company › Subscription**. A missing connected messaging account removes messaging-based sources but does not hide permitted audit changes. Deleted or newly inaccessible filter values stop matching, are shown as **Filter not available** on the filter chip and **Selection unavailable** in the value picker for safe removal, and never expose their raw ids.

**Link:** the **Dashboard** page, `/dashboard`, and for the role rows `/settings/roles`. **Mate:** `navigate` and `highlight_element` with `nav-dashboard` or `nav-settings-roles`; the timeline and the role rows are not highlight targets, so Mate names them.

## How do I create or edit a chart or timeline widget in the widget editor? [#how-does-the-widget-editor-work]

Adding a widget starts with **Choose a Widget**, where every option is a card of the same shape. **Recommended for your data** (`#widget-gallery-heading`) comes first: starters built from the record types and fields your workspace actually has, such as the open total of a currency field, a funnel over a pipeline stage, a value per month over a date field (or the creation date), the top related records by value, or open items per assignee. A starter appears only when the model has its ingredients, so a type without a currency or number field gets no value starter, and the list changes when your data model changes. **Start from scratch** (`#widget-modal-kind`) offers Chart and Activity timeline, followed by shared templates (`#widget-template-heading`) when available. A starter opens in the same editor, so you can change it before and after saving. The editor shows the settings (name, data, filters, appearance and sharing) next to a **Live preview** (`#widget-preview-heading`) that renders the widget with real data at the size it will take on the dashboard and refreshes as you change the settings; the refresh button (**Preview measure**) reloads it on demand. On narrow screens the preview follows the settings. Record filters appear under `#widget-entity-filters-heading`; timeline filters sit with the timeline source. The footer offers **Back** and `#widget-modal-save` (**Create widget**). Editing uses the same layout with the existing definition loaded.

To change a widget, click it, or click one of its filter chips to open the editor at that filter. The editor is titled **Edit** plus the widget's name; `#widget-modal-save` reads **Save changes**, and `#widget-modal-reset` (**Reset**) appears once you change something and restores the saved values. The trash icon **Delete widget** in the editor's header removes the widget after a confirmation. A new widget takes the first free spot with a size that suits its chart style. Drag a widget to move it and drag an edge or corner to resize it; moving is off on touch devices. The layout is saved for you only.

**Link:** the **Dashboard** page, `/dashboard`. **Mate:** `navigate` and `highlight_element` with `dashboard-add-widget`, then `widget-modal-kind` on step 1, then `widget-modal-save` (**Create widget** / **Save changes**) or `widget-modal-reset` (**Reset**) once the editor shows them: **Create widget** after the user picks a kind (`list_ui_targets` gives `widget-modal-kind` as its prerequisite), **Reset** after the user has clicked a widget (`a widget card`); the tabs, **Back**, **Delete widget** and the widgets themselves are not highlight targets, so Mate names them.

## Which controls does the sidebar add?

The sidebar is the same on every screen; its controls are documented here because the Dashboard is where you land.

Entries sit in two groups: **Overview** (**Dashboard** and **Knowledge Base**, plus **Inbox** and **Routines** on Cloud) and **Data** (accessible configured record types, including customer-created types, under their workspace labels, plus **Configure** (`#nav-configure-records`, `/configure`) for members who may change the data model). Settings are not in the sidebar: the workspace name at the top opens the workspace menu (`#nav-workspace-menu`) with **Invite members**, **Members**, **Settings**, **Billing** with the plan badge and, for operators, the **Operator Console**; your avatar at the bottom opens the personal menu (`#nav-personal-menu`) with **Profile & preferences**, **Theme**, **Language**, **Documentation**, **Send feedback**, **Customize sidebar** and **Sign Out**. Inside **Settings** the sidebar shows the settings pages instead, grouped under **Account** (`#nav-settings-profile`, `#nav-settings-channels`, `#nav-settings-api-keys`) and **Workspace** (`#nav-settings-members`, `#nav-settings-roles`, `#nav-settings-billing`, `#nav-settings-activity`, `#nav-settings-webhooks`), with **Back** (`#nav-area-back`) returning to the page you came from. An entry your role cannot read is hidden, a group with no visible entries disappears, and opening a hidden page by its URL brings you back to the Dashboard. Mate can navigate to each settings page your role can open, and highlight its entry while Settings is open. Three places show a number: **Inbox** counts unread conversations, the tasks entry shows members with **Edit** on **Users & Roles** how many **Approve User** tasks are open, and **Channels** counts channels that need attention.

Each person can arrange their own sidebar; nobody else sees the change. Drag an entry to reorder it or to move it into another section, or open its **…** menu (also by right-click or a long press) for **Move up**, **Move down**, **Move to section** (including **New section**) and **Hide from sidebar**, which also works by keyboard and on touch screens. Click a section title to collapse or expand it; the **…** menu of a section (also by right-click) moves it, and personal sections can be renamed or deleted. Deleting a personal section asks first and lists where each of its entries goes: back to its usual section, hidden entries stay hidden. **Customize sidebar** in the personal menu shows hidden entries again, lists your personal sections with **Rename** and **Delete section**, creates a **New section**, and offers **Reset to default** (after a confirmation) once you have changed anything. New lists always appear under **Data**.

## What happens on first run?

New accounts land in the [onboarding wizard](/docs/app-onboarding) at `/onboarding/wizard` before they see the Dashboard. It has four steps: your profile, Knowledge Base, team invites, and connecting an AI client. The owner can skip website import, invitations, and an AI connection; invited members see only their profile. Only the invite step carries the stable ids `#onboarding-next` and `#onboarding-back`.

**Link:** the onboarding wizard, `/onboarding/wizard`, until onboarding is finished; afterwards the **Dashboard** page, `/dashboard`. **Mate:** the wizard controls are not highlight targets; website setup displays the read-only conversation inline. On the Dashboard, `navigate` and `highlight_element` with `nav-dashboard`.

## Tips for agents

- The hosted assistant can navigate here and highlight `#nav-dashboard` and `#dashboard-add-widget`, and inside the widget editor `#widget-modal-kind`, `#widget-modal-save` and `#widget-modal-reset` (see the Mate lines above for when each is shown); it creates widgets through `manage_widgets` instead of driving the modal.
- Open a record at `/records/<typeId>/<recordId>`, with the locale prefix. Use the list’s **Add** control or the sidebar type picker to create a record.
- Prefer [MCP](/docs/mcp) tools over clicking when a tool exists. Reading and writing CRM data is faster and safer through tools than through the UI.
- `#nav-search`, `#nav-workspace-menu` and `#nav-personal-menu` can be highlighted but are not navigation targets. `#nav-add`, `#nav-assistant`, the two chips and the menu items are not assistant targets.
