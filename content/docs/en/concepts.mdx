---
title: "CRM data model: records, links, and calculations"
description: "How lists, fields, relationships, deal totals, permissions, widgets, and webhooks work together."
---

Customermates keeps CRM data in configurable lists. A workspace starts with Contacts, Organizations, Deals, Services and Tasks and can add its own, such as Projects or Applications, without a release. Every record keeps a stable ID, so renaming a list or field breaks no references. A saved view shows records of a list without copying them.

## Which record types exist, and which does a workspace start with? [#which-five-record-types-exist]

| Starting list | Purpose | Starts with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Contacts](app:records/contact) | People | Name, channels, notes, assignments |
| [Organizations](app:records/organization) | Companies or accounts | Name, notes, assignments |
| [Deals](app:records/deal) | Sales opportunities | Name, line items, calculated value and quantity, optional weighted value |
| [Services](app:records/service) | Offerings or catalog items | Name, live unit price |
| [Tasks](app:records/task) | Work items | Name, assignments, status; membership-approval tasks keep protected actions |

Starting lists are ordinary lists: you can extend or change their fields, relationships and layout like any list you create. To match messages to records, add a **Channels** field (emails, phones and profiles) to a list; one identifier can match several records. Ordinary email and phone fields do not take part in matching.

## How is a deal's total value calculated? [#how-is-a-deals-total-value-calculated]

A deal's **Line items** are a sub-list of Deals: each line belongs to one deal, is edited inside it, shares its access and is removed with it. Each line has a service, a quantity, a pricing mode and an effective unit price. **Line amount = quantity × effective unit price**. The deal value is the sum of the line amounts, the deal quantity the sum of the quantities. The same service can appear on several lines. Live pricing follows later changes to the service price; saved pricing keeps the captured price. Every money field carries its own currency, and amounts in different currencies are never silently added.

The total is a configured calculation, so a schema manager can change it or turn it into a manual value after previewing the affected records and widgets. A missing input, a value you may not see, zero and a calculation error are different states. See [weighted pipeline](#how-does-a-weighted-pipeline-work).

## How do relationships link records, for example a contact to several organizations? [#how-do-relationships-link-records]

A relationship connects two lists. Each side has its own label, and each side allows one or several records, so one contact can belong to several organizations and a project can link contacts and deals. Both records show the link. In the record form a relationship is a field: pick the records, then click **Save**. On a list's [configure page](app:configure/contact) you add one with **Add** → **Relationship**, or by dragging from one list to another in [Configure](app:configure). The relationship drawer also has one switch per direction to show the linked records' messages on the other list.

The app checks list types, cardinality, duplicates and what happens on deletion. A lookup or rollup over several linked records needs an aggregation such as sum or count.

## What are fields, and which field types are there? [#what-are-custom-columns]

Every list starts with some fields, and you can add your own; tables show them as columns. Field types are Text, Formatted text, Number, Money, Yes or no, Date, Date and time, Date range, Date and time range, Single choice, Multiple choice, Email, Phone, Web address and Member. Text, email, phone and web address fields can **Allow several values**; email, phone and web address fields can open or copy their value on click. Choose this under **On click**: **Open** (the default) starts an email or a call or opens the link, and **Copy** copies the value. A copy button always sits next to the value, and clicking empty space in a table cell still edits it. Choice options keep a stable ID when you rename them. Switching a Single choice field to Multiple choice keeps every value; switching back works only while each record holds at most one option.

A field is entered by hand or calculated: a formula, a lookup through a relationship, a rollup over linked records, or a snapshot taken at a configured event. Formulas use a checked expression language and never run arbitrary code; cycles, mismatched types and overly expensive definitions are rejected. Fields can be used in filters, sorting, views, widgets, routines and Mate, as far as access allows.

## How do I add, change or delete fields, including the deal stage field? [#how-do-i-add-change-or-delete-a-custom-column]

Open [Configure](app:configure), which shows all lists as a graph, and open a list, for example [Contacts](app:configure/contact). Its **Add** menu creates a field, a calculated field, a relationship, a **Channels** field or a sub-list, each in its own drawer. The deal stage field lives on the [Deals configure page](app:configure/deal). Before a change is applied it previews the affected records, views, widgets, routines and formulas, and changes that would break something are blocked until you resolve them.

To delete a custom field, a list or a relationship, use **Delete** in its drawer or in the page's menu. The confirmation lists the references it removes automatically (view filters, columns, sorts, widget filters) and blocks only on things that would compute wrong, such as calculations and routines. Deleted items move to **Recently deleted** in Configure, where you can **Restore** them or **Delete permanently** after typing the name; a permanent deletion also removes the stored values, and for a list its records and links.

Names are unique: list names, field names within a list, relationship labels per side and option labels per field, ignoring case and accents. Managing the data model is its own permission, separate from editing records; it grants neither record access nor role management.

## How does a weighted pipeline work, and how is the weighted value calculated? [#how-does-a-weighted-pipeline-work]

**Weighted deal value = deal value × stage probability / 100**. The stages are a Single choice field whose options each carry a probability from 0 to 100 percent. A 2,600 deal at 60 percent contributes 1,560; a zero-percent stage contributes zero, and a missing probability leaves the weighted value empty. A changed live service price recalculates dependent values; a saved quote price does not.

A schema manager edits the stages, their probabilities or the formula on the [Deals configure page](app:configure/deal), and the preview shows the effect on existing deals and widgets. People who cannot read every input of a calculation see a restricted value instead of the total, and the formula itself is shown only to people who can read all its inputs.

## What are widgets? [#what-are-widgets]

A chart widget on the [Dashboard](app:dashboard) picks a list, an aggregation and a value field, optional filters, a grouping and a display style, for example the sum of Deal value by Stage or the count of Projects by Owner. Each record counts once at the list it comes from; a deal linked to two organizations counts fully in both groups, so grouped totals can exceed the overall total. Service charts count line items. Widgets follow the same access rules as lists. Activity widgets show a feed instead of a number. See [Dashboard](/docs/app-dashboard).

## What are webhooks? [#what-are-webhooks]

A [webhook](app:settings/webhooks) or a routine can subscribe to record events of one or more lists, optionally filtered by fields. Deliveries are queued after the change is saved. A failed delivery is retried with the same event and delivery identity, so a receiver can see one event more than once. Access is checked again at send time, and past deliveries are redacted for the person viewing them. See [Webhooks](/docs/webhooks).

## What are notes, and are they markdown? [#what-are-notes]

Notes are formatted text on a record, stored as a checked document rather than a markdown string. Wherever notes appear as plain text or markdown, that is a conversion of the same document, not a second copy.

## Who are users, roles, and the company, and what does Assigned read access mean? [#who-are-users-roles-and-the-company]

Members are the people in a workspace. [Roles](app:settings/roles) grant create, read, edit and delete per list. Read access **All** covers every record of the list, **Assigned** only records assigned to that member, **None** nothing. Editing and deleting also need access to the specific record. New lists start without access. Every search, calculation, export, widget and Mate answer applies the reader's access.

## For agents [#for-agents]

Read list and field IDs with `discover_record_types` and `get_record_model`. Every record reference carries both `typeId` and `recordId`. Link or unlink records with `mutate_crm_record` using relation IDs from `get_record_model`. Notes are written as a typed rich-text field value. `configure_record_model` previews and applies a schema change with an expected revision and an idempotency key; a stale revision needs a fresh preview.
