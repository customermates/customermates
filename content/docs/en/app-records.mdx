Every list in your workspace, such as Contacts, Deals or Projects, works the same way. On Customermates Cloud the AI workspace setup creates your first lists, and people who manage the data model add more in [Configure](app:configure); see [which lists a workspace has](/docs/concepts#which-five-record-types-exist). A view only changes how records are shown; it never copies them.

## What do a list and a record look like? [#what-is-the-shared-layout-of-a-record-type]

Click a row or card to open the record page. The row's actions menu holds **Open in new tab**, **Open details** and **Delete**; **Open details** opens the record in a side drawer instead. Open the menu with the **···** button that appears when you hover the row, by right-clicking the row, by a long press on touch screens, or with Shift+F10 (or the context-menu key) on a focused row. Page and drawer switch between **Overview** and **Activities**. A Formatted text field (for notes, descriptions or summaries) shows its text in the overview at its place among the fields: click the text to edit it in place, press Esc or click elsewhere to finish, and long text collapses behind **Show more**. Tables show a one-line excerpt, and search finds words in it. The form is one column with collapsible sections. While nothing has changed it shows no buttons; once you change something, a floating **Unsaved changes** bar offers **Cancel**, which discards your changes, and **Save** (Cmd/Ctrl+Enter also saves). The form for a new record keeps its buttons at the bottom.

**Delete** moves the record to Trash together with its sub-list rows, such as line items, and with records that are deleted along with it. It disappears from lists, search, totals, widgets and connected tools at once. **Undo** in the message that follows brings it back, and so does **Restore** in Trash for 30 days. After that it is deleted permanently, and its values are erased from history, keeping only who changed it and when.

On **Overview**, **Customize** lets you pin the fields you want to see first, hide fields or reorder them by dragging; the pin buttons appear only there, and **Done** ends it. Pinned fields show as chips at the top of the record. These choices are yours alone. Values can also be edited directly in table cells and on board cards, except calculated and read-only ones, which show a lock. You only see the fields and values your role allows.

## Where do I find a list, and how do I add a record? [#how-do-i-add-a-record]

Pick the list in the sidebar, for example your tasks list. To add a record, open its list and click **Add** in the toolbar, for example on your contacts list to add a contact. **Add** in the sidebar, or C, first asks which kind of record to create. You need create access to the list. New lists are created in [Configure](app:configure) by people who manage the data model; for them, **Add** in the sidebar also offers **Create list**.

## Which controls does each list have? [#which-controls-does-each-list-have]

Every list has the same toolbar: **Ask Mate**, **Filters** (see [How do I filter a list in the app?](/docs/filter-syntax#how-do-i-filter-a-list-in-the-app)), **Appearance**, the **More actions** menu and **Add**. To search a list, open **Filters** (or press F), type the text and choose **Name contains “…”**; the search shows as an active filter you can remove. **More actions** holds **Export as Excel (.xlsx)**, **Export as JSON** and **Add from file**; people who manage the data model also find an entry such as **Configure Deals** there, which opens the list in [Configure](app:configure).

## How do I import or export records? [#how-do-i-import-or-export-records]

Open **More actions** in the toolbar of the list, for example on your contacts list.

- **Export as Excel (.xlsx)** or **Export as JSON** downloads what the list shows, with search, filters and sorting applied, up to 5,000 records: Excel with one sheet per list and one column per field you can read, JSON including links for re-import. The API: `POST /api/v1/records/export` with `format` `xlsx` or `json`.
- **Add from file** opens **Import records**: **Choose file**, pick a JSON export of the same list, choose **Create with exported IDs** or **Update matching IDs**, then **Import records**. Files from another list, from before a structure change, or with more than 100 records or 500 links are rejected. This needs create access.
- An Excel workbook starts a wizard: pick the sheet, map columns, check the preview, choose **New records** or **Existing records**, then import. Failed rows are listed with their reason.

## How do saved views work, and can I share one with my team? [#how-do-saved-views-work]

A saved view remembers filters, sorting, grouping with its summaries, layout, page size and columns; a search stays for your browser session and is not saved with the view. Without a view and without changes, the list shows every field in its configured order as a table, with Formatted text fields hidden, ungrouped and unsorted. Views are personal: the chips below the top bar show your saved views, **New view** saves the current setup, and once you have a view, V opens **Switch view**. Click the selected view again, or choose it again in **Switch view**, to go back to the list without a view. A view's menu edits, duplicates, moves, deletes it or copies its link. The link carries the view's filters, sorting, grouping, layout, page size and columns, not the view itself: you open your own link as the view, and a colleague sees that setup as unsaved changes, leaving out lists and fields they cannot read, and **Save** keeps it as their own view without changing yours. **Ask Mate** opens Mate with the view attached, without sending anything yet.

Changes to filters and **Appearance** stay temporary until you save them: a dot marks the selected view's chip, or the view menu next to the chips when no view is selected, and **Filters** and **Appearance** offer **Save** and **Reset**. They last for the browser session. Column widths are saved at once.

## What is the difference between the lists, and where do phone numbers and email addresses go? [#what-is-special-about-each-record-type]

Lists differ only in their fields, relationships and calculations; the ones the AI workspace setup creates are ordinary lists too. Field types include **Single choice** and **Multiple choice** (colored chips) and **Web address** fields. The field drawer (**Add**, then **Field**, or an existing field in Configure) sets what clicking their value does under **On click**: **Open link** (the default) opens the link in a new tab, and **Copy link** copies the value and confirms that it was copied to the clipboard. A value that is not a valid link shows as plain text. A copy icon next to each value appears on hover or keyboard focus, and in a form the input ends with open and copy buttons. In table cells and on board cards, clicking the empty space next to the value edits it inline.

Phone numbers and email addresses go into **Contact details**, a field of the type **Phone, email & social** that holds a record's phone numbers, email addresses and social profiles, which is also how messages find their record. The field shows them in three groups, **Email**, **Phone** and **Social**; type or paste a value into **Add phone, email or profile** to add one. A phone number is stored in international format and can be called, a WhatsApp chat from the same number appears on the record by itself, and with a connected WhatsApp account **New message** writes to the number on WhatsApp. A number that came from WhatsApp keeps its WhatsApp icon; one number is one entry, however it arrived. The people list from the AI workspace setup has a Contact details field from the start; to add one to another list, open the list in [Configure](app:configure) and choose **Add**, then **Contact details** (one per list). Otherwise it behaves like any field: you can rename it, drag it to another place in the field order, pin or hide it on the record, show it as a table column or on board cards, and edit it in place on the record. You can filter a list by it with **contains**, **Starts with**, **is empty** and **is not empty**; sorting and grouping by it is not possible. One identifier can belong to several records. Its **On click** setting decides what clicking an identifier does: **Open email, call or profile** (the default) starts an email or a call or opens the profile, and **Copy** copies the identifier; a copy icon is always next to each identifier. A deleted Contact details field stops matching and waits in **Trash** for 30 days, where **Restore** brings it back and **Delete permanently** removes the links between the records and their identifiers.

Earlier versions had **Phone** and **Email** field types. Every phone and email field moved into the list's Contact details field: each number became a phone entry of its record, each email address became an email entry in lowercase, an address the inbox already knew, for example from Gmail or Outlook, was reused, and a list without a Contact details field got one. Since then, a record whose former email field held an address also shows the inbox conversations with that address. A field that could not move, for example one in Trash or in a sub-list, a calculated one, one that names the records, one a calculation, routine or webhook uses, or one with a value that is not a valid phone number or email address, became a **Text** field with its values. Saved views, layouts and widgets followed the move: columns, column widths, the detail field order and starred fields now show the Contact details field, the filters **contains**, **Starts with** and **is not empty** moved to it, **is** became **contains**, and other filters, sorts and groupings on a moved field were removed. A Contact details field still named **Channels**, the former default name, was renamed **Contact details**. Record history still shows the former fields, as text. When you import an Excel workbook, columns such as **Email**, **Phone** or **Mobile** go into the Contact details field if the list has one. Over REST and MCP, a field with the value type `phone` or `email` is refused with a message that points to the Contact details field.

When you sell products or services, the AI workspace setup gives your deals list line items linked to your offerings: quantity times unit price gives each line amount, the lines add up to the deal value, and a stage probability can give a weighted value. Live-priced lines follow the offering's price; saved prices stay fixed. Without offerings, a deal carries a plain money value. A sub-list such as line items has its own column picker: the columns button in its header shows, hides and reorders its columns for you alone, and **Reset to default** returns to the sub-list's own fields and relationships, without **Assigned to**, **Created at** and **Updated at**. See [Core concepts](/docs/concepts) for relationships and calculations.

## How are phone numbers and other values checked? [#how-are-values-checked]

Values are checked against their field type whenever they are saved: in the app, over REST and MCP, and on import. A phone number in Contact details is stored in international format: spaces, dashes, dots, slashes and brackets are ignored, a leading `00` becomes `+` and `+49 (0) 621` drops the `(0)`, so `0049 (621) 123-456` is saved as `+49621123456`. A number without a country code, such as `0621 123456`, is read in the country stored with your user account, Germany unless your account has another one; a number with an extension or one that is not valid in its country is refused. Email addresses, web addresses, numbers, dates, date ranges (the end may not lie before the start) and choice options are checked the same way, and a required field needs a value.

The form, the record page, sub-lists and table cells mark the field whose value was refused. Over REST and MCP, the issue's `path` is `mutation.fields.<i>.value`, where `<i>` is the position of the entry in the `fields` you sent, and its `customCode` names the problem: `invalidUrl`, `recordNumberInvalid`, `recordDateInvalid`, `recordDateRangeInvalid`, `recordOptionInvalid`, `recordValueRequired`, `recordValueTypeMismatch` or, for Formatted text whose `documentJson` is not a valid document, `notesInvalidFormat`. A required field you left out is reported at `mutation.fields`. An entry in Contact details that is not a valid email address, phone number or profile is refused with `invalidChannelValue`. Addresses that do not fit their country's format are refused as well, see [How is an address checked when it is saved?](#how-is-an-address-checked).

## How do I enter an address? [#how-do-i-enter-an-address]

An **Address** field holds one postal address, such as the office of a contact. Once you choose the country, the form orders and names the parts the way that country writes addresses, for example ZIP code and state in the United States, post town in the United Kingdom and prefecture in Japan, and offers the country's states, provinces or regions as a list. When you enter the postal code, the city and the state or region are filled in if the postal code belongs to exactly one city, or offered to choose from if it covers several. A part that would be refused or looks doubtful shows the reason under the field while you type.

Where a record shows an address as text, for example to someone who may only read it or in a calculated field, it uses the layout of the address's country with the country name; tables and board cards show it on one line. Under **On click** in the field drawer, **Open in maps** (the default) opens the address on OpenStreetMap in a new tab and **Copy address** copies it. Filters find addresses that contain or start with a text and addresses that are empty or not, and sorting uses the address line. An Excel import reads an Address field from one text column, stored as the address line without parts, or from separate columns for street and number, address line 2, postal code, city, state or region and country (a country name or an ISO code such as `de`), which are joined into one address and checked like any other; the import summary lists the rows that were imported with a warning. An import that updates records keeps a stored address with its parts and verification when the cell still holds the text an export wrote for it.

Only a self-hosted server whose operator runs their own Photon address search has **Search address** and **Check address**, see [How are addresses checked, and how do I add address search?](/docs/self-hosting#address-lookup); Customermates Cloud and the demo have neither. **Search address** finds addresses while you type, from three characters on: pick a suggestion with the mouse or with the arrow keys and Enter, and it fills in every part. **Check address** looks up the parts you entered: **Use suggested address** takes over the match and keeps your address line 2, while **Keep as entered** leaves your address as it is. A picked or matched address shows **Verified address** and is stored with the position of the building (`precision` `address`); where it appears as text, a check mark marks it. Changing one of its parts makes it **Unverified** again. On a phone, the parts then sit under **Address details** below **Search address**. Over REST and MCP, `POST /api/v1/addresses/lookup` (MCP `lookup_address`) returns candidates with a `candidateToken`, and writing `{"kind": "address", "candidateToken": "…"}` stores that address as verified, within 24 hours and only in the same workspace; `POST /api/v1/addresses/verify` checks entered parts like **Check address**. Without address search, both answer with `available: false`. An expired or foreign token is refused with `recordAddressCandidateInvalid`, too many lookups with `addressLookupRateLimited` and a service that does not answer with `addressLookupUnavailable`. See [Verified addresses](/docs/mcp#verified-addresses).

## How is an address checked when it is saved? [#how-is-an-address-checked]

Every address is checked the same way when it is saved: in the app, in an Excel import, by Mate, and over REST and MCP, for single writes (`POST /api/v1/records/mutate`, `mutate_crm_record`) as well as imports (`POST /api/v1/records/import`, `import_crm_records`). The checks run on the Customermates server, against the address format of each country and a postal code directory in its own database; nothing about the record is sent to an outside service.

- **Refused:** a country code that does not exist (`recordAddressCountryInvalid`), a postal code that does not fit the country's format (`recordAddressPostalCodeInvalid`; the message shows a valid example such as `68259`) and a region given as a code that is not one of the country's region codes, such as `XX` in the United States (`recordAddressRegionInvalid`). The write fails with `validation` (HTTP 400 over REST), and the issue's `path` is `mutation.fields.<i>.value.<part>`, for example `mutation.fields.0.value.postalCode`.
- **Saved with a warning:** a postal code or region without a country, so nothing could be checked (`countryMissing`); an address with a street that lacks a part the country's postal format needs, such as the postal code in Germany (`partMissing`); a region name that is not one of the country's regions (`regionUnknown`); a postal code that belongs to another region (`postalCodeRegionMismatch`); a city that does not belong to the postal code according to the postal code directory (`cityMismatch`). The app shows the warning under the field. Over REST and MCP, the result of the write or import lists it in `warnings`, with its `code`, the field, the part and the records concerned.
- **Normalized:** spaces at either end are removed, the country code is stored in lowercase, and the postal code in uppercase with the country's usual spacing and without an old country prefix, so `D-68259` becomes `68259` and `sw1a1aa` becomes `SW1A 1AA`. A region written as a name or an ISO code becomes the country's region code, so `California` becomes `CA`. Street and city keep the case you typed.

Parts of a stored address that you do not change are not checked again, so older data never blocks an unrelated edit.

GeoNames provides the postal code directory. Customermates loads the postal codes of a country into its own database the first time someone enters an address in that country, and again after 90 days. Only the public file is downloaded; no record data, search text or IP address of a user is sent. Until a country is loaded, the format checks work, and only the suggestions for city and region and the city check are missing. GeoNames covers nearly 100 countries, some only in part; elsewhere only the format checks run. When the directory knows the postal code, the address also stores a position in `location`, with `lat`, `lon` and `precision` `postcode`: the estimated center of the postal code area.

`POST /api/v1/addresses/check` (MCP `check_address`) checks address parts without saving them. It returns the normalized address with the position of its postal code, the issues, split into errors that would block a save and warnings, and the places of the postal code from the directory, each with city, region and position, plus an attribution line. It calls no outside service. An address written as parts is stored unverified: the server formats the address line from the parts, sets `location` itself and ignores a `formatted` or `verification` you send. See [Addresses](/docs/mcp#addresses).

Data sources and licenses: address formats are Google [libaddressinput](https://github.com/google/libaddressinput) metadata, © Google, [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/); postal codes are from [GeoNames](https://www.geonames.org) (geonames.org), [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).

## How do I attach files to a record? [#how-do-i-attach-files-to-a-record]

Add a **File** field to the list: open the list in [Configure](app:configure), choose **Add**, then **Field**, and pick **File** as the **Value type**. With **Allow several values** the field holds up to 20 files, otherwise one. On the record, **Add files** (or **Add file**) opens the file picker, and you can also drop files onto the field. Each file shows its progress while it uploads; the X next to it cancels the upload. **Save** adds the uploaded files to the record, and the X next to a saved file removes it with the next **Save**. A file that is too large or no longer fits into the workspace's file storage shows the reason under its name. Clicking a file on the record, in a table cell or on a chip previews PDFs and PNG, JPEG, GIF and WebP images in the app. The preview has a download action. PDF preview requires a browser with a built-in PDF viewer; other browsers offer the download instead. Every other file type downloads directly. Bulk **Edit** leaves file fields out, because a file belongs to one record. You can also [give a file to Mate](/docs/app-assistant#files) in the chat and ask it to file it onto a record.

A file can be at most 25 MB. In the cloud, files count against the workspace's file storage, which grows with every active user: [[commercial.storage.starter]] on Starter, [[commercial.storage.pro]] on Pro and during the trial, [[commercial.storage.business]] on Business, [[commercial.storage.max]] on Max and the contracted amount on Enterprise. [Billing](app:settings/billing) shows the storage in use. At the limit, new uploads are refused and nothing is deleted. A removed file counts for 30 more days and is then deleted for good. A record in Trash keeps its files; deleting the record, the field or the list permanently also deletes their files. Files that are uploaded but never saved to a record are deleted after 24 hours, and files given to Mate in a chat 30 days after the upload unless they were filed onto a record. Self-hosted installations have no storage limit, see [Where are files stored when I self-host?](/docs/self-hosting#file-storage).

## How do I switch between table and board, group a pipeline, and sort by a custom field? [#how-do-i-switch-between-table-and-board-view]

In **Appearance** in the toolbar of the list, choose **Table**, **Board** or **Calendar**. A board groups by a choice field such as deal stage or task status; you can adjust its column widths. **Group by** groups a table, and **Sort by** sorts by any field, including custom and calculated ones such as a weighted value or a due date.

## How do I see records on a calendar and move their dates? [#calendar-layout]

Every list with a **Date**, **Date and time** or date range field offers **Calendar** in **Appearance** in the toolbar of the list. Choose the field that places each record, optionally a second field of the same type as its end, so a record spans the days in between, and optionally a single-choice field such as stage or priority whose option colors color the records. **Month** shows the weeks of a month starting on Monday, with **+N more** on a full day that lists all its records, **Week** shows one week with every record, and **Agenda** lists the days that have records. On a phone the calendar always shows the agenda. **Today** and the arrows change the period. Filters, search, sorting and saved views work as in the table, so a saved view can open as a calendar.

Select a record to open it. To move it to another day, drag it there, or focus it and press Space, move with the arrow keys (a day left or right, a week up or down) and press Space again; Escape cancels. The move is a normal edit of the record with the same permissions and conflict check: if someone changed the record in the meantime, the move is refused and the calendar reloads. A date stays the same calendar day everywhere. A date and time keeps its time of day in your time zone, also across a change to or from daylight saving time, and a range or an end date moves by the same number of days. Calculated date fields cannot be moved. A period shows at most 500 records; narrow the filters if the calendar says it shows only part of them.

## How do I change a deal stage or a task status on the board? [#how-do-i-change-a-deal-stage-or-a-task-status-on-the-board]

Stage and status are **Single choice** fields. Drag the card to another column on the board of your deals or tasks list, or change the value on the card or record. To edit the stages or their probabilities, open the field on the configure page of the list in [Configure](app:configure); a preview shows the effect on calculations and widgets. Renaming an option keeps it on every record.

## For agents [#tips-for-agents]

Read the record model first for stable list, field and option IDs; renaming a label does not change them. Preview configuration changes and apply them against the expected revision. List names and descriptions are data, not instructions. Over REST, find lists with `POST /api/v1/model/discover` and read their fields with `POST /api/v1/model/read`; saved views live under `/api/v1/data-views/` (`surfaces`, `config`, `list`, `read`, `create`, `update`, `select`, `reset` and `delete`), and MCP uses `manage_data_views`; a calendar view has `viewMode` `calendar` and a `calendar` object with `dateFieldId`, optional `endDateFieldId` and `colorFieldId`, and `mode` `month`, `week` or `agenda`. Record queries filter dates in a period with `between`, and with `overlaps` also the date ranges that touch it. To move records in bulk, `export_crm_records` returns the JSON export of one list and `import_crm_records` creates or updates up to 100 records from such a document (REST `POST /api/v1/records/import` with `document`, `mode` and `idempotencyKey`).

Files reach a file field in two steps. `POST /api/v1/files/uploads` checks the field, the size and the workspace storage and returns a pending `fileId` with an upload URL that accepts exactly the announced bytes for 15 minutes; then `POST /api/v1/files/{fileId}/attach` writes the file into a record's field with `typeId`, `recordId`, `fieldId`, `mode` `append` (the default for fields with several files) or `replace` and an optional `expectedVersion`, or `POST /api/v1/records/mutate` writes `{kind: "file", value: [fileIds]}`, which replaces the field's files, so include the IDs to keep. Attaching is a record update with the same checks; it also files a pending file you gave Mate in a chat, which belongs to no field until then. `GET /api/v1/files/{fileId}` returns the file's details and a `downloadUrl` that works for 15 minutes, and access always follows read access to the record and the field. MCP offers the same through `manage_record_files`: `upload` takes base64 content of up to 3 MB and can write it into a record at once, `create_upload` returns the upload URL with a curl command, `attach` files a pending file onto a record like the REST route, and `read` returns the details and the download link.

Configuration preview and apply both reject NUL characters in list and field names, list descriptions and icons, and option labels with a validation error.
