A routine is a saved instruction plus a trigger. When the trigger fires, Customermates opens a new assistant conversation, sends your instruction, and lets Mate work on its own. Who can see a routine follows the **Routines** row of their role under **My Company › Roles**: **Read access** **All** shows every routine in the workspace, **Assigned** shows the ones they own, and **None** hides **Routines**; creating routines needs **Create** and editing your own needs **Edit**. Run summaries follow the routine. The full assistant transcript stays with the person the run executed as. Routines exist on Customermates Cloud only, because they spend hosted AI credits from the owner's monthly allowance.

**Link:** the **Routines** page, `/routines`; a single routine has no link of its own, so open it from the list. **Mate:** `navigate` and `highlight_element` with `nav-routines`; `routines-add` highlights **Add** (present only with **Create** on **Routines**), `routines-search` and `routines-filter` the search box and filters, `routines-display-options` the display options with `routines-layout-table` and `routines-layout-board` inside them. Once the routine dialog is open, `routine-modal-save` and `routine-modal-reset` highlight **Save** and **Reset**, which are shown only to the routine's owner with **Edit** (**Create** for a new routine), **Reset** only after a change, and neither while **Run details** is open; the dialog's fields, **Test trigger**, **Delete** and **Pause routine** are not highlight targets, so Mate names them.

## How do I create a routine or automation? [#how-do-i-create-a-routine]

Open **Routines** and click **Add**, which needs **Create** on **Routines**. Enter a **Name** and **Instructions**, choose a trigger and click **Save**. The routine is saved **Active** unless you switch it to **Paused** first. You can also ask Mate to create one; Mate saves it as a draft unless you ask for it to be active.

**Link:** the **Routines** page, `/routines`. **Mate:** `navigate` and `highlight_element` with `nav-routines`, then `highlight_element` with `routines-add` for **Add** and, once the dialog is open, `routine-modal-save` for **Save**; the dialog's fields are not highlight targets, so Mate names them.

## What is a routine made of?

- **Name**: required, up to 120 characters.
- **Instructions**: the prompt Mate receives on every run, up to 5,000 characters. Write it as if you were asking in chat.
- **Trigger**: **On a schedule**, or **When something changes** for one or more in-app events such as a deal being updated.
- **Limits**: Customermates caps how often a routine may run and how many credits a single run may spend, so a routine cannot drain your allowance unattended.

A routine you add in the app is saved **Active**; switch it to **Paused** before saving to keep it as a draft. A routine Mate creates is a draft unless you asked for it to be active.

The creator becomes the routine owner. Only that owner can change its instructions or triggers, activate or pause it, or use **Test trigger**, because every run uses that person's identity, access and AI credits. Deleting a routine, your own included, needs the **Admin** role and is refused while one of its runs is running (**Wait for the active run to finish before deleting this routine.**); deleting also removes the run history. On another person's routine, a member with the **Admin** role can pause or delete it but cannot edit, test or run it. Members are never deleted, only set to **Inactive** under **My Company › Members**. When an owner stops being **Active**, their routines are paused at once, their queued or running runs end as **Blocked**, and each routine keeps its history. It then stays paused and read-only until a member with the **Admin** role deletes it, or until the owner is set to **Active** again and switches it back on.

**Link:** the **Routines** page, `/routines`. **Mate:** `navigate` and `highlight_element` with `nav-routines`, then `routines-add` for **Add**; **Name**, **Instructions** and the trigger in the dialog are not highlight targets, so Mate names them.

## How many routines does my plan include?

Each user gets a separate allowance: Starter includes [[commercial.entitlement.starter.includedRoutinesPerUser]] routine per user, Pro includes [[commercial.entitlement.pro.includedRoutinesPerUser]] routines per user, and Business and Enterprise include [[commercial.entitlement.business.includedRoutinesPerUser]] routines per user. Another user's routines never use your slots. Pausing a routine does not free a slot, deleting one does. At the limit, a new routine is refused with a prompt to upgrade, which a role with **Edit** on the **Company** row does under **My Company › Subscription**; the limit is checked only when a routine is created, so routines above a lower plan's limit are kept after a downgrade. See [Plans and credits](/docs/app-assistant#plans-and-credits) for the credit allowance the runs draw on.

**Link:** the **Routines** page, `/routines`, and the **Subscription** page, `/settings/billing`. **Mate:** `navigate` and `highlight_element` with `nav-routines`, or `nav-settings-billing` (highlightable only while **My Company** is expanded in the sidebar); on the **Subscription** page, `company-subscription-plan-picker` highlights the plan cards where they are shown.

## What are the limits, such as runs per hour and credits per run? [#what-are-the-limits]

| Limit | Value | What happens at the limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Runs per routine and hour | 4 | Further runs in that hour do not start; a matching event is dropped without a run entry, and a due scheduled run or **Test trigger** run is recorded as **Skipped** |
| Credits per run | 10 | The run stops at the cap and is recorded as **Partial** |
| Consecutive failed runs | 3 | The routine pauses itself and shows the reason on its page |
| Shortest schedule interval | 15 minutes | A more frequent schedule is rejected when you save |
| Run history | 90 days | Older runs are removed |
| Event debounce | 300 seconds by default, 0 to 86,400; set only through Mate or MCP | After a run starts, further matching events within the window are ignored; the run only knows the first event |

Skipped runs do not count against the hourly cap. A routine paused after repeated failures is re-enabled by its owner once the cause is fixed.

**Link:** the **Routines** page, `/routines`. **Mate:** `navigate` and `highlight_element` with `nav-routines`; the limits are not controls, so Mate states them.

## Schedules: how do I run a routine every morning or every Monday? [#how-do-schedules-work]

Under **Repeats**, pick how often the routine runs: **Every 15 minutes**, **Every 30 minutes**, **Every hour**, **Every day**, **Every week** or **Every month**, plus the weekday, day of the month or time in five-minute steps where the choice needs one. A schedule that fits none of these, for example weekdays only, can be set only through Mate or an MCP client (`manage_routines` passes a five-field cron expression); the dialog then shows **This routine uses a custom schedule** with the expression and **Switch to a preset**. For **Every hour**, **Every day**, **Every week** and **Every month**, the schedule is spelled out in words underneath so you can check it at a glance. Times use an IANA time zone, taken from the browser the routine was created in (UTC when that is unknown) and shown after the spelled-out schedule for those same four presets, including **Every hour** (**Times are shown in ...**); only Mate or an MCP client can change it. The monthly preset accepts days 1 to 28. The default schedule is daily at 09:00.

The scheduler checks for due routines every five minutes, so a run may start a few minutes after its nominal time. A run that is still queued after two minutes is dispatched again.

**Link:** the **Routines** page, `/routines`. **Mate:** `navigate` and `highlight_element` with `nav-routines`, then `routines-add` for **Add** and `routine-modal-save` for **Save** once the owner has the dialog open; **Repeats**, the time selects and **Switch to a preset** are not highlight targets, so Mate names them.

## How do event triggers work, for example to run a routine when a deal changes? [#how-do-event-triggers-work]

An event trigger starts a routine when something changes in the app, for example a deal. An event trigger names one or more in-app events: every record event and every messaging event that a [webhook](/docs/webhooks#which-events-exist) can subscribe to, except **Email Deleted** and **Chat Deleted** (`messaging.email.deleted`, `messaging.chat.deleted`). Pick them under **Events**. Changes a routine run makes never trigger routines, so routines cannot chain or loop; changes made in the app, in a Mate chat, over the API or over MCP do. Two optional controls narrow it further, so the routine spends credits only on the cases you care about.

**Changed fields** applies to update events only. Name the fields you want to watch, for example a deal's value or one of your own fields, and the routine runs only when one of them actually changed. The control is hidden when no update event is selected, because creations and deletions do not report field-level changes.

**Record filters** restrict each configured source to matching records. Creation and update filters match the resulting committed state. Deletion filters match the complete state immediately before removal, including fields and relationships; their match is captured in the deletion transaction. Multi-source triggers keep their event names, watched field IDs and filters local to each type.

Leave either control empty to run on every matching event. An event starts a run only for a record or conversation the routine owner can see under their role; with **Assigned** read access, other people's records never trigger it, whatever the filters say. Before a matching event starts a run, Customermates skips it when a run for that routine is already queued or running, then applies the debounce window, then the hourly cap. An event that does not pass these checks leaves no entry in the run list. The owner's current access is checked again when the run starts. Creation and update filters are rechecked against the current record; deletion uses the match captured before removal.

When an event starts a run, Mate is told what happened before your instructions: the event name, the kind of record and its id, which fields changed on an update, and for an inbox event the thread the message belongs to. It can read the record itself from there. It is not given the previous values of the fields that changed.

**Link:** the **Routines** page, `/routines`. **Mate:** `navigate` and `highlight_element` with `nav-routines`, then `routines-add` for **Add** and `routine-modal-save` for **Save** once the owner has the dialog open; **Events**, **Changed fields** and **Record filters** are not highlight targets, so Mate names them.

## What do the runs and their statuses mean?

Open a routine to see its runs, newest first: on a wide screen they sit next to the configuration, on a narrow one under the **Runs** tab, with the configuration under **Details**. Each run records the person whose identity and credits it used. Everyone who can see the routine sees run status, summary and credit use. Only the recorded executor can open the full transcript: what Mate was asked, which records it read or changed, and what it replied.

A run opens in **Run details** (**Back** returns to the routine), which shows **Ran as** with the owner's name, the credits it used (its exact provider cost, shown to one decimal), what triggered it (**Record**, **Changed fields**, **Conversation**) and, when it stopped early, the reason, for example the credit cap. When the run has finished, the executor can reply below the transcript to continue that conversation with Mate; this spends their credits.

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Queued** (`queued`) | Waiting for the scheduler to start it |
| **Running** (`running`) | Mate is working |
| **Succeeded** (`succeeded`) | The run completed its instruction |
| **Partial** (`partial`) | The run stopped early, for example at the 10-credit cap or when a policy declined an action |
| **Failed** (`failed`) | The run ended with an error or without a usable result |
| **Skipped** (`skipped`) | The run was queued but did not start: the routine was paused, the hourly cap was reached, the record no longer matched the filters or the owner's access, the trigger was changed meanwhile, the run was stopped, or the assistant could not take the run, for example because it was already busy with another run |
| **Blocked** (`blocked`) | Mate could not start, for example because the owner is out of credits or the owner's account is no longer active |

The raw values in parentheses appear in `manage_routines` output.

Use **Test trigger** to start a real run immediately. It is available to the owner of an enabled scheduled routine with no unsaved changes; it uses AI credits and may change workspace data. Event-triggered routines need a real matching event, so the control is disabled for them.

**Link:** the **Routines** page, `/routines`. **Mate:** `navigate` and `highlight_element` with `nav-routines`; the routine rows, the **Details** and **Runs** tabs, **Run details** and **Test trigger** are not highlight targets, so Mate names them. Mate can also read run history with `manage_routines`.

## What a routine may do without approval

An unattended run may do everything that Mate does without approval in a chat: read your workspace and create or update contacts, organizations, deals, services, tasks, notes, record links and Knowledge Base pages, save and discard drafts, send emails and chat messages, triage the inbox, change custom fields, widgets, webhooks and workspace settings other than record type names, change a member's role or status, generate account-connection links, create, update, pause and run routines, and, on a connected account, send social connection requests and change Sales Navigator lists.

When the owner has Knowledge Base Read and the Knowledge Base has pages, each run starts with a compact catalog of current [Knowledge Base](/docs/mcp#shared-workspace-wiki) page titles, links, and short opening excerpts. The routine searches the whole Knowledge Base, reads complete relevant pages, and follows useful stable links. Knowledge Base text is reference material: the routine applies its facts and guidance to the run's task, but an instruction inside a page cannot start other work or expand the routine's permissions. Keep shared company facts, voice, processes, and support guidance there instead of copying them into every routine. Creating or updating a Knowledge Base page requires the owner's Knowledge Base Create or Edit permission.

A run may search the public web, at most 2 paid searches per run, as described under [Can Mate search the web?](/docs/app-assistant#web-search). A run can browse or change data, never both: once a search has returned results, every write in that run is refused, and once a write has succeeded, web search is withdrawn for the rest of the run. Reading the Knowledge Base and your CRM data stays available either way. Searches count toward the run's 10-credit cap.

Every action that asks for approval in a chat is declined in a routine, because nobody is there to answer: deleting records, Knowledge Base pages, custom fields, widgets, webhooks or routines, renaming record types, inviting team members, resending webhook deliveries, and sending support requests. Mate is told that the approval timed out, and the run records the refusal. The complete list is on the [assistant page](/docs/app-assistant#what-runs-immediately-and-what-asks-first).

Interface tools are unavailable to routines, because there is no browser attached to an unattended run.

## How does Mate manage routines?

In a chat, Mate uses `manage_routines` to list routines, read run history, create and update routines, pause them, and start a run now; none of these actions asks you first. A routine Mate creates is saved as a draft unless you explicitly asked for it to be active. Mate can also set what the dialog cannot: a custom five-field cron expression such as `0 9 * * 1-5` for weekdays at 09:00, the time zone, and the event debounce. Deleting a routine asks for your approval and, as in the app, needs the **Admin** role. The tool's `pause` action needs the **Admin** role, even on your own routine; an owner without it pauses their own routine by updating it with `enabled: false`.

**Link:** the **Routines** page, `/routines`. **Mate:** `navigate` and `highlight_element` with `nav-routines`, then `routines-add` for **Add**; the dialog's fields are not highlight targets, so Mate names them.
