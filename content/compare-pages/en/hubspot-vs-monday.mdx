## Is Monday CRM better than HubSpot?

It depends on what your team actually needs. Monday CRM is better if you want visual simplicity, fast setup, and CRM integrated with project management. HubSpot is better if marketing-to-sales alignment, lead scoring, and built-in communication tools (email, VoIP, SMS) are your priorities. Neither is objectively "better", they solve different problems with different trade-offs.

Monday CRM's strength is its intuitive board-and-column interface with drag-and-drop pipelines, 16 preset CRM automations, and customizable scoring. Its weakness is CRM depth: contact enrichment, email sequences, organizational hierarchies, and deal scoring feel adapted from a project management data model rather than built for sales.

HubSpot's strength is its comprehensive marketing-to-sales pipeline with AI-assisted dynamic lead scoring, real-time updates, built-in email, VoIP, SMS, and call recording. Its weakness is cost escalation: features that are standard in other CRMs, custom reporting, workflow branching, A/B testing, require the Professional plan at $90 per seat, plus a one-time onboarding fee of about $1,500.

Customermates gives you dedicated CRM without either compromise. Full pipeline management, contacts, organizations, deals, tasks, services, and n8n automation, plus external AI clients that operate the CRM through native MCP and a unified inbox across email, LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Instagram, and Telegram on Pro and above, all from [[commercial.price.starter.monthly]]/user/month with no core-feature tier gates.

## Is Monday good for CRM?

Monday is good for lightweight CRM when your team already uses Monday.com for project management. The visual pipeline builder, Kanban views, and drag-and-drop interface make it easy to track deals without learning a new tool. Monday CRM includes customizable deal scoring, 16 preset automations on lower tiers, and integrations with Gmail, Outlook, and LinkedIn.

Where Monday falls short is CRM depth. Contact enrichment, email sequences, organizational relationship mapping, and advanced sales analytics are either basic or missing. Automation is quota-capped at 250 actions/month on Standard and 25,000 on Pro. Monday CRM is built on a project management foundation, which means CRM-specific features will always be secondary to the platform's core identity.

For teams whose CRM needs extend beyond basic deal tracking, managing complex sales cycles, organizational hierarchies, service workflows, or detailed contact histories, a purpose-built CRM like Customermates delivers more value at lower cost.

The answer depends on what "better" means for your team. HubSpot excels at marketing-to-sales alignment with native email campaigns, A/B testing, lead capture forms, and AI-assisted dynamic scoring. No other CRM matches its integrated marketing depth.

## The HubSpot cost problem

HubSpot's free CRM is one of the best entry points in the market. Contact management, basic deal tracking, and email logging work well at zero cost for up to 2 users. The problem starts when your team needs more.

The Starter plan at $15/seat/month is reasonable. But Professional at $90/seat/month is where sticker shock hits, plus a one-time onboarding fee of around $1,500. Features most teams consider standard, custom reporting, advanced workflow automation, email sequences, sales forecasting, all require Professional. Enterprise at about $150/seat/month adds predictive lead scoring, advanced permissions, and custom objects.

For a 10-person sales team on Professional, the seats alone are about $900/month, and once Marketing Hub add-ons and marketing-contact charges are included, monthly costs climb further. HubSpot's pricing model rewards starting on the platform but penalizes growth through tier escalation, per-contact charges for marketing features, and onboarding fees.

## The Monday CRM depth problem

Monday CRM is approachable and visually satisfying. Teams set up pipelines quickly using the familiar board interface. For lightweight deal tracking alongside project management, it works. Monday CRM includes 16 preset CRM automations and customizable lead scoring on lower tiers.

The limitation surfaces when teams need real CRM depth. Monday CRM pricing starts with a 3-seat minimum: Basic at $36/month, Standard at $51/month, Pro at $84/month. Individual seat prices are $12, $17, and $28 respectively. Standard is required for email integration and automation (capped at 250 actions/month). Pro unlocks advanced reporting and 25,000 automation actions per month.

More fundamentally, Monday CRM is built on a project management data model. Contact relationships, organizational hierarchies, service management, and deal scoring are not native concepts. Monday AI is still in beta, generating email content and suggesting tasks but not yet matching the sophistication of HubSpot's AI-assisted scoring. Teams that need these capabilities hit a ceiling that no plan upgrade resolves.

## Data protection comparison

HubSpot uses a global data infrastructure with primary hosting in the US. EU data processing addendums are available but do not guarantee exclusive EU residency for all data types. The platform's extensive subprocessor list includes US-based companies, creating compliance complexity under GDPR. For European businesses in regulated industries, this creates ongoing audit requirements.

Monday.com hosts data primarily in the US via AWS. Enterprise customers can request EU data residency, but Standard and Pro plans use US infrastructure. The platform's privacy documentation acknowledges international data transfers, leaving a compliance gap for teams on non-Enterprise plans.

Managed Customermates keeps its application database in an EU region. Its published privacy and subprocessor pages disclose cloud providers and international-transfer safeguards, so this is not an exclusively EU processing claim. Self-hosting lets the operator choose the database region, but connected email, calendar, AI, monitoring, and other services can still process data externally.

HubSpot offers powerful workflow automation, but the capability scales steeply with pricing. Free and Starter plans allow only basic automation.
